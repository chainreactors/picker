As a Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) Registered Practitioner (RP), I frequently encounter government contractors subject to compliance requirements related to Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), including CMMC, who do not understand what CUI is. This post is an attempt to help contractors understand exactly what CUI is and isn’t, when contractors should and should not apply CUI markings to documents, and how to handle CUI.

Anyone interested in a deeper dive into the history, structure, and requirements of the CUI program should take a look at our previous post [Government Contractor’s Ultimate Guide to CUI](https://trustedsec.com/blog/government-contractors-ultimate-guide-to-cui).

TrustedSec is a CMMC RPO with years of experience helping federal contractors implement and assess compliance related to the handling of CUI in accordance with the NIST SP 800-171 requirements under DFARS 252.204-7012 and other contract clauses that predate the CMMC program. If you'd like assistance with compliance, [get in touch with us](https://trustedsec.com/contact)!

## What is CUI

The official definition of CUI at [32 CFR 2002.4(h)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/part-2002#p-2002.4(h)) is very dense and is written primarily for internal government use. To simplify the concept of CUI as much as possible, from a contractor’s perspective, CUI is either of the following:

* Information the contractor receives directly or indirectly from the U.S. government (e.g., via another contractor) that was marked as CUI by the government
* Information the contractor creates for the U.S. government that the government instructs the contractor to apply specific CUI markings to

Any CUI documents, including email, that a contractor receives from the government should be very obvious because [government agencies are required to mark all CUI before disseminating it](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/part-2002#p-2002.16(a)(3)). The markings are standardized as per [32 CFR 2002.20](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/section-2002.20) and the [NARA CUI Marking Handbook](https://www.archives.gov/files/cui/documents/20161206-cui-marking-handbook-v1-1-20190524.pdf), so all CUI should include at least the following before a contractor receives it:

* A banner at the top of each page that says either “CUI” or “CONTROLLED”
* The name of the agency that designated the information as CUI (agency letterhead is acceptable for this purpose)
