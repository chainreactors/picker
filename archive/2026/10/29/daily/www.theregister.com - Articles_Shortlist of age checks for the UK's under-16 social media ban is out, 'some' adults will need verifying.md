The UK’s comms watchdog has put forward four methods of checking netizens aren't kids – dubbed highly effective age assurance (HEAA) – as it prepares for the proposed under-16 social media ban.

The regulator says some adults may not need to prove their over-16-ness to social media platforms that will make inferences about their age based on previous activity, but confirmed that "many" are likely to have to undergo the same verification process.

Big Brother Watch's (BBW) Director Silkie Carlo said: "This plan for a government 'social media ban' is a national ID system by the back door. Both the government and Ofcom have admitted that children will get around the ban, which means the real effect will be normalizing adults and children alike to handing over their passports just to get online."

Ofcom says four methods remain from the seven it initially outlined when the Online Safety Act (OSA) came into force last year.

REG AD

The seven were capable of verifying whether a subject was 18 years old, but there are fewer viable options for determining the age of 16-year-olds, it said today.

REG AD

The methods put forward include:

* Photo-ID matching: Checking information displayed on identity documents and analyzing the photo against a live image of the subject taken during the verification process
* Banking checks: An estimated 90 percent of 16-17-year-olds in the UK have a bank account, which they would have provided their identity documents to open. The information processed by the bank can be used to verify whether someone is aged 16 or older
* Digital ID services: An interesting entry, given how quickly these were circumvented once the OSA age-checking requirements came into force. These third-party services analyze a person’s photo once, and if it matches the age they claim to be, let them verify their age across other services
* Facial age estimation: A method that even Ofcom admitted isn’t quite reliable enough to be used on its own. The regulator said that online services can use technologies to analyze a subject’s facial features to determine their age. The same technologies also have a margin of error of a few years, so online services may wish to use a second method of HEAA to verify the results of this check.
