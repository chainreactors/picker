# 👮 Flock Searches for the LOLs | EFFector 38.16

![](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/effector-green-web.png?itok=fMSatfxr)

Mass surveillance isn't a joke. But police are treating it like one when using automated license plate reader (ALPR) networks. In [our latest EFFector newsletter](https://eff.org/effector/38/16), we're covering [a new EFF report](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/high-crime-lmao-how-cops-are-treating-mass-surveillance-joke?utm_source=effector) on how officers across the country are routinely logging completely nonsensical "reasons" for their Flock searches, including "LOL," "LMAO," and even (yuck) "Sexy."

[JOIN OUR NEWSLETTER](https://www.eff.org/effector/)

For over 35 years, [EFFector](https://eff.org/effector) has been your guide to understanding the intersection of technology, civil liberties, and the law. This issue covers a settlement [enshrining Meta's harmful surveillance](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/metas-17-billion-settlement-bad-deal-teens-and-all-social-media-users?utm_source=effector) into law, [states pushing back against ALPR](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/texas-and-florida-step-back-alprs?utm_source=effector), and how police are [turning our privacy into a punchline](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/high-crime-lmao-how-cops-are-treating-mass-surveillance-joke?utm_source=effector).

Prefer to listen in? EFFector is now available on all major podcast platforms. This time we're asking EFF's Adam Schwartz what has united people against Flock cameras — and how we can make sure that today's backlash leads to lasting change.
