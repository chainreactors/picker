EFF is on the front lines of the fight against tech-enabled tyranny, but we aren't alone. Our team depends on your help to fight back against the surveillance state.

[JOIN EFF](https://supporters.eff.org/donate/spring--DL6)

People around the world are pushing back against the mass surveillance that undermines privacy and free expression for everyone. [You can help during EFF's spring membership drive.](https://supporters.eff.org/donate/spring--DL6)

One of the people who joined the fight for digital rights is EFF client Will Freeman. Will created the website DeFlock.me to reveal the dangers of automated license plate readers ([ALPRs](https://sls.eff.org/technologies/automated-license-plate-readers-alprs))—cameras that collect location data on every vehicle they see and upload that to a massive nationwide police database. Deflock.me turns the tables by enlisting ordinary people to track the locations of tens of thousands of ALPR cameras.

But when the police spy-tech company [Flock Safety went after Will's website](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/02/anti-surveillance-mapmaker-refuses-flock-safetys-cease-and-desist-demand) with legal threats citing trademark law, he saw it for what it was: an attempt to silence critics and dim the light on mass surveillance.

The company will try everything it can to downplay the criticism, but EFF will be right there demanding accountability.
