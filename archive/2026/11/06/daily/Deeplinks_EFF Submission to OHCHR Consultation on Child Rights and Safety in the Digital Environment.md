# EFF Submission to OHCHR Consultation on Child Rights and Safety in the Digital Environment

![two kids on a huge laptop, spied on by an eye in magnifying glass](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/ageverification-banner2-3a.png?itok=5FZm5TQ7)

*“**The safety of young people in the digital environment cannot be assessed without also considering the safety of young people in offline environments. For millions of young people around the world, the digital environment is their safe place, their haven from an unsafe physical environment, community, or family situation. The digital environment may be the best, perhaps only, place for them to find critical safety information and community, to explore ideas that are controversial in their families and larger physical environments, and to otherwise nourish their personal autonomy.*

*“For these young people, regulatory efforts that limit their access to online services or to certain features they find valuable, make their lives, in total, less safe, even if there has been some marginal safety benefit to their digital environment.*

*“The OHCHR must thus consider the whole of young persons' lives in any recommendations that come out of this process and not treat ‘safety, physical or mental health, and rights in the digital environment’ as disconnected from safety, physical or mental health, and rights elsewhere.”*

So begins [EFF’s submission](https://www.eff.org/files/2026/11/06/eff_submission_to_ohchr_consultation_on_child_safety_.pdf) to the global Call for Inputs on [Child Rights and Safety in the Digital Environment](https://www.ohchr.org/en/calls-for-input/2026/call-inputs-child-rights-and-safety-digital-environment) conducted by the United Nations Office of the High Commissioner on Human Rights. The OHCHR is preparing a report on the issue to be presented to the UN Human Rights Council at its 64th session in the first quarter of 2027. The Human Rights Council requested the OHCHR to solicit input from a wide range of stakeholders, including child and youth perspectives, and convene five regional consultations to assess the risks to the safety of the child in the digital environment and best practices to address these risks in different geographical areas, bearing in mind current and emerging business models.
