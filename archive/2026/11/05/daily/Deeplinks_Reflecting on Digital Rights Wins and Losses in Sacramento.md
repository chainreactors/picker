# Reflecting on Digital Rights Wins and Losses in Sacramento

![Bear hugging CA state, with lightning bolts on plum bg](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/california-bear2023.png?itok=dZuTxNn6)

California’s 2026 legislative session has officially adjourned and it’s time to reflect on the wins and losses that have shaped Californians’ digital rights landscape this year. This was the second year of a two-year legislative session, which meant several bills [resumed their legislative paths from 2025](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/12/trends-watch-california-legislature).

EFF monitored nearly 100 bills in the state over the two-year session. These included proposed regulations on 3D printers, access to social media platforms, police, commercial, and workplace surveillance practices, AI, and other privacy concerns. While we saw some victories, alarming developments also raised serious concerns about the future of privacy protection in the state.

## **Celebrating Wins**

### **Use of automated decision-making and surveillance in the workplace**

Governor Newsom signed vital protections on the use of automated decision-making in the workplace and workplace surveillance tools, specifically [No Robo Bosses](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/09/yes-californias-no-robo-bosses-act) ([SB 947](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billStatusClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB947)) and [AB 1883](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB1883).

Championed by our [labor partners](https://calaborfed.org/press-releases/governor-gavin-newsom-signs-california-federation-of-labor-unions-sponsored-bills-regulating-ai-in-the-workplace/), [SB 947](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billStatusClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB947) limits how employers can use automated decision-making to fire or discipline workers, including provisions that ensure workers get vital information about automated decisions that affect them, and that an algorithm is not the only authority for these consequential decisions. Alongside it, [AB 1883](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB1883) prohibits the use of workplace surveillance to predict emotions or collect neural data. This stops employers from using potentially biased, invasive, and exploitative forms of AI surveillance at the workplace, marking an important step toward creating workplaces that respect the privacy and autonomy of workers. We look forward to continuing to ensure that technology in California’s workplaces is deployed fairly, thoughtfully, and with respect for all workers.
