Problems that AI cannot solve, are problems of our own making. These problems will not be solved by wishful thinking. It will take time. More importantly, it will take education, training and funding. This is not possible, without educated and wise leadership. That does not exist. Patience, understanding and humility are skills sorely lacking in politics.

Not only are there direct harms arising from cuts to departments and agencies, indirect impacts have proven detrimental to science and research in the United States, as flow-over effects take their toll. Terminations of grants for scientific research, that were originally mandated by Congress, have knock-on effects that hamper innovation. This creates skills shortages in areas that are fundamental to maintaining key sectors of the economy.

Staff in U.S. government agencies and departments have already experienced the effects. Burnout, institutional memory and experience loss, significant capability depreciation, equipment failure, lack of maintenance and resources needed to meet the requirements for properly functioning institutions. Similar issues will impact all sectors of the American economy, including agriculture, manufacturing and the tech industry. The current policies set by the White House will make the problem worse, reducing competitiveness of U.S. firms.

The level of damage done to U.S. scientific research will have a very detrimental effect.
<https://fedscoop.com/records-show-white-house-doge-involvement-in-national-science-foundation-grant-terminations/>

The review panels of the **Australia’s National Health and Medical Research Council** were dismantled in the name of efficiency. This has left Australia without the qualified peer-review and funding panels that would normally discern the credibility of research, and offer feedback and guidance where needed. Without rigorous quality control, publications are flooded with submissions of a low caliber. Making it hard to distinguish genuine advances in scientific research, from the numerous other papers that offer little.

**Australia provides an example of how funding cuts have hobbled science and research.** Nearly 40% of early and medium-term researchers are considering leaving academia due to the difficulty of securing funding. In the education sector, a lack of science students is creating large shortages in workforce capability. Current and past government policies have ensured a lack of science facilities and STEM subjects to fill that gap. Over the next decade Australia will struggle to innovate due to a skills shortage crisis.
