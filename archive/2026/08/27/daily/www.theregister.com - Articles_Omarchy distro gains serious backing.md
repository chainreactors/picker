The controversial Omarchy distro is attracting both criticism and fans – and financial support, too.

[Omarchy](https://omarchy.org/) is an opinionated respin of Arch Linux and a pet project of Ruby on Rails creator David Heinemeier Hansson, better known as DHH. Although the [first release](https://world.hey.com/dhh/omarchy-is-out-4666dd31) was as recent as June last year, this week sees the release of [Omarchy 4.0.1](https://github.com/basecamp/omarchy/releases/tag/v4.0.1) – a security fix for the mid-August [Omarchy 4 “Quattro”](https://github.com/basecamp/omarchy/releases/tag/v4.0.0).

Although it started out just over a year ago, Omarchy now has a sibling project [Omakub](https://omakub.org/), which is based on Ubuntu, and an organization behind it called [Omacom](https://learn.omacom.io/3/omacom). A week ago, DHH announced the launch of the [Omacom Foundation with $8 million](https://omarchy.org/news/2026/08/omacom-foundation-launches-with-8-million/). Its founding patrons include figures behind Shopify, Stripe, Dell, Block, Cloudflare, Sesame, and 37signals - [closely followed](https://omarchy.org/news/2026/08/omacom-foundation-funding-hits-10m/) by people behind Dropbox and OpenClaw, taking the total to $10 million.

The project, like the man behind it, is controversial. But that means attention, and an “opinionated distro” gets opinionated reviews. Some of the criticism is strong stuff: for instance, [Merchants of Insecurity](https://happyfellow.bearblog.dev/merchants-of-insecurity/), whose top line is:

REG AD

“First, a PSA: **Do NOT use Omarchy if you care about security of your machine even a little bit.**”

Author “One Happy Fellow” is not the first: last year, a member of Framework's community forums posted [Omarchy is not a secure distribution and should be taken off the Linux installation options](https://community.frame.work/t/omarchy-is-not-a-secure-distribution-and-should-be-taken-off-the-linux-installation-options/77363). Others [like it](https://dev.to/fstrube/is-omarchy-any-good-3069) or give it a [guarded thumbs up](https://tedium.co/2025/10/13/omarchy-linux-distro-commentary/) while saying it’s not for them.

There really is no such thing as bad publicity. As P. T. Barnum [put it](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2020/01/20/spell-name/):

"Say anything you like about me, but spell my name right."

DHH is no stranger to controversy. We suspect he doesn’t mind at all. *The Register* [reported in late 2025](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/10/14/framework-flame-war-erupts-over-linux-controversy/1466815) that Framework, known for its repairable laptops, was sponsoring Omarchy and Hyprland, and in turn, multiple people criticized Framework for sponsoring such controversial projects. That piece linked to some of the criticism of DHH, but he has been attracting criticism [since at least 2014](https://tomstu.art/the-dhh-problem).
