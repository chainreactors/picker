# Dario Is a Player, Not the Game

He talked about what might happen when AI takes the work, and people heard a plan

November 22, 2026

by Daniel Miessler

People act like [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/), or Dario, is making this happen. He’s not. **He’s a player in the game, for sure, but the game was going to take place with or without him.**

He has decided to play because he thinks that he can help steer it in a positive direction. You are free to do the same.

📚 Dario laid out his own picture of the good version of this future in [Machines of Loving Grace](https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace).

People confuse talking about this video game virtual reality thing as a possible future, or a possible pressure release, as being a global plan which he is enacting. That is not what he’s doing, and is not what he’s saying.

What he is saying is that when work is taken away through automation, there will be a loss in meaning, and that [some people will find meaning through super elaborate and super advanced games](/blog/gaming-is-metaverse-1-0). We have known this for decades. There is tons of sci-fi about this, in both books and movies.

🎮 [Ready Player One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready_Player_One) is the famous example: a broken world where most people live inside a game.
> Much of humanity’s meaning will move to the virtual world. And gaming companies will move more towards infinitely sized, constantly-evolving game worlds that mimic reality in many ways.
>
> [Some Thoughts Going Into 2021 (January 2021)](/blog/some-thoughts-going-into-2021)

## Describing a future isn’t building it [​](#describing-a-future-isn-t-building-it)

It’s really extraordinary that when someone says, “hey, this might be an implication of this future that is unfolding in front of us”, people immediately assume the person who mentions it is trying to make the dystopian version of that thing happen.

First, **saying that this is a potential future that could come of this doesn’t mean you’re trying to make it happen**. And second, even if they were trying to make it happen, it doesn’t mean they’re trying to make the dystopian version.

The amount of cynicism about this guy and about AI in general is just remarkable. And I’m starting to think there might be a lot of reflection going on. Like maybe they assume that’s what someone would do with such a thing because it’s what they would do with such a thing.

There are actually good actors in all of this. Maybe try harder to see them.

## Summary [​](#summary)

1. The AI race was going to happen with or without Dario
2. He said some people might find meaning in advanced games once automation takes the work
3. That idea has been around for decades in sci-fi, and in my own posts too
4. Talking about a possible future is a different thing from trying to make it happen
5. I think some of the cynicism is people assuming he'd do what they would do
