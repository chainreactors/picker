The cyberattacks on commercial refrigerators and freezers in Europe continue, as a slew of grocery stores in Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg, and Sweden report widespread food wastage as a result of miscreants raising the temperatures to highs of 50ºC (122°F).

In Belgium, around 40 Delhaize stores were affected after the attackers reportedly switched fridges and freezers into defrost mode overnight, between Sunday and Monday, resulting in stock issues.

Diego Louwers, a Proxy Delhaize store manager in Overijse, near Brussels, told [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/conso/ne-peut-pas-avoir-pire-dans-notre-metier-les-magasins-belges-victimes-dune/2026-11-02/article/805251) that the consequences of the cyberattacks were "catastrophic," forcing the store to throw out the entirety of its fresh and frozen products.

"We can't have anything worse in our line of work," he said. "We have to throw away all of our merchandise, and it's both heartbreaking and very complicated operationally."

A Delhaize spokesperson said it took steps to ensure stores remain stocked, although fresh and frozen products are "temporarily somewhat limited."

"On Monday, 2 November, we identified an IT incident affecting the refrigeration systems in several dozen of our stores," they said.

"We immediately took the necessary measures to keep our stores operational and ensure that our customers can continue to do their shopping as usual.

"In the affected stores, the range of fresh and frozen products is temporarily somewhat limited. Our distribution centres are operating normally, and there has been no disruption to deliveries to our stores.

"We are, of course, continuing to ensure that our products are stored and presented under optimal conditions, so that we can provide our customers with the quality and freshness they have come to expect from us."

Around 15 Carrefour stores are also reportedly affected, as are sites operated by Intermarché, Spar, and Shop & Go stores located at Q8 service stations.

Belgium’s De Laet & Van Haver chain of butcher shops reported temperature rises in its appliances too, but followed up with a statement on Monday saying there was no impact on its produce, and all stores remain open.

Simultaneously, similar issues have affected Swedish grocery chain ICA, which confirmed attacks on a "small number of stores."
