Nine countries including the UK have signed a US declaration pledging to secure supply chains for AI tech, as pressure grows for tighter export controls following the high-profile resignation of Nvidia CEO [Jensen Huang](https://www.theregister.com/legal/2026/11/13/singapore-says-us-authorities-and-supermicro-hardly-helped-investigation-of-jensen-huangs-chinese-friend/5309579).

The US is firming up its Pax Silica initiative on AI and supply chain security. Under the [San Francisco declaration](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesman/2026/11/the-san-francisco-declaration-on-the-secure-foundations-of-enduring-prosperity/), signed on November 15, the coalition says it is entering a new phase that turns shared principles into a working partnership with shared responsibilities.

Pax Silica was announced in December last year, ostensibly for the participants to work together on vital parts of the global technology supply chain, including frontier foundation models, compute and semiconductors, advanced manufacturing, minerals refining and processing, and energy.

REG AD

But the list of participants was notable for not including one country – China – leading many to suspect that Pax Silica is yet another effort by Washington to squeeze out its main rival in the global technology and economic stakes. Hong-Kong based South China Morning Post [refers to it](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3374393/trump-press-southeast-asia-nations-join-pax-silica-asean-summit-manila) as Trump's "campaign to China-proof AI and chip supply chains."

REG AD

The latest declaration states that "the security of our peoples cannot be separated from the security of our economies and those of our closest partners," and sets out what must be protected, as well as committing to "protect it together."

It asserts that protection should cover key foundational technologies essential to economic security, listing "software, materials, knowledge, components and inputs…essential to AI supply chains."

This includes anything that "theft, illicit transfer, or acquisition by hostile actors would confer a meaningful military or intelligence advantage, or undue economic leverage over the participants, or whose unauthorized transfer or misuse could pose significant risks to the economic and national security of the participants."
