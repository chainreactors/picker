A phone’s push notifications can contain a significant amount of information about you, your communications, and what you do throughout the day. They’re important enough to government investigations that [Apple and Google now both require a judge’s order](https://apnews.com/article/apple-push-notification-data-court-order-4dc52e631220a987022a528c08d2c457) to hand details about push notifications over to law enforcement, and even with that requirement Apple [shares data on hundreds](https://www.apple.com/legal/transparency/push-token.html) of users. More recently, we also learned from a 404 Media report that law enforcement forensic extraction tools [can unearth](https://www.404media.co/fbi-extracts-suspects-deleted-signal-messages-saved-in-iphone-notification-database-2/) the text from deleted notifications, including those from secure messaging tools, like Signal. The good news is that you can mitigate some of this risk.
