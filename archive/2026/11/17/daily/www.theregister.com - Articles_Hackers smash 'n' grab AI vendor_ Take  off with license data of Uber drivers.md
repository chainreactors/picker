Uber has confirmed that nearly 150,000 people had their personal information compromised after hackers broke into one of its AI suppliers.

A[filing with Oregon's attorney general](https://justice.oregon.gov/consumer/databreach/) puts the number of victims at 149,346. Among the data the crooks got their hands on were images of drivers' licenses and information extracted from them, including names, home addresses, dates of birth, and license numbers.

Uber doesn't name the supplier in its filings with US state attorneys general, but told [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/uber-confirms-hackers-stole-thousands-of-drivers-license-information-in-data-breach/) it was Fireworks AI, the inference outfit whose security woes we[covered last week](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/11/10/fireworks-ai-gets-burned-twice-as-customer-data-exposure-widens/5308722).

REG AD

According to a[breach notification filed with Iowa's attorney general,](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/media/cms/11102026_Uber_Technologies_Inc_089721E61CBAB.pdf) Fireworks alerted Uber to the intrusion on October 9. Uber cut off the supplier's access to its data and, four days later, established that the attackers had accessed drivers' personal information on October 7.

REG AD

Uber had set up a dedicated cloud storage environment for the AI biz, complete with security monitoring overseen by its own cloud security team. Unfortunately for Uber and its drivers, the crooks were using legitimate credentials, so none of the alarms went off.

The ride-hailing firm says those credentials couldn't be used to access its other systems, and says it has since revoked or changed the credentials associated with the supplier.

Uber hasn't said why it handed images of thousands of driver's licenses to an AI inference outfit. The Register has put that question to both companies, and asked Uber if any drivers outside the US had their data exposed.

Fireworks, which provides infrastructure for developers to run and fine-tune AI models, had a particularly miserable October. The company first disclosed a security incident on October 9 after discovering that someone had been using its internal credentials without permission.

Fireworks initially said it had no evidence that the intruders had gotten their hands on personal information or customer datasets. The company was hacked again later that month and, on November 9, disclosed that some customer data had indeed been compromised.
