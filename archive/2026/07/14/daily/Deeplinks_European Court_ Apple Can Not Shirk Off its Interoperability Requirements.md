One of the best bulwarks against monopoly is interoperability—that is making a new product or service work with an existing product or service. Interoperability allows users, and not the manufacturers of their devices or largest player in a market, to decide what application best serves them. Unsurprisingly, companies like Apple have worked hard to resist interoperability requirements.

On July 8, the General Court of the  European Union (General Court) [ruled](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-07/cp260096en.pdf) against Apple in several cases the company brought against the European Commission ([joint cases](https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/tout?lang=EN&searchTerm=%2522T%252D1080%252F23%2522)), affirming the company’s obligations under the Digital Markets Act (DMA). Apple argued in the cases that it should be exempted from the law’s requirements *especially with regards to interoperability* on multiple grounds. We applaud the General Court’s  decision, and congratulate the Free Software Foundation Europe (FSFE) as well as others who [intervened in support of the Commission against Apple's attempt](https://fsfe.org/news/2026/news-20260708-01.en.html) to shirk off its responsibilities, thus ensuring fair competition in European markets.
