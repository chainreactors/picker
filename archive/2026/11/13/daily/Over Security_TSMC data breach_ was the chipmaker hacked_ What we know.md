TSMC, the world’s largest contract chipmaker, confirmed on 12 November 2026 that personal data on some current and former employees has been posted online without authorisation. It says the data matches records held by a cloud service provider and that it has found no evidence its own systems were breached. A seller on two cybercrime forums wants 3 bitcoin for what it claims are 46,000 employee records.

TSMC has not named the vendor, said how many people are affected or explained how the data was taken. Ransomnews checked how often TSMC logins turn up in infostealer logs, the usual raw material for break-ins at cloud services. TSMC barely registers: 37 staff logins, against 1,567 for Intel and 2,146 for Samsung. If a stolen login was involved, the place to look is the vendor, which nobody has named.

### In brief

* TSMC confirmed on 12 November 2026 that data on some current and former employees was posted on an external website. It says the data matches records in a cloud service provider’s system and that its own systems show no sign of compromise.
* A seller on DarkForums and BreachForums claims 46,000 records in 6.9 GB and wants 3 bitcoin, about NT$7.4 million. Those numbers are the seller’s and have not been confirmed.
* A sample reportedly shows 19 fields per person, including national ID numbers, home addresses, bank details and emergency contacts.
* TSMC has not named the vendor, the number of people affected or the way in. It says production and customer data are not involved.
* The Alerts.bar index that Ransomnews licenses holds 37 infostealer-captured logins for TSMC staff addresses, one of them from the last 30 days. TSMC ranks 14th of the 18 chipmakers we checked.
* The seller threatened to publish everything if no buyer appeared within a week. The listing spread on social media on 11 November, which puts the deadline around 18 November.
