## Rewiring Democracy Series on The Renovator

Nathan E. Sanders and I are writing a series of essays on real-world examples of democratic technologies for The Renovator. I haven’t been posting the full text on the blog because they’re a bit long, but here are links.

[Part 1](https://therenovator.substack.com/p/rewiring-democracy-now) is about the Japanese digital democracy party, Team Mirai.

[Part 2](https://therenovator.substack.com/p/rewiring-democracy-now-switzerland) is about the Swiss Public AI model, Apertus.

[Part 3](https://therenovator.substack.com/p/rewiring-democracy-ai-and-the-struggle) is about the civic technologists of Open Knowledge Brazil.

And the new one, [Part 4](https://www.schneier.com/essays/archives/2026/08/rewiring-democracy-citizen-science-leads-to-civic-ai-in-scotland.html), is about civic AI in Scotland.

@Clive

I beware everything the government does. It is not on “the peoples’ side”. It is a self-sustaining system that is on its own side, constrained only by imperfectly (and after-the-fact) enforced constitutional dogma. An analogue version of AI, if you will.

I can imagine a future in which the practice of voluntarily applying for means-tested benefits is replaced with AI-powered stimulus to a machine-identified subset of the population. Sounds super efficient, but in reality is prone to all sorts of malevolence.

Although it is no longer socially acceptable to say so, there is no agreement about either what a disability is (physical only, or mental too?; partial or complete?) or what role the state should play in paying the way for the disabled.

On the one hand, social programs prevent poverty. On the other hand, they also prevent those who benefit from them from accumulating any private property (lest they lose their eligibility) and that effectively institutionalizes them in much the same way they would be institutionalized were they in an open-air prison.

The other thing one cannot say for fear of being branded socially unacceptable is that every system worries about malingering. So there is an endless stream of data which attempts to catch cheaters. Imagine what one could do with AI to feed an algorithm designed to decide that someone is not really disabled enough to qualify for further benefits. And maybe even make them pay back what was already given to them — hence RoboDebt, as you say.
