DEEPLINKS BLOG

By [Cooper Quintin](/about/staff/cooper-quintin) and [India McKinney](/about/staff/india-mckinney)

October 23, 2026

![](https://www.eff.org/files/banner_library/OG-GovernmentHacking-Playpen.png)

# Computer Security Is About to Get a Lot Worse, Here’s What You Can Do

![](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/OG-GovernmentHacking-Playpen.png?itok=jXSYxEXb)

It is a commonly held truth among computer geeks that there is no such thing as bug free software, or a secure computer. Any piece of software that anyone actually uses [will have a security vulnerability somewhere in the code](https://hackaday.com/2026/04/15/doom-on-a-fancy-smart-toaster/). The more complex that software is, the more likely there will be a vulnerability. Fortunately for everyone, finding severe vulnerabilities was generally considered an expensive and difficult task requiring years of training and deep knowledge of how computers and software worked. Until recently there was a steady background hum of vulnerabilities being found and patched, with major vulnerabilities that could cripple the entire internet only happening every few years. Now that is no longer the case.

You may have noticed your nerdiest friends freaking out about the “Vulnerability Apocalypse” this week and waving their arms in panic about something called PHP. You may have also noticed that some of your favorite websites were down for a bit. In the last two weeks of October alone, there have been multiple, potentially internet-breaking vulnerabilities reported and patched. Many of those vulnerabilities were in the open-source software that is the foundation of the vast majority of the internet.

**For more detailed information on what happened, and why supporting open-source projects matters, [check out our other blog](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/vulnerability-apocalypse-and-why-we-should-fund-open-source).**

This is all because frontier AI models, such as those created by OpenAI, Anthropic, and others, have made it incredibly cheap and easy to find previously unknown vulnerabilities (zero-days) in open- and closed-source code. **Three percent of all software vulnerabilities ever found in history were found just in the month of October. This year alone researchers have found nearly a quarter of all software security issues which have ever been discovered.**

If exploiting critical software is now cheaper and easier than ever, what does that mean for us as individuals and what does that mean for society as a whole? **TL; DR**: It’s going to get worse before it gets better. But you can protect yourself with basic digital security hygiene that is now more important than ever.

### **What Do I Need to Do?**

There absolutely needs to be a bigger conversation about how we got here and how to fix the systemic issues involved, including how corporations that depend on open-source code should contribute to their upkeep, and how big tech should be regulated.
