# Self-hosted does not have to mean stagnant

A self-hosted SOC can keep pace with modern threats when customers have control over deployment... and full confidence in what comes next.

![Three figures' silhouettes are visible in an abstract photo with overlapping shades of green, pink, and green, as a metaphor for the perimeter that is built with Sekoia self-hosted.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69b190e7f47e4c44b0ee07d8/6ad9d5868cd7b64b3a340a2d_blog-self-hosted-soc-platform-principles-img-cover%20(2).webp)

## Key takeaways

Self-hosting gives security teams control over their environment while keeping the platform current, supported, and ready for change.

* You decide where the platform runs and when updates are applied, while the provider keeps the product and detection content moving forward.
* Updates should be signed, predictable, and clear enough to test and schedule without turning every release into a project.
* With clear responsibilities and release plans, teams know what to expect and can keep their security operations moving.

[Self-hosting](/glossary/self-hosted) should put your SOC in control, not leave it stuck with yesterday’s detection. The platform still needs to keep pace with threats, make responsibilities clear, and improve without slowing your team down.

That standard is achievable. A credible self-hosted platform can support modern security operations when it is designed around five principles:

1. Secure deployment
2. Signed and predictable updates
3. Current detection content
4. Clear operational ownership
5. A transparent release model.

Self-hosting changes who manages the infrastructure. It should not reduce the platform’s ability to keep pace with the threat environment.

## Self-hosted means control over the operating environment

Organizations choose [self-hosted deployment](/blog/the-best-of-cloud-built-safely-inside-your-own-perimeter) for practical reasons. They may need to keep security data within a specific jurisdiction, operate in a restricted network, meet internal infrastructure requirements, or maintain direct control over platform access and administration.

Those requirements are common in large enterprises, public-sector organizations, regulated industries, and environments with limited connectivity.

The decision comes with responsibility on both sides.

* **The organization:** Infrastructure, access, maintenance, and availability
* **The platform provider:** Product quality, detection content, security fixes, documentation, and release discipline

A strong self-hosted model makes those responsibilities clear. It gives the customer control without leaving them to build and maintain the entire security platform alone.
