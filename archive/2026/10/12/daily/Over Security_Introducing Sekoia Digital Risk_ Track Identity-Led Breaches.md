# With Sekoia Intelligence, we gave you half the picture. Here's the other half.

Stolen credentials can pass through several hands before an attacker uses them to access your organization, and each step leaves clues security teams can act on.

![Abstract close-up of overlapping translucent green glass panes with blurred shadows and photographic grain, suggesting hidden information gradually coming to light.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69b190e7f47e4c44b0ee07d8/6accd98988f797aab238d211_blog-with-sekoia-intelligence-we-gave-you-half-the-picture-heres-the-other-half-img-cover.webp)

## Key takeaways

An identity-led breach often starts long before the first suspicious login, so security teams need visibility into both the exposed identity and the threat actor using it.

* Infostealers collect passwords, session cookies and browser data from personal devices.
* Stolen logs move through Telegram channels, log markets and access brokers.
* Sekoia Digital Risk helps identify exposed credentials and track where they surface.
* Sekoia CTI connects each exposure to the actors, infrastructure and malware behind it.
* Bringing both views together helps teams prioritize detection and response.

In the spring of 2024, customers of the cloud data platform Snowflake started reporting data theft. Ticketmaster. Santander. AT&T. Advance Auto Parts. By the time [Mandiant finished its investigation](https://www.securityweek.com/snowflake-attacks-mandiant-links-data-breaches-to-infostealer-infections/), at least 165 organisations were affected and hundreds of millions of records were on the market.

Our own Threat Detection & Research team has since traced the full arc of the group behind it, from the Snowflake intrusions to the ShinyHunters extortion operation that followed. Their write-up on [ShinyHunters](/blog/gotta-breach-em-all-the-journey-of-shinyhunters) is worth your time. But what's striking, reading it, is how unremarkable the initial technique was. No vulnerability in Snowflake itself. The attackers, tracked as UNC5537, had simply bought credentials. Those credentials had been harvested by infostealer malware, in some cases years earlier, from the personal and contractor machines of people who had logged into Snowflake. Some of the logs dated back to 2020. The accounts had no multi-factor authentication, the passwords had never been rotated, and the attackers walked in.
