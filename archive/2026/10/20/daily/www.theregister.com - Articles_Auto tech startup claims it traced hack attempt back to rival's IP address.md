An automotive tech startup claims that its IT systems were attacked by individuals trying to steal its driving model, and that those attacks came from an IP address associated with a rival in the space.

The tech startup, IQ.Lvbs, described the alleged security incident in a recent [blog post](https://iqlvbs.com/blog/novaspark-provenance-security-incident/).

On October 3, IQ.Lvbs introduced the private beta of[NovaSpark](https://discord.com/channels/469524606043160576/538741329799413760/1556143169756856411), the first model for the company's IQ.Pilot app, in the [Comma.ai](http://comma.ai) Discord channel. They put it in that channel because the model requires the use of Comma.ai's camera hardware, which is built for self-driving vehicles.

From there, things got weird.

"From October 7 to 14, we faced repeated attempts to steal NovaSpark, access users’ devices and private driving files, and exploit our sign-in system," the IQ.Lvbs blog post claims. "The first requests came from an IP assigned to Comma.ai Inc."

In addition, on October 8 – shortly after the alleged attacks began – somebody banned IQ.Lvbs' team from the Comma.ai Discord channel. Posts on the IQ.Pilot Discord channel indicate that individuals doing private beta testing for IQ.Lvbs have also been banned from that channel.

One of the three people involved in IQ.Lvbs, a UI/UX designer who asked to be identified only as "Cat," speculated to The Register about the reasons for the alleged hack and Discord ban.

"Our model, NovaSpark, is a good deal ahead of Comma's latest small and large models, which directly threatens their core business of making AI models good enough to sell their hardware," he said. "Comma for the most part has been stagnant in their models' improvement and that was a main driver for us making NovaSpark."

An [October 7, 2026 Discord message](https://discord.com/channels/469524606043160576/954493346250887168/1557559085489193011) from user Torq\_boi, said to be Harald Schäfer, Comma.ai’s CTO, [implies NovaSpark was distilled](https://discord.com/channels/469524606043160576/624672116515536906/1557448729592725576) from Comma.ai's Rebellious Hope model.

“I’m glad people are training models and experimenting with this stuff," the post starts. "But don’t lie to people about what you’re doing, and saying it’s not a finetune of existing comma models when it is. That’s not cool.”
