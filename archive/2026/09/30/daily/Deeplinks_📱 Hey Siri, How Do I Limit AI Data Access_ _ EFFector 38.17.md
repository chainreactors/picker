# 📱 Hey Siri, How Do I Limit AI Data Access? | EFFector 38.17

![](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/effector-green-web.png?itok=fMSatfxr)

With the launch of iOS 27, Apple is rolling out a variety of new AI features to its familiar voice assistant, Siri. But how are AI tools like these handling our data? In [our latest EFFector newsletter](https://www.eff.org/effector/38/17), we're talking about [the privacy complications](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/how-limit-what-apples-new-siri-ai-can-access-ios-27?utm_source=effector) of AI phone features.

[JOIN OUR NEWSLETTER](https://www.eff.org/effector/)

For over 35 years, [EFFector](https://eff.org/effector) has been your guide to understanding the intersection of technology, civil liberties, and the law. This issue covers [drones and our right to record the law enforcement](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/dc-circuit-must-vacate-drone-flight-restriction-criminalized-recording-immigration?utm_source=effector), [video doorbell footage privacy](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/cold-take-amazons-new-encryption-method-still-doesnt-deliver-real-privacy?utm_source=effector), and [how to limit what data Apple's new Siri AI can access](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/how-limit-what-apples-new-siri-ai-can-access-ios-27?utm_source=effector).

Prefer to listen in? EFFector is now available on all major podcast platforms. This time we're asking EFF's Thorin Klosowski about the difference between AI phone features that are computed on-device and ones that are computed on external servers—and what that means for data protection.
