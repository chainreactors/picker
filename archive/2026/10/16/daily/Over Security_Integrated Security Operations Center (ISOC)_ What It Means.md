# ISOC: When the SOC stops being a stack of tools

And why integrated security operations are the first step toward Autonomous Cyber Defense.

![A summary of the Sekoia autonomous cyber defense platform, showing the different layers within it. ](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69b190e7f47e4c44b0ee07d8/6ad234b642dbf2c128c0f017_blog-isoc-when-the-soc-stops-being-a-stack-of-tools-img-cover.webp)

## Key takeaways

ISOC brings security operations into a connected operating model. This article explains why the category is emerging, what buyers should assess, and how Sekoia approaches integration with deployment and operating-model flexibility.

* ISOC connects core SOC capabilities in one operating model.
* Integrated operations can reduce maintenance work and investigation friction.
* Buyers should weigh faster time to value against potential vendor lock-in.

Most SOCs do not need another security product.

They need the products they already own to work as one system.

Ask a security team what slows them down and the answers are usually familiar:

* The SIEM takes months to tune.
* Security tools do not share enough context.
* Analysts spend their time moving between consoles.
* Every new integration becomes a project.
* Too much time is spent investigating noise instead of real threats.

For years, the industry responded by adding more tools around the SIEM. That expanded the stack, but not necessarily the team’s ability to detect, investigate and respond.

In March 2026, Gartner published a Market Overview for Integrated Security Operations Center solutions, or ISOC. The research describes ISOC as an emerging approach to bringing threat detection, investigation and response into a more integrated operating model.

The practical questions behind this shift are straightforward: What does ISOC change in practice, and what should buyers look for? This article is my answer.

## What is an ISOC, really?

An [ISOC](/glossary/isoc-integrated-security-operations-center) is not simply a better SIEM.

It is a different way of delivering security operations.

A traditional [SIEM](/glossary/siem) provides a powerful and flexible engine. The customer then builds the SOC around it: connectors, detection rules, threat intelligence, investigation workflows, case management, automation and response.

That model can work. But it also transfers much of the complexity to the customer.

An ISOC takes a different approach. The vendor delivers more of the SOC as an integrated system, with technologies, content and workflows designed to work together from the start.
