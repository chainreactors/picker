A common misconception among contractors handling controlled unclassified information (CUI) is that all CUI must remain in the United States and/or be handled by U.S. persons or citizens. Another associated misconception is that all export-controlled information is also CUI. There is also some confusion about the meaning of CUI Export Controlled markings.

This post will explain how to recognize CUI that is export controlled and clear up some of the misconceptions about the intersection between the CUI program, Export Administration Regulations (EAR), and International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR).

CUI handling requirements can be complex. TrustedSec has years of experience working with both contractors and federal agencies to protect CUI using NIST SP 800-171, NIST SP 800-53, FedRAMP, and various other contractual requirements. If you need help to make sense of these programs and minimize the impact of compliance, [get in touch with us](https://trustedsec.com/contact)—we're available to assist!

## Overview

The CUI designation alone does not impose any export control or citizenship requirements. By default, CUI may be released to anyone with an authorized lawful purpose to access the information ([32 CFR 2002.16(c)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/part-2002#p-2002.16(c)(1))).

Export control markings associated with EAR or ITAR also do not automatically mean that information is CUI. There are many ways in which information can be controlled by EAR and ITAR without meeting the definition of CUI.

A document that contains CUI must carry CUI markings regardless of any export controls. Similarly, a document that contains export-controlled information must carry export control markings (either associated with the CUI program, EAR, or ITAR, depending on the circumstances). A document that is both CUI and export controlled must carry both sets of markings.

There are three (3) scenarios where CUI will be export controlled:

* A law, regulation, or government-wide policy restricts U.S. government agencies from sharing the information with non-U.S. persons, in which case the CUI program imposes an export-related Limited Dissemination Control on the CUI that will be marked on the document in accordance with CUI marking standards before the document is disseminated to contractors.
* Export control regulations outside the CUI program, like EAR or ITAR, apply to civilian handling of information that also happens to be CUI because a law, regulation, or government-wide policy controls how U.S. government agencies safeguard or disseminate the information.
* Contracts or other agreements with the government prohibit contractors from sharing information, potentially including CUI, with non-U.S. persons.

Note that CUI EXPT marking (used for information in the CUI Export Controlled category) does not indicate that the CUI is export controlled. This category marking indicates that the CUI is about export controls. The Limited Dissemination Control markings, described below, are instead used to mark information that is export controlled under the CUI program.
