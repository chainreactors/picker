EFF, along with the national ACLU, the ACLU affiliates in Maryland, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Virginia, and the National Association of Criminal Defense Lawyers (NACDL) filed an [amicus brief](https://www.eff.org/document/2025-10-28-belmonte-cardozo-4th-cir-amicus-brief) in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit urging the court to require a warrant for border searches of electronic devices under the Fourth Amendment, an [argument](https://www.eff.org/issues/border-searches) EFF has been making in the [courts](https://www.eff.org/cases/alasaad-v-duke?type=document#related-content) and [Congress](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2021/10/come-back-warrant-congress-should-pass-protecting-data-border-act) for nearly a decade. The Fourth Circuit heard oral arguments on May 8. The Knight Institute at Columbia University and Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press also filed a helpful brief focusing on the [First Amendment](https://knightcolumbia.org/cases/united-states-v-belmonte-cardozo) implications of border searches of electronic devices.
