ARTEX is an open-source penetration testing platform that uses AI agents to find and exploit weaknesses with little human input. The FBI [warned on 13 November 2026](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2026/261113.pdf) that criminals and state-aligned groups have used it against organisations worldwide since at least August, in some cases reaching privileged access in under three hours. Its developer made the project closed-source on 8 October, but copies remain online.

### In brief

* No agency or company has linked ARTEX to the outages at Microsoft 365, the FAA and US banks in mid-November. The FBI has said the advisory dates from 13 November and is not a new announcement.
* ARTEX has no AI model of its own. Operators connect it to commercial or open models, and Chinese models were the most common in the attacker set-ups researchers have read.
* Microsoft says the China-aligned group Storm-0940 used ARTEX after the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise to attempt sign-ins at more than 2,250 Microsoft 365 tenants.
* The intrusions described so far began with leaked credentials, secrets left in public code repositories and ordinary web application flaws.
* Employee logins already sit in stealer logs for 89.6% of the 1,856 large organisations in the Ransomnews infostealer dataset.

## What is ARTEX?

ARTEX is a platform written in Go that takes a goal typed in plain language and has AI agents plan and run the commands of a penetration test. A developer using the handle Autumn-27 published it on GitHub in July 2026, according to the FBI, and [Censys](https://censys.com/blog/artex-agentic-asbestos/) dates the release to 26 July. Censys also reports the author’s position that the tool was meant for testing inside an authorised scope.

The platform has no AI model of its own. Operators connect it to outside providers, and the FBI lists Anthropic’s Claude, OpenAI’s GPT, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok and Kimi among the models it works with.
