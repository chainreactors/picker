# How to Recover deleted Photos, Videos and Files From Your Phone

Budding Forensic Expert · Digital Forensics Desk · Reviewed for accuracy against official Apple, Google, Samsung and NIST documentation

You delete a photo. It disappears from your gallery. But what exactly happened to the file? The honest answer is: it depends — on where the photo lived, whether it was backed up, and what your phone did in the seconds after you tapped delete.

**Can deleted photos sometimes be recovered? Yes, sometimes — but recovery depends heavily on where another copy of the data exists and how your device handled the deletion.** This guide walks through the legitimate, official recovery pathways available to you on your own device and your own accounts, explains why modern phones behave very differently from the hard drives that gave rise to the old "nothing is ever really deleted" myth, and is honest about the cases where recovery simply isn't possible.

⚖️ Scope of this guide

This article covers recovering **your own** data from devices and accounts **you own or are authorised to access**. It does not — and will not — explain how to bypass a passcode, defeat encryption, access someone else's phone, or extract data without authorisation. If you need to access a device that belonged to someone who has died, or you suspect someone else's account or device has been compromised, that is a legal and forensic question, not a do-it-yourself recovery task — see the "When Should You Contact a Professional" section below.

Modern smartphones rarely store a photo in just one place. A single image can exist, at different moments, in local storage, an app's private database, a cloud backup, a "recently deleted" folder, a messaging app's media folder, or a synced copy on another device. So before opening a recovery app, the first question shouldn't be "what software do I install?" It should be:

***"Where might another copy of this data still exist?"***

## Before You Try Recovery: Stop Using the Phone

The single most useful thing you can do in the first few minutes after realising something important is missing is also the simplest: stop using the device more than necessary.

Here's why that matters. On flash-based storage — the type used in essentially all modern smartphones — deleting a file does not necessarily erase it instantly at the physical level. But every photo you take, every app update, every cache refresh, and every automatic sync is an opportunity for the system to write new data into space the operating system now considers "free." Persistence of deleted data on flash storage depends on factors including the garbage-collection process, the TRIM command, the flash media type, the manufacturer, the file system, and the operating system in use.

Practical steps for the first hour:

1. **Avoid unnecessary activity.** Don't take new photos, install new apps, or do a large download right away.
2. **Don't install random "100% recovery" apps immediately.** Installing new software writes new data to the device and may not deliver what it promises.
3. **Do not factory-reset the device.** This is one of the most common — and most damaging — mistakes people make when panicking over lost data.
4. **Check backups first.** Often the fastest path back to your photo is a cloud account, not a recovery tool.
5. **If the data is genuinely irreplaceable** — legal evidence, the only copy of a once-in-a-lifetime photo — consider a reputable professional data-recovery service rather than trial-and-error apps.

🔎 The forensic point

Exact recoverability is never a single yes/no answer. It depends on the phone's storage technology, its operating system and encryption scheme, its backup configuration, and what has happened on the device since the deletion. Anyone who promises guaranteed recovery without knowing these details is guessing.

## Step 1: Check "Recently Deleted" / Trash

Most photo and file apps today don't delete instantly — they move the item to a temporary holding area first.

### iPhone

The Photos app keeps a **Recently Deleted** album. If you accidentally deleted a photo or video, you can recover it within 30 days from the Recently Deleted album by unlocking the album with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode, selecting the item, and tapping Recover. Deleted photos and videos are kept in Recently Deleted for 30 days before being permanently removed from all devices, and during that time you can recover or permanently remove them. The same 30-day album exists on iCloud.com under Photos.

### Android

Android has no single, universal recycle bin — recovery options differ by app and by manufacturer. Google Photos keeps deleted items in its Bin for roughly 60 days, while Files by Google keeps deleted items in its own Trash for roughly 30 days, and emptying one does not empty the other. If a backed-up photo or video is deleted, it stays in the Google Photos trash for 60 days; if it was never backed up, it is permanently deleted after 30 days.
