Officers unlawfully uploaded 179 people's details to Auror without required privacy checks or corporate approval

The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) has reprimanded London's Metropolitan Police Service (MPS) and issued an enforcement notice after officers used a retail crime platform without the force's approval or required data protection checks.

Between March 2023 and September 2024, MPS officers registered around 500 free accounts on Auror, a platform retailers use to record crimes such as shoplifting. The force had not formally procured the service or completed the required data protection checks.

Retailers such as supermarkets encouraged officers to use Auror and directed them to the platform to access CCTV footage.

REG AD

Auror Europe, registered in England and Wales, is a subsidiary of New Zealand-based Auror.

REG AD

The platform allows retailers to share incident reports, images, and CCTV footage. Retailers pay for access through a browser or mobile app, while police partners use it free of charge.

The force had not completed a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) or established data-sharing agreements with Auror Europe, the ICO says.

Between August and September 2024, two separate internal security reports were filed regarding officers' use of Auror.

The first related to officers uploading the names of suspects they recognized from CCTV footage, and the second pertained to officers collecting CCTV footage from the platform despite no checks being made on it.

Access to Auror was blocked as of September 26, 2024.

The MPS data protection officer (DPO) informed the ICO of the force's use of Auror in December 2024 during discussion about a separate matter. The watchdog launched a formal probe in April 2025.

The ICO found that officers unlawfully uploaded personal data relating to 179 people, including three children, to Auror. For 128, this consisted of names alone; for 49, names and dates of birth; and for two, names, dates of birth, and home addresses.

The ICO said the MPS failed to ensure adequate oversight was implemented when deciding what third-party platforms officers were allowed to use – failures that persist today.

REG AD

It claimed the MPS is overly reliant on soft controls to prevent officers from using unapproved software, such as policy reminders and staff training, which often go unnoticed, rather than technical blocks.

The ICO also said the force had failed to test whether its web controls worked: officers used Auror for more than 18 months without triggering an alert.

The regulator described a repeat failure, pointing to officers' use of Clearview AI in 2021 without proper oversight.

The ICO considers the failures ongoing because the force has not demonstrated that its safeguards can prevent similar incidents.
