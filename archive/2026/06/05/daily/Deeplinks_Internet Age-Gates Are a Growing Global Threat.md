The internet is an [essential resource](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/03/thousands-young-people-told-us-why-kids-online-safety-act-will-be-harmful-minors) for young people and adults to access information, explore community, and find themselves—both inside countries and across continents. Yet governments around the world continue to introduce and implement legislation requiring all online users to verify their ages before accessing the digital space. In some cases, politicians are going further, putting forth proposals to ban social media for younger users.

In late 2025, **Australia’s** government [rolled out](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/12/09/australia/australia-social-media-ban-starts-intl-hnk) the first complete ban on users under 16 from having social media accounts. In this sweeping regime, platforms are required to introduce [age assurance tools](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/10/age-verification-estimation-assurance-oh-my-guide-terminology?language=en) to block under-16s, demonstrate that they have taken “reasonable steps” to deactivate accounts used by under-16s, and prevent any new accounts being created, or face fines of up to 49.5 million Australian dollars ($32 million USD).
