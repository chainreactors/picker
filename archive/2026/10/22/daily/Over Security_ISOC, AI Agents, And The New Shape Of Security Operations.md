# ISOC, AI agents, and the changing role of the analyst

In this conversation, Megan speaks with David Bizeul, Sekoia’s Chief Solution Officer, about ISOC, AI agents, analyst training, and beyond.

![Abstract pink-and-white nebula clouds glowing against deep space, with bright stars, layered dust, and a sweeping diagonal composition.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69b190e7f47e4c44b0ee07d8/6ad8b9c86bb1e2afe6241c83_blog-isoc-ai-agents-and-the-changing-role-of-the-analyst-img-cover.webp)

## Key takeaways

David Bizeul shares his view on how ISOC and AI agents will change security operations, from daily analyst work and training to the metrics that show whether a SOC is improving.

* David explains how Gartner’s ISOC category challenges the traditional SIEM model and brings more security operations capabilities into one platform.
* AI agents can take on repetitive triage and investigation work, giving analysts more time for complex decisions.
* Human judgment becomes more important as analysts review agent reasoning and adapt it to their environment.
* Runbooks can turn AI-assisted investigations into a practical training tool for junior analysts.
* The conversation looks at how teams can improve speed, reduce alert fatigue, and understand the cost of running a SOC.

AI agents are moving into the SOC for good (whether we like it or not). They can investigate at greater speed and take on repetitive tasks across large volumes of data.

The teams that benefit most will understand where AI can improve the work and where human judgment still matters. In this conversation, David Bizeul, Sekoia’s Chief Solution Officer, explains why the next SOC will combine people and AI agents. He also explores Gartner’s [ISOC category](/glossary/isoc-integrated-security-operations-center) and the training challenge facing the next generation of cybersecurity experts.

You can watch the full episode below, or keep reading.

## A career on the blue team

**Megan:** What first drew you to cybersecurity?

**David:** I started working in cybersecurity more than 20 years ago. At the time, it was called computer security rather than cybersecurity, and I’ve seen the industry change completely.

I quickly moved to the blue side of cybersecurity. That’s where you protect organizations, understand threats, respond to cyber attacks, and work out what is happening behind them.

When you work on this side of cybersecurity, you help other people. I think that has always been a deep interest of mine. I wanted to help companies protect themselves, and I felt good about that work.
