Some attacks take real skill. Others just need someone to leave a door open. This week has both. Exposed passwords, old bugs still getting exploited, and trusted software doing things it shouldn't.

There's a strange mix of clever tricks and careless mistakes. Hackers are borrowing trust from familiar apps, websites, and AI tools. Some are using AI to speed things up. Others are leaving their own attack files exposed. Not exactly careful work.

Different stories, familiar weak spots. Here's what showed up this week.

**The threats change every week. Subscribe, and we’ll alert you when each new ThreatsDay Bulletin is out.**

1. PHP2Shell attacks persist

   [No WordPress Site Compromised by PHP2Shell... Yet](https://www.wordfence.com/blog/2026/10/php2shell-update-still-no-confirmed-wordpress-compromises-and-answers-to-the-questions-were-hearing-most/)

   WordPress security company Wordefence said it's not aware of any WordPress site compromised through [PHP2Shell](https://www.sysdig.com/blog/php2shell-cve-2026-109618-and-cve-2026-109619-detecting-unauthenticated-remote-code-execution-in-php-core) (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) as of October 28, 2026. "We continue to see [PHP2Shell](https://pulse.infra-trust.org/october-2026/)-related requests across the more than 5 million sites we protect," it [said](https://www.wordfence.com/blog/2026/10/php2shell-update-still-no-confirmed-wordpress-compromises-and-answers-to-the-questions-were-hearing-most/). "Those requests show ongoing scanning and exploitation attempts; they do not show whether an attempt succeeded. Our site-cleaning and incident response teams have not handled a WordPress compromise attributed to these vulnerabilities."
2. 40,573 secrets exposed

   [FortiBleed Investigation Uncovered Additional Exposed Secrets](https://www.infostealers.com/article/fortibleed-2-0-975-new-domains-40573-exposed-secrets-claim-your-ethical-disclosure/)

   Hudson Rock's investigation into the [FortiBleed](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/fbi-warns-fortibleed-remains-active.html) credential harvesting campaign has uncovered 975 newly identified domains and 40,573 exposed secrets, including 37,076 local-user records and 1,576 Active Directory LDAP bind records. The data comes from newly obtained 1,703 FortiGate source files associated with 1,689 host identifiers. "The configuration material dates to around February 2026, earlier than the likely May/June material examined in the first investigation," Hudson Rock [said](https://www.infostealers.com/article/fortibleed-2-0-975-new-domains-40573-exposed-secrets-claim-your-ethical-disclosure/). The latest discovery has been codenamed FortiBleed 2.0.
3. Institutional DNS hijacked

   [Using Domain Shadowing to Place Illegal Content](https://www.infoblox.com/blog/threat-intelligence/borrowed-reputation-domain-shadowing-and-gambling-seo/)

   Threat actors have used domain shadowing to place illegal gambling content beneath legitimate school and government domains in two unrelated operations. "In both cases, an unauthorized party appears to have gained access to DNS administration and created records that the original domain owners may not have noticed," Infoblox [said](https://www.infoblox.com/blog/threat-intelligence/borrowed-reputation-domain-shadowing-and-gambling-seo/). "Because the resulting hostnames sat within established institutional namespaces, they provided especially useful infrastructure for search-engine optimization (SEO) and traffic acquisition." One cluster, linked to a threat actor called Hidden Hawk, has been found to add individual CNAME records that mapped institutional hostnames to gambling pages hosted largely on Cloudflare Pages. The second campaign exploiting an Indian government domain "delegated entire subzones to third-party authoritative nameservers and used a wildcard, allowing many hostnames to be managed outside the victim's DNS environment." Put differently, the unauthorized DNS access creates new institutional hostnames, which resolve to infrastructure serving illegal gambling doorway pages. The pages also leverage the established reputation of school and government namespaces to ensure that the malicious URLs show up prominently in search engine results.

4. AI banking credential warning

   [Bank of America Warns Against Sharing Credentials with AI Agents](https://bsky.app/profile/btoporek.bsky.social/post/3myuzetgir2u6)

   Bank of America is [reminding](https://bsky.app/profile/btoporek.bsky.social/post/3myuzetgir2u6) customers not to share banking credentials with AI agents. In an email blast to customers titled "Protecting your finances in the AI World," the financial institution urged customers to never share their username and password with another person or an outside service, "including an AI-enabled tool or agent that offers to act on your behalf."
