# Doxxing Safety Pt I: Prevention and Footprint Management

![People tend to a community garden of wifi poppies ](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/communityprivacy-kittens.jpg?itok=EGIQhaYW)

Doxxing is the deliberate disclosure of personal information in order to bully, harass, intimidate, or instigate a chain of harms against someone. It's a tricky thing to protect against when the jerk doing it is often able to use legal and accessible means to do so. The odds are stacked against everyday internet folk when there's little to no comprehensive data privacy legislation keeping us safe. The responsibility is on each of us to protect ourselves, but the good news is that there's a lot you can do to [reduce your digital footprint](https://ssd.eff.org/module/how-to-manage-your-digital-footprint) and take control of your data.

This post is part one of a [two-part series](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/08/doxxing-safety-part-ii-incident-response) discussing safety and response to doxxing. This first part focuses on prevention and ways to reduce your overall footprint. [The second](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/08/doxxing-safety-part-ii-incident-response) focuses on incident response, as in, steps to take if you're in the midst of being doxxed. There will be some crossover and redundancy between these two posts, so it's worth reading each and gaining familiarity with the steps well ahead of time.

## OSINT

Open source intelligence (OSINT) is a broad term within information security. It focuses on the tools and means available to us for investigation and information retrieval. OSINT sits at the heart of doxxing campaigns but is also an important part of the process of preventing them. Typically it is a way of describing a methodology of piecing together scraps of information to form a dossier on a subject.

There are fancy multipurpose tools (like Maltego or Lampyre) that combine many datapoints into accessible graphs and datasets. As helpful as they can be for traditional penetration tests or corporate OSINT campaigns, they’re best used for investigations focused on organizations, mapping together details like employee email charts, LinkedIn profiles, and company network maps. They may not fit the needs of everyday people or liberation movement workers. Instead, we recommend referring to different [OSINT resource](https://start.me/p/L1rEYQ/osint4all) [lists that index](https://osintframework.com/) together a bunch of different tools, then using those resources to create a list for yourself of which tools may be most helpful.

Many, if not all, of the resources we cover below will be referenced in those guides, and themselves fall under the OSINT category. It’s important to note that the tools we reference in this particular blog post are only relevant at the time of publishing. The bigger ideas have a much longer shelf life than various tech tools.
