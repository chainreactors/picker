Security researchers exploited flaws in Android apps to gain remote access to connected cars from two major automakers, allowing them to start engines, flash headlights, and sound horns.

Shubham Shah, co-founder at Assetnote, and researcher Sam Curry worked together on the exploits earlier this year. By tinkering with the cars’ Android apps, they devised exploit paths to issue remote commands to vehicles in [Australia](https://shubs.io/unlocking-any-car-from-x-brand-in-most-of-the-world/) and the [US](https://samcurry.net/forgerock).

The pair published separate blogs covering different aspects of the research; Shah said the US attack "required a lot more work" because the automaker "spent much more time securing the US, Canadian, and European OpenIDM environments."

REG AD

Both exploit chains centered around weaknesses in the ForgeRock identity platform relied upon by both automakers, the identities of which were kept anonymous by the researchers, who have previously highlighted flaws in the likes of [Hyundai](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/02/15/hyundai-and-kia-issue-software-upgrades-to-thwart-theft-hack/441285), [Kia](https://www.theregister.com/security/2024/09/30/kias-can-be-hijacked-with-just-a-phone-and-plate-number/441789), [Subaru](https://www.theregister.com/security/2025/01/27/cloudflare-cdn-spills-location-data-with-simple-hack/1195771), and [many others](https://samcurry.net/web-hackers-vs-the-auto-industry).

REG AD

In the Australian version of the app, the researchers first found a vehicle identification number (VIN) using public licence plate checkers, and used those in queries to internal APIs, which provided personal information and owner email addresses.

They also found [hardcoded admin credentials](https://www.theregister.com/security/2024/10/23/millions-of-mobile-app-users-at-risk-from-hardcoded-creds/1158942) for the ForgeRock OpenIDM that were meant for the staging environment but also worked in production. After bypassing the heavy WAF filtering by exploiting path traversal flaws, they were able to invoke the internal admin APIs and take over target accounts using only their email addresses.

This let them mint authentication tokens, which they could issue to send arbitrary commands to affected vehicles through the mobile app.

Issuing commands on the US version also required bypassing WAF filtering through a path traversal, though with a more involved technique: exploiting path confusion to reach the Apache Felix admin console.
