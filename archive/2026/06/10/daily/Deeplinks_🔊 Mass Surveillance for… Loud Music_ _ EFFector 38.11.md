Across the country, surveillance companies have spun a vast web of tens of thousands of license plate cameras. The people selling this tech want you to believe that it's for your safety, but how are authorities really using automated license plate readers (ALPR)? In [this week's EFFector newsletter](https://www.eff.org/effector/38/11), we're looking at how these powerful surveillance networks have become universal people-trackers used for noise complaints and other low-level investigations.

[JOIN OUR NEWSLETTER](https://www.eff.org/effector/)

For over 35 years, [EFFector](https://eff.org/effector) has been your guide to understanding the intersection of technology, civil liberties, and the law. This week's issue covers [a victory for facial privacy](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/victory-meta-strips-facial-recognition-code-smart-glasses-app-after-public-outcry?utm_source=effector), [EFF's testimony to Congress](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/eff-testifies-congress-protecting-americans-rights-government-ai?utm_source=effector) about AI and surveillance, and troubling [new examples of ALPR mission creep](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/05/more-license-plate-reader-mission-creep-school-residency-verification-background?utm_source=effector).
