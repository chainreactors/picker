# Interoperability vs. Security: Why not both?

![Personified mushrooms communicating from underground homes](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/decentralization-banner.png?itok=l-MklJuy)

An open internet where people can freely build, share, and remix each other's work is a powerful engine of grassroots innovation we’ve all benefited from. In recent decades, however, corporations have taken control of an increasing share of this work, giving them a greater ability to stifle challengers and limit our choices. When regulators address this by mandating that  tech giants play fair by requiring interoperability, the gatekeepers respond with a tired refrain: Playing fair puts users’ security at risk.

Unfortunately for these trillion-dollar companies, this [*gotcha*](https://www.dict.org/bin/Dict?Form=Dict2&Database=*&Query=gotcha) isn’t the end of the conversation. Regulators can ensure that meaningful choice *and* security thrive.

Lawmakers have been catching up, recognizing that the best way to rein in big tech is to require these [oligopolies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligopoly) to open up their platforms to independent developers. As we [explained six years ago](https://www.eff.org/wp/interoperability-and-privacy?language=en), mandating interoperability helps prevent the misuse of people’s data and enables more privacy-respecting alternatives. Tech giants, in contrast, have been arguing that our data is safer when they have exclusive control.

## Tech Giants Claim Interoperability Means Insecurity

The European Union’s [Digital Markets Act](https://www.eff.org/pages/adoption-dsadma-notre-analyse) (DMA) put interoperability into law under Article 6(7): “gatekeeper shall allow providers of services and providers of hardware, free of charge, effective interoperability with” a designated [Core Platform Service](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/gatekeepers-portal_en) they operate. The language describes an unambiguous requirement to remediate vendor lockouts and anti-competitive behavior of the major service operators—it is not optional.

Apple [claimed](https://developer.apple.com/security/complying-with-the-dma.pdf?track_code=3eb23490-7b93-4003-8c81-e85b5c2b132a&element=BODY&productLocationId=BODY&position=null&url=https%3A%2F%2Fdeveloper.apple.com%2Fsecurity%2Fcomplying-with-the-dma.pdf) that enforcing this obligation in mobile app stores would put their customers at risk and do irreparable harm to trust in their brand.
