## Your PLCs aren't internet-connected, right? Right?!

![icon](/design_picker/d518b499f8a6e2c65d4d8c49aca8299d54b03012/graphics/icon/vulture_red.svg)
[Jessica Lyons](/Author/Jessica-Lyons "Read more by this author")

Tue 7 Apr 2026  //
23:30 UTC

![](/design_picker/d2e337b97204af4aa34dda04c4e5d56d954b216f/graphics/icons/social_share_icon.svg)

Iranian-affiliated actors have escalated intrusions targeting critical US water and energy facilities, in some cases disrupting operations, the FBI and American cyber defense agencies said on Tuesday.

The US government alert comes as the war lted by the US and Israel enters its sixth week, with President Donald Trump threatening to [wipe out Iran's civilization](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/trump-warns-a-whole-civilization-will-die-tonight-if-a-deal-with-iran-isnt-reached) before Pakistan convinced him to agree to a [two-week ceasefire](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/07/trump-iran-ceasefire-hormuz-strait.html).

Iran's cyber intrusions targeting critical infrastructure have been [ongoing since March](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-097a), according to the feds, and they aim to disrupt operational technology (OT) devices, specifically programmable logic controllers (PLCs) manufactured by Rockwell Automation/Allen-Bradley.

PLCs are used to control and monitor industrial equipment in water treatment plants, food production sites, oil refineries, power grids, and other critical facilities, and they've been a [longtime favorite target](https://www.theregister.com/2024/02/02/critical_infrastructure_ot_ransomware/) of Iranian cyber crews.
