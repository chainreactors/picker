# Real Life Is Already a Game

Maybe we dismiss immersive games because we can't see the one we're already in

November 26, 2026

by Daniel Miessler

 Krahing…

[![A knight raising a glowing crystal and a shopper raising a house key beside a full cart, both hanging on marionette strings from a purple DNA helix](/images/real-life-is-already-a-game.webp)](/images/real-life-is-already-a-game.webp)

I've been thinking about the dismal prospect of so many of us potentially [getting meaning from really good immersive games](/blog/ubi-games) in the future. Like Ready Player One type stuff. Because of AI and robots basically doing most of the work.

📚 [Ready Player One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready_Player_One) is Ernest Cline's 2011 novel about a future where most people live inside a VR world called the OASIS.

Like in some sense I see these types of games as necessary, to give people some kind of meaning. But I also think it's much more shallow than other meaning sources we have traditionally had as humans. Like working a job, raising a family, etc.

> Because real life is becoming less attractive to the masses due to automation taking jobs, Game Designers will suddenly take on the monumental responsibility of providing humans with meaning.
>
> [Game Design is About to Become a Critical Career (2017)](/blog/game-design-is-about-to-become-a-critical-career)

But if I analyze my bias here, my assumption is that **real life is the real thing**. And then there are games, which are different.

Like in games you do stuff like collect resources, try to upgrade your gear and your base, and do social things, and try to gain standing, and go on adventures, and experience fun things, and challenges, and....

🔄 Game designers call this the core loop: the repeated cycle of playing and getting rewarded that keeps players coming back.

Wait. Isn't that what we're doing out here too? Supposedly outside the game?

## Evolution built the first game [​](#evolution-built-the-first-game)

And isn't this whole thing a game anyway, [orchestrated by evolution to get us to propagate genes](/blog/battle-mechs-genes)? In that sense we're the supremely sad **NPCs** who think they matter. But in some sense, we don't really.

NPCA non-player character: a character in a game that the game itself controls, following a script with no player behind it.[Video game terminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-player_character)

Pop quiz: what's your great grandfather's first name?

🤔 Everyone has eight great-grandparents. Try naming all eight.

You don't know? That's only a couple of generations from you, and you have no idea about them. And that's how it'll be for you after a few short years. We come, we go, and nobody really remembers for more than an instant in overall time. Everything we build, buy, and collect, none of it is ours. We take nothing with us.

So basically we're being born into a game, created by evolution, to have us want things and crave things, and pursue things, which causes us to do all this crazy stuff throughout our lives. War, courtship, art, taxes, marriage, band-aids, laughter and tears. They're all part of this game. And the only reason we want any of it is because evolution programmed us to want it.

🧬 Richard Dawkins made the gene-centered version of this argument in [The Selfish Gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Selfish_Gene) (1976).

Every single thing you desire is the result of [programming from evolution](/blog/evolution-source-meaning).

So are we not also in a game? Do our actions, much like an NPC in a game, ultimately not matter?

Of course I don't really believe that, because I'm inside this game, and this game matters to me. Love and friendship are not less real to me because I know what they are at another layer of reality. I live in this one.

But.

What it does is remove the **binary distinction between "real" life and games**. The implication is that one is permanent and real and the other is fleeting and not real. But as we see, that's not true.

## Rethinking immersive games [​](#rethinking-immersive-games)

So this puts deep immersive games in another light for me. They still don't seem super healthy to me if you're not getting real relationships, and exercise, and actually reproducing to keep the planet populated.

But assuming that's all ok, it feels like people playing the "normal life" game of going to work, clocking in, clocking out, spending some of that currency on food and entertainment, etc., is very much the same as what we do in games today.

They're all just **meaning loops**. And the game ones match ours because they're modeled after the loop provided by evolution. Get more powerful. Get more attractive. Reproduce. It's all the same shit whether we're in [World of Warcraft](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/) or Costco.

So maybe we shouldn't be so dismissive of immersive games as a way to maintain or even elevate human meaning. With the qualifications I mentioned above, of course.

Maybe it's quite arrogant to think we're so much better and more real, and maybe that's just because the game we're trapped in is invisible to us.

## Summary [​](#summary)

1. I used to think meaning from immersive games would be a shallow replacement for real life
2. But games are just copies of the loop we already run: resources, gear, status, adventure
3. And that loop came from evolution, which programmed every single thing we want
4. Nobody remembers their great grandfather, so even the real game is pretty temporary
5. It still matters to me, because I live inside it
6. So the line between real life and games is way blurrier than we think
7. Games are fine if you still have relationships, exercise and kids in the mix
8. Maybe we only feel superior because we can't see the game we're in
