DORA TLPT Requirements Explained: Where Threat Intelligence Fits

The DORA TLPT requirements push selected EU financial entities beyond standard penetration testing. TLPT must be based on realistic threat scenarios, relevant threat actors, and current intelligence about how attacks are actually carried out.

That makes [threat intelligence](https://any.run/threat-intelligence-lookup/?utm_source=anyrunblog&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=dora-tlpt-requirements-explained&utm_term=161026&utm_content=linktotilookuplanding) a core part of the process. It helps define what should be tested, which TTPs matter, and which attack paths are most relevant to the organization.

Let’s look at the main TLPT requirements explained in practice, where threat intelligence fits into DORA TLPT, and what teams need to get right before testing begins.

## What Are the DORA TLPT Requirements?

The Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), established under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, sets cybersecurity and operational resilience requirements for financial entities across the European Union.

Under [DORA Article 26 TLPT](https://www.digital-operational-resilience-act.com/Article_26.html), certain financial entities must carry out threat-led penetration testing at least once every three years. The DORA TLPT frequency can be adjusted depending on the entity’s risk profile and operational circumstances.

The framework is maintained by the European Central Bank (ECB), while the DORA TLPT standards were developed by the European Supervisory Authorities — EBA, EIOPA, and ESMA — in cooperation with the ECB.

![DORA TLPT requirements overview: scope, frequency, and provider rules](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/dora-tlpt-requirments-scope-1024x364.png)

*DORA TLPT requirements overview*

The DORA TLPT scope must cover several or all critical or important functions and include the ICT systems, processes, and technologies that support them. Testing is carried out on live production systems, which is one of the main differences between TLPT and more conventional security assessments.

The detailed methodology is set out in the Regulatory Technical Standards under Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1190. These rules define how TLPT should be scoped, prepared, executed, and followed by remediation.

## How TIBER-EU Fits into DORA TLPT

The TIBER-EU framework DORA connection is straightforward. DORA sets the regulatory requirements for TLPT, while TIBER-EU provides a practical framework for carrying out threat intelligence-based red team testing.

The updated TIBER-EU framework closely follows the DORA TLPT process, covering scoping, threat intelligence, red team testing, purple teaming, and reporting.

For financial organizations, this means TLPT is not just about testing systems for weaknesses. The exercise should reflect the threats, techniques, and attack paths that are actually relevant to the organization.

## The Main TLPT Phases Under DORA

The TLPT phases DORA follows are grouped into three main parts: preparation, testing, and closure. The testing phase covers both threat intelligence and active red team testing, while reporting, replay, purple teaming, remediation planning, and attestation come later in the process.

![TIBER-EU framework phases for DORA TLPT compliance](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Key-phrases-of-TLPT-under-DORA-1024x503.png)

*Key phrases of TLPT under DORA*

### 1. Preparation Phase

The preparation phase sets up the exercise. The financial entity forms the control team, defines the scope, selects the threat intelligence and red team providers, and puts the necessary risk management and communication arrangements in place.

The relevant National Competent Authority (NCA) or TLPT authority also oversees key parts of the process, including the scope and overall test setup.

Because TLPT is performed against live production systems, this phase also includes assessing the risks of the test and agreeing the controls needed to keep it safe.
