# [ThreatsDay: 10M NGINX Services at Risk, 250+ Malicious Extensions, AI Server Hijacking + 17 More Stories](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/threatsday-250-rogue-extensions-10m.html)

**Ravie Lakshmanan**Nov 26, 2026Hacking News / Cybersecurity News

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhlwb4cL2xbF38yynT-hp8rHaGntsYzuQwL4-68g3SxEW4Taw6_Kl4WFfL4CuNRNXGxO_KS9prZaqk0A6SA6fib3k8j9miTymP8PzABv9OA21eSDsRv1j9NEgbIEVMR7u1C7VmFyFxdBf0Zcj2sckIyHDkRSjm76oFMDd10drUHa1ctv6190vdYHhuVuRPk/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/thday.jpg)

A website can look perfectly fine to the person running it. Log out, visit the same page, and things might look different. Something similar is happening with browser extensions, where even the way you find and install one can change what it does. Checking whether software works as expected only tells you so much.

Then there’s the question of who gets to use what. Stolen logins are opening doors across connected systems, while some AI servers are sitting online for anyone to use at someone else’s expense. Even software responsible for keeping the power on has found its way into an attack.

Meanwhile, authorities are taking down large scam operations, extortion gangs are making comebacks, and ransomware activity is picking up. Some of the more worrying findings this week involve things most people would have little reason to suspect. The details are worth a look.

**The threats change every week. Subscribe, and we’ll alert you when each new ThreatsDay Bulletin is out.**

1. Stolen credentials expose customer data

   [Todoist Discloses Data Breach](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/get-started/security-incident-in-november-2026-cG0EIACNU)

   Todoist has disclosed that an attacker gained unauthorized access to its production systems and retrieved some customer information on November 23, 2026. The incident affects 6% of its users. "Between November 22 and 23, an attacker used a stolen credential associated with our version control system to modify automated workflows in three repositories and obtain additional credentials," it [said](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/get-started/security-incident-in-november-2026-cG0EIACNU). During this time, they could also read our source code. Our investigation found no changes to application code. One of the credentials they obtained allowed them to change how employees access our internal systems. The attacker used it to take over an employee’s work account and gain access to our cloud infrastructure. On November 23, the attacker downloaded a file containing account information for some early Todoist users whose passwords were stored using an older hashing method. They also made targeted queries for account tokens, searched some customer content for credentials, and accessed email information through connected Todoist for Outlook accounts."
2. Record cybersecurity acquisition

   [CrowdStrike to Acquire Zscaler in Record $43.9B Deal](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/crowdstrike-to-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero-trust-to-the-falcon-platform/)

   CrowdStrike said it will [acquire Zscaler](https://www.zscaler.com/press/crowdstrike-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero-trust-falcon-platform) in a deal worth $43.9 billion, making it the biggest cybersecurity acquisition on record. "Security used to be about keeping the adversary out. Today, safety is required to let AI in," CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz [said](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/crowdstrike-to-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero-trust-to-the-falcon-platform/). "The network used to connect people to places. Now it connects agents to data. With Zscaler, we're building cybersecurity's operating system: a single control plane to deploy AI, control AI, and enable any agentic application to run safely. We see a path to become the largest AI safety and security company the world has ever seen."
3. Paid ads activate hidden payloads

   [Ad-Driven Installs Trigger Hidden Browser Extension Behavior](https://microsoftedge.github.io/edgevr/posts/SleeperWidget-How-Ad-Driven-Installs-Activate-Hidden-Browser-Extension-Behavior/)

   Microsoft's Edge Extensions Security Team has documented SleeperWidget, a set of over 250 extensions across Microsoft Edge Add-ons, Chrome Web Store, and Mozilla Add-ons that work as advertised unless installed through a paid search ad. Once installed that way, they collect browsing data, inject ads, and change shopping links. "Its extensions pose as maps, recipe finders, package trackers, and other everyday tools. Most deliver the features they advertise, which makes hidden behavior easier to miss," it [said](https://microsoftedge.github.io/edgevr/posts/SleeperWidget-How-Ad-Driven-Installs-Activate-Hidden-Browser-Extension-Behavior/). "In the extensions we analyzed, installation through a paid-advertising funnel also activated browsing-data collection, ad injection, and shopping-link modification."
4. LLMjacking without exploits

   [Internet-Exposed Hosts Could Become Targets for AI Abuse](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/tr-self-hosted-ai-renting-compute-exposed-servers/)

   An analysis of 20,354 internet-facing hosts has revealed that thousands of servers where AI services or notebook APIs have been deployed and left accessible without authentication, potentially enabling attackers to abuse them for their own purposes. "More than half of the exposed Ollama servers in this scan have already been touched by someone who is not the owner," Rapid7 [said](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/tr-self-hosted-ai-renting-compute-exposed-servers/). "79% of exposed Ollama hosts list at least one :cloud model, mostly GLM, DeepSeek, MiniMax and Kimi variants. An exposed, signed-in server can provide access to someone else's paid subscription, with the bill landing on the owner. This is the self-hosted version of LLMjacking, and it requires no exploit at all...
