# Italian regulator fines Meta $886 million for violating data privacy law

Italy’s data protection regulator announced a €805 million ($886 million) fine against Meta on Saturday, saying the social media giant accessed user photos and videos without their specific consent or properly informing them.

The fine, announced Saturday, is one of the biggest the Italian regulator — known as the Garante — has ever issued.

The crackdown on Meta’s Facebook platform involved the company’s alleged use of its “camera roll” suggestions option, launched in 2023, to browse Android users’ photo galleries to recommend posts they could enhance with personal photos and videos. In addition to the photos and videos, the regulator said, Facebook accessed related metadata.

The investigation found Meta asked users to access their camera roll through a single permission that “applies across the entire app covering multiple features with different requirements,” according to a Garante [press release](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/10321080#english).

The permission is preceded by a “non-specific notice” to enable other features, the press release said, failing to make clear to users that they are consenting to suggestions from the camera roll, the regulator said. It also alleged the feature was turned on by default.

“When given in full, such permission allows the app to access the entire photo library on the device, usually on a permanent basis, without the user or [regulators] being able to verify what information is actually being used,” the press release said.

The practice violates Italian data protection regulations safeguarding “the smartphone as part of the individual’s private sphere,” according to a Garate press release.

“Consequently, any access to the information stored on such devices requires free and informed consent, regardless of whether data is transmitted or any harm occurs,” the press release said.

Meta had argued the Garante lacked jurisdiction to issue the fine, but the regulator dismissed that claim.

A Meta spokesperson did not immediately respond to a request for comment, but told other news outlets in a statement that the company strongly disagrees with the decision and plans to appeal what it called the “disproportionate” fine amount.

The tech giant has been under fire worldwide in recent months, losing multiple court cases in the U.S., including an October decision in which the company was [fined](https://therecord.media/meta-new-mexico-penalty-cambridge-analytica-privacy-case) $25 billion for deceiving consumers about how it uses their data.

In addition to the fine, the Garante has ordered Meta to make several changes in the next 60 days, including by obtaining consent through a request that is “separate and specific to the feature” and clearly explaining what it is used for. Meta also must begin disabling the option by default.

The unusually high fine is due to the fact that about 20 million Italian Android users were impacted, the long time period over which the violation occurred and the company’s lack of cooperation during the probe, the press release said.
