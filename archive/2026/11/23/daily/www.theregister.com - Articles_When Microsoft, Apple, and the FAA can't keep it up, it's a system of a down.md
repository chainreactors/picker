No Access November is going well this year.

Among the many organizations to adopt Outage As A Service (OAAS) were [Microsoft](https://www.theregister.com/saas/2026/11/16/microsoft-blames-monster-email-outage-on-software-defect-introduced-in-july-fix/5310026), [Apple](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/apple-works-restore-services-after-outage-caused-by-internal-update-2026-11-17/), the [FAA](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2026/11/13/faa-blames-ground-stop-on-faulty-copper-wiring/5309912), and any number of [European supermarkets](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/09/france-blames-iran-for-supermarket-hack-meltdowns/5308591). Even [the NSA](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/17/daily-disruptive-outages-that-ground-planes-and-halt-work-welcome-to-the-new-normal/5310634) has succumbed. For us, at least, outage is word of the year. It's certainly deeply embedded in the public mind, with schoolchildren reportedly referring to the 19th century Irish famine as "the potato outage." There's no danger of an outrage outage.

OAAS is synonymous with infrastructure failure, a more useful term that begins to hint at cause and cure as well as effect. In all of the latest crop, what we know is depressingly familiar. Microsoft's O365 meltdown was a cascading failure caused by a botched upgrade that took stuff offline and melted down as it tried to come back. The FAA was a migration from Jurassic hardware that wasn't end of life so much as living fossil. The Eurofridge-cum-fondue-maker was a control system so badly written it fell apart if you raised an eyebrow at it. Clearly, nobody had. Until now.

REG AD

These are all different classes of OAAS that have been around for decades. Security-based outages are still outages, and a packet flood is still a packet flood whether it's a botnet DDoS or finger trouble in the config dashboard.

REG AD

Each of them has a root cause with obvious fixes – a faulty mental model of network/server interaction, chronic underfunding, a complete supply chain with little cyber-security expertise. All these, like the poor, will be with us always. They won't fix themselves. Infrastructure that works is invisible, and invisible things are forgotten until they break. What's needed is an infrastructure that watches itself, that is designed to be adaptive and resilient, one that refuses to connect to danger and nulls out any that gets through.
