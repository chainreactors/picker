The most common password stolen by infostealer malware in 2026 is `123456`, captured 612,110 times so far this year, followed by `12345678` (268,742), `1234` (161,322), `admin` (157,849) and `123456789` (152,494). It is the third year in a row that `123456` has led the list, after 851,369 captures in 2024 and 1,612,518 in 2025. It is also the password that, by the attacker’s own account, opened Denmark’s population register in September. The ranking comes from the 200 most frequent password values recorded each year in the Alerts.bar infostealer index, which Ransomnews licenses and has cleaned of parser noise. The ranking itself is the least interesting part. Administrator passwords are climbing, and the passwords that corporate complexity rules produce, `Pass@123`, `Admin@123`, `Welcome@123`, have more than doubled their share since 2024.

### In brief

* `123456` is the most common password in infostealer logs for the third consecutive year: 612,110 captures in 2026 to date, a fifth of all captures in the top 100.
* Six of the top ten are digit strings; the other four are `admin`, `password`, `Aa123456` and `admin123`. Digit-only passwords make up 67 per cent of the top-100 volume in 2026, down a little from 71 per cent in 2024.
* The `admin` family (`admin`, `admin123`, `Admin@123`, `admin@123` and six variants) was captured 291,149 times in 2026. `admin123` climbed from 19th place in 2024 to 10th, and `Admin@123` from 25th to 19th.
* The `@123` family, the shape produced by “one capital, one symbol, one number” rules, more than doubled, from 3.6 per cent of the top-100 volume in 2024 to 7.8 per cent in 2026. `Test@123` rose from 104th to 42nd, `Welcome@123` from 78th to 37th.
* Keyboard walks are falling: `qwerty` slipped from 39th to 49th and `qwertyuiop` from 40th to 58th.
* Parser artefacts, the empty, “not saved” and “decryption failed” strings that stealers write when they cannot read a browser’s password store, grew from 43 per cent of the raw top-200 volume in 2024 to 70 per cent in 2026.
