# From mobile threat detection to autonomous investigation: Connecting Zimperium with Sekoia

Zimperium Mobile Threat Defense now sends its mobile threat telemetry to the Sekoia SOC platform. Sekoia Elevate AI agents investigate each mobile alert alongside identity, cloud and network evidence, so analysts can see what it means for the whole organization.

![](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69b190e7f47e4c44b0ee07d8/6ad89945d6f181ec02ef626d_blog-from-mobile-threat-detection-to-autonomous-investigation-zimperium-img-cover.webp)

## Key takeaways

What the Zimperium integration brings to your SOC, and how Sekoia Elevate turns mobile alerts into investigations your analysts can review.

* Mobile devices are now a primary attack target: mobile phishing, SMS phishing, malicious apps, network attacks and device compromise. When these alerts sit in a separate console, SOC teams lose sight of the wider attack.
* Zimperium Mobile Threat Defense detects threats directly on corporate and personal (BYOD) devices running Android, iOS and ChromeOS. It covers four vectors: device, network, application and phishing, even when the device is offline.
* The integration pulls Zimperium threat events into Sekoia through the Zimperium APIs. Analysts then investigate mobile activity in one platform, next to detections, cases and threat intelligence.
* Sekoia Elevate AI agents investigate mobile alerts on their own. They correlate related signals, add context from Sekoia Intelligence and write a report that shows every search and every piece of evidence behind the verdict.
* Take an EvilTokens device code phishing alert. Elevate links it to Microsoft Entra ID sign-ins, Microsoft 365 activity and DNS and proxy traffic. That tells the SOC whether it was a blocked attempt, a compromised account or a wider campaign. Analysts keep the final decision.

Mobile devices have become essential to how organizations work. Employees use smartphones and tablets to access business applications, authenticate to corporate services, exchange sensitive information, and stay connected from almost anywhere.

They have also become a key target for attackers.

As cybercriminals adopt a mobile-first attack strategy, organizations face threats including mobile phishing (mishing), including SMS/Text (smishing), malicious applications, network attacks, device compromise, and zero-day threats. Yet mobile security events are still frequently handled separately from the rest of the security environment. This creates blind spots for SOC teams and makes it harder to understand whether a mobile alert is an isolated event or part of a broader attack.
