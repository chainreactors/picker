## One way to deal with bug hunting LLMs: ditch the old drivers

![icon](/design_picker/d518b499f8a6e2c65d4d8c49aca8299d54b03012/graphics/icon/vulture_red.svg)
[Liam Proven](/Author/Liam-Proven "Read more by this author")

Fri 24 Apr 2026  //
14:15 UTC

![](/design_picker/d2e337b97204af4aa34dda04c4e5d56d954b216f/graphics/icons/social_share_icon.svg)

One tactic to deal with LLM-powered vulnerability detection is simple – just speed up the removal of old code. If it's gone, it no longer matters if it's buggy.

Bot-powered bug-busting is [in the news of late](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/24/ai_finding_bugs/), with scary-sounding reports of automated tools detecting flaws and vulnerabilities far faster than any unaided humans. Some of these are long-standing howlers, such as a [27-year-old bug in OpenBSD](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/07/anthropic_all_your_zerodays_are_belong_to_us/) and a [23-year-old flaw](https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/stable/linux.git/commit/?id=5133b61aaf437e5f25b1b396b14242a6bb0508e2) in the Linux in-kernel NFS code. Even [stable-kernel supremo Greg Kroah-Hartman says it works](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/26/greg_kroahhartman_ai_kernel/).

The good news is that there's one fairly dramatic but simple approach to handling this: if the bugs are in very old drivers for very old hardware, then don't even try to fix them – just [remove them](https://lwn.net/Articles/1068928/). This is a theme behind multiple recent changes in the kernel. Andrew Lunn's [18-patch series](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/20260421-v7-0-0-net-next-driver-removal-v1-v1-0-69517c689d1f%40lunn.ch/) removes the drivers for 3Com's 3C509, 3C515, 3C574, 3C589 and 3C59x hardware. The *Reg* FOSS desk suspects he may still have some of these in his parts boxes somewhere. It also removes 13 other devices, including some old Xircom parallel-port and PCMCIA slot cards. If we manage to find one of them, perhaps it will still work in our Amiga 1200.
