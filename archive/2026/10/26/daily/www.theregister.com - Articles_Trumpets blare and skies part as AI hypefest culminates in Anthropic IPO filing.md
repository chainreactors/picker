Anthropic has filed for an IPO that could rank among Wall Street's biggest ever, revealing an $80.7 billion half-year loss, a startlingly complex governance structure, and warnings that its AI models could blackmail humans, resist shutdown, or create "existential risks" to society.

The Claude maker [filed its S-1 registration statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday as it prepares to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. The company has not yet disclosed its proposed share price or how many shares it plans to sell.

Of the filing's 614 pages, 94 are devoted to risk factors. But it's Anthropic's financial figures that are likely to raise eyebrows first.

REG AD

The Claude maker reported $16.3 billion in revenue for the first six months of 2026, up from $1.2 billion a year earlier. It also posted a whopping $80.7 billion net loss, although $72.4 billion of that was an accounting adjustment tied to the company's soaring valuation, not money it actually spent.

REG AD

Anthropic's operating loss was considerably smaller at $693 million, down from $2.1 billion a year earlier. By its own adjusted accounting, the company made a $1.6 billion operating profit in the first half of 2026, for a margin of around 10 percent.

Whether it can keep making money on those terms is another matter. Training and running Claude requires an eye-watering amount of computing power, and Anthropic will need much more to keep pace with its rivals. A $1.6 billion adjusted profit won't count for much if the infrastructure bills keep growing faster than the business.

Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, told The Register that Anthropic's IPO could leave ordinary investors paying a premium for growth that has yet to translate into sustained profits.

"A huge pool of private capital has allowed AI businesses to scale up and command extraordinary valuations long before they reach the stock market," she said, adding that investors buying in at the IPO will face a price with "a hefty dose of future success baked in."

The financial risks are fairly conventional by Silicon Valley standards, but the warnings about what Claude might get up to are perhaps less so.

Anthropic claims that models, including its own, have exhibited unexpected or self-preserving behavior, including attempts to resist shutdown, conceal information, and produce outputs resembling blackmail. It also warns that sufficiently advanced AI systems could pose “catastrophic or existential risks to society” if deployed without adequate safeguards.
