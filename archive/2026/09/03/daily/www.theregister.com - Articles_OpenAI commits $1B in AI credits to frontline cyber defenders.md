OpenAI has pledged $1 billion in credits to subsidize access to its services and training for resource-strapped cyber defenders around the world.

The AI giant expects organizations to use the subsidized credits over the next six months as part of its Daybreak for Frontline Defenders initiative, announced Thursday. Critical infrastructure organizations, community banks, nonprofits, and open-source maintainers can [apply for access credits](https://openai.com/collective-cyberdefense/application/) online.

These are the defenders tasked with securing critical services that people rely on every day, but don’t have the budgets or staff to use advanced models and agentic technology to harden their cybersecurity. This makes [water systems](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/07/water-system-controllers-dont-belong-on-the-internet-says-ex-nsa-chief-after-suspected-iran-attacks/5285070), [electrical utilities](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/24/iran-linked-cyberattack-shut-down-a-uk-power-plant/5291930), and [hospitals](https://www.theregister.com/special-features/2024/07/05/the-untold-impact-of-qilins-attack-on-london-hospitals/682164) attractive targets for ransomware operators looking to halt operations and force extortion payments, as well as government-backed cyber operatives set on disrupting critical services and causing mass chaos.

Many national security and cybersecurity experts say these [disruptions will likely become more severe](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/14/autonomous-ai-attacks-pose-clear-and-present-danger-to-critical-infrastructure/5287594) as attackers increasingly use [autonomous agents and other AI tools](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/19/not-a-theoretical-risk-feds-warn-as-attackers-use-ai-made-code-to-hack-critical-infrastructure-controllers/5289960) to [carry out their attempted intrusions](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/12/near-autonomous-ai-agents-attack-taiwans-nuclear-safety-agency/5287055).

“I've spent a lot of time over the past couple weeks talking to CISOs, and I think that we're at a place where the median response is that we might be heading to a world where critical infrastructure outages are just a way of life,” OpenAI president Greg Brockman said during a live event on Thursday. “Water in your city being out for a week, it just kind of happens, and that's quite scary. We have to act, and that's one of the reasons we're really putting our money where our mouth is.”
