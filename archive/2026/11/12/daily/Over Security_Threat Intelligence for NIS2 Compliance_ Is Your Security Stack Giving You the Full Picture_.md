Threat Intelligence for NIS2 Compliance: Is Your Security Stack Giving You the Full Picture?

Investing heavily in cybersecurity is a positive trend observed across the EU. Companies implement SIEM, EDR, threat intelligence and dedicated SOC teams. Everything seems to be in place, the budgets have been spent.

But what does it take for security leaders to conclude that these investments are well-spent and keep their organization protected?

With the EU’s NIS2 Directive putting greater emphasis on effective cybersecurity risk management, this question carries more weight than ever.

## Key Takeaways

* As the EU’s NIS2 Directive raises expectations for cybersecurity risk management, security leaders need to look beyond the solutions they have deployed and evaluate how effectively they work together.

* Having a SIEM, EDR, threat intelligence providers, and a dedicated SOC does not automatically mean your organization has complete visibility into cyber threats.

* Independent threat intelligence and behavioral analysis can help identify potential blind spots, strengthen existing security controls, and support more defensible security decisions.

## **Your Security Stack Is in Place. But How Confident Are You in Its Coverage?**

Investing in cybersecurity is just the first step to [compliance](https://any.run/compliance/?utm_source=anyrunblog&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=threat-intelligence-nis2-compliance&utm_term=121126&utm_content=linktocompliance) and risk mitigation. Most large companies have [established security operations](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/threat-intelligence-for-soc/), deployed detection technologies, and integrated threat intelligence into their workflows.

The EU’s NIS2 Directive reinforces the need to assess how efficient these measures are.

Article 21 requires covered entities to implement appropriate and proportionate technical, operational, and organizational measures to manage cybersecurity risks.

What do these requirements entail?

* **Risk analysis and security policies:** Understanding exposure and establishing appropriate controls.

* **Incident handling:** Detecting, investigating, and responding to security incidents.

* **Supply chain security:** Managing cybersecurity risks associated with suppliers and service providers.

* **Effectiveness assessment:** Evaluating how well cybersecurity risk-management measures work.

The keywords here are **appropriate** and **proportionate**. Not just any measures will do.

A SIEM may collect events, yes, but still miss emerging malicious indicators. An EDR may flag suspicious activity but lack the context needed to determine its significance. Multiple threat intelligence providers may deliver overlapping information while leaving gaps in coverage.

That’s why NIS2 readiness calls for more than having security tools in place. CISOs and SOC leaders need to assess whether their defenses can detect relevant threats, provide enough context for informed decisions, and support timely incident response.

Unfortunately, not every company is ready for that.

## **Get a Second Opinion on Threats Your Existing Intelligence May Miss**

Threat intelligence is a critical component of modern detection strategies. However, even subscribing to multiple intelligence providers doesn’t guarantee comprehensive threat coverage.

These providers may rely on similar collection methods, shared datasets, or established reporting sources. As a result, organizations can receive large volumes of intelligence without gaining significantly broader visibility into emerging threats.

So, as counterintuitive as it may sound, multiple intelligence providers can still leave you with the same blind spots.
