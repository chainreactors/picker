# [Webinar: How to Build AI Threat Readiness Into Your Security Operations](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/learn-how-to-build-security-operations.html)

**The Hacker News**Oct 29, 2026Artificial Intelligence / Webinar

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehacker.news/ai-threat-readiness)

Security teams have spent years trying to detect threats faster. AI is changing the harder part: how much time defenders have left to act.

Advanced AI models can now help attackers discover vulnerabilities, generate exploit code, and move through weaknesses faster than traditional security processes were built to handle.

The challenge is no longer just finding another vulnerability or generating another alert. It is knowing which exposures matter, what an attacker can reach, and which issues need to be fixed first.

That is the focus of next week's webinar, **[How to Build AI Threat Readiness Across Your Security Operations](https://thehacker.news/ai-threat-readiness)**, featuring an expert from Wiz. The session will show how security teams can improve visibility, prioritize real risk, and shorten the path from detection to remediation.

Most security teams already have plenty of data. They have vulnerability findings, cloud alerts, identity signals, application telemetry, and threat detections. The harder problem is connecting those signals quickly enough to answer the questions that drive action.

Is this vulnerability reachable? Does the exposed asset contain sensitive data? Can an attacker move from it to something more important? Who owns the affected system? What should be fixed first?

When those answers live across different tools and teams, investigations slow down. That delay matters more when attackers can automate parts of the discovery and exploitation process.

📅 **Save Your Spot Today:** [Register for the Webinar Here](https://thehacker.news/ai-threat-readiness).

The webinar focuses on two practical needs: broad visibility and faster action.

Security teams need enough context to understand risk across cloud infrastructure, code, identities, SaaS, AI services, and the software supply chain. They also need a way to identify exploitable paths, investigate suspicious activity, and deliver remediation to the right owner without having to rebuild the same context every time.

A Wiz expert will explain how organizations can use unified security context to separate urgent exposure from background noise, understand attack paths, and bring security agents into investigation and remediation workflows.

For SOC teams, that can mean less time spent manually assembling context. For vulnerability management, it means knowing which findings deserve immediate attention. For cloud and security engineering teams, it means connecting risk to the people and systems that can actually fix it.

The goal is not to automate every security decision. It is to remove delays caused by fragmented tools, repetitive investigation, and unclear ownership.

Attendees will leave with a framework for assessing whether their current security operations are ready for faster, AI-assisted attacks. The key questions are practical: Can you see enough of the environment to understand an attack path? Can you quickly determine whether a new issue affects something attackers can reach? Can you move a validated risk from detection to remediation fast enough?

AI is increasing the speed at which weaknesses can be found and used. Security teams need to respond with better context and less delay.
