# Framing the News: Anti-Piracy Press Release Turned a 2016 Raid Into a 2026 Story

today by
[Ernesto Van der Sar](https://torrentfreak.com/author/ernesto/)

[Home](https://torrentfreak.com "Go to TorrentFreak.") > [Opinion Articles](https://torrentfreak.com/category/opinion/ "Go to the Opinion Articles category archives.") >

In August, City of London Police and anti-piracy group FACT announced a six and a half year prison sentence for a 68-year-old pirate IPTV operator. Without dates or a service name in the release, some outlets reported it as a recent bust. Records obtained by TorrentFreak show the operation was raided in 2016, the operator pleaded guilty, and the service was called Ex Pat TV.

![](https://torrentfreak.com/images/pipcu-press-600x395.png)On August 27, City of London Police [published](https://www.cityoflondon.police.uk/news/city-of-london/news/2026/august/man-in-his-60s-jailed-for-6.5-years-after-making-over-980000-through-illegal-streaming/) a press release headlined: “Man in his 60s jailed for 6.5 years after making over £980,000 through illegal streaming.”

In the days that followed, this exact framing was repeated throughout many online news articles.

The release came with statements from the Federation Against Copyright Theft (FACT), the Premier League, Sky and BBC Studios. Additionally, FACT published the same release on [its own website](https://www.fact-uk.org.uk/man-in-his-60s-jailed-for-6-5-years-after-making-over-980000-through-illegal-streaming/) the next day.

*Headlines*
[![Headlines from news coverage of the Milan Ibrahim sentencing](https://torrentfreak.com/images/headlines-collage.png)](https://torrentfreak.com/images/headlines-collage.png)

City of London Police said that a 68-year-old from Chorley was “found guilty of conspiracy to defraud” at Preston Crown Court. His business ran on 80 servers, sold subscriptions to customers worldwide, particularly British expats, and generated £980,812 “over a three-year period.”

As is often the case with UK enforcement efforts, details are scarce. The official press releases didn’t mention the pirate service at stake, nor did they say anything about when these offenses took place, or what the reasoning of the court was.

Some news reports filled in these blanks themselves. The Mirror, for example, used the following [headline](https://www.mirror.co.uk/tech/sky-tv-illegal-streaming-block-37613305): “Massive streaming crackdown blocks more UK homes from watching Sky for free.”

The Mirror reported that a “major closure has taken place after a man behind a popular illegal streaming service was recently arrested.” The server seizure left customers “with nothing more than blank screens,” it added, suggesting that this was a recent action.

*The Mirror, September 1*
[![The Mirror article stating the operator was recently arrested](https://torrentfreak.com/images/mirror-recently-arrested.png)](https://torrentfreak.com/images/mirror-recently-arrested.png)

[IBC](https://www.ibc.org/piracy-security/news/man-jailed-after-making-almost-1m-through-illegal-streaming/22794) reported that all 80 servers “have now been seized,” and a [French site](https://www.letribunaldunet.fr/faits-divers/iptv-pirate-prison-milan-ibrahim-chorley.html) wrote that British justice had “just dismantled” the operation. Meanwhile, [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/68-year-old-imprisoned-after-making-13-million-by-pirating-iptv-services/) suggested the seized servers “may lead to the identification of users” who could face fines.

After some digging, we can now confirm that this case isn’t recent at all. In fact, the raid on Ibrahim’s operation dates back to 2016, more than a decade ago.

## Pleaded Guilty

The release got the three-year period right, but the rest of the picture looks different.

After several emails and a check of our press credentials, Preston Crown Court confirmed that Ibrahim pleaded guilty to two counts. For the indictment, the court referred us to the Crown Prosecution Service ([CPS](https://www.cps.gov.uk/)), which also named the service and answered follow-up questions, partly on background.

According to the CPS, Ibrahim pleaded guilty five days into his trial.

A guilty plea sounds different than being “found guilty”. When we asked the City of London Police about the wording in its press release, it said: “The jury did find him guilty but he plead guilty before. The terminology is fine to use for either.”

When a defendant changes his plea after the jury has been sworn in, the jury is typically asked to return a formal guilty verdict. The conviction rests on the plea.

## Which Three Years?

The indictment also fills in the dates, revealing that this case is far from new. All three counts run from June 1, 2013 to August 10, 2016.

That end date is the day of a raid. On August 10, 2016, [PIPCU searched a business and three homes in Chorley](https://www.fact-uk.org.uk/three-arrested-in-illegal-tv-set-top-boxes-raid/) and arrested three men, aged 36, 40 and 58, on suspicion of conspiracy to defraud and money laundering. Ibrahim was 58 at the time. The 2026 release does not mention this raid.

*From the indictment*
[![count one](https://torrentfreak.com/images/count-1.png)](https://torrentfreak.com/images/count-1.png)

TorrentFreak reported on the raid [the next day](https://torrentfreak.com/pipcu-raids-pirate-tv-streaming-operation-three-arrested-160811/). At the time, sources already pointed to a Chorley company selling IPTV packages to expats.

The CPS now confirms that the previously unnamed service was “initially known as ‘Ex Pat TV’ and latterly ‘My TV Abroad’.” This rebrand happened before the raid.

With those names, TorrentFreak was able to locate the service’s old website. An [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20160715174744/http%3A//www.mytvabroad.com/) of mytvabroad.com from July 15, 2016, less than four weeks before the raid, carries a banner announcing the switch: “BIG RE-BRAND! Ex-Pat TV is now My TV Abroad.”

*My TV Abroad, July 2016*
[![Archived copy of mytvabroad.com from July 15, 2016](https://torrentfreak.com/images/mytvabroad-2016.png)](https://torrentfreak.com/images/mytvabroad-2016.png)

The site promised “live UK channels, anywhere in the world” from £19 per month, with no VPN required. Its footer credits a web designer in Chorley.

That means FACT’s identification of the service, which it highlighted in the release, took place more than a decade ago. That wasn’t mentioned either. There’s more new information too.

## From Three Defendants to One

The press release mentions just one defendant, but the indictment names three. In addition to Milan Ibrahim, it lists two other men, who were also charged with conspiracy to defraud copyright holders, through live TV streams, movie streams and satellite decoders supplied to commercial premises.

Milan Ibrahim pleaded guilty to the first two counts but the other two men were not convicted. The Crown did not proceed against either of them. TorrentFreak understands that Milan Ibrahim was seen as the main instigator, and that the case against the other two was resolved as part of his plea. The exact arrangement hasn’t been confirmed.
