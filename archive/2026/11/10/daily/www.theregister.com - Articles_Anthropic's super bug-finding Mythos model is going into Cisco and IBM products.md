Anthropic has started extending access to its bug-finding Mythos model by allowing Cisco and IBM to make it part of their security products.

Cisco announced its integration today at an event in Melbourne, Australia, where it revealed that the red teaming tools in its AI Defense suite, which looks for weaknesses and inappropriate activity in AI models, agents, and applications they power, will be able to call Mythos to test customers’ setups.

“At a very high level, AI Defense has the ability to look at models and break them,” said Jeff Schultz, Cisco’s senior vice president for portfolio strategy and marketing. He said the tool is now “super-charged by Mythos” and will identify more danger that AI Defense will then address.

REG AD

AI Defense already uses AI-powered red teaming to find flaws. Mythos will become another option. Cisco will make it available under users’ existing licenses, without additional cost.

REG AD

Natalie Mead, Anthropic’s head of applied AI for the Asia-Pacific, said the company has a long queue of buyers who want to use Mythos, and working with Cisco will make it easier to satisfy that demand.

Anthropic initially made Mythos available under "Project Glasswing," a program that gave vetted organizations access to the model to test their codebases, but has since expanded access through other controlled programs, an arrangement that makes considerable sense because in the wrong hands Mythos could help attackers exploit software flaws.

The Cisco deal exposes the functionality of Mythos to end-users, without letting them use the model itself.

*The Register* asked Mead if Anthropic has similar deals with other third parties and she demurred. That may be because a few hours after Cisco told us about its use of Mythos, IBM [announced](https://www.ibm.com/new/announcements/ibm-plans-to-bring-anthropic-s-claude-mythos-cyber-reasoning-into-ibm-concert) that it will also integrate Anthropic’s model into a product – namely its Concert tool, which uses AI to scan applications for risks and weaknesses.

IBM says it will use Mythos “to uncover weaknesses traditional scanners miss, determine which pose real risk and generate fixes for developer approval.”

We have asked Anthropic if these integrations represent a decision to open Mythos to more users in more ways. We will update this story if we receive a substantial response.
