## Background

“EtherHiding” is a technique that we keep seeing with increased popularity in the threat landscape. Threat actors store data (for example C2 configuration) or code on a public blockchain, where they face no risk of a takedown due to the decentralized nature. Additionally, by using smart contracts, they are also able to update the stored information on the fly. This technique comes with the benefit that they can access it through a legitimate API endpoint which by itself often doesn’t ring any alarm bells, as no malicious infrastructure is used at this stage. We’ve talked about EtherHiding in the past for SharkStealer ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7386393828737347585/), [Webinar](https://www.vmray.com/resources/shark-stealer-etherhiding-captchas-threat-intel-webinar/)) and ArechClient2 ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vmray_alert-evolution-of-etherhiding-in-arechclient2-activity-7447284191920160768-u3mr/)).
