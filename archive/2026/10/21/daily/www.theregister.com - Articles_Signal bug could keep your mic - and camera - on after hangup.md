Make sure you don’t say anything mean or secret after hanging up a call on Signal, as a bug in the encrypted messaging app’s calling library could prevent a call from ending properly, potentially leaving audio and possibly video active.

The vulnerability, [CVE-2026-113380](https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2026-113380), was published by MITRE on Tuesday and given a CVSS score of 9.3, marking it as a critical issue in Signal’s open-source RingRTC library in every single version before 2.72.0. That update was [pushed out](https://github.com/signalapp/ringrtc/blob/a1d2ffed1de6ac5b22458a3dced8374513cc44f8/CHANGELOG.md) last month, but the vulnerability wasn’t made public until now.

RingRTC is Signal’s own calling library built on top of [WebRTC](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2013/01/15/wtf-is-webrtc/825467), a technology introduced in 2011 that has since become widely used for voice and video communication over the internet. RingRTC provides Signal-specific calling functionality using WebRTC, which already encrypts media traffic, but that isn't stopping RingRTC from failing to hang up calls in certain conditions, according to the CVE report.

REG AD

What’s happening is, in essence, a denial-of-service attack on RingRTC’s finite state machine (FSM) event queue, which handles events involved in managing calls. If one of the people on the call saturates the FSM queue with requests, it can become overwhelmed and fail to process a hangup request, preventing the call from terminating properly. The vulnerability is classified as [CWE-754](https://cwe.mitre.org/data/definitions/754.html), or improper checking for unusual or exceptional conditions, according to a [report](https://github.com/Imahian/Notes/tree/main/docs/CVEs/CVE-2026-113380) posted to GitHub.

REG AD

A conceptual proof of concept is available in the GitHub report linked above, using a Python script to simulate how flooding an event queue can prevent a hangup request from being processed.

Were a malicious actor to exploit this, the person on the other end may think the call has been terminated, but either or both of their video and audio channels could still be open.

The attack complexity is considered low according to the CVE, and while CISA’s known exploited vulnerabilities catalog doesn’t indicate this has been spotted in the wild, Signal users ought to ensure they’ve updated their apps - mobile and desktop - to the latest versions to ensure they don’t end up being caught on a hot mic without any notice.
