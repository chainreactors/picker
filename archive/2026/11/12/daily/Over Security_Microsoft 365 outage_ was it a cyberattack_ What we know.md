**Update, Friday 13 November 2026, 08:30 UTC:** Microsoft 365 is still not fully working about 15 hours after the outage began. Microsoft reported only “marginal success” from its repairs at 06:56 UTC, then said at 07:25 UTC that a new patch was improving server stability. It has given no time for full recovery, says it is not aware of any malicious activity, and has promised a post-incident report. What changed overnight is set out below, followed by the original report and an extended timeline.

Microsoft 365 went down for users around the world on Thursday 12 November 2026, taking Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, Copilot and Defender XDR with it. Microsoft says the cause was a large number of its own servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable, not a surge in traffic, and nothing it has published points to a cyberattack. By 3:30 p.m. ET (20:30 UTC) it was restarting servers and restoring service.

Outage trackers also lit up for Amazon Web Services, Google and Gmail, which is why the day felt like half the internet had failed. Their own status pages did not agree. This is what is confirmed, what is not, and why an outage like this is still a security problem.

## What has changed since Thursday?

The outage ran through the night and the first fixes did not hold. At 08:30 UTC on Friday 13 November, Microsoft had not declared incidents EX1550543 and MO1550821 over and had given no time for full recovery. Its most recent update, at 07:25 UTC, said a patch applied to part of its infrastructure was improving server stability and was being extended to more servers. This section was added on 13 November. The sections after it are the report as published on Thursday.
