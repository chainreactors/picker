# Publishers Seek $19.5m From Pirate Library WeLib, Recycle Anna’s Archive Claims

today by
[Ernesto Van der Sar](https://torrentfreak.com/author/ernesto/)

[Home](https://torrentfreak.com "Go to TorrentFreak.") > [Law and Politics](https://torrentfreak.com/category/law-politics/ "Go to the Law and Politics category archives.") >

A group of major publishers has asked a New York federal court to issue a $19.5 million default judgment against pirate library WeLib. The site allegedly copied its code and most of its collection from Anna's Archive, which lost a nearly identical case in May. Ironically, the publishers' brief appears to 'copy' from Anna's Archive as well, or at least from their own lawsuit against the shadow library.

![welib logo](https://torrentfreak.com/images/weliblo.png)
Last year, Anna’s Archive warned its users about WeLib, a “[new entrant](https://annas-archive.gl/blog/an-update-from-the-team.html)” in the shadow library ecosystem.

The site appeared to have mirrored most of Anna’s collection and used a fork of its codebase. Since WeLib wasn’t sharing new collections or code improvements in return, Anna’s Archive cautioned prospective users.

“We recommend not using them,” the team [wrote](https://annas-archive.gl/blog/an-update-from-the-team.html) at the time.

In June, major book publishers came to a similar conclusion, but for different reasons. They filed a lawsuit [against WeLib](https://torrentfreak.com/major-publishers-sue-welib-a-pirate-site-built-on-annas-archive-code/) in New York federal court, alleging that the site “was created after its operators copied the source code and most of the contents of” Anna’s Archive.

*WeLib*

![welib website](https://torrentfreak.com/images/welibfeat1.png)

Despite these and other copyright infringement allegations, WeLib’s anonymous operators never responded to the lawsuit. This prompted the publishers to move forward and the court clerk entered a default in August, opening the door to a final judgment request.

## $19.5 Million, Again

On Monday, the publishers, including [Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/), [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.com/), and [HarperCollins](https://www.harpercollins.com/), asked U.S. District Judge Victor Marrero to issue a default judgment against WeLib and its unnamed operators.

“Defendant WeLib is a pirate site whose sole business is the distribution of protected content in violation of the rights of Plaintiffs and other copyright owners,” their brief reads.

The publishers request the maximum statutory damages of $150,000 for each of the 130 works listed in the complaint, ten per publisher. That adds up to $19.5 million, the same amount Judge Jed Rakoff [awarded](https://torrentfreak.com/annas-archive-hit-with-19-5m-default-judgment-and-global-domain-takedown-order/) in the Anna’s Archive case.

The brief also puts a spotlight on WeLib’s revenue model. The site can be used for free, but it asks users for “donations” of up to $90 a month. According to the publishers, these are simply payments for faster downloads.

In addition to the damages, the publishers want a permanent injunction. This would require domain registries and registrars to disable welib.org, welib.st, and welib-public.org, and to block their transfer to anyone other than the publishers. Hosting providers would have to stop serving the sites.

*Named intermediaries*

![intermediaries](https://torrentfreak.com/images/namedintermed.png)

The injunction would apply to all applicable intermediaries, but the proposed order names five specifically: [Public Interest Registry](https://thenew.org/), [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/), [Tucows](https://www.tucows.com/), and two [Njalla](https://njal.la/) entities. That’s a much shorter list than the Anna’s Archive judgment, which named more than twenty.

## Borrowed From Anna’s Archive

It is not unusual that arguments from a similar case are reused and that also applies here. Large parts of the WeLib brief mirror the one that law firm [Oppenheim + Zebrak](https://www.oandzlaw.com/) filed against Anna’s Archive in May, on behalf of the same publishers.

However, not everything fits. According to the brief, WeLib diverts revenue from the publishers “at a scale made worse by their use of the viral BitTorrent protocol.”

We haven’t seen any evidence that WeLib uses BitTorrent, and the WeLib complaint doesn’t mention it either. Instead, the cited paragraphs discuss various harms including lost sales and harm to the market for AI training data.

The same sentence appears, word for word, in the brief against Anna’s Archive. There, it was backed up by the complaint, which described how Anna’s Archive offers torrent files for bulk downloads on a dedicated “Torrents” page.

*Same sentence, different site*

![welib vs anna](https://torrentfreak.com/images/welib-bittorrent-compare-2.png)

WeLib’s own complaint mentions another download mechanism. It describes how the site serves files directly from its servers, through “slow” and paid “fast” downloads.

The brief also mentions that WeLib’s infringement continued “despite this and several other cases against them.” That entire paragraph comes straight from the Anna’s Archive brief. While Anna’s Archive was sued before, we are not aware of any other lawsuits against WeLib’s operators.

Even a typo and a garbled sentence with a double negative were copied. Both briefs mention what happens if the court “*is satified*” that the default standards are met, and both argue that the defendants “cannot establish that they have not presented any meritorious defenses.”

## A Paper Victory?

It’s now up to Judge Marrero to decide on the request. Whatever the outcome, the publishers are unlikely to collect much, if anything, as WeLib’s operators remain anonymous.

Anna’s Archive lost several domains to an earlier injunction obtained by music companies. However, it [remains online](https://torrentfreak.com/annas-archive-owes-340-million-lost-several-domains-but-its-still-online/) through .gl, .pk, and .gd domains, which are tied to registries and registrars outside the United States.

If Anna’s Archive is any indication, the injunction may cost WeLib some of its domains. However, that is no guarantee that the site will shut down permanently.
