Britain is threatening subsea cable saboteurs with up to 14 years in prison and unlimited fines, having apparently decided that Victorian-era laws aren't quite up to the job of keeping Russian submarines away from critical infrastructure.

The government [on Thursday set out plans](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/subsea-cable-saboteurs-face-jail-and-unlimited-fines-under-new-security-crackdown) to overhaul legislation dating back to 1885 and introduce tougher penalties for damaging the underwater cables and pipelines that keep Britain's internet and energy supplies running.

According to Whitehall, subsea cables carry around 99 percent of the UK's international data traffic, while underwater pipelines account for 74 percent of its gas supply. Yet much of this infrastructure is protected by laws written when the telegraph was cutting-edge technology.

REG AD

The Submarine Telegraph Act 1885 originally imposed a £100 maximum fine for damaging a cable through culpable negligence. That might have been a reasonable deterrent back then, but it's unlikely to give today's would-be saboteurs much pause.

REG AD

Under the proposals, those who intentionally damage critical subsea energy or telecoms infrastructure could face up to 14 years behind bars. The proposals also set tougher penalties, including unlimited fines, for reckless or negligent damage.

Subsea telecoms operators would face new legal duties to identify and manage security risks, while ministers would gain powers to direct companies and cable repair providers to take action where necessary and proportionate to protect national security.

The government also wants to regulate fiber-optic sensing technology, which uses subsea cables to detect activity at sea but could pose a security risk if sensitive data falls into hostile hands.

Unsurprisingly, Russia features prominently in Whitehall's thinking.

In April, British forces exposed a covert Russian submarine operation near critical undersea infrastructure.[The operation reportedly involved](https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/06/01/putin-sends-submarines-to-survey-britains-subsea-cables-uk-deploys-royal-navy-mobilizes-parliamentary-draftsmen/5248978) an attack submarine acting as a decoy while two specialist vessels surveyed Britain's cable routes.

"The threats we face have changed dramatically, with suspicious Russian activity around critical undersea infrastructure, yet some of the laws protecting these vital connections date back more than a century," said telecoms minister Ian Murray.
