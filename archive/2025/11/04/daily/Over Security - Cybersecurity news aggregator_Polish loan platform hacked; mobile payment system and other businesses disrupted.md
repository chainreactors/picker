# Polish loan platform hacked; mobile payment system and other businesses disrupted

Polish authorities are investigating a series of cyberattacks that disrupted digital services and exposed personal data from several major companies, including a leading online lender and the country’s top mobile payment system.

Digital Affairs Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski [said](https://x.com/KGawkowski/status/1984902997239832822) cyberattacks targeting Poland’s public and private infrastructure are becoming “commonplace.”

“We’re seeing thousands of incidents reported daily,” he [added](https://tvn24.pl/biznes/tech/ataki-hakerskie-itaka-blik-supergrosz-wicepremier-i-minister-cyfryzacji-komentuje-st8733626).

The largest breach hit online loan platform SuperGrosz, operated by AIQLABS, which [confirmed](https://web.archive.org/web/20251102091113/https%3A//supergrosz.pl/kontakt) that cybercriminals had stolen personal data belonging to at least 10,000 customers. The leaked information includes names, addresses, ID and tax numbers, phone contacts, employment details and bank account numbers, the company said in a statement. It warned that the true scale of the attack could be higher and urged clients to monitor for fraudulent credit activity.

In a separate incident, hackers [launched](https://x.com/KGawkowski/status/1984566539467550826) a distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack on Poland’s payment infrastructure, briefly disrupting Blik, the country’s leading mobile payment system used for instant transfers and cash withdrawals, according to Gawkowski. Blik [said](https://x.com/BLIKmobile/status/1985335859915239447) on Monday that services had been restored after “temporary problems with processing payments.”

Another attack [targeted](https://x.com/KGawkowski/status/1984554820187967896) Nowa Itaka, Poland’s largest travel agency, leaking names, emails, and phone numbers of customers, according to Gawkowski. The company [said](https://businessinsider.com.pl/wiadomosci/atak-hakerski-na-nowa-itake-wyciekly-dane-klientow-najwiekszego-biura-podrozy/y4t0m9g) booking details, financial data, and account passwords were not affected.

Authorities have not confirmed whether the incidents are linked, but Gawkowski [said](https://tvn24.pl/biznes/tech/ataki-hakerskie-itaka-blik-supergrosz-wicepremier-i-minister-cyfryzacji-komentuje-st8733626) the attack on Blik “leads to Russia,” calling it “the next stage of hybrid warfare.” Officials across Europe have [warned](https://therecord.media/french-intelligence-chief-russia-threat) about Moscow’s [expansion](https://therecord.media/russia-hybrid-war-europe-von-der-leyen-speech) of influence, espionage and sabotage campaigns.

Poland, one of Ukraine’s key allies and a NATO member, has faced a growing number of cyber intrusions since Moscow’s invasion in 2022. Gawkowski [warned](https://tvn24.pl/biznes/tech/ataki-hakerskie-itaka-blik-supergrosz-wicepremier-i-minister-cyfryzacji-komentuje-st8733626) that 2025 could become a record year for cyberattacks, with both state and criminal actors expanding their focus from local utilities to financial and energy systems.

“Russian activity is the most severe because it targets critical infrastructure essential to maintaining normal life,” he said in a recent [interview](https://www.reuters.com/technology/poland-says-cyberattacks-critical-infrastructure-rising-blames-russia-2025-10-10/).
