We'd all like the internet to be a better place—for kids and adults alike. But in the name of online safety, governments around the world are racing to impose a dangerous new system of control. Are age gates the silver bullet to the internet's problems they're being promoted as? Or are we being sold a bill of goods? We're answering this question and more in [our latest EFFector newsletter](https://www.eff.org/effector/38/9).

[JOIN OUR NEWSLETTER](https://www.eff.org/effector/)

For over 35 years, [EFFector](https://eff.org/effector) has been your guide to understanding the intersection of technology, civil liberties, and the law. This latest issue covers an attack on [VPNs in Utah](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/04/utahs-new-law-regulating-vpns-goes-effect-next-week), a livestream on [how to disenshittify the internet](https://www.eff.org/event/effecting-change-enshittification), and California's proposed [social media ban](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/04/act-now-stop-californias-paternalistic-and-privacy-destroying-social-media-ban) that could set a dangerous new precedent for online censorship.
