# Cyberattack hits Norwegian energy giant Equinor, disrupting internal services

The Norwegian energy giant Equinor said Wednesday it was working to contain a cyberattack on its IT systems.

The company, one of Europe's largest natural gas suppliers, detected the attack on Tuesday and said its oil and gas production had not been disrupted and there was no indication that data had been stolen.

In an [update](https://www.equinor.com/news/20261711-equinor-detected-cyber-attack) Wednesday, Equinor warned that restoring the affected systems could take time.

"Work is ongoing to neutralise the attack and restore full integrity to the affected systems," the company said. "It is too early to determine how long this will take."

According to Equinor, the attack did not involve a computer virus and therefore posed no risk of spreading through viral infection. The company added there was no indication the incident had affected employees, business partners or suppliers.

Equinor has not identified the hackers, disclosed how they gained access to its systems or specified which parts of its IT infrastructure were compromised.

Company spokesperson Ellen Maria Skjelsbæk [told](https://www.bt.no/innenriks/i/9BvoRM/equinor-har-avdekket-et-paagaaende-cyberangrep-it-systemer) Norwegian news agency NTB that employees could experience problems accessing corporate applications and IT services, although the company still had access to its systems. Reuters separately [reported](https://www.reuters.com/technology/equinor-detects-ongoing-cyberattack-output-unaffected-2026-11-17/), citing an unnamed source at Equinor, that the company's internal email system was down.

Equinor said it was working with Norwegian national security authorities to investigate and contain the incident.

The state-controlled company operates major offshore installations in the North Sea and plays a critical role in Europe's energy supply. It employs around 25,000 people and operates in more than 20 countries. The Norwegian government owns 67 percent of the company, whose operations span oil and gas production, transportation and renewable energy.

The attack comes amid growing concerns in Norway over cyber threats to businesses, government agencies and critical infrastructure.

Earlier this month, Norwegian retail technology provider Front Systems [suffered](https://egsoftware.com/no/detaljhandel-engros/front-systems/pressemeldinger/informasjon-om-datainnbrudd-hos-front-systems) a cyberattack that exposed information stored on its point-of-sale platform, potentially affecting customers and employees of clothing retailers across the country. The company's technology is used by more than 10 percent of Norway's fashion retailers.

In late October, Norway's development aid agency, Norad, also [disclosed](https://www.norad.no/en/news/news/2026/norad-targeted-by-cyberattack/) a cyberattack that may have exposed the personal information of more than 20,000 people worldwide, along with sensitive grant applications submitted by organizations operating abroad.

An attack in August [disrupted](https://therecord.media/norway-cyberattack-ddos-government) nearly a dozen Norwegian government digital services for more than a day, affecting systems used for identity verification, public service logins and information exchanges between government agencies and businesses. Norway's National Security Authority (NSM) [raised](https://nsm.no/aktuelt/nsm-hever-det-nasjonale-cyberrisikonivaet-til-hoyt) the country's cyber risk level to four on a five-point scale in October, saying that  successful cyber operations against Norwegian organizations had tripled over the preceding six months.

Norwegian security and civil protection authorities have also [warned](https://nsm.no/aktuelt/ber-befolkningen-forberede-seg-pa-cyberangrep) that cyberattacks targeting critical infrastructure could disrupt essential services, including electricity, water supplies, mobile communications and internet access.
