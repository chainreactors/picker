# Between Graves and Uncertainty: The Management of the Dead After Venezuela's Earthquake

July 6, 2026

* [Earthquake](/tag/earthquake)
* [Venezuela](/tag/venezuela)

Bellingcat has geolocated [footage](https://www.facebook.com/watch/?v=1451641893396902) circulating on social media that appears to show coffins placed in newly dug trenches following the recent deadly earthquakes in Venezuela.

The [site](https://www.google.com/maps/search/10.567123%2C-67.066367?sa=X&ved=1t:242&ictx=111) identified extends over two hectares beside an existing cemetery in La Esperanza, a town near La Guaira on the country’s northern coast.

It was visited by a representative of our Latin American reporting partners who captured pictures of work ongoing at the site. They also report speaking to a resident who said that refrigerated trucks with several bodies had been coming and going.

![](https://www.bellingcat.com/app/uploads/2026/07/Screenshot-2026-07-06-at-21.08.53.jpg)

*Stills from a video published by Colombian* outlet *TV and showing a group* of coffins *in a dug trench. Source: RTV* [*Facebook*](https://www.facebook.com/watch/?v=1451641893396902)

The location matches a site identified in July 6 reports by [AFP](https://www.france24.com/es/minuto-a-minuto/20260706-venezuela-entierra-cuerpos-no-identificados-tras-los-terremotos-que-dejan-m%C3%A1s-de-3-000-muertos) and [Deutsche Welle](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DadHsODjqo4/) (DW), which detailed that 150 unidentified bodies had been buried in a long row of individual graves.

AFP and DW published pictures of individual crosses and stones, quoting a resident of the town who stated that the burials were “numbered by plots and also by the code” so they could be identified at a later date.

It is not known if the coffins visible in the social media footage relate to the 150 unidentified bodies later referred to by AFP and DW, or if they are separate burials at the same general location.

Reuters also [published](https://www.reutersconnect.com/all?id=tag%3Areuters.com%2C2026%3Anewsml_RC2C8MAA73S1%3A1768466125&media-types=portrait_landscape&search=all%3Ala%20esperanza) pictures of the site on July 6 and showed [video](https://www.reutersconnect.com/all?id=tag%3Areuters.com%2C2026%3Anewsml_RW566306072026RP1%3A3&search=all%3Ala%20esperanza) of coffins arriving on the back of flat bed trucks.

More than  3,500 people are [confirmed](https://es.euronews.com/2026/07/06/terremotos-venezuela-dejan-3535-muertos-16740-heridos) to have died as a result of the earthquakes so far. But that figure is expected to rise significantly, with the UN [reporting](https://news.sky.com/story/venezuela-earthquakes-latest-aftershock-hits-as-miracle-rescues-reported-days-after-deadly-double-quake-13557399) that the death toll could reach [10,000](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/06/1167837).

Oran Finegan, Director of Forensic Action International and former Head of Forensics for the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), told Bellingcat that, while it is best practice for the burial of deceased persons to take place in individual graves, it is not uncommon to see long trenches like those seen in the social media footage when there are high numbers of unidentified deceased and it is not practical to immediately provide individual graves.

He pointed to [documentation](https://shop.icrc.org/management-of-dead-bodies-after-disasters-a-field-manual-for-first-responders-pdf-en.html) from the ICRC that details best practices in such circumstances. He also made the important distinction between common graves, where bodies are temporarily kept until identification can take place, and mass graves, where bodies are dumped clandestinely without any care or process. What is seen in the footage appears to be the former, he said. There has been no evidence of the latter.

Finnegan emphasised that it was vital that burials were mapped and recorded properly during any burial process so that identification could take place at a later date. Documenting where bodies were coming from, laying each body with enough distance from each other and ensuring each coffin or body bag had a unique number was key, he said.

While it has not been possible to ascertain the exact processes being followed at or preceding burials at the La Esperanza site, media reports in nearby La Guaira have recorded complications with identification and burial processes.

According to [the BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c20y2p9qqqko)  the scale of the disaster has overwhelmed local services, forcing institutions to improvise. Some bodies were being placed outside, exposed to the sun, at a port facility in La Guaira, the BBC reported.

A further complication is that many of the bodies recovered have [reportedly](https://www.euronews.com/2026/07/03/venezuelas-interim-leader-defends-earthquake-response-as-death-toll-tops-2500#:~:text=Forensic%20staff%20estimate%20it%20could%20take%20up%20to%20three%20months%20to%20collect%20all%20the%20victims%2C%20warning%20that%20mass%20graves%20will%20be%20necessary.) been unrecognisable.

One woman [told](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DaWrno7tqeV/) the independent Venezuelan publication [RunRun.es](http://runrun.es) that she was sent a tag and number for a body bag that did not match the bodies of her relatives. She was then told that her relatives’ bodies had been misidentified and sent for burial at a site in the town of Los Teques.

The New York Times [reported](https://www.nytimes.com/es/2026/07/01/espanol/america-latina/venezuela-terremotos-muertos-cuerpos.html) last week that overwhelmed morgues were filling with unidentified bodies, forcing authorities to consider mass burials.
