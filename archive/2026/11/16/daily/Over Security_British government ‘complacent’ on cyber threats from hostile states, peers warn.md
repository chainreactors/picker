# British government ‘complacent’ on cyber threats from hostile states, peers warn

The United Kingdom is “underprepared” for “potentially catastrophic risks” including cyberattacks from Russia, China and Iran, a House of Lords committee said Monday.

The National Resilience Committee’s [report](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld5902/ldselect/ldresilience/79/79.pdf), published eight days before a major vote on Britain’s landmark Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, warns that the legislation falls short of introducing the necessary security standards needed to protect the country.

The [much-delayed](https://therecord.media/uk-cybersecurity-law-update-csrb-delayed-again) bill, which was [introduced](https://therecord.media/british-gov-cybersecurity-law) to the House of Commons last year, has now been more than five years in development. The committee wrote that the “government’s legislative programme is not keeping pace with the resilience challenges identified by this inquiry.”

It urged ministers to “widen the scope of cyber security regulation… ensuring that regulators have sufficient resources and capacity to enforce requirements effectively.”

The committee said the government “appears complacent” and stressed the “need to move fast to turn things around.”

The public “don’t have a full picture of the level and frequency of current sub-threshold hostility against the UK from Russia, China and Iran in the form of cyberattacks, proxy acts of violence, or threats to undersea cables and pipelines,” the report said.

The influential committee said the government “should stop being squeamish about telling it straight to the public about the kind of threats we face.”

While the new cyber bill “would provide useful baseline requirements,” the committee said “its scope is limited.”

In the House of Lords last month, peers defeated the government twice in votes calling for regular reviews to check whether the legislation was fit for purpose, and for a register of foreign powers that pose cybersecurity threats to Britain.

The report additionally echoes concerns raised when the government published its own cyber [action plan](https://therecord.media/uk-government-cyber-action-plan) in January, with many onlookers noting that most of the public sector is not covered by the bill.

The government’s National Cyber Action Plan, the government's forthcoming strategy for defending the wider economy against state-backed and criminal hacking, has also [faced delays](https://therecord.media/launch-of-uk-national-cyber-action-plan-delayed). Sources who spoke to Recorded Future News earlier this month said they expected it to be published in the middle of November.

Michael Brunton-Spall, the deputy director of cyber services at the Government Cyber Unit, told the committee the government and public sector face “critically high cyber and digital resilience risks” and “extremely low levels of resilience.”

Paddy McGuinness, a former British deputy national security adviser, was asked during a witness session whether the government’s plans matched the threat. “The answer to your core question about whether it is adequate to the severity of the cyber threat is no,” he said.

A spokesperson for the Cabinet Office said: “We thank the Committee for their report, which we will carefully review. It highlights the need for action across government and society to boost our collective resilience - closely involving local, regional and national bodies. That is exactly what this government is doing.”
