After being instructed to "NEVER stop, NEVER give up" on a difficult task, Google’s Gemini AI agents tried to hack a an organization, impersonating its staff and attempting a SQL injection attack.

The good news? It was all part of a test.

The Gemini 4 Argon agents were given an impossible task to complete – come up with the “official” answers to Epoch AI’s FrontierMatch problems. In some runs they had internet access. Even with their production safeguards enabled, Trajectory Labs says the agents “engaged in misaligned behavior.”

REG AD

“Across four scenarios, they attempted SQL injection against Epoch AI’s live website, claimed to be Epoch staff to get answer keys, turned a shared package cache into a message board, and obfuscated their commands to avoid their safeguards,” the AI safety company [said](https://www.trajectorylabs.com/blog/gemini-4-argon-behavior-during-misalignment-testing) in a Friday report.

REG AD

If this all sounds similar, it should. Google’s AI agents behaved in much the same way as OpenAI’s models did when they [broke out of their testing environment](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/06/openai-reveals-its-rogue-agent-swarm-went-a-little-bit-borg-ahead-of-hugging-face-hack/5283741) and [hacked Hugging Face](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/27/openai-explains-how-its-naughty-ai-agents-attacked-hugging-face/5292780).

Google did not respond to The Register's questions.

According to Trajectory, the Hugging Face intrusions prompted it to test misalignment in other models. While OpenAI’s models were running with their safety guardrails turned off in the Hugging Face scenario, Trajectory “wanted to know whether a current frontier model would behave the same way with its production safeguards enabled.”

Trajectory ran swarms of up to 80 Gemini 4 Argon agents, gave them an impossible task, and prompted them not to give up until they completed it – which, we’re told, was “intentionally” done to “elicit misaligned behavior, if it existed.”

Turns out it existed.

“We told the agents to persist with an impossible math benchmark task, but we did not tell them to hack anyone or pose as Epoch staff,” McIntyre said.
