# Canadian regulator opens probe of IDScan for allegedly violating data privacy laws

Privacy Commissioner of Canada Philippe Dufresne has launched a probe of [IDScan.net](http://idscan.net) in the wake of reports that a bad actor penetrated company databases to steal personal data and scans of 153 million people's drivers’ licenses.

IDScan.net’s technology is widely used by industry — particularly in the retail and hospitality sectors — to verify official IDs.

The investigation, announced Monday, will probe IDScan’s security practices and whether victim notifications were adequate under Canada’s federal private-sector privacy law, the regulator said in a [press release](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2026/nr-c_260921/?mkt_tok=MTM4LUVaTS0wNDIAAAGkaQ3Lujiw5dB8Ge7B7wIOybVwgnnbx9Q3eyl1U5cKghQc8_fMlo4iRbh21gVqWMjnA0R4eJaIb0_rJVYAXQTOzjHTXN52hAu5a6ZBMaLzaEjaQg).

On September 4, the identity verification company [announced](https://idscan.net/notification-data-security-incident/?7194ef805fa2d04b0f7e8c9521f97343) hackers obtained user data held in its cloud platform, but did not say how many customers were affected. The statement came amid a series of reports asserting that the breach [exposed](https://therecord.media/idscan-data-breach-notice-drivers-licenses) driver’s licenses belonging to tens of millions.

IDScan learned of the breach on or around September 1, hours after journalist Brian Krebs [revealed](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/09/fbi-probes-service-selling-153m-drivers-licenses/) the scans were on sale on the dark web.

The identity verification company did not respond to a request for comment.

The investigation is ongoing.
