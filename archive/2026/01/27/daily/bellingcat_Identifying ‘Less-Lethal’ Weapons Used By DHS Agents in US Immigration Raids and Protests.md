# Identifying ‘Less-Lethal’ Weapons Used By DHS Agents in US Immigration Raids and Protests

January 27, 2026

* [ICE](/tag/ice)
* [Immigration](/tag/immigration)
* [Police](/tag/police)

*To stay up to date on our latest investigations, join Bellingcat’s WhatsApp channel [here](https://www.whatsapp.com/channel/0029VbB7PAn9RZAd6F1R1m2r)*.

Federal agents have frequently used so-called “less-lethal” weapons against protesters, including impact projectiles, tear gas and pepper spray, since the Trump administration’s [nationwide immigration raids began last year](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c04ngq3lwl2o).

The use of less-lethal weapons (LLWs) has been controversial. While designed to incapacitate or control a person [without causing death or permanent injury](https://legal-resources.uslegalforms.com/l/less-lethal-weapon), they can cause serious or fatal injuries, [especially when used improperly](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2023/03/global-dozens-killed-and-thousands-maimed-by-police-misuse-of-rubber-bullets/).

Earlier this month, two protesters in California were [reportedly blinded](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/17/protesters-blind-us-federal-agents) after US federal agents fired less-lethal rounds at their faces from close range. These incidents were part of a wave of violent clashes between agents from the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and protesters across the country after the [deadly shooting](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/2026/01/13/analysing-footage-of-minneapolis-ice-shooting/) of US citizen Renee Good by an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agent in Minneapolis.

![](https://www.bellingcat.com/app/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-09T131013Z_1714818413_MT1SIPA000N1BQR0_RTRMADP_3_SIPA-USA-1-1200x800.jpg)

*Federal agents armed with less-lethal weapons in Minneapolis on Friday, Jan. 9, 2026. Source: Cristina Matuozzi/Sipa USA via Reuters Connect*

In protests in Minneapolis immediately following Good’s death, one Customs and Border Patrol (CBP) officer was captured on camera firing a 40mm less-lethal launcher [five times in less than five minutes](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/2026/01/16/border-patrol-minneapolis/), with several of these shots appearing to target protesters’ faces, which is against CBP’s own [use-of-force policy](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2024-09/exhibit_09_-_cbp_use_of_force_policy_final_jan_2021.pdf#page=20).

A [Bellingcat investigation](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/2025/10/31/illinois-immigration-protests/) of DHS incidents in October 2025 also found about 30 incidents that appeared to violate a temporary restraining order (TRO) issued by an Illinois judge restricting how DHS agents could use LLWs.

It is not always obvious whether the use of a LLW is authorised or not, as DHS component agencies such as ICE and CBP have [varying guidance](https://www.oig.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/assets/2021-04/OIG-21-31-Mar21.pdf) on factors such as the level of resistance an individual needs to show before a certain type of force can be used, as well as how specific types of less-lethal weapons and munitions can be used.

While CBP’s use-of-force policy as of January 2021 is [available on its website](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2024-09/exhibit_09_-_cbp_use_of_force_policy_final_jan_2021.pdf#page=20), ICE does not include specific guidance on less-lethal weapons in its 2023 [“Firearms and Use of Force” Directive](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ilnd.487571/gov.uscourts.ilnd.487571.284.39.pdf), and does not appear to have any publicly available policy that outlines this guidance.

DHS did not respond by publication time to Bellingcat’s request for the most recent DHS, CBP and ICE use-of-force policies, or to questions about what less-lethal weapons were authorised for use by the department and its component agencies.

The [DHS use-of-force policy](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2023-02/23_0206_s1_use-of-force-policy-update.pdf), updated in February 2023, states that the department’s law enforcement officers and agents may use force, including LLWs, “only when no reasonably effective, safe and feasible alternative appears to exist”. It also says agents may only use a level of force that is “objectively reasonable in light of the facts and circumstances” that they face at the time.

DHS has [repeatedly](https://abc7chicago.com/post/federal-judge-orders-ice-dhs-agents-dial-back-aggressive-response-journalists-protesters-chicago-area/17972763/) [defended](https://abcnews4.com/news/nation-world/portland-nonprofit-may-sue-feds-over-protest-munitions-near-housing-complex-grays-landing-protest-immigration-customs-enforcement-pirc-south-portland-facility-detention-federal-officers-agents-donald-trump-homeland-security) its use of riot-control weapons in protests across the country, stating that it was “taking reasonable and constitutional measures to uphold the rule of law and protect [its] officers”.

Here’s how to identify some of the less-lethal weapons that DHS agents, including those from ICE and CBP, have been seen using during recent immigration operations.

## Compressed Air Launchers or ‘PepperBall Guns’

![](https://www.bellingcat.com/app/uploads/2026/01/pepperball-gun-agents-1200x1075.jpg)

*Left: A Border Patrol Agent in Chicago carrying an orange TAC-SF series PepperBall gun in Illinois on Oct. 24, 2025. Right: Agent aiming a Pepperball gun at someone filming them in Illinois on Oct. 19, 2025. Source:* [*Youtube / @BlockClubChicago*](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/TQuADuvH1vY) *and* [*Tiktok / @ericcervantes25*](https://www.tiktok.com/%40ericcervantes25/video/7562993396009159950)

Compressed air, or pneumatic launchers, are essentially paintball guns that fire [0.68mm balls](https://pepperball.com/projectiles/) which break on impact. Often, this releases a powdered chemical irritant such as oleoresin capsicum (OC) or PavaPowder – the same compounds typically found in pepper spray.

Compressed air launchers can also be used with other projectiles, such as “marking” projectiles that use paint to mark an individual for later arrest, and projectiles intended to break glass.

These weapons are often referred to as “PepperBall” guns, named after the leading brand PepperBall. However, DHS agents have also [been seen carrying](https://www.tiktok.com/%40anthony.depice/video/7514818626751319326?_r=1&_t=ZT-8xCsafjycNq) compressed air launchers from different brands, such as the FN303, produced by FN America.
