When governments shut down spaces for dialogue, dissent, and collective organizing, the damage extends far beyond a single event. The [abrupt cancellation](https://www.techpolicy.press/rightscon-canceled-after-zambia-requires-full-alignment-with-national-values/) of [RightsCon](https://www.rightscon.org/) 2026—the world’s largest annual global digital rights conference—is not just a logistical disruption for thousands of researchers, journalists, technologists, and activists—it is part of a growing global pattern of shrinking civic space and increasing hostility toward free expression and independent civil society.

Just days before the conference was set to begin and as participants had begun to arrive in Lusaka, organizers [announced](https://www.rightscon.org/rc26-statement/) that RightsCon would no longer proceed in Zambia or online after mounting political pressure and demands that would have excluded vulnerable communities and constrained discussion.
