**Final update, 00:05 UTC on 17 November 2026 (7:05 p.m. ET on 16 November):** Chase says transactions are posting normally again. Live updates have ended.

Customers of Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and U.S. Bank reported problems with online banking on Monday 16 November 2026. There is no evidence of a cyberattack. Chase [said on X](https://x.com/Chase/status/2122433813955379268) that the cause was “a software bug in on-premise hardware systems” and that there is no evidence it was cyber-related. At about 6:30 p.m. ET, roughly eight and a half hours after the first reports, a Chase spokesperson [told NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/outages-chase-banking-online-branches-rcna609673): “Transactions are now posting normally.”

The reports did not start together. Downdetector logged Chase first, at 9:53 a.m. ET, and the other banks one to four hours later. Plaid, the service many finance apps use to connect to bank accounts, [opened an incident for Chase](https://status.plaid.com/) at 10:11 a.m. ET and has none for the other banks. A fault shared by all of them would more likely show up at the same moment. CNBC, citing a source, reports no sign of one bug affecting several banks, [according to Reuters](https://koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261117/downdetector-reports-outages-at-major-us-banks).

Chase says other banks were slowed only in transactions that involved Chase. Zelle and U.S. Bank describe the same thing from their side. Zelle says its own network is working, and a spokesman [told NBC News](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/national-international/chase-bank-of-america-wells-fargo-bank-outages-reported/3888781/) the delays started with Chase. U.S. Bank says its systems “are not directly impacted”, but that Zelle payments to or from another bank may be slow, [Hearst Television reports](https://www.wbaltv.com/article/bank-outages-chase-wells-fargo-bank-of-america/74443090).
