# Texas and Florida Step Back from ALPRs

![Flock cameras and surveillance cameras among barbed wire in a dystopian scene](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/flock-dystopia-scene-1.png?itok=zOEZofYt)

Within the last few days, two important state actions have dealt a big blow to automated license plate reader (ALPR) networks. This is just the latest proof of the growing tide of public opposition to mass surveillance. After [years of successful](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/12/procurement-power-when-cities-realized-they-can-just-say-no-2025-review) [grassroots battles](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/cities-terminate-flock-contracts-at-record-pace-in-august/) to pull these cameras from local streets, [bipartisan momentum](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/us/snplus/politics/2026/08/31/flock-safety-cameras-bipartisan-scrutiny-misuse) is sweeping the country.

On August 28, Texas Governor Greg Abbott [banned state agencies from spending public funds on Flock cameras](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/28/texas-greg-abbott-flock-cameras-order-state-money/). The order dropped just as *The Texas Tribune* prepared to [publish an investigation](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/28/texas-flock-cameras-auto-insurance-fee-mvcpa-grants/) revealing that a state agency had quietly funneled at least $30 million into building a sprawling surveillance network.

Then on August 31, the Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) [issued a memo](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/design/bulletins/eom26-01.pdf), [announced](https://www.wctv.tv/2026/08/31/gov-desantis-set-remove-flock-cameras-state-roadways/) by Governor Ron DeSantis, ordering the removal of all ALPRs from the right-of-way on state highways within 30 days. The order revokes all previously approved permits to install ALPRs, and bars transportation officials from issuing future permits.
