DEEPLINKS BLOG

By [Betty Gedlu](/about/staff/betelhembetty-zewge-gedlu) and [Joe Mullin](/about/staff/joe-mullin)

November 9, 2026

# Why Fewer People Are Challenging Bad Patents

![A warning sign with a picture of a troll says "do not feed the troll"](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/patent-troll-warning_0.png?itok=02sf1aKL "Warning: Patent Troll")

The number of petitions being filed at the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB) has collapsed. That’s not reason for celebration. The PTAB is an important line of defense against low-quality software patents being used in lawsuits against people and companies.

The drop didn’t happen because suddenly patents have become higher quality. It’s simply because USPTO leadership is increasingly refusing to consider good challenges to patents, for reasons unrelated to the merits. The process has become so unreliable that many would-be patent challengers aren’t even trying.

Inter partes review (IPR) is a process that allows people to challenge patents at the Patent Office, rather than having to fight an expensive lawsuit in federal court. It’s one of the most important ways to get bad patents out of the system.

In the third quarter of 2026, July through September, only [97 IPR petitions](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/trial_stats_september2026.pdf) were filed at the PTAB, compared to [327 petitions](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Trial_StatsFY25_Q4.pdf) during the same period in 2025. Similarly, only [37 IPR petitions](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/trial_stats_september2026.pdf) were filed in September 2026, compared to [129 petitions](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Trial_StatsFY25_Q4.pdf) in December 2024—the last full month before the current USPTO leadership began reshaping how the Board handles patent challenges.

This dramatic decline shouldn’t just concern patent lawyers. EFF has fought bad patents because so many software and internet patents make claims to the basic tools of free expression online, like sending email, using online ads, or publishing web pages. When bad patents go unchallenged, they affect everyone who cares about the freedom to write new code and make new technology.

That’s why EFF has defended IPR since it came on the scene more than a decade ago. It provides a faster and less expensive way to challenge patents that should never have been granted. Congress created IPRs because the Patent Office makes mistakes. District court litigation is an extraordinarily expensive way to correct them. Without a meaningful administrative review process, bad patents remain powerful weapons in the hands of patent trolls and others seeking to extract settlements from companies that often did nothing wrong.

Now, USPTO leadership is dismantling that safeguard.
