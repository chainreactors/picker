## 'Online platforms can rely on our app,' says Commish, 'there are no more excuses'

![icon](/design_picker/d518b499f8a6e2c65d4d8c49aca8299d54b03012/graphics/icon/vulture_red.svg)
[Lindsay Clark](/Author/Lindsay-Clark "Read more by this author")

Wed 29 Apr 2026  //
12:03 UTC

![](/design_picker/d2e337b97204af4aa34dda04c4e5d56d954b216f/graphics/icons/social_share_icon.svg)

The European Commission has recommended EU member states adopt an age verification app designed to protect children from harmful online content.

In an announcement, the EU’s executive branch said it had approved the roll-out the technology, which member states can deploy as a standalone app or integrate into a European Digital Identity Wallet - a system they are due to develop to agreed specifications by year's end.

![kids on social media](https://regmedia.co.uk/2026/01/20/shutterstock_2333643265.jpg?x=174&amp;y=115&amp;crop=1)

The recommendation builds on the regulatory groundwork laid by the Digital Markets Act (DMA) and the Digital Services Act (DSA), both [passed in 2022](https://www.theregister.com/2022/07/06/eu_dsa_dma_laws_big_tech/) to boost the bloc's power in tackling the dominance of big tech companies in citizens lives. The DSA requires online platforms to ensure high level of privacy, security and safety for minors.

The EC's app is designed to let users prove they comply with age requirements without revealing their actual age, identity or any other personal details. In April, it announced the app was ready to deploy, and has now formally recommended member states adopt it.
