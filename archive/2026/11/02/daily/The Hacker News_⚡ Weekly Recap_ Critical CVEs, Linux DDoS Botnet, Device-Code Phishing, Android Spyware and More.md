Some attacks this week didn't need much of a trick. A fake support call, a trusted extension, a shared link, or a device with no password. Small things that people barely think about. Yet each was enough to cause serious trouble.

Then there were the bigger messes. Billions stolen, hidden networks of hacked devices, poisoned software, and attackers finding new ways to turn normal tools against their users. Even the places meant to keep money and systems safe ended up with problems of their own. The strange part is how often the damage started somewhere nobody was looking.

And that's before getting to the long list of fresh bugs, shady services, and scams making the rounds. Quite a bit to sort through. Here's what made the list.

## **⚡ Threat of the Week**

**[N. Korea Sets New Record with Binance $6.43B Crypto Theft](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/fbi-links-north-koreas-tradertraitor-to.html)** — The U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) has officially attributed the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance to a North Korean hacking group called TraderTraitor. The haul eclipses the $1.46 billion plundered from Bybit in February 2025, making it the largest cryptocurrency theft in history. The funds were stolen across 11 blockchains, 82% of which were on Ethereum and Bitcoin. Of the $6.43 billion stolen by TraderTraitor, $2.85 billion of the assets have been frozen on-chain by Tether, Circle, Ripple, Arbitrum, and Optimism.

## **🔔 Top News**

* **[Volt Typhoon Uses Covert Silent Train Network in 0-Day Attacks](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/volt-typhoon-used-covert-silent-train.html)** — Chinese state-sponsored hacking group Volt Typhoon has been using a framework that relies on rotating chains of connected devices compromised on demand, including to facilitate zero-day attacks targeting high-value systems. It's codenamed [Silent Train](https://www.zetter-zeroday.com/silent-train-volt-typhoons-sophisticated-infection-chain-into-us-targets-exposed/) because the threat actor uses it against select targets, cleans it up after each operation to minimize detection and attribution, and refreshes it continuously. Silent Train is a relay infrastructure formed of compromised devices to hide the origin of its attacks. The findings highlight evidence of efforts to pre-position access within what it described as "strategic communications, critical infrastructure and other ecosystems that become essential during geopolitical tensions or crises." Silent Train is built around "rapidly rotating chains of compromised SOHO, IoT and enterprise devices." The nature of these victims "indicates deliberate efforts to maintain access to critical ecosystems that support defense, logistics, communications, military and civilian operations, especially during heightened tensions or crises in the Indo-Pacific region."
* **[Polymarket Briefly Suspended Trading After Third-Party Hack](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-steal-about-27-million-from.html)** — Prediction market platform Polymarket temporarily [suspended](https://status.polymarket.com/cmvtuswgi00221bkzps7w21pt) trading after hackers compromised DebugBear, a third-party service, to distribute cryptocurrency-draining JavaScript that siphoned approximately $2.7 million in assets from 33 deposit wallets in under an hour. "Our DNS server records were compromised through our domain registrar. We are currently unable to receive emails sent to our domain," DebugBear [said](https://x.com/DebugBear/status/2116306975403250070) in an update shared on October 31, 2026. "Based on our initial investigation this attack targeted the website of a specific customer. We have not seen any indication of a compromise of our internal systems, though we will continue to investigate this." In June, Polymarket [disclosed](https://x.com/polymarkettrade/status/2070155882906730671) another third-party compromise that injected a malicious script into its frontend, leading to reported losses of around $3 million.
* **[Shared ChatGPT Links Lead to Code Execution](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/a-shared-chatgpt-link-could-run.html)** — Researchers from Parameter Labs disclosed a now-patched flaw in ChatGPT that turned an ordinary share link into an attacker-controlled JavaScript running inside the signed-in session of whoever opened it. "An attacker could create a malicious conversation, publish it using ChatGPT's normal sharing feature, and send the link to another user," Parameter Labs said. "When the victim opened the share, the payload executed as chatgpt.com with access to the victim's authenticated web session." Successful exploitation could allow an attacker to enumerate the victim's conversation list, retrieve content from a private conversation, and send data from that conversation to an external server.
