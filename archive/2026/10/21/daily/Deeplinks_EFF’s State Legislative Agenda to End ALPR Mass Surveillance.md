# EFF’s State Legislative Agenda to End ALPR Mass Surveillance

![A flock camera with spying eye, silhouetted against a dark purple ground.](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/flock-vector-1b.png?itok=k4eE3A6M)

Police agencies across the U.S. have deployed Automated License Plate Readers (ALPRs) to capture images of passing vehicles along with time, date, and GPS coordinates. While the technology itself isn’t new (ALPRs have existed in various forms for [more than 50 years](https://www.anpr-international.com/history-of-anpr/), and in [the U.S. since 1998](https://sam.gov/workspace/contract/opp/c623f29a9eb8599abec842570558e453/view)), the surveillance landscape has shifted dramatically. Rapid advances in artificial intelligence and national data sharing have transformed police ALPRs into an interconnected, nationwide tracking network. It is pitched to city councils by law enforcement and private vendors like Flock Safety, Motorola Solutions (Vigilant), and Axon as an essential tool for solving violent crime.

In reality, it is an inherently dangerous infrastructure of mass surveillance.

Since [2012](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2013/05/alpr), EFF has worked with others to expose the severe digital rights harms of ALPR systems. For example, we’ve recently documented how these camera networks allow law enforcement to [search for immigrants](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/are-your-local-police-using-flock-safety-alprs-scan-immigrants), [track peaceful protesters](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/11/how-cops-are-using-flock-safetys-alpr-network-surveil-protesters-and-activists), target [Romani communities](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/11/license-plate-surveillance-logs-reveal-racist-policing-against-romani-people), and [track women seeking reproductive healthcare](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/10/flock-safety-and-texas-sheriff-claimed-license-plate-search-was-missing-person-it). Police also routinely use ALPRs for low-level matters like enforcing [school residency requirements or investigating noise complaints](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/05/more-license-plate-reader-mission-creep-school-residency-verification-background). Many officers also use ALPR databases to [stalk past romantic partners](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/08/02/how-police-officers-used-vast-network-cameras-spy-their-exes/). [Recent EFF analysis](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/high-crime-lmao-how-cops-are-treating-mass-surveillance-joke) revealed officers using the system without legitimate justification—logging search reasons like "LOL" and "LMAO."

Understandably, the public backlash to police ALPR technology is accelerating, and state lawmakers across the country are taking action.
