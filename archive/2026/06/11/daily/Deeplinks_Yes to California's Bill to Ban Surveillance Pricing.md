Corporations harvest and monetize ever-growing amounts of our personal data, such as our [browsing history](https://www.eff.org/document/behind-one-way-mirror-deep-dive-technology-corporate-surveillance) and [physical](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/01/online-behavioral-ads-fuel-surveillance-industry-heres-how) [location](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/08/inside-fog-data-science-secretive-company-selling-mass-surveillance-local-police). One bitter fruit of this poisonous tree is known as “surveillance pricing”: corporations offer the same product to two different people at two different prices, based on scrutiny of these people’s respective personal data.

Surveillance pricing is bad for privacy, equity, and price transparency. So EFF [supports](https://www.eff.org/document/20260526-eff-letter-supporting-cal-ab-2564) a California bill, [S.B. 2564](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB2564), which would ban this creepy practice.
