## The base layer [​](#the-base-layer)

Pretty much everything that we care about as humans goes back to evolution. Basically, we are the mech suits for evolution. Specifically, for genes.

Evolution has a master game: to produce more and more competent organisms that can propagate the most competent genes, and it works by injecting these organisms with goals, using biological systems such as hormones and emotions to steer us.

We have anger. We have jealousy. We have ambition. We have all these different things, and other animals have different versions of those. There's a spectrum there.

The central concept is that we only want things because evolution gave those wants to us. Everything about human nature is based on this. We enjoy things and dislike things and are steered toward and away from things because evolution built it that way.

Our entire incentive system, our goal system, our preferences, and our likes. We can't choose which foods we like. We can't choose if we like blondes or brunettes. We just want things. We just prefer things.

This is in the substrate of biology in our system, and more importantly, evolution, which has all the incentives and disincentives and punishments.

So that's the base here.

## The LitRPG system [​](#the-litrpg-system)

The next piece is LitRPG.

LitRPG is a genre of literature in which you have characters who are injected into a gaming system. The gaming system has parameters: clothing, armor, weapons, magic, psionics, and different components of capabilities.

Over the course of a LitRPG series, which tends to be very long, you see constant escalations.

I'm going to use one particular series, [*The Primal Hunter*](https://aethonbooks.com/book/the-primal-hunter/), as my favorite example. I'm on book 12 or 13 or something. The central character is this guy named Jake, who used to have some random job. He wakes up in a system version of Earth, which is completely controlled by this air-quote “system.”

[![The Primal Hunter, Book 1 by Zogarth](/images/primal-hunter-book-cover.jpg)](https://aethonbooks.com/book/the-primal-hunter/)The Primal Hunter, Book 1 by Zogarth

The system is kind of like evolution. It determines all the rules. Clothing, armor, damage, magic, capabilities, escalation paths. Everything.

As you proceed with this character, he starts figuring out:

* I need shelter.
* I need armor.
* I need basic defenses.
* I need to learn how to fight.

He has to choose a class. He has to choose a race. He decides to be human, but he could have chosen lots of different races. He could have chosen something other than hunter, but he chose to be a hunter. Then he gets to choose all these other sub-paths.

It's a really cool system because each time he chooses something, it opens up completely custom things. Characters are vastly different based on the choices they've made.

And then there's [the narrator](https://www.travisbaldree.com/audiobooks).

The narrator also narrated [*Cradle*](https://www.willwight.com/cradle.html), the first series I got into. In *The Primal Hunter*, he says, “Ding!” He says it at the same tone and pitch every time.

[![Unsouled, Cradle Book 1 by Will Wight](/images/unsouled-book-cover.png)](https://www.willwight.com/cradle.html)Unsouled, Cradle Book 1 by Will Wight

Even my partner, who's in the car with me when I'm listening, has already Pavlov-associated this with a good thing, because he says it at the same tone and pitch every time.

Human race advanced to level 274. Class advanced to level 274. Or a level in alchemy, which is the profession he chose. Alchemy has thousands of variants, and every choice he makes produces new variants.

The entire LitRPG system is a perfect analog to evolution itself.

## Ding! [​](#ding)

Look at what we do as humans. We're constantly optimizing for all these different things. The only question is whether we're aware of it.

We are the mech suit. Evolution is on the inside, pressing pedals and pulling levers. It's determining what ultimately matters. It's deciding what we're going to enjoy and not enjoy.

And the way it incentivizes us to advance is through these dings.

When the narrator in a LitRPG says “Ding,” that's the same thing as evolution squirting a reward into our system. Suddenly, we feel the spurt.

We train in karate or volleyball or whatever it is. We fight and grind. We struggle. We try to push through hardship.

When you choose something difficult and push through and achieve something, maybe you got third place in the karate competition. Maybe you got first place. Maybe you got into the Olympics. Maybe you didn't get that far.

You're getting a ding.

That ding in LitRPG books is the same ding we get from evolution.

Another thing that's crazy about it is that evolution is fucking harsh. It is super fucking mean.

How long do you get to enjoy your Olympic trophy? How long do you get to enjoy that new Hermès bag, that new Rolex, or that new Porsche? Whatever it is that you like.

You spend all this time grinding and trying to make more money and trying to meet the right mates, all for the purpose of getting to the next thing.

As soon as you get there, evolution devalues that thing in your mind. You look at it on the shelf and you're like, “Nah. Not really a big deal.” It's in your past. It gets marked as uninteresting.

You can collect trophies, and the only trophy you care about is the one you don't have.

The hedonic treadmill is exactly the mechanism I'm talking about. It's one of the features of evolution that keeps us wanting more.

This is fucking profound. And it's the exact same game.

I learned this from *Dragon Ball Z* retroactively, once I started getting into LitRPG. It was all about escalation. Levels, sub-levels, branching, comparison, competition.

This is all the language of evolution.

The guy next door was like a god in my mind. He was older than me. There's a thing called “going up against,” and he went up against someone in the local area, just a few streets over.

This other guy destroyed my mentor.

I was like, “Oh my god.” That's the first time I realized there's levels to this game.

Again, deeply evolutionary psychology language. Evolutionary biology language.
