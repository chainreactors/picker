## Executive assessment

The updated collection identifies ***two distinct threat streams***: Iranian public rhetoric about dirty bombs and chemical weapons that predates November 16, and subsequent U.S. intelligence reporting alleging Iranian contingency planning against senior Trump administration officials in Washington, D.C.

The most consequential new development is an October 23 U.S. intelligence memorandum, followed by November 18 congressional testimony, describing substantially increased Iranian lethal plotting against Americans.

Three findings stand out:

* **Pre-publication rhetoric:** Hamzeh Safavi reportedly discussed dirty bombs and chemical weapons in August and October interviews. Separately, a June statement attributed to Iranian economist Mohammad Reza Yazdani threatened Washington. These statements require examination in their original Persian-language context.
* **Official intelligence warning:** An October 23 memorandum described Iranian lethal plotting against Americans as having reached advanced stages in the Middle East and certain other countries. It did not publicly identify a dirty-bomb plot in Washington.
* **No public operational corroboration:** The available material does not establish a deployed radiological device, chemical weapon, or imminent attack against President Trump or another named official.

The distinction between ideological signaling, reported intelligence, and verified operational preparation remains central to this assessment.

## New developments since November 16

October 23, 2026 | Declassified memorandum

**U.S. intelligence reports advanced Iranian lethal plotting**

A memorandum published by Iran Watch states that intelligence indicates Iranian efforts to target Americans have increased substantially and that plots against U.S. citizens have reached advanced stages in the Middle East and certain other countries.

The document does not publicly substantiate a Washington radiological or chemical attack scenario.

November 17, 2026 | Presidential response

**Trump acknowledges investigation of reported threat**

President Trump stated that the administration was examining the alleged dirty-bomb threat and taking it seriously. The White House simultaneously stated that it had no credible threats to the homeland at that time.

Iran’s UN mission and Foreign Ministry rejected the allegations.

November 18, 2026 | Congressional testimony

**Intelligence director reports increased Iranian threats**

Reuters reports that Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told the House Homeland Security Committee that threats from Iran against Americans had increased substantially.

When questioned about the alleged Washington dirty-bomb plot, he declined to discuss specifics. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said he did not believe there was an imminent threat to Washington.

## Pre-November 16 collection: New corroboration

The updated search produced a stronger historical lead than the earlier Telegram aggregator results.

June 1, 2026 | Original interview

**Mohammad-Reza Yazdizadeh: Dirty-bomb discussion and Washington threat**

Iranian economist Mohammad-Reza Yazdizadeh appeared on the JomhouriStreet YouTube channel and discussed manufacturing radiological weapons from Iran’s uranium stockpile.

According to a June 3 MEMRI translation, Yazdizadeh also claimed Iran could retaliate against Washington if subjected to an American nuclear attack.

This is particularly relevant because it predates CNN’s November 16 reporting by more than five months and explicitly identifies Washington as a potential target.

However, it is **conditional retaliation rhetoric**, not evidence of an approved attack plan. The Washington threat also should not be interpreted as a verified plan to use a dirty bomb specifically against the capital.

**Hamzeh Safavi: Dirty bombs, chemical weapons, and regime survival**

A November 18 OSINT account describes Safavi’s remarks as originating in interviews conducted during August and October, rather than a single November 2 statement. Safavi reportedly characterized dirty bombs and chemical weapons as possible alternatives to nuclear weapons, linked their potential use to the survival of Iran’s government, and questioned whether Iran had disclosed its full enriched-uranium inventory.

***The quoted target was Israel. No direct U.S. operational target was identified in these statements.***
