# Amazon Ring’s New Indoor Surveillance Drone Is Not Compatible with Privacy

![The shadow of a police officer looms in front of a Ring device on a closed door.](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/ring_banner.png?itok=V8OoFjwm "Amazon Ring + Law Enforcement")

Amazon Ring has finally announced their [long-dreaded indoor drone](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/09/24/amazon-ring-flying-security-camera-drone-announced.html), a small [flying surveillance device connected to their security suite of products](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/amazons-ring-debuts-security-robots-drone-rcna608538) that can fly around your house filming and recording things. The device is supposedly designed to fly preset paths across your home and will also activate if a sensor or alarm has been triggered.

Customers should think twice about purchasing these devices for a number of reasons. These devices are problematic in many of the ways [we’ve said Ring’s stationary home surveillance products are a problem](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/07/amazon-ring-cashes-techno-authoritarianism-and-mass-surveillance), plus some new ways. At a very basic level, they are essentially a window into your house that can be accessed by anyone with access to your account—which has historically included [bad actors looking to embarrass or harass Ring users](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/05/rings-privacy-failures-led-spying-harassment-through-home-security-cameras) and [employees of the company](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-51048406).

What’s worse, Ring has [supercharged](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/ring-cameras-go-mobile-as-amazon-debuts-ai-powered-security-drone-and-robot/) the units with its ‘AI Pilot’ to map routes, model the interior of the home, and control movement. This means not only is the footage being sent to corporate servers, but the company is also making inferences about your home to carry out tasks. How much home usage data this is based on, and if it can be used to infer when certain household activities are happening, are both open questions. And presumably, to maintain the preset routes, inferred data about your home will still be held by Ring even after any stored video footage is deleted.
