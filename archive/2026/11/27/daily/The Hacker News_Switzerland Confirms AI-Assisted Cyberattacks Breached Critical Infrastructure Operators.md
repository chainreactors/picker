# [Switzerland Confirms AI-Assisted Cyberattacks Breached Critical Infrastructure Operators](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/switzerland-confirms-ai-assisted.html)

**Swati Khandelwal**Nov 27, 2026Artificial Intelligence / Cyber Attacks

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjjviiPK0v6Kpjxjo_jDNE9vaZ8-JKiq6Oct4uU6qPDfTXq66QnxuV2iExRo2Rx2FyHhyphenhyphenDquMIoxmlfo5GwxfKoEXRG8Pj38FDOzEKitDdhXBNOGN7b6sddsMozMuhI44c-3mimoE4OxU-Xg7h8IzhDiyrXmP6qBO-wW6xmitkIEv_EkU-FJUJBTNtQ8uY/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/swiss.jpg)

Attackers using AI agents have broken into computer systems at Swiss organizations in four of five confirmed attacks, the first such cases in the country, the country's cybersecurity agency said yesterday.

Three of the attacks targeted critical infrastructure operators, and two of those succeeded, with no major disruptions reported. The victims have not been named, and it is not known who is behind the attacks.

**Florian Schütz**, who directs Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre, gave the figures in an interview with public broadcaster SRF.

All five cases were reported in recent days. Small and medium-sized companies were among the organizations hit, and they came forward voluntarily.

The agency's first notice, a [security advisory](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/26-ki-agenten-1-en) published on Tuesday, said the agents had compromised the victims' IT systems in every case known at that point. Neither the notice nor yesterday's [broadcast interview](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/fuenf-bestaetigte-faelle-erstmals-cyberangriffe-mit-ki-agenten-in-der-schweiz) explains why the count changed.

"These cases represent a shift in the cyber threat landscape," the advisory said.

The targeted operators are in at least two sectors. Electricity, drinking water, and telecommunications suppliers are examples of what Switzerland counts as critical infrastructure, according to a [news agency report](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-ai/switzerland-reports-first-ai-based-cyberattacks-on-critical-infrastructure/92440400) on the interview.

The attacks involve what the Swiss agency calls agentic offensive frameworks, AI agents that find security weaknesses and carry out attacks on their own. Its advisory named ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI as examples of agents reachable over the internet, not as the tools used in the five cases.

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/threat-protection-d)

The agents are often used to scan for known, unpatched weaknesses and can run many attacks in parallel, the agency says. When defenses respond, the agents switch methods, leaving companies significantly less time to detect an intrusion and respond.

The number of AI attack tools freely reachable online has nearly doubled since October, according to the agency. The figure counts available tools, not attacks or victims.

**Raphael Reischuk**, co-founder of Switzerland's National Test Institute for Cybersecurity, expects a larger wave of attacks and says companies underestimate the risk.

Italy's national computer security incident response team, CSIRT Italia, described mass compromise campaigns in an [official bulletin](https://csirt.regione.toscana.it/impiego-di-agenti-di-ia-in-campagne-di-compromissione-automatizzate-bl01-261123-csirt-ita/) this week. In the cases it observed firsthand, AI-directed autonomous agents broke into a large number of systems within a few hours.

The agents adapt their actions within seconds, run in parallel without human input, and chain vulnerabilities, misconfigurations, and compromised credentials together, the bulletin says. It names [ARTEX](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/chinese-hackers-use-ai-powered-artex-to.html), [CyberStrikeAI](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/cyberstrikeai-uses-44-ai-agents-to.html), [PentAGI](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/google-finds-hackers-using-ai-agents-to.html), and [DeepSeek Harness](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/deepseek-harness-flaw-let-ai-agents.html), an open-source agent whose Red Team Mode uses a built-in prompt to get past the underlying model's safety limits.

Each action on its own looks like normal user or API traffic. Detection depends on context: where requests come from, how often they arrive, how much data moves, and the order in which things happen.

Britain's National Cyber Security Centre reported the same pattern in [agency guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blogs/familiar-weaknesses-exploited-faster-defending-against-agentic-cyber-attacks) this week. "Since September 2026, the NCSC has seen a marked rise in AI-assisted cyber attacks," it said.

Some incidents caused downtime on internet-facing services, and others gave attackers a foothold for further agent-driven activity. The weaknesses are old ones: credentials committed to code repositories, misconfigured infrastructure-as-code, default or missing authentication, and weaknesses in APIs.

What has changed is the speed, persistence, and low cost of the attacks, the British agency says. The frameworks are also noisy, which makes them easier to detect.

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/network-defense-dd)

The European Commission has already been hit. Attackers using ARTEX broke into its human resources, medical reimbursement, and translation systems, the Commission has confirmed, in an [earlier intrusion](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/ai-assisted-cyberattacks-trigger.html) first detected on October 22.

### What Defenders Should Do

The strongest common advice from the three agencies is to shrink the time attackers have. Their recommendations overlap:

* Install critical security updates on internet-facing systems within 24 hours of release (Switzerland), and patch flaws already being exploited first (Italy).
* Disconnect or isolate internet-facing systems that cannot be patched quickly (Italy).
