The Clop ransomware group has been active since February 2019. It exploits flaws in file-transfer and enterprise software to steal data from many organisations at once, then demands payment to keep it unpublished. The [Ransomtracker](https://ransomnews.com/ransomtracker/group/clop/) holds 2,339 Clop leak-site listings and 133 attacks confirmed against public sources. The group’s latest campaign hit PTC Windchill in mid-2026, and its most recent listing is dated 21 October 2026.

### In brief

* The Ransomtracker index holds 2,339 Clop leak-site listings dated from 9 September 2021, 247 of them logged in 2026.
* The Ransomnews confirmed-attacks dataset holds 133 Clop incidents tied to a public source, 46 of them dated 2025.
* Clop named 43 organisations on 12 and 13 August 2026 in a campaign that Ransom-ISAC and ReliaQuest tie to [CVE-2026-12569](https://www.ptc.com/en/about/trust-center/advisory-center/active-advisories/windchill-flexplm-rce-vulnerability), a flaw in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM.
* [ShinyHunters](https://ransomnews.com/ransomtracker/group/shinyhunters/) defaced Clop’s leak site on 18 September 2026, and Clop moved to a new server within a week.
* Ukrainian police arrested six suspects tied to Clop in June 2021, and we found no arrest announced since.

## Who is behind Clop ransomware?

Clop is a ransomware strain first seen in February 2019 and the name of the criminal group that deploys it and runs a leak site under the same name. A joint FBI and CISA advisory, [AA23-158A](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa23-158a), says the malware evolved from the CryptoMix family and was offered as [ransomware-as-a-service](https://ransomnews.com/what-is-raas-ransomware-as-a-service-2026/). It treats the gang as the same actor as TA505, which it describes as one of the largest phishing and malspam distributors worldwide, and it estimates that TA505 has compromised more than 3,000 organisations in the United States and 8,000 globally.
