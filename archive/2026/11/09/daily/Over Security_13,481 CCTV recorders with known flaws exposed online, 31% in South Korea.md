Mysterium VPN has counted 13,481 CCTV recorders that are reachable from the open internet and running firmware with publicly known flaws, spread across at least 39 countries. The research, published on 9 November 2026, places 31% of them in South Korea alone and finds that nine of the ten networks hosting the most devices are consumer broadband telecoms.

The same passive sweep found 1,207 QNAP storage boxes running firmware affected by a critical 2024 login flaw, 8,078 smart-home message brokers that accept any connection without a password, and 3,192 Windows Remote Desktop servers with Network Level Authentication switched off. No device was contacted. The researchers worked from the aggregate counts of an IoT search engine, and 58% of the recorders had been visible in it for more than six months. The full dataset and method are in [Mysterium VPN’s research on vulnerable CCTV recorders](https://www.mysteriumvpn.com/blog/data-and-research/camera-in-the-corner), which follows the company’s June 2026 study of 21,786 camera feeds viewable with no login at all.

## Which flaws are the exposed recorders running?

Two firmware families account for every recorder in the count, and both flaws have been public for years. The larger group, 12,655 devices, runs firmware built for HiSilicon-based hardware that contains a local file inclusion flaw, the class of bug that lets an attacker read arbitrary files from a device and, often, go on to run code on it. Problems in this firmware family were widely documented in 2020, including a hidden remote-access mechanism; the study notes that Huawei, whose HiSilicon unit designs the chips, attributed that mechanism to the device manufacturers rather than to its own software.
