# The Digest Method: How I Follow a Whole Motorsport Season in One Curated Evening

I do not follow motorsport by scrolling. I follow it by digest.

Every Sunday night I sit down with one page of notes: what happened, what matters, what to watch next. No endless feed, no 40 tabs, no spoiler from a group chat at 7am. Just a digest. Five items. One evening. Done.

That is why I like the name Automotive Digest. A digest is not a warehouse. It is a choice. And motorsport, more than any other sport, needs a choice.

## Why Motorsport Needs a Digest, Not a Feed

> “No other sport is sliced up quite like motorsport.”

That line is on the homepage of Automotive Digest, and it is the reason a digest works. One weekend can be Formula 1 practice on Friday, a MotoGP sprint on Saturday, a World Superbike double header on Sunday morning, and NASCAR in the evening.

If you try to watch it all as a feed, you drown. If you curate it as a digest, you enjoy it.

The site says it even more bluntly about channel lists:

> “Forty thousand channels in alphabetical order is a warehouse, not a service. Pick your series and the first login arrives already arranged by weekend.”

That is the digest idea. Not more channels. Better shelving.

On the homepage, it is a motorsport viewing service for the 2027 season: Formula 1, MotoGP, rally, endurance and American racing in one list, with a 24-month pass listed at 4.60 dollars per month, 110.40 paid once, against a standard 14.99 monthly rate. It offers a free 24-hour test with no card, two screens at once, and genuine 4K on selected races.

On the About page, it is something else: a personal blog of varied interests, with Emily Carter as content strategist, focused on engaging content and community growth. The page describes it as a place to explore stories that inspire and inform, not just a shop.

I like that split. A digest needs both: a shop that sells access, and an editor who tells you what is worth your evening. Without the editor, you have 40,000 channels and no idea where to start. Without the access, you have great taste and no race to watch.

The homepage also claims a longer history, saying:

> “Because AutomotiveDigest spent two decades as an automotive community, and the question members kept asking in the end was where to watch the race.”

Whether you believe the full timeline or not, the question is real. I ask it every week.

## My Weekly Digest Template: 5 Items Only

Here is the exact template I use. I copy it into my notes every Monday.

**1. The One Race That Mattered.** One sentence. Who won, why it mattered, what changed in the championship. Not the full results. Just the story.

**2. The One Session to Rewatch.** Sometimes it is qualifying. Sometimes it is a MotoGP sprint. Sometimes it is a single rally stage. I watch this one properly, on the big screen, with sound on.

**3. The One Thing I Missed.** A support race, a junior category, a night stint at Le Mans. I watch the highlights, 10 minutes, no guilt.

**4. The One Thing to Watch Next.** Next weekend, one anchor event. I put it in my calendar in my local time.

**5. The One Photo or Clip to Save.** A pit stop, a save, a sunset at a rally stage. This is my visual diary. It makes the season feel like a magazine, not a spreadsheet.

Five items. That is it. If a race does not make the digest, I let it go.

## What Goes Into This Week Digest, By Series

This is how I fill the template, series by series.

**Formula 1.** My anchor. I want the full weekend story, not just the race. In the United States, Formula 1 in 2026 is on Apple TV as the exclusive home, with every session live. In the United Kingdom, Sky Sports has exclusive live rights. In many other markets, F1 TV Pro is the main option, with onboards and team radio.

For my digest, I pick one session to rewatch. Usually qualifying, because it is the purest 18 minutes of the weekend. My planning page for this is [Formula 1 guide for the full weekend](https://www.automotivedigest.com/formula-1/)

**MotoGP and World Superbike.** I put these together in my digest because they are both bike stories, but very different ones. MotoGP is about the sprint and the lean. World Superbike is about close contact and two races on Sunday. MotoGP VideoPass carries every MotoGP, Moto2, Moto3, and MotoE session live. WorldSBK VideoPass does the same for Superbikes.

My one clip to save is almost always from here. A save in MotoGP is art.

**Rally.** Rally is my slow TV item. I do not try to watch every stage live. I watch one stage day, with timing on my phone, then I read the splits. Rally.TV is the official platform for the World Rally Championship and European Rally Championship, with all events live and on demand.

The guide that helped me stop being confused by rally is [rally coverage that actually makes sense](https://www.automotivedigest.com/rally/)

**Endurance.** This is my long form item. I do not watch 24 hours. I watch the start, the night, and the finish. FIAWEC+ is the official home for WEC and Le Mans. My direct page for planning the night is:

https://www.automotivedigest.com/endurance

The homepage describes the season like this:

> “A season is not four races. It is practice at nine in the morning, qualifying at three, a sprint nobody expected, and a rally stage running while you make dinner.”

Endurance is the proof of that. The best moments are not always the finish. They are the 3am class battles you almost slept through.

**US Racing.** NASCAR and IndyCar are my late night bonus. NASCAR in the US is now split across FOX, Prime Video, NBC, and CW. IndyCar is on FOX in the US and on INDYCAR LIVE internationally. For my digest, I watch one of them live and one on catch-up. No guilt.

**BTCC, Goodwood, and Car Shows.** These are my light items. BTCC on ITV4 in the UK is around eight hours live per race day, perfect background for a Sunday. Goodwood on YouTube is my family pick, beautiful cars, no pressure.

## How I Test a Service Before It Goes Into My Digest

A digest is only as good as its sources. So I test everything before I recommend it, even to myself.

My test is the one the site repeats:

> “Check it on a race, not a trailer”

I load the feed five minutes before a session start. I watch the first ten minutes. I try a channel change. I try two screens. If it holds at lights out, it goes into my digest. If it dies, it does not.

I also check the basics the site lists: 15 Mbps for comfortable FHD, 25 Mbps for 4K, wired where possible. Jitter hurts more than headline speed, especially at night during endurance.

And I check the guide. Does it list sessions in my local time? Is it accurate for next weekend? A digest with a wrong calendar is worse than no digest.

## How to Build Your Own Automotive Digest

If you want to try this, start small. Do not build a perfect system. Build a one page digest for this week.

* On Monday, write down the one race that mattered last weekend, in one sentence.
* Pick one session to rewatch this week, 20 minutes maximum.
* Pick one anchor race for next weekend, and put it in your calendar.
* Save one photo or clip. Just one.
* Delete everything else.

After four weeks, you will have a month of motorsport in four pages. You will remember the season. You will not remember the scrolling.

A feed makes you anxious. A digest makes you curious.

A feed says: you missed everything. A digest says: here is what mattered, here is what to watch next, and here is one beautiful moment to keep.
