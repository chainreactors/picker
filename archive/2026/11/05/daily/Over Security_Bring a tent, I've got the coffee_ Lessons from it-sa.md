# Bring a tent, I've got the coffee: Lessons from it-sa

By
[Thorsten Rosendahl](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/author/thorsten/)

Thursday, November 5, 2026 14:00

[Threat Source newsletter](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/category/threat-source-newsletter/)

Welcome to this week's edition of the Threat Source newsletter.

I'm just back from an interesting and demanding week of booth duty at [it-sa Expo&Congress](https://www.itsa365.de/en/it-sa-expo-congress) in Nuremberg, where Talos had a Point of Demonstration at the Cisco booth.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/11/Untitled-2.png)

I personally see it-sa as the German or European counterpart to the RSA Conference in San Francisco, with a slightly different center of gravity in at least two dimensions.

First, the expo is the main event, not the congress. The congress is organized by associations, industry groups, and exhibitors, and plays more of a supporting role. Second, the focus is more local and European. Major topics were (of course) AI for defense tooling, securing AI systems themselves, and post-quantum cryptography. And here comes the local touch (in my opinion): digital sovereignty and regulatory compliance, with NIS2, CRA, and DORA.

But local doesn't translate to small, or to the city of Nuremberg. With more than 1,000 exhibitors and 33,000 visitors from 70 countries over three days of exhibition, preceded by a congress day, it was huge. Even as a trade show veteran, I was overwhelmed.

My conversations at the booth had a different focus, though. It was a lot of chatter about vulnerabilities like [php2shell](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/two-critical-rces-in-php-core/) or [broccoli2shell](https://broccoli2shell.com/) and state-sponsored actors, but again with a local touch: education.

I was very surprised how many teenagers stopped by to ask for advice. Here in Germany, you mainly have two paths for your career. One is the university route, and the other is vocational (apprenticeship). Across all industries the split is roughly [50](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2025/11/PD25_426_21.html)/[50](https://www.bibb.de/de/1563.php), while in IT it is more like [80%](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-09/bitkom-studienbericht-it-fachkraefte-2025.pdf) university, [20%](https://www.bibb.de/dokumente/pdf/naa309/naa309_2025_tab067_0bund.pdf) apprenticeship. The common minimum age to start university is 18, while it is 16 for apprenticeships. Reality is way more complex, but the details don’t matter for the point I want to make.

For university opportunities in cyber, I count close to 30 [bachelor programs](https://www.studieren-studium.com/studium/cyber-security). For apprenticeships, I count ZERO. There is the "IT Specialist" (Fachinformatiker), but it only has specializations like application development, system integration, digital networking, or data and process analysis. That is why an [initiative](https://www.eurobits.de/wp-content/uploads/Eurobits_Ausbildungsberuf_ITS_2025_Screen.pdf) exists to get an "IT Security" specialization going.

Even the degree route is thin. There are no official statistics, but my guess is 800 to 900 graduates a year, compared to a demand of more than 100,000. We need a second route into the field for the 16-year-old who finished various CTFs or Hack the Box challenges and wants to join the good side now. Honestly speaking, I would rather have them on our side of the fence at 16 than wonder at 26 where they ended up.

How does it look in your country?

On a personal note: If you consider visiting it-sa in 2027, book your accommodation early. The hotels are gone fast. That is the one downside, because Nuremberg isn't exactly a city with the infrastructure for more than 30,000 visitors. (Don't ask me how I learned that there are also camping spots close to the exhibition area.)

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/11/Untitled-1.png)

See you in Nuremberg next year. Bring a tent, just in case. I've got the coffee.

## The one big thing

[Cisco Talos recently discovered VOLTA](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/appraisal-by-ai-model-driven-victim-prioritization-in-the-volta-infostealer/), a mature, self-deleting Windows infostealer that uses AI to act as a personal intelligence analyst for cybercriminals. After harvesting a massive haul of credentials, sessions, and cryptocurrency wallets, VOLTA sends a structured evidence bundle to a large language model to rank the victim’s assets by monetization value. The malware even uses stolen session access to enrich this data, searching the victim's emails for purchase receipts to corroborate home addresses. If an AI model refuses to perform the target assessment, VOLTA seamlessly fails over to a multi-model chain to ensure the attackers get their prioritized dossier.

### Why do I care?

VOLTA replaces the manual work of data sorting with automated, AI-driven victim profiling. By letting an LLM determine a target's worth, attackers drastically reduce the human effort required to monetize stolen data. This commoditized threat allows even low-level actors to bypass security measures like Chrome's App-Bound Encryption and receive ranked target assessments, escalating the risk of severe follow-on attacks.

### So now what?

Organizations should implement application control policies to restrict or alert on processes launching Chrome with the `--headless` flag. Defenders must also enforce best practices for storing credentials in hardware-backed stores.

Revoke any potentially compromised tokens and change passwords. For cryptocurrency wallets, if the seed phrase was in plaintext on disk, treat the wallet as fully compromised and migrate funds to a new wallet...
