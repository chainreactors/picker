# The Obsidia Project Launches

Adam Shostack, Shostack + Associates

The Obsidia Project is stepping up to document important incidents.
![The release image](/images/blog/img/2026/obsidia-1600w.png)

Why we believe what we believe, and how we update those beliefs are
at the heart of any community of practice. Science, engineering,
medicine and more have hierarchies of knowledge, starting with
case studies, and going up to double-blind randomized trials to
test new treatments. In cybersecurity, we rarely even get independent case
studies, where the facts are captured and protected against
link-rot and fading memories.

That independent history and analysis were amongst the reasons I
was so happy to see the US (and then Australia) stand up
Cyber Safety Review Boards. Sadly, the United States has left ours
unstaffed.

That's why I’m excited that the Obsidia Project is stepping up to
create “authoritative public case studies of the world’s most
consequential cybersecurity incidents, built so the field can stop
repeating its disasters.” It’s also why I’m happy to be an
advisor as they release their first study, on [Colonial Pipeline](https://www.obsidiaproject.org/casebooks).

The 23 page report includes a 2 minute briefing, a verified
timeline of events, technical analysis, findings and
recommendations, and is accompanied by a 12 page tabletop.
