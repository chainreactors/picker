An old-fashioned smash-and-grab job on a photographer’s vehicle threatens the security of pupils’ data as a UK secondary school says it faces a potential data protection pickle.

Jon Curtis-Brignell, headteacher at Priory School in Lewes, said in an email this week that a company the school hired to take pupils’ annual school photos has alerted it to a data theft case after a vehicle was broken into.

“Pret-a-Portrait has informed us that, overnight between 6 and 7 October, one of its vehicles was broken into and a camera bag containing SD memory cards was stolen,” Curtis-Brignell wrote.

REG AD

“Some of these cards may have contained photographs taken at Priory School, together with images of the [QR code](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/04/21/panasonic-creates-device-locked-qr-codes-for-biometrics/5221140) cards used for school photographs, which show pupils’ names and class names.”

REG AD

The headteacher said that the same equipment used to take the photos at Priory School was also used for three additional photography jobs after Pret-a-Portrait visited the school on October 1.

Pret-a-Portrait, which has offices in the UK, Qatar, and the UAE, told the [school](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/01/englands-schools-are-getting-better-at-mopping-up-cyber-incidents/5300465) that it believes “most, if not all” of the files containing pupils’ data will have been overwritten as a result, but it cannot be certain.

Curtis-Brignell said that the potentially affected information included photographs, pupils’ names, and their “class information associated with the photography process.”

Other types of data, such as addresses, contact details, financial information, medical information, and safeguarding records stored in the school’s own systems remain untouched.

Nick Kerr, owner of Pret-a-Portrait, told The Register that no physical, printed photos were stored in the vehicle at the time of the break-in, and that it was not possible to determine the potential scope of the [data protection](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/15/metropolitan-polices-unchecked-use-of-shoplifting-platform-lands-force-in-trouble-with-data-watchdog/5303061) violation.

“As the email from Priory School explains, it is likely to be somewhere between a small subset of their pictures and none of them,” said Kerr.

On a positive note, he said that there is nothing to suggest the files have been improperly accessed.

“Our analysis reveals that no improper access to pictures from this job occurred in between the time of the theft and the pictures being taken offline.”
