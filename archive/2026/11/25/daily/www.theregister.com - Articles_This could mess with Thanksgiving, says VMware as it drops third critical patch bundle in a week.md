VMware has issued its third critical security advisory in a week, this time to warn of four critical flaws and address a total of 23 issues.

[VMSA-2026-0011](https://support.broadcom.com/web/ecx/support-content-notification/-/external/content/SecurityAdvisories/0/39626) landed on November 24, just five days after two other advisories. VMware’s [FAQ](Because%20of%20coordinated%20disclosure%20and%20release%20pipeline%20timing%20this%20VMSA%20comes%20on%20the%20heels%20of%20VMSA-2026-0009%2C%20and%20atop%20a%20major%20holiday%20in%20the%20United%20States.%20The%20security%20vulnerability%20landscape%20is%20more%20dynamic%20than%20ever;%20please%20know%20that%20while%20we%20acknowledge%20this%20timing%20is%20not%20optimal,%20your%20ability%20to%20operate%20and%20maintain%20a%20secure%20environment%20amidst%20modern,%20AI-assisted%20threats%20is%20paramount%20to%20us.) for the new advisory includes the following remarks:

Because of coordinated disclosure and release pipeline timing this VMSA comes on the heels of VMSA-2026-0009, and atop a major holiday in the United States.

The security vulnerability landscape is more dynamic than ever; please know that while we acknowledge this timing is not optimal, your ability to operate and maintain a secure environment amidst modern, AI-assisted threats is paramount to us.

REG AD

Make of that what you will as you ponder how and when to patch the four critical flaws, including the 9.8-rated CVE-2026-123903, which allows a malicious actor to bypass authentication and gain unauthorized access to vCenter, the management tool VMware users rely on to manage hosts and VMs.

REG AD

This is the third 9.8-rated authentication issue VMware has fixed in vCenter since July.

Two of the other critical flaws in this new advisory allow an attacker to run code on an ESX host, earning the bugs a 9.8 rating. CVE-2026-123910 makes that possible thanks to a heap out-of-bounds write vulnerability in the I/O filter service, while CVE-2026-123911 is a use-after-free vulnerability.

The other critical flaw, CVE-2026-123912, can result in a guest escape from VMware’s desktop Workstation and Fusion desktop hypervisors. It’s also present in VMware’s ESX hypervisor, but attackers thankfully can’t escape that product’s sandbox. This one’s easy to mitigate because the problem is in a USB controller that users can turn off.

Most of the other flaws require installation of updated software.
