Making Threat Intelligence Work for SOC Teams: ANY.RUN & Elastic Webinar Insights

Threat intelligence on its own is only data. Its value depends on how effectively SOC teams can turn it into action.

Simply put, this was the premise of our recent webinar.

The [SOC and business impact](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/threat-intelligence-for-soc/) of threat intelligence depends largely on how quickly analysts can use it to validate threats, make confident decisions, and take action.

Exploring how to make that happen was the focus of [ANY.RUN](https://any.run/?utm_source=anyrunblog&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=ti-feeds-elastic-webinar&utm_term=81026&utm_content=linktolanding)‘s recent joint webinar with Elastic, “Turn Threat Intelligence Into SOC Action with ANY.RUN and Elastic Security.”

[Watch the full webinar](https://anyrun.webinargeek.com/turn-threat-intelligence-into-soc-action-with-any-run-and-elastic-security?cst=blog)

## How It Went

ANY.RUN CTO Dmitry Marinov and Elastic Principal Solutions Architect Tammy Torbert came together for a live discussion on making threat intelligence work in [daily SOC operations](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/soc-maturity-with-threat-intelligence/).

And we didn’t stop at theory. The webinar included interactive tasks for the audience, a live demo of the [ANY.RUN Threat Intelligence and Elastic Security integration](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/any-run-elastic-security-integration/), and a Q&A session that gave us a chance to dive into even more practical questions from our viewers.

A big thank you to the Elastic team for making this session happen, and to everyone who joined us, took part, and brought great questions to the discussion!

## Executive Takeaways

![](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Screenshot-2026-10-07-at-15.03.53-1024x576.png)

*Three key requirements for threat intelligence, as discussed by ANY.RUN and Elastic*

So, what actually makes threat intelligence useful in daily SOC work? During our discussion, we narrowed it to three things:

### 1. Threat Intelligence Has to Stay Relevant

Threat infrastructure changes quickly, and indicators can lose relevance just as fast.

**Solution:** ANY.RUN [Threat Intelligence Feeds](https://any.run/threat-intelligence-feeds/?utm_source=anyrunblog&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=ti-feeds-elastic-webinar&utm_term=81026&utm_content=linktotifeedslanding) deliver continuously validated, high-confidence IOCs from real-world investigations by more than 16,000 SOC teams and 700,000 security professionals. This helps SOC teams keep detection workflows aligned with active threats.

**Outcome:** [Earlier identification](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/streamline-your-soc/) of known malicious activity, less manual IOC validation, and more analyst time for complex investigations.

### 2. Context Helps Analysts Make Better Decisions

An IOC match can validate suspicious activity, but analysts still need to understand what happened and [how urgently they should respond](https://any.run/cybersecurity-blog/efficient-soc-for-fast-response/).

**Solution:** ANY.RUN Threat Intelligence connects IOC matches to behavioral evidence from [Sandbox](https://any.run/features/?utm_source=anyrunblog&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=ti-feeds-elastic-webinar&utm_term=81026&utm_content=linktosandboxlanding) analyses, helping analysts assess threat severity without relying on indicator matches alone.

**Outcome:** Faster, more confident triage, better prioritization of high-risk incidents, and fewer unnecessary escalations.
