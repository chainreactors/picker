# Publisher Wants Nhentai’s Alleged Owners in Lawsuit, Subpoenas Bank of America

today by
[Ernesto Van der Sar](https://torrentfreak.com/author/ernesto/)

[Home](https://torrentfreak.com "Go to TorrentFreak.") > [Piracy](https://torrentfreak.com/category/piracy/ "Go to the Piracy category archives.") >

Nhentai's operator is fighting a subpoena that publisher PCR Distributing sent to Bank of America, requesting financial records for the company and others linked to the popular adult manga site. The publisher also wants to add the site's alleged owners and additional claims to its piracy lawsuit. Nhentai's operator opposes this request, arguing that PCR has known the names of its members for more than 500 days.

![nhentai](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nhentai.jpg)
With more than 75 million visits in October, according to [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/) data, Nhentai is one of the most visited adult sites on the Internet.

The site offers adult manga, broadly known as hentai, without charging any subscription fees. California-based publisher PCR Distributing, which operates the J18 and JAST USA brands, sees it as a pirate site and [took the matter to a federal court](https://torrentfreak.com/pirate-site-nhentai-sued-in-u-s-court-for-copyright-infringement-240903/) in Los Angeles in 2024.

After the court ruled that [the operators couldn’t litigate anonymously](https://torrentfreak.com/nhentai-operators-ordered-to-expose-themselves-in-u-s-copyright-lawsuit-250415/), Delaware company X Separator was listed as Nhentai’s owner. Last year, this company [filed a counterclaim](https://torrentfreak.com/nhentai-rejects-piracy-claims-hits-back-with-500k-damages-claim-for-fraud/) accusing PCR of fraud, claiming more than $500,000 in damages.

According to Nhentai’s operator, PCR had previously given it permission to use the works.

## Following the Money

After two years have passed, the publisher has turned its attention to the money train. On November 4, PCR served a subpoena on [Bank of America](https://www.bankofamerica.com/), requesting records on six “Account Holders”: X Separator, Acheron Interactive LLC, Neal Akash Palavali, Nikita Nikishin, Michael Kim, and anyone doing business under the Nhentai name.

*The Bank of America subpoena*

![The Bank of America subpoena](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nhentai-boa-account-holders.png)

Among other things, PCR wants to know who signs for and owns each account, which payments came in from online advertising networks, and what was paid to [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/), [Namecheap](https://www.namecheap.com/), and other online intermediaries including hosting and domain providers.

According to PCR, this subpoena is needed because Nhentai’s own disclosures can’t be trusted to identify the people behind the site and the money that’s at stake.

“The Subpoena seeks records identifying who owns, controls, and is paid by the nHentai enterprise. That is merits discovery,” PCR writes.

## Nhentai Objects

However, X Separator sees things differently. It asked the court to quash the subpoena, describing it as post-judgment discovery efforts in a case where there is no judgment yet. In addition, Nhentai’s owner stresses that five of the six account holders are not parties to the lawsuit.

PCR rejects these arguments and also points out that California allows plaintiffs to attach financial assets before a judgment is entered.

Last week, Magistrate Judge A. Joel Richlin discussed the dispute with both sides in a short videoconference. The parties agreed to put the subpoena on hold, and the judge said he would sign off on their agreement.

*On hold*

![On hold](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nhentai-boa-stipulation.png)

Under the agreement, Bank of America won’t provide any information until the court decides. If anything was already shared, PCR’s counsel can’t review it until the court decides.

## Adding the Owners

The bank subpoena isn’t the only active front. Earlier this month, PCR asked the court for permission to file a second amended complaint, which names Acheron, Palavali, Nikishin and Kim as additional defendants.

*New defendants*
[![New defendants](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nhentai-sac-defendants.png)](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nhentai-sac-defendants.png)

According to the proposed complaint, Palavali lives in the Los Angeles area, while Nikishin, described as the “founder of nHentai,” lives in Michigan.

Acheron allegedly owns the Nhentai domain name and pays for four servers that cost roughly $15,660 per month. Kim, meanwhile, is listed as the domain’s owner contact according to PCR.

PCR further alleges that Nhentai doesn’t rely on user uploads, but uses an automated system to copy works from selected third-party sites. In addition, the new complaint adds 27 more works, as well as trademark claims, including counterfeiting.

## ‘The Die Has Been Cast’

On November 20, X Separator asked the court to deny the amended complaint. It says it identified Palavali and Nikishin as its members on June 27, 2025, in supplemental disclosures that PCR’s motion doesn’t mention. According to X Separator, PCR has known the names for 511 days.

The company also rejects the suggestion that its people were hiding. PCR knew since January that Nikishin lived in Michigan, he was deposed at the office of PCR’s own lawyer, and Palavali was offered for a deposition in Santa Monica, it notes.

According to X Separator, the amendment would multiply the case fivefold, from seven copyrights to 34. The trademark claims would be futile too, it adds.

“At this stage of the litigation, the die has been cast. Plaintiff should not be permitted to roll it again by radically expanding the parties and the claims,” X Separator writes.

Judge Cynthia Valenzuela is scheduled to hear the motion on December 11. For now, the bank subpoena remains on hold. PCR wants the court to reject X Separator’s motion, but says it would accept narrower requests if the court finds them too broad.
