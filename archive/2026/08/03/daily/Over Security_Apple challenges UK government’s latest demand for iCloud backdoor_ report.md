# Apple challenges UK government’s latest demand for iCloud backdoor: report

Apple is challenging another legal demand by the U.K. government to access the encrypted data of its users in the country, [according to Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/2cc9c96a-0e5b-4c33-a95a-3d11072a145c?syn-25a6b1a6=1).

The company reportedly filed a complaint with the U.K.’s Investigatory Powers Tribunal, a court that hears cases related to government surveillance, after the government last year [issued a “technical capability notice.”](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/uk-government-tries-again-to-access-encrypted-apple-customer-data-report/) The notice is a secret government legal order for demanding access to users’ data, even if the data is encrypted.

For critics, [this kind of demand is the equivalent of requesting a backdoor](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/uk-government-tries-again-to-access-encrypted-apple-customer-data-report/) into Apple’s cloud backups that have Advanced Data Protection (ADP) switched on, which makes the data end-to-end encrypted and inaccessible to anyone other than the customer, including Apple.

This new challenge comes a year after a similar fight between Apple and the U.K. government. In early 2025, London [issued a secret order](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/07/uk-government-demands-apple-backdoor-to-encrypted-cloud-data-report/) to Apple demanding access to users’ encrypted iCloud backups, [which was later dropped](http://techcrunch.com/2025/08/19/us-spy-chief-says-uk-has-dropped-its-apple-backdoor-demand/) following the intervention of the Trump administration.

After the first order, [the company reacted](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/21/apple-pulls-icloud-end-to-end-encryption-feature-for-uk-users-after-government-demanded-backdoor/) by removing the ability of U.K. users to use ADP. Then, in October, [the U.K. issued a second order](http://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/uk-government-tries-again-to-access-encrypted-apple-customer-data-report/), which Apple is now challenging.

Apple did not respond to TechCrunch’s request for comment.
