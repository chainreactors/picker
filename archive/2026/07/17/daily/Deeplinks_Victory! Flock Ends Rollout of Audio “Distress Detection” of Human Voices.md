Reversing course, Flock Safety—the surveillance technology vendor most known for its extensive network of [automated license plate readers](https://sls.eff.org/technologies/automated-license-plate-readers-alprs)—[has announced](https://www.flocksafety.com/blog/how-flocks-audio-detection-works) that it will end a pilot for its [acoustic gunshot detection devices](https://sls.eff.org/technologies/gunshot-detection) to identify signs of “human distress.”

In October 2025, [EFF warned the public](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/10/flocks-gunshot-detection-microphones-will-start-listening-human-voices) that Flock was rolling out a new feature called “Distress Detection” that would be deployed through their acoustic gunshot detection devices (formerly known as Flock Raven, now called Audio Detection). This feature purported to use high-powered microphones scattered throughout a city to search for sounds of human distress, with original advertisements from the product indicating it would search for “screaming.” (Since the publication of our [original blog post](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/10/flocks-gunshot-detection-microphones-will-start-listening-human-voices), Flock quietly amended the ad on this [webpage](https://www.flocksafety.com/distress-early-access) to say “distress” instead of “screaming.”)
