# [Suspected Vietnam-Linked Hackers Target Southeast Asia in South China Sea Espionage Campaign](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/suspected-vietnam-linked-hackers-target.html)

**Ravie Lakshmanan**Nov 27, 2026Cyber Espionage / Government Security

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj19T-kU1dmVfKt2PtUQ2I25MGovj8kmv6lMkk6inlif4yLeezJS4kVRTvsARL5UkpTT66jYru6k2b5bmWfjjlkmG4JznSKmkmd4ldXcyxTw3xsU5nP6_jxfWMw9thW37m5usfr-eWAZBHq6bPuS613CIx9XgWxPcSVFcZVgz_9tWd5Nu8YWAxexuqcVPrN/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/data.jpg)

A Vietnam-sponsored hacking group has been attributed to attacks targeting Southeast Asian military, government, and legislative organizations with an aim to gather intelligence on South China Sea defense and military agreements.

The previously undocumented incursion set, detailed by SOCRadar, has been codenamed **WeaveLotus**. The activity is assessed to have comprised five intrusions in Thailand and the Philippines and reconnaissance efforts against Indonesian government organizations.

"Initial access was achieved primarily by exploiting web application vulnerabilities and default credentials in open-source software, including GeoServer, JasperReports, osTicket, and exposed Laravel debug mode configurations," SOCRadar [said](https://socradar.io/blog/redops-weavelotus-southeast-asian-governments/) in an analysis published Thursday.

"The threat actor established persistent internal network access using reverse SSH tunnels and moved laterally into downstream networks and applications."

The links to Vietnam are based on the presence of native Vietnamese-language code comments, operational activity aligning with UTC+07:00 working hours, regional strategic targeting, and custom scripting. The sustained campaign, codenamed RedOps, was observed between August and September 2026.

The threat actor has also been observed conducting open-source intelligence (OSINT), setting up automated Bing search scripts that scraped news and documents on military agreements involving the Philippines, China, and the U.S., with a particular emphasis on the South China Sea, a region of [strategic and geopolitical importance](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/eggstreme-hackers-rebuild-malware-with.html).

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/threat-protection-d)

Earlier this August, Hunt.io [disclosed](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/snowflake-github-actions-flaw-lets.html) details of another cyber espionage operation undertaken by a suspected Chinese threat actor targeting a nuclear research organization and a marine engineering and shipbuilding company in the Philippines to steal sensitive documents after gaining access through known security vulnerabilities.

"Following the OSINT phase, the operators enumerated government, military, and legislative entities across the Philippines, Thailand, and Indonesia, compromising targets in Thailand and the Philippines across five confirmed intrusions," SOCRadar said. "Activity against Indonesian targets did not progress beyond reconnaissance."

"The core objective of RedOps appears to be the exfiltration of internal records and documents from Southeast Asian governments in support of Vietnam's foreign policy and military intelligence priorities."

A brief overview of the intrusion activity is as follows -

* **Thai military organization**, which likely involved the exploitation of a critical GeoServer flaw ([CVE-2024-36401](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/unpatched-geoserver-zero-day-targeted.html), CVSS score: 9.8) to deploy a SOCKS5 proxy and open a reverse SSH tunnel, followed by hands-on reconnaissance, persistence, and unauthenticated access to Directus CMS API's file collection and asset download endpoints to enumerate records and download a subset of those records. The threat actor also ran Optical Character Recognition (OCR) on the collected data to extract English and Thai text from PNG images, and unsuccessfully attempted to gain access to surveillance video feeds hosted on Wowza to possibly gain real-time visibility into military operations.
* **Thai legislative organization**, which involved the attacker leveraging an exposed JasperReports server with default administrative credentials (jasperadmin:jasperadmin) to upload a malicious report that ultimately executed SQL scripts on the backend Microsoft SQL Server and then deleted the report to cover up the tracks. The SQL commands, in turn, employed [xp\_cmdshell](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/system-stored-procedures/xp-cmdshell-transact-sql?view=sql-server-ver17) to launch a reverse SSH SOCKS proxy tunnel over port 443 to WeaveLotus infrastructure ("172.235.245[.]49"). The attackers deployed a lightweight Python C2 server to automate post-exploitation and targeted a third-party document management system (DMS) by launching password spraying attacks using real employee names paired with National ID numbers as passwords. Once authenticated, the attackers collected the entire system configuration, the complete application user directory comprising 1,502 records), along with system audit logs and 5,000 ZIP archives containing sensitive data.
* **Thai government organization**, which involved the attacker gaining root SSH access to a server running Keycloak 3.4 and dumping its credential database, following cracking the PBKDF2-SHA256 hashes offline with wordlists built around Thai Buddhist Era years and then validating them in real-time against the Resource Owner Password Credentials (ROPC) token endpoint.
