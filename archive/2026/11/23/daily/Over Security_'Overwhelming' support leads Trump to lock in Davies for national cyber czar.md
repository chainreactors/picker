# 'Overwhelming' support leads Trump to lock in Davies for national cyber czar

President Donald Trump intends to nominate Pentagon official Kirsten Davies to be the country’s next cyber czar, according to a White House official.

Davies, the military’s chief information officer, was named the acting national cyber director last week after the departure of Sean Cairncross, a former Republican National Committee official.

However, following the “overwhelming” support Davies received from industry and government leaders after the announcement, Trump decided to fill the position permanently, the White House official said.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, who already chairs the administration’s senior-level commission on artificial intelligence, will step into the role while Davies awaits Senate confirmation.

“I’m excited to work more with Kirsten, who has the experience and capabilities needed to help America navigate our increasingly complex and powerful digital infrastructure as our next National Cyber Director,” Clayton said in a statement. “I look forward to working with the ONCD team to ensure Kirsten has a seamless transition into the role upon her confirmation by the Senate.”

Prior to the Pentagon, Davies was the chief information security officer at Unilever, a role she previously held at Estée Lauder Companies. She was also the managing director and group chief security officer for Barclays Africa, now known as ABSA.

She also held cybersecurity roles at tech giant Hewlett Packard and Siemens, as well as leadership positions at Booz Allen Hamilton and Deloitte Australia.

The leadership moves come amid [reports](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/20/politics/fbi-hacked-again-target-crime-database) that the FBI’s National Crime Information Center has been hacked. The agency was struck with a major cyberattack in September that gave hackers access to the personal information of thousands of FBI agents as well as Justice Department staffers in U.S. attorney offices.

Meanwhile, the country grapples with a wave of outages across the public and private sectors. Last week Clayton and other administration insisted the blackouts were not caused by foreign adversaries.

Speaking to Fox News on Saturday, Trump said the latest FBI hack could have “positive” impacts.

“I will say that it’s not good — I don’t like that,” he said. “But maybe we’ll learn things that we would have never learned otherwise because, you know, you had the Russia, Russia, Russia hoax.”

“Maybe we’ll learn about the voter fraud, all the voter fraud that took place,” Trump said, without citing evidence.
