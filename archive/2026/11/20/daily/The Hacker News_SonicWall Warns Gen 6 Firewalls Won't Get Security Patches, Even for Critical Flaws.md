# [SonicWall Warns Gen 6 Firewalls Won't Get Security Patches, Even for Critical Flaws](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/sonicwall-warns-gen-6-firewalls-wont.html)

**Ravie Lakshmanan**Nov 20, 2026Network Security / Vulnerability

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiGn5oji8gd4l_d6wKemSflqTX-q-eiuu4CbbV2ROvzK6Gzpeq8zPsQyGb-UO80f_M8L2pDNNyNqF1ZIdyMPGReyW0KmPb7uuAuRu1M-ZVyNu-GJvx1CRaBsaEOVjV-asFG0P__0MK0Jp3WPJmcNxATiSd6NeBzcCmy-6dLvS5yvpen0cRrwLZvgVpYAvn0/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/sonicwall.jpg)

SonicWall has urged all customers still running [Generation 6 firewalls](https://www.sonicwall.com/support/notices/product-advisory-retire-unsupported-gen-6-firewalls/kA1VN000002iouP0AQ#:~:text=still%20be%20secured.-,Affected%20products,-This%20advisory%20covers) to retire and replace them immediately and stop exposing the devices on the internet edge, citing security risks fueled by AI-assisted attacks.

"Attackers now use automation and AI to find and exploit unsupported edge devices faster than ever," SonicWall [said](https://www.sonicwall.com/support/notices/product-advisory-retire-unsupported-gen-6-firewalls/kA1VN000002iouP0AQ). "We expect internet-facing Gen 6 firewalls to be actively targeted. Treat any Gen 6 firewall still in service as a high-risk exposure, and migrate to a supported Gen 8 firewall running SonicOS 8."

The affected products running SonicOS 6.x include -

* TZ350, TZ350W, TZ500, TZ500W, SOHO 250, SOHO 250W
* NSA 4600, NSA 5600, NSA 6600
* NSa 4650, NSa 5650, NSa 6650
* TZ400, TZ400W, TZ600, TZ600P, NSA 3600
* NSa 3650
* NSa 2650
* SOHO, SOHO W, TZ300P, NSa 9250, NSa 9450, NSa 9650
* NSv 10 to NSv 1600 (Gen 6 virtual)
* SuperMassive 9200, 9400, 9600, 9800
* NSsp 12800, NSsp 12400
* TZ300, TZ300W
* NSA 2600

In recent years, threat actors have [increasingly](https://censys.com/blog/postcards-from-the-edge-verizon-dbir-reveals-sharp-increase-in-targeting-of-edge-security-devices/) targeted edge devices as a launch point to obtain initial access, even as the window between disclosure and confirmed exploitation has collapsed significantly, in part driven by threat actors' use of automation and AI to scan the internet for unsupported devices, analyze patches and vulnerability disclosure announcements, and build working exploits faster.

According to [Google Mandiant's M-Trends report](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026) published in March 2026, the mean time to exploit vulnerabilities has turned negative, reaching an estimated -7 days, meaning exploitation, on average, begins before a patch is available, down from 63 days in 2018 and -1 day in 2024.

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/network-defense-dd)

"This trend is compounded by threat actors' evolving interest in security appliances and networking infrastructure," Google [said](https://www.gstatic.com/security-marketing/m-trends-2026-en.pdf). "Because these assets provide critical inspection of data ingress and egress points, they grant significant visibility and permissions to those who gain access to them."

Cisco Talos' 2025 Year in Review report has also found that 23% of CVEs discovered in the year directly impact network devices like VPN appliances, next-generation firewalls (NGFWs), load balancers, routers, and others.

"Since these systems sit at the perimeter of enterprise environments, compromise may lead to direct access to the critical networks," Talos [said](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/2025-talos-year-in-review-speed-scale-and-staying-power/). "This aligns with real-world trends from CISA'sKnown Exploitation Vulnerability (KEV) catalog, where exploitation consistently favors edge devices because they are internetfacing, often lag in patching, and provide direct operational leverage once compromised."

With Gen 6 no longer being actively supported and receiving patches, even for critical flaws, users are recommended to follow the steps below without any delay -

* Inventory every Gen 6 firewall in the production environment and retire them.
* Prioritize units that are exposed to the internet or provide remote access.
* Replace them with a Gen 8 firewall running SonicOS 8
* Contact an authorized SonicWall partner to plan the migration

Until the firewall is replaced, it's advised to take the below actions to minimize exposure -

* Disable WAN management (HTTPS, SSH, and SNMP on WAN interfaces).
* Disable SSL VPN, or restrict it to known source addresses.
* Enforce multi-factor authentication on every remaining remote-access and admin account, and reset local passwords.
* Disable services and interfaces not in use.
* Monitor logs closely for unusual logins, configuration changes and traffic.

"The risk to an unsupported firewall grows every day it stays online," SonicWall said. "One breach reaches everything. A compromised perimeter firewall gives attackers a foothold for credential theft, lateral movement and ransomware deployment."
