# On-premises vs. cloud: How to choose a deployment model for your SOC

Cloud or on-premises? Self-hosted or hybrid? Weigh cost against control to find the deployment model that fits your SOC.

![Lines of green code organized in an abstract layout](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69b190e7f47e4c44b0ee07d8/6afc76362831701f3e2fc703_blog-on-premises-vs-cloud-how-to-choose-a-deployment-model-for-your-soc-img-cover.webp)

## Key takeaways

Where your security platform runs affects cost, control, compliance and how your SOC works day to day.

* Cloud computing swaps upfront hardware spending for pay-as-you-go costs and fast scaling.
* On-premises infrastructure gives you full control, along with full responsibility for hardware, patches and staffing.
* Hybrid cloud keeps sensitive data local while other workloads run in the cloud.
* Self-hosted describes who runs the software, so a self-hosted platform can sit on-premises, in a cloud account you control or in an air-gapped network.
* Sekoia offers its SOC platform as SaaS or as Sekoia Self-Hosted, so you can match the deployment model to your data and your team.

Every security team eventually has to decide where its tools will run.

For years, the on-premises vs. cloud debate was mostly about IT infrastructure and who keeps the servers running. For security operations, the stakes are higher. The answer decides where your security data lives, where detection runs, and whether analysts can keep working when an internet connection drops.

Cloud computing has become the default for many organizations, and for good reason. Yet plenty of enterprises, public bodies and operators in highly regulated industries still keep sensitive data inside their own perimeter. Many end up somewhere in between.

This guide explains the key differences between cloud and on-premises infrastructure, how hybrid cloud service providers fit in, and what each model means for a security operations center (SOC). It also clears up a term that often gets mixed into the conversation, self-hosted, which is a different thing from on-premises.

## What is cloud computing?

Cloud computing is the delivery of computing resources, such as servers, storage, databases and software applications, over the internet. Instead of buying physical hardware, you rent virtualized computing resources and pay for what you use.
