# Palantir exec nominated to lead CISA; lame-duck Senate vote is uncertain

President Donald Trump has nominated the chief information security officer of Palantir to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, potentially marking a turning point for an organization that has endured massive staff cuts and years of Republican attacks.

The president [selected](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/nominations-and-withdrawals-sent-to-the-senate-ba2b/) Michael LoSapio on Monday as the next head of the top civilian cyber defense agency, which has been without a Senate-confirmed leader since Trump returned to the Oval Office. A previous nominee, Sean Plankey, [withdrew from consideration](https://therecord.media/trump-pick-to-lead-cisa-withdraws-from-consideration) earlier this year after spending months in limbo on Capitol Hill; Nick Andersen has led CISA in an acting capacity since February.

The Homeland Security Department’s cyber wing lost more than 1,000 personnel — one-third of its workforce — and has seen its budget slashed by billions during the second Trump administration, following years of GOP complaints that its efforts to combat online disinformation during elections singled out conservative voices and infringed upon free speech rights.

As a result, CISA remained largely behind the scenes ahead of last week’s midterm elections. However, a spokesperson said the organization “provided voluntary, no-cost services” like  vulnerability scanning ahead of Election Day and activated its Incident Coordination Group to serve as a threat information sharing hub.

It is unclear if the Senate will take up LoSapio’s nomination during the already jam-packed lame duck session of Congress, with lawmakers looking to approve a must-pass government funding measure and the annual defense policy bill, among other priorities.

Should lawmakers adjourn next month without approving his nomination, Trump would have to nominate LoSapio again next year for consideration by the incoming Democratic majority. A spokesperson for Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), the chair of the Senate Homeland Security Committee, did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Sen. Gary Peters (MI), the panel’s top Democrat, said in a statement that CISA “needs a steady hand at the wheel who is committed to rebuilding its capabilities, supporting its workforce, and ensuring it can effectively support critical infrastructure operators navigate evolving threats.”

LoSapio must “demonstrate that he possesses the independent judgment and qualifications to decisively place national security and the national interest above political pressure,” added Peters, who is retiring in January.

If the nomination is resubmitted next year, LoSapio could face increased scrutiny due to his role at Palantir, a software and artificial intelligence company with ties to the Trump administration. LoSapio joined the firm in 2017, leading its infrastructure and network security group before becoming CIO in late 2024, according to his LinkedIn profile.

Rep. Bennie Thompson (D-MS), who is expected to retake the reins of the House Homeland Security Committee, [has signaled he plans to conduct](https://democrats-homeland.house.gov/news/correspondence/thompson-demands-dhs-comply-with-all-document-preservation-requirements) thorough oversight of all DHS components, including CISA.

The committee’s current chair, Rep. Andrew Garbarino (R-NY) praised Andersen, the acting director for his “exceptional leadership.”

“I look forward to further reviewing the qualifications of Michael LoSapio and urge the Senate to do so with the necessary urgency,” he added.

Andersen is widely seen as an effective leader and is well-liked. His temporary term expired September 24 based on federal statute limiting how long acting directors can stay in place. He remained on the job afterward through a bureaucratic move. By nominating LoSapio, the administration starts the clock again.

A former government cybersecurity official said via text that it is hard to know what to make of the LoSapio selection, “with the nomination coming out right at the beginning of the lame duck and exactly at the same time as Nick Andersen needed this to happen in order to remain Acting Director.”

LoSapio’s name had been floated several months ago, the former official said, but he questioned whether the administration would really push for him to be confirmed in 2026 and stick with him in 2027.

Other AI and cybersecurity experts said that while LoSapio’s background at Palantir gives him a unique perspective at a time when AI is becoming increasingly embedded in national security and every day cyber operations, that perspective isn’t sufficient for running CISA.

“There’s a big difference between securing a company that builds AI-powered technology and understanding how to defend against AI-driven threats” and running CISA, said Chris Cochran, who is vice president of AI Security at the SANS Institute.

“What I’ll be watching for is whether he can bridge that gap, particularly as we move toward autonomous attacks, AI agents, and adversaries operating at machine speed.”
