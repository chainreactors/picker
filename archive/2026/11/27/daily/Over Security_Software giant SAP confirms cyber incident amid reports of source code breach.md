# Software giant SAP confirms cyber incident amid reports of source code breach

German software giant SAP confirmed a cybersecurity incident following reports that hackers infiltrated its software development infrastructure, potentially gaining access to the company’s source code.

In a statement to Recorded Future News on Friday, SAP said it had identified suspicious activity and immediately taken steps to protect its customers and secure its services. The company did not disclose the nature or extent of the intrusion.

“Based on our investigation to date, we have found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data,” SAP said. “We will continue to strengthen our security to provide the secure and resilient environment our customers depend on.”

German magazine Der Spiegel first [reported](https://www.spiegel.de/netzwelt/sap-wird-opfer-von-hackerangriff-a-6648b5ce-5d74-48dc-86ea-9469705bb91d) the incident Thursday, citing internal company communications and sources familiar with the matter.

According to the magazine, hackers targeted SAP’s software development environment hosted on GitHub, obtaining employee authentication tokens that  allowed them to copy and modify the company’s source code.

The German Press Agency (dpa), which independently reviewed portions of internal SAP communication, [reported](https://www.tagesschau.de/wirtschaft/unternehmen/sap-hacker-angriff-100.html) that the company had alerted employees to suspicious activity within its GitHub environment several days before the incident became public. Employees were advised to assess whether they needed to change their login credentials or take additional security precautions.

SAP [launched](https://www.handelsblatt.com/technik/ki/sap-it-sicherheitsvorfall-haben-verdaechtige-aktivitaeten-identifiziert/100273138.html) an internal incident response operation code-named “Project Granite,” according to German business newspaper Handelsblatt.

The company’s emergency security measures reportedly affected several software development teams. SAP restricted access to GitHub, halted automated development processes and replaced digital keys used to verify that its software was genuine and had not been tampered with.

It remains unclear whether the hackers accessed SAP’s proprietary source code or whether any altered code ended up in software delivered to customers.

SAP has not publicly identified who was behind the attack, how the hackers gained access to its systems or whether the incident has been fully contained.

SAP is one of the world’s largest enterprise software companies whose products are used by businesses and public institutions worldwide to manage critical operations, including accounting, procurement, supply chains and human resources.

The incident follows a separate [software supply chain attack](https://community.sap.com/t5/technology-blog-posts-by-sap/cap-developers-call-to-action-to-mitigate-and-apply-solution-provided-in/ba-p/14387683) in April, when hackers injected malicious code into several open-source software packages developed by SAP in an attempt to steal sensitive information from developers and automated software development environments. It is unclear whether the two incidents are connected.
