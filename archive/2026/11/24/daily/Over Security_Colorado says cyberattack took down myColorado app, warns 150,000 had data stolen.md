# Colorado says cyberattack took down myColorado app, warns 150,000 had data stolen

A critical app used by thousands of Colorado residents for state services was taken down by a cyberattack last week.

The Governor’s Office of Information Technology (OIT) said the incident was discovered on the evening of November 18 and involved hackers breaching the myColorado app — which Colorado residents use for digital identification, document storage and accessing state government services.

OIT shut down the app and began an investigation. The app is still unavailable as of Tuesday afternoon.

The state noted that the app has 2.1 million users and the cyberattack likely involved the theft of data tied to nearly 150,000 people. Investigators declined to provide more information about what data may have been stolen by the cybercriminals.

“We know how much Coloradans rely on these services, and we understand how this is frustrating and inconvenient. Protecting our users’ data is our first priority, and we will bring systems back online as quickly as we can once security is restored,” said Sarah Tuneberg, executive director of OIT.

Colorado urged residents to bring their physical IDs if they are traveling for the Thanksgiving holiday.

Those seeking to access state services can still do so through Colorado agency websites.

The state previously dealt with major cyberattacks on the [Department of Higher Education](https://therecord.media/hackers-accessed-colorado-school-data) and [ransomware attacks](https://therecord.media/colorado-police-investigating-ransomware-attack-on-small-town) on several [smaller municipalities](https://therecord.media/local-governments-across-us-dealing-with-ransomware).

Several city and state governments are currently dealing with ransomware attacks and cyber incidents, including agencies in New York, Mississippi and [California](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_AfNP2ufcdY). On Friday, West Virginia’s legislature was [knocked offline](https://therecord.media/west-virginia-legislature-cyberattack) due to a cyberattack.

Queens Public Library in New York City [confirmed](https://connect.queenslibrary.org/splash/) it has nearly recovered from a [ransomware attack two weeks ago](https://therecord.media/queens-library-ransomware-causes-disruptions) that shut down services at its 66 branches. The library, which serves the borough’s more than 2 million residents, has been forced to operate without digital services.

The attack was [claimed](https://x.com/DarkWebInformer/status/2125319185538408752) on Sunday by The Gentlemen ransomware gang — a cybercriminal group that claimed 398 attacks last quarter. The group far surpassed all other ransomware gangs with the amount of claimed victims, according to researchers at [Halcyon](https://www.halcyon.ai/ransomware-evolution-report/q3-2026).

In Mississippi, Lauderdale County’s more than 70,000 residents were also impacted by a cyberattack on the sheriff’s office on Monday.

The law enforcement agency [said](https://www.facebook.com/LCSOMS/posts/pfbid02mGroC5Fz9ZHNox2W55T3mjhTuUkH7mKir8DDpme5x6WwGBmEcrtVbJAc597vJiVDl) it was forced to take its systems offline after the cyberattack. Several services, including the inmate roster listing, are no longer available to the public.
