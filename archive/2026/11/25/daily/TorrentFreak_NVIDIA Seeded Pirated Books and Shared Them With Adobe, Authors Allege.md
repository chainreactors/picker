# NVIDIA Seeded Pirated Books and Shared Them With Adobe, Authors Allege

today by
[Ernesto Van der Sar](https://torrentfreak.com/author/ernesto/)

[Home](https://torrentfreak.com "Go to TorrentFreak.") > [AI](https://torrentfreak.com/category/ai/ "Go to the AI category archives.") >

Several book authors have filed an amended complaint accusing NVIDIA of seeding pirated books via BitTorrent, after downloading them from shadow libraries including Anna's Archive and LibGen. The chip giant allegedly also shared pirated books with business partners, including Adobe. According to the authors, NVIDIA took some steps to limit seeding, but didn't completely prevent it.

![nvdia logo](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nivdia-logo-600x408.png)In a class action lawsuit, several authors, including Abdi Nazemian and Susan Orlean, accuse NVIDIA of [training its AI models on pirated books](https://torrentfreak.com/authors-sue-nvidia-for-training-ai-on-pirated-books-240311/).

Earlier this year, NVIDIA asked the California federal court to dismiss all BitTorrent allegations from the case.

The request [didn’t get very far](https://torrentfreak.com/nvidias-shadow-library-scripts-have-no-other-purpose-than-infringement-judge-rules/). Judge Jon Tigar denied it, noting that the complaint mentioned BitTorrent only once, in a description of the pirate site Bibliotik, while describing the file-sharing protocol as “merely a tool, not a library or dataset.”

That tool is now front and center in the case. Based on NVIDIA’s own torrenting code, the authors allege that the company didn’t disable uploading. In a second amended complaint, they now accuse NVIDIA of sharing pirated books with others.

## Authors Amend Complaint

On Monday, Judge Tigar granted the authors permission to amend, based on documents NVIDIA produced between April and July.

A day later, the authors filed the new complaint. Unlike the proposed version they filed in July, it no longer redacts the names of the torrent tools and business partners, and it also shows several quotes from internal NVIDIA documents.

## ‘Did Not Prevent Seeding’

According to the amended complaint, NVIDIA downloaded pirated books from [Anna’s Archive](https://torrentfreak.com/nvidia-contacted-annas-archive-to-secure-access-to-millions-of-pirated-books/), [LibGen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_Genesis), and other shadow libraries using [aria2c](https://aria2.github.io/) and [put.io](https://put.io/). The first is a command line download tool, the second is a [long-running cloud service](https://torrentfreak.com/download-or-stream-your-torrents-with-put-io-091208/) that downloads torrents for its users.

These tools can download files via BitTorrent, and the authors and NVIDIA both agree that whether they also upload data to others depends on the settings used. According to the authors, NVIDIA didn’t prevent seeding altogether.

“When NVIDIA ran the BitTorrent client, NVIDIA uploaded pieces of pirated book files to third parties during the downloading process (i.e., before the download was complete). NVIDIA also ‘seeded’ pirated books (i.e., made available and uploaded parts of a completely downloaded file). While NVIDIA took some steps to limit seeding, it did not prevent seeding altogether,” the complaint reads.

*Seeding*

![Seeding](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nvidia-seeding.png)

The authors say they found out in June, when NVIDIA handed over the code it used for torrenting. Based on that code, they were “able to determine that NVIDIA did not disable uploading,” the authors told the court.

*Uploading*

![Uploading](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nvidia-uploading.png)

The complaint states that the authors’ own books were among those NVIDIA downloaded. However, the claim that they were also uploaded to others is made “on information and belief,” which essentially means the authors don’t have direct knowledge of it.

## Pirated Books for Adobe

In addition to the seeding claims, the authors also allege that NVIDIA shared the pirated books with business partners, including [Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/), through a “PDF Extraction Project” involving 250 million pages. These pages were acquired in part from shadow libraries such as Anna’s Archive and LibGen.

According to the complaint, NVIDIA shared its pirated books with Adobe because it wanted to “get tokens out of PDFs to create differentiated models” and “include PDF extraction into our NeMo pipeline so that customers can leverage their PDF content”.

*The Adobe project*

![The Adobe project](https://torrentfreak.com/images/nvidia-adobe.png)

In return, the complaint says, Adobe committed to using NVIDIA’s language models in some of its products. That made the deal a big opportunity for NVIDIA, with its NeMo AI models potentially “powering every one of Adobe PDFs,” the authors write.

Adobe wasn’t the only partner. The complaint also mentions partnerships with [Cohere](https://cohere.com/) and [Imbue](https://imbue.com/) that involved sharing NVIDIA’s base training data and offering books acquired from shadow libraries. The authors believe their own books were shared with all three companies, but this is alleged *on information and belief*.

With these allegations, the amended complaint now accuses NVIDIA of distributing the books itself and of helping others to infringe, which falls under its existing direct and contributory infringement claims.

## Meta’s Seeding Fight

NVIDIA isn’t the first AI company to face these seeding claims. In the authors’ lawsuit against Meta, Judge Vince Chhabria previously ruled that Meta’s AI training [was fair use](https://torrentfreak.com/meta-secures-bittersweet-fair-use-victory-in-ai-piracy-case-250626/), but the BitTorrent claims outlived that ruling.

Evidence in that case also [showed](https://torrentfreak.com/rightsholders-cant-use-openai-and-anthropic-to-dismantle-metas-seeding-defense/) that a Meta engineer wrote a script to prevent seeding, but apparently not leeching, the downloading phase during which uploads also take place.

That could matter here too. Citing an earlier ruling, Judge Tigar noted that distributing the works for commercial purposes could affect NVIDIA’s fair use defense.

Whether NVIDIA’s seeding claims take the same path has yet to be seen, but its response to the amended complaint is already due in December.
