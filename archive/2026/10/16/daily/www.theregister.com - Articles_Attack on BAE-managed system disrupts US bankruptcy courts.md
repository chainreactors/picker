The US' bankruptcy courts are currently down owing to what officials are calling a “cybersecurity incident.”

The Administrative Office of the US Courts said the websites and systems that support the Bankruptcy Noticing Center (BNC), which manages electronic comms related to bankruptcy cases, were pulled offline while the vendor investigates.

BAE Systems runs the affected systems and websites on behalf of the Federal Judiciary, and is “working to restore operations,” according to an [announcement](https://www.uscourts.gov/data-news/judiciary-news/2026/10/15/cybersecurity-incident-disrupting-bankruptcy-court-operations) by the US Courts on Thursday.

REG AD

“As a result of the incident, all access to the BNC systems and websites that BAE operates on behalf of the judiciary and the related bankruptcy noticing process are unavailable at this time, including bnc.uscourts.gov, bankruptcynotices.uscourts.gov, notices.uscourts.gov, and the National Creditor Registration Service," it said.

REG AD

“The incident has also halted the BNC’s production and distribution of bankruptcy notices for bankruptcy courts.”

While the public notice was published on Thursday, the issues affecting the BNC systems began a few days earlier, on October 9, according to the Bankruptcy Court of the Eastern District of Texas.

Alongside its own [announcement](https://www.txeb.uscourts.gov/news/bnc-service-interruption-update), the same court published a general order indicating that for any case filed on or after September 14 with an active motion to stay, that delay would be extended by an additional 30 days.

The court said it will continue to accept filings, open cases, and process documents, but said there may be a delay in the entry and service of notices and orders until the issues are resolved.

The courts' ability to process electronic and paper bankruptcy notices is affected, and it applies to federal bankruptcy courts across the country.

A spokesperson at BAE told The Register: "BAE Systems, Inc. operates the Bankruptcy Noticing Center (BNC) on behalf of the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts (AOUSC). We are aware of a security incident that is isolated to the BNC system and have taken immediate steps to address it. We are coordinating closely with AOUSC on the response."

The disruption comes at a difficult time for the US public sector, which has struggled with multiple recent cyberattacks.
