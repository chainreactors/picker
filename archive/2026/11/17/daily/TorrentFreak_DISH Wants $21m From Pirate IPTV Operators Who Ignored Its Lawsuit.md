# DISH Wants $21m From Pirate IPTV Operators Who Ignored Its Lawsuit

today by
[Ernesto Van der Sar](https://torrentfreak.com/author/ernesto/)

[Home](https://torrentfreak.com "Go to TorrentFreak.") > [Lawsuits](https://torrentfreak.com/category/lawsuits/ "Go to the Lawsuits category archives.") >

DISH asks a New York federal court for a $21.15 million default judgment and a broad injunction against the alleged operators of pirate IPTV service DMTN. The two men, who are believed to reside in North Africa, are aware of the lawsuit but did not appear in U.S. court. Meanwhile, the service's website remains online, selling annual subscriptions for €70.

![](https://torrentfreak.com/images/mdtnlo.png)
In February, DISH Network [took](https://torrentfreak.com/dish-sues-dmtn-iptv-in-21m-piracy-lawsuit-operator-posed-as-breaking-bad-creator/) the people allegedly behind DMTN IPTV to court in New York.

The pay-TV provider accused Moroccan resident Idriss Elkasmi of running the service, which was also sold as Idriss Premium TV and Manx TV. According to the complaint, payments were processed through an online store called Genuine Leather, and Elkasmi swapped his Facebook photo for one of Breaking Bad creator Vince Gilligan.

A month later, [DISH](https://www.dish.com/) amended its complaint, adding Tunisian resident Makrem Salem as a second alleged operator. In its latest filings, DISH adds that a company that previously hosted the service’s server identified Salem as the owner of the account used to manage it.

Elkasmi, for his part, pushed back. In an email to DISH’s counsel, he claimed to be “only a freelance website designer,” the amended complaint notes.

## Served by Email

In June, the court allowed DISH to serve the summons and amended complaint by registered email. The order also gave the defendants until July 16 to respond.

DISH sent the papers on June 25. Elkasmi and Salem then both confirmed in separate emails to DISH’s lawyer that they had received them.

*Acknowledged*

![ack](https://torrentfreak.com/images/dish-dmtn-acknowledged.png)

However, the defendants did not formally respond to the complaint. As a result, the Clerk of Court certified their default in August.

Last week, DISH asked the court to turn this into a default judgment. According to the pay-TV provider, the defendants simply continued operating after they were served.

“Defendants’ infringement has continued unabated, even after they each personally acknowledged to DISH that they had received the Amended Complaint in this lawsuit,” DISH writes.

## Still Selling

The operators of the IPTV service did make some changes. According to DISH’s motion, they briefly disabled their website after the lawsuit was filed, to then launch a new one with new features. They also released a new Android app.

Gregory Duval, CEO of NagraStar, which investigated the service for DISH, adds that the service was still streaming the channels at issue in the United States last week.

“They seek to evade enforcement of U.S. copyright laws by working from secret locations in Morocco and Tunisia, using an array of constantly changing servers, and operating through fictitious shell companies with names like ‘Genuine Leather’ and ‘VPS Designs,'” the motion reads.

The pirate IPTV service is also still openly for sale. When we checked this week, the DMTN IPTV website offered a 12-month subscription for €70, discounted from €85, and promised “Instant WhatsApp activation.”

## $21 Million and a Broad Injunction

In its motion, DISH asks the court to award the maximum statutory damages of $150,000 for each of the 141 registered works listed in the complaint. That adds up to $21.15 million, with both defendants liable for the full amount.

DISH also requests a permanent injunction. This would bar the defendants from offering DMTN IPTV, or any comparable service that carries DISH’s programming, “wherever they may be found,” and it would also apply to anyone who receives notice of the order.

To shut the service down, DISH lists several third-party companies that should be bound to the injunction as well. These include hosting companies and domain registrars, but also WhatsApp, Gmail and live chat provider tawk.to, which the service uses to communicate with customers.

For now, DMTN IPTV continues to sell its subscriptions as usual. Whether the companies named in the proposed injunction will have to cut off their services depends on the court’s decision.
