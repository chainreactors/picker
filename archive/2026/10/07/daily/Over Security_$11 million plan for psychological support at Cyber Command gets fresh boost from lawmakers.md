# $11 million plan for psychological support at Cyber Command gets fresh boost from lawmakers

A bipartisan coalition of federal lawmakers this week urged congressional appropriators to support additional funding for mental health support to personnel at U.S. Cyber Command.

The push comes in the wake of several suicide deaths this summer at the command and the National Security Agency, which share a campus and resources at Fort Meade, Maryland. The reports have been [a wakeup call for the Pentagon](https://therecord.media/cyber-command-suicide-deaths-pentagon-memo) and Capitol Hill, which are concerned about the strain being put on military personnel and civilians as the demand for exquisite intelligence and digital warfighting remains high.

In separate letters, two groups — [one composed of members](https://elfreth.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/elfreth.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/citi-cybercom-approps-letter.pdf) from the House Armed Services cyber subcommittee, the other of federal lawmakers [from Maryland](https://elfreth.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/elfreth.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/md-cybercom-approps-letter.pdf) — pressed the leaders of the House and Senate Appropriations panels to back an $11 million request for “High Performance Team Training” at the command.

The request was the No. 2 item on the command’s “Unfunded Priority List” sent to Congress earlier this year. The document said the training would provide “cognitive, psychological, physiological, and spiritual sustainment for high-tempo cyber operations. Without funding, burnout and force degradation will continue.”

The House approved the request in its fiscal 2027 defense spending bill, but the Senate never moved on its draft. Both chambers previously passed a short-term continuing resolution that keeps the government open until December 11; another stopgap measure is likely.

[Recorded Future News first reported](https://therecord.media/congress-eyes-support-for-cyber-command-suicide-deaths) that Capitol Hill is eyeing Cyber Command’s request as a way to begin addressing the summer’s troubling events.

In their missive, members of the Armed Serves cyber subcommittee noted that during a recent visit to the command they “saw firsthand that the Cyber personnel operate in a uniquely stressful environment” with many personnel “effectively ‘deployed in place’ for two- or three-year assignments, conducting real-world operations against capable adversaries without the clear start and end points, protected recovery periods, or unitlevel reconstitution cycles associated with conventional deployments.”

Those conditions, plus a dearth of access to medical professionals due the secrecy of the command’s mission, “can isolate personnel, limit timely access to appropriate care, and ultimately weaken readiness, retention, and operational effectiveness.”

In their letter, Maryland lawmakers said the recent deaths “have a severe impact on the family, friends, and wider community.”

“The concentration of these tragedies within a small, highly specialized workforce demands sustained attention and an institutional response proportionate to the operational strain these personnel bear.”

Both missives describe the $11 million as a “modest investment” compared to the command’s overall budget.

The House members also argued that the money would ensure the Defense Department’s response is “not limited to short-term crisis intervention after a tragedy, but instead establishes accountable, enduring prevention and care.”

“One loss of life is one too many,” they wrote. “We must do everything we can to make sure our servicemembers and everyone at NSA and Cyber Command has the full mental health support and resources they need, so that a tragedy like this does not occur again.”
