# When No ID Means No Internet: Age Verification and the Right to Access Information

![A person with a yellow umbrella stands beneath clouds with raining eyes. Behind them is a globe.](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/ageverificationworldwide-banner.png?itok=kwTDPjE5 "Age Verification Worldwide")

*This post was co-authored by* [*Sheila B. Lalwani*](https://journalism.utexas.edu/graduate-profiles/sheila-lalwani)*, a doctorate student and recent*[*COMPASS Fellow*](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/research/centers/compass) *hosted by EFF.*

Age verification proposals are [often presented](https://www.eff.org/issues/age-verification) as a simple tradeoff: sacrifice a little privacy to better safeguard children online. But that framing overlooks a more fundamental question. What happens to people who cannot verify their age at all? This is particularly a problem when users are required to prove their age with identity documents.

Age-related restrictions are developing quickly around the world. While they differ radically across jurisdictions, in the past year, we’ve witnessed a sharp uptick toward mandatory age assurance for social media access but also for other high-risk digital services.

For example:

* In late 2025, **Australia** [became the first country](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/12/09/australia/australia-social-media-ban-starts-intl-hnk) to implement a minimum age requirement for prohibiting children under age 16 from creating or holding social media accounts.
* **India** passed the [Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) that includes mandatory verifiable parental consent and has undertaken ongoing discussions for social media restriction.
* **The United Kingdom** passed the *Online Safety Act* [requiring age verification](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/08/no-uks-online-safety-act-doesnt-make-children-safer-online) across a swath of services hosting content considered harmful to users under 18. More than half the states across the **U.S.** [have enacted age verification laws](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/state-age-verification-law-rulings-leave-trail-of-uncertainty).
* An **EU Commission**’s expert [report](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/d833504d-5ec3-4fac-945f-38e7d0bd5326_en?filename=Special-panel-report.pdf) recommends a ban on social media access for users under 13. It also recommends mandatory age verification for platforms to ensure that users are ‘age appropriate.’
