France has turned up the heat on Iran over [destructive cyberattacks](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/13/cyberattacks-crank-french-supermarket-refrigeration-systems-up-to-50c/5302784) targeting refrigeration controllers at more than 700 grocery stores in October, spoiling food and sparking fires. The country vowed to take action using “all available means of response.”

The [supermarket attacks](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/15/researchers-warned-attackers-could-defrost-the-food-months-before-french-supermarkets-got-hit/5303425), which affected Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour, and Intermarché groups, started at least 15 fires and caused food losses in the tens of millions of euros, according to a Monday [report](https://cert.cyber.gouv.fr/cti/CERTFR-2026-CTI-009/) by France’s Cyber Crisis Coordination Centre (C4).

The C4 includes the National Cybersecurity Agency of France, the Directorate General for External Security, the Directorate General for Internal Security, the Directorate General for Armament, and the Cyber Defence Command.

REG AD

“This is an unprecedented campaign in terms of its scale and level of aggressiveness, thus constituting the first cyber offensive campaign carried out purely for the purpose of sabotage on national territory,” France’s Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs said in a [statement](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/presse-et-ressources/decouvrir-et-informer/actualites/declaration-d-attribution-a-l-iran-de-cyberattaques-visant-la-france).

### All signs point to CyberAv3ngers

Both the report and the statement attributed the refrigeration controller hacks to the Iranian Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps’ (IRGC) Shahid Kaveh cyber unit. The Shahid Kaveh unit, which French national security officials say is led by Reza Salarvand and Mahdi Lashgarian, is better known by its hacktivist personas, including [CyberAv3ngers](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/11/29/us-investigates-water-system-cyberattack-linked-to-iran-crew/1153421).
