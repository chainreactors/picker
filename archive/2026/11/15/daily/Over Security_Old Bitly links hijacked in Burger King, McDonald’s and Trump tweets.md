**Update, 15 November, 23:40 UTC:** [CNN reports](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/15/business/political-and-corporate-x-posts-have-links-redirecting-to-vulgar-racist-or-random-websites) redirected Bitly links in old posts from Bill Gates, Justin Trudeau, Mike Pence, the FBI and the US Department of Defense as well. Our third check of the Trump links, at 23:35 UTC, found nothing new: 49 links re-created years after their tweets still work.

**Update, 15 November, 22:30 UTC:** Bitly has confirmed the problem. In a post on X at 19:13 UTC it said it had identified an issue that “allowed bad actors to repurpose certain old, inactive links” and send people to unrelated content. Bitly said it has suspended the accounts involved and is disabling the affected links. It added that its Trust and Safety team is working on protections against similar misuse.

> Earlier today, we identified an issue that allowed bad actors to repurpose certain old, inactive links to redirect people to content unrelated to the original posts. We have suspended the accounts involved and are disabling the affected links. Our Trust and Safety team is working…
>
> — Bitly (@Bitly) [November 15, 2026](https://x.com/Bitly/status/2122045450786152588?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw)

**Update, 15 November, 19:30 UTC:** The Burger King and McDonald’s links no longer redirect. Both were still working at 15:05 UTC and returned Bitly’s “Link Deactivated” page by 19:22 UTC. The same has happened to the two extra Trump links we verified and to six of the seven links in our sample that pointed somewhere new. The seventh now shows a Bitly warning that the account that created it has been suspended. The 49 re-created links that look like the originals still work.

Burger King’s X account was not hacked. A Bitly short link in a tweet the chain posted on 24 July 2011 was registered again ...
