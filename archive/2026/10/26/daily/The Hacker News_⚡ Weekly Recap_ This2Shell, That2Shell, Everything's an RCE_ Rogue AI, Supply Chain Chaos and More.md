This2Shell, That2Shell, Everything2Shell. That's pretty much how the last week went. Critical bugs that let attackers run code remotely are showing up everywhere. Hackers are scanning for targets, some flaws are already being used in attacks, and security teams have more urgent patches than they can keep up with.

And that's not even the full story. A leaked key led to a software supply-chain attack. AI agents are being tricked into following harmful instructions. Old bugs are still being used to break into systems, and some attacks are so simple it's hard to believe they still work. Some websites even went offline to avoid taking chances.

It's been a rough week for anyone trying to keep systems safe. Here's what happened.

## **⚡ Threat of the Week**

**[PHP2Shell Flaws Puts Organizations on Alert](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/php2shell-exploitation-hits-government.html)** — The PHP Foundation released patches for two critical vulnerabilities in PHP, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, that could lead to unauthenticated remote code execution. The security flaws have been codenamed PHP2Shell. Since public disclosure, the shortcomings have witnessed widespread scanning efforts, with Cloudflare stating it detected millions of exploitation attempts after public disclosure. To date, no successful exploitation has been observed. The severity of the flaws, coupled with the [ubiquity of PHP on the web](https://w3techs.com/technologies/details/pl-php), prompted several websites to go offline as a precautionary measure.

## **🔔 Top News**

* **[eMudhra Exits TLS Certificate Market After Compromise of Weak Private keys](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/emudhra-to-exit-global-ssltls-business.html)** — Eight intermediate CA certificates from Indian digital security firm eMudhra were found to use [ridiculously weak private keys](https://emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com/), potentially allowing bad actors to break them and potentially issue trusted certificates for arbitrary domains. Major web browsers stepped in to block the affected CAs. "On 19 October 2026, Apple began blocking these 12 certificates on Apple devices," Apple [said](https://support.apple.com/en-us/149555). "Updates to certificate configuration data are sent to devices automatically and as needed, so you don't need to install a software update to get this change. Apple is making additional updates to this block, which will also be delivered automatically. On 23 October 2026, Apple also began blocking the emSign ECC Root CA - G3." Python has also [released](https://discuss.python.org/t/information-for-python-users-regarding-emudhra-emsign-ca-certificate-security-incident/) updated versions of the packages certifi, pip, uv, sigstore, conda, and pixi to remove the emSign root CA certificate from CA bundles. The development prompted eMudhra to exit the certificate-issuing business.
* **[Critical Flaw in Brotli Fixed](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/brotli-decoder-flaw-behind-nodejs-rce.html)** — A severe vulnerability has been addressed in Google's Brotli decoder library (CVE-2026-111497) that could lead to unauthenticated remote code execution. The flaw, also called Node2Shell and HTTP2Shell, has also been addressed in other popular libraries that rely on Brotli, including libbrotli, Python, C++, Node.js, Bun, Envoy, and Istio. The disclosure comes as Ruby released versions 4.0.9, 3.4.12, and 3.3.13 to [address](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/ruby-fixes-three-regex-engine-bugs.html) several bugs in Ruby's regular expression engine, Onigmo (CVE-2026-89078, CVE-2026-93577, CVE-2026-97229, and CVE-2026-110264) that could be used for remote code execution on GitLab servers.
* **[Two CVSS 9.0 Linux KSMBD RCE Flaws Disclosed Ahead of Fixes](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/two-cvss-90-linux-ksmbd-rce-flaws.html)** — Linux servers running KSMBD, the kernel's built-in SMB server, are exposed to two publicly reported remote code execution flaws tracked as ZDI-CAN-33774 and ZDI-CAN-33549, along with a local privilege escalation flaw referred to as ZDI-CAN-33367. None of the vulnerabilities has been assigned a CVE ID, and no in-the-wild exploitation has been reported. "Affected organizations should immediately restrict unprivileged user namespaces and enforce RuntimeDefault seccomp where they can, find out whether ksmbd is running anywhere in their estate, and patch ZDI-CAN-33367 the day their distribution ships the fix," Sysdig said.
* **[Plex2Shell Flaws Exploited in the Wild](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/hackers-exploit-plex2shell-flaws-to.html)** — Four vulnerabilities in Plex Media Server, CVE-2026-96652, CVE-2026-96654, CVE-2026-96655, and CVE-2026-96656, are being chained into an exploit called Plex2Shell to deliver cryptocurrency miners and proxy software.
