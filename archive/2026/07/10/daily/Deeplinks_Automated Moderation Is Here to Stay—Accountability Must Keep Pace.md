*This post is part 2 in a series about automated content moderation. Read the first post [here](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/07/part-1-automated-moderation-here-stay).*

When whistleblower Frances Haugen leaked a set of documents from Meta in 2020, among the revelations was a jarring statistic: The company’s algorithms designed to detect terrorist content incorrectly [deleted nonviolent Arabic-language content](https://www.politico.eu/article/facebook-content-moderation-posts-wars-afghanistan-middle-east-arabic/) 77 percent of the time, while [failing to detect hate speech](https://www.isdglobal.org/media-mentions/the-facebook-papers-hate-speech-filters-through-in-middle-east-thanks-to-lack-of-arabic-language-monitoring-and-faulty-ai-tech/) under the company’s own policies in many instances. Meta’s own transparency report released later that year [demonstrated similar findings](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2020/10/facebooks-most-recent-transparency-report-demonstrates-pitfalls-automated-content). Five years later, researchers in the region report that [overzealous moderation remains a problem](https://7amleh.org/post/meta-suppression-of-palestinian-content-en), while paths to remedy have all but collapsed.
