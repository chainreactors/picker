# [U.S. Treasury's Financial Crimes Agency Takes Some Systems Offline After Cyber Intrusion](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/us-treasurys-fincen-takes-some-systems.html)

**Swati Khandelwal**Nov 25, 2026Financial Security / Cybersecurity

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgJn89Gr-RFxS6UFEzrbUZ5Ys11fK1OwvmMYUPl29eJudnlmmp7Q34p8O0DqV8SvGu4whZke8tiysvZNQWeE4Tm3zRhJSpojYAagBXGUEVM5w6lAw2Z5C-lfRaC2IwOYrnwQSWxwso31woM4CSoluNEjA3N0gFe0RQpsV0r3MCobiKFArLMa1or-QTIBcQ/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/fincern.jpg)

The U.S. Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (**FinCEN**) has been hit by a cybersecurity incident that resulted in "unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems," the bureau said on Tuesday. As a precaution, it has taken some systems offline.

The bureau collects suspicious activity reports and other Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) filings from banks and other financial institutions and analyzes them to fight money laundering and terrorist financing.

The BSA E-Filing website is still up, although some services may be temporarily unavailable. The agency has not named the systems it reached or those it took offline.

To support filers affected by the disruption, the bureau is extending some filing deadlines: covered reports originally due between November 18 and December 29 can now be submitted until December 30.

A [relief order](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/100/ORDER-FIN-2026-R002.pdf) issued the same day, FIN-2026-R002, applies to three groups:

* Financial institutions that used Secure Direct Transfer Mode (SDTM), the channel for sending batches of BSA reports directly from their own servers, as of November 18
* Companies required to file a beneficial ownership information report during the covered window
* Individuals required to update a FinCEN Identifier during the same window

All other Bank Secrecy Act obligations stay in force, including the duty to monitor for suspicious transactions. The [service alert](https://bsaefiling.fincen.gov/) says urgent suspicious activity information can still reach law enforcement through the Financial Institutions Hotline, 1-866-556-3974, which is not affected by the disruption.

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/threat-protection-d)

The disclosure followed days of filing trouble. Filing providers reported failures in automated batch submissions beginning November 18, and the bureau posted a notice about the disruption to SDTM late last week, according to a [filing outages report](https://thedeepdive.ca/treasurys-financial-crimes-watchdog-confirms-unauthorized-access-after-days-of-bank-filing-outages/) by the markets news site The Deep Dive.

The bureau has not said whether the intrusion caused those outages. The order grants the relief because of a "service disruption" and does not mention the cybersecurity incident.

Nor has the agency said when the access began, whether any data was viewed or taken, or who was responsible. No group has publicly claimed the intrusion so far. Those answers matter because of what the bureau holds: reports of suspicious financial activity filed by financial institutions nationwide.

The incident is not Treasury's first in recent years: in December 2024, a China-linked group used a compromised BeyondTrust API key in an [earlier Treasury breach](https://thehackernews.com/2024/12/chinese-apt-exploits-beyondtrust-api.html) that reached department systems and documents. No link between the two incidents has been reported.
