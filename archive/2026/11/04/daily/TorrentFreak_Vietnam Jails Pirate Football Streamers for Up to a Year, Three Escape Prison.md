# Vietnam Jails Pirate Football Streamers for Up to a Year, Three Escape Prison

today by
[Ernesto Van der Sar](https://torrentfreak.com/author/ernesto/)

[Home](https://torrentfreak.com "Go to TorrentFreak.") > [Piracy](https://torrentfreak.com/category/piracy/ "Go to the Piracy category archives.") >

A Vietnamese court has sentenced 14 people who worked on the pirate football streaming site Bún Chả TV, handing out the first prison terms we've seen since the country's piracy crackdown began earlier this year. Eleven defendants received sentences ranging from eight to twelve months, while three commentators got suspended sentences. The alleged organizer of the group does not appear to be among those sentenced.

![buncha TV logo](https://torrentfreak.com/images/bunchalogo.png)
In May, the U.S. Trade Representative opened a [Section 301 investigation](https://torrentfreak.com/vietnams-online-piracy-failures-trigger-section-301-investigation-tariffs-on-the-table/) into Vietnam, arguing that the country’s piracy prosecutions lacked a deterrent effect.

USTR pointed to recent cases in which defendants received suspended sentences and relatively low fines. A few weeks earlier, Vietnam’s Prime Minister had ordered a nationwide crackdown on copyright infringement. Several criminal cases have since moved forward, including those against the alleged operators of [HiAnime](https://torrentfreak.com/vietnam-indicts-hianime-operators-over-12-85-million-in-pirate-ad-revenue/) and the [Xoi Lac TV](https://torrentfreak.com/as-us-trade-pressure-mounts-vietnam-prepares-to-prosecute-74-xoi-lac-sports-pirates/) sports streaming network.

Another site in the crosshairs was Bún Chả TV, which offered Premier League and Champions League streams with Vietnamese commentary. According to police, the site drew hundreds of thousands of visits a day.

Investigators described the site as an underground company, with separate teams for programming, commentary, SEO and accounting. The main revenue came from ads and from redirecting viewers to illegal gambling sites.

On May 15, police from several provinces moved in. They summoned suspects, searched homes and workplaces, and seized 38 computers and 42 phones. Less than two weeks later, Vietnam’s Ministry of Public Security announced that Bac Ninh police had charged ten people.

## Up to a Year in Prison

That case has now reached a verdict. In a first-instance ruling reported last week, a court in Bac Ninh convicted 14 defendants of infringing copyright and related rights under Article 225 of Vietnam’s Penal Code, [VTC News](https://vtcnews.vn/14-bi-cao-van-hanh-website-bong-da-lau-bun-cha-tv-linh-an-ar1048098.html) reports.

Four defendants, including the heads of the content, programming and operations teams, were sentenced to 12 months in prison. Five others received ten months, and two were sentenced to eight months.

Notably, three of the site’s commentators were not sent to prison. Their ten-month sentences were suspended.

All 14 defendants reportedly admitted the charges and cooperated with the authorities. According to investigators, Bún Chả TV streamed more than 150,000 sports matches and it allegedly made more than 20 billion dong, roughly $760,000, in illegal profits from betting and gambling ads.

The verdict reports don’t mention a total. Instead, they list illegal gains per person or team. The leader of the content team allegedly gained more than 430 million dong, while commentators made from more than 31 million to more than 480 million dong each.

## ‘Strong’ Penalties

The verdict was picked up by [Rouse](https://rouse.com/), the law firm that represents the Motion Picture Association in Vietnam. In an [article](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=32d2aa09-6752-42ad-b2f5-0e0adfae809b) headlined “Strong Vietnam criminal penalties,” published on Lexology on November 3, the firm calls the ruling another victory for Vietnam’s IP landscape.

“The defendants were all sentenced to terms of 8-12 months in prison,” Rouse writes.

*From Rouse’s article*
![lexology rouse's article](https://torrentfreak.com/images/lexologyrouse.png)

While Rouse is correct on the length of the terms, it doesn’t mention that three of the sentences were suspended. Instead, Rouse stresses that the ruling is part of a tougher enforcement trend.

“This ruling reflects a shift in enforcement strategy by Vietnamese authorities, who are increasingly moving past preliminary domain blocking to deliver criminal convictions against the operators of commercial-scale piracy rings,” the firm notes.

“This perhaps reflects the strength of the national crackdown following the USTR pressure in May,” the firm adds.

## Organizer Not on Trial?

The 14 defendants worked on the site, but according to the indictment reported by VTC News, the operation was directed by Bùi Bá Hiến, who manages and runs a company called S24. Hiến allegedly instructed employees and hired collaborators to build and run Bún Chả TV.

Hiến is not among the 14 defendants named in the reports, as far as we can see. The reports don’t name any of the impacted rightsholders either.

Interestingly, in September, Bac Ninh police issued a wanted notice for a man of the same name, Bùi Bá Hiến, CEO of esports company Box Vietnam. This person is also charged under the same copyright statute.

Police said [this man fled](https://vnexpress.net/ceo-cua-box-viet-nam-bi-truy-na-vi-xam-pham-ban-quyen-5120802.html) on May 13, two days before the Bún Chả TV raids, but none of the reports we’ve seen confirm that this esports executive is the same person that’s named in the Bún Chả TV indictment.

The USTR has until November 29 to make its Section 301 determination, although it can extend the investigation by three months if needed. On paper, Vietnamese law allows for sentences up to three years for copyright infringement. Whether the 8-12 month sentences are what Washington had in mind when it called for more deterrent punishments has yet to be seen.
