LearnWorlds, a software and learning course platform, says an attacker sent 13,000 phishing emails to customers over seven days by using automated tools to open free trial accounts. The company insists its systems were not breached.

Panos Siozos, Co-founder and CEO at LearnWorlds, said the miscreant created around 570 free trial accounts on the platform between November 7-14, which they used to issue fake copyright infringement notices to customers.

"We have found no evidence of unauthorized access to existing customer schools or personal data held for our customers and their end users," he told The Register.

REG AD

According to Siozos, the attacker bypassed the platform's per-customer limit on trial subscriptions and exploited its welcome email feature to send the phishing emails.

REG AD

"In each trial they added their own list of target addresses and rewrote the standard welcome email as a fake copyright notice," he said. "Adding those users triggered the welcome email from our sending address."

The first email was sent on November 7, after which the company detected the suspicious signups and blocked the first sender on November 9, although the attacker returned to send a second wave of emails within 40 minutes.

LearnWorlds removed all the trial accounts by November 10 and one paid account, which was used to launch more emails, on November 14.

 "In total, around 13,000 emails were sent to addresses on external recipient lists supplied by the sender," said Siozos. "The trial accounts sent 9,853 between November 7-9, and the paid account sent 3,723 on November 14.

"We can’t tell how many people clicked, because the link was disguised to hide clicks. The recipient addresses were the sender’s own list."

The CEO confirmed that LearnWorlds has since enhanced its bot checks and is placing holds on new accounts if their emails trigger fraud flags.

"We have also examined the novel patterns the attackers used to create more advanced scoring mechanisms. We have reported the phishing website so it can be taken down. Further measures are currently being examined.

"Anyone who signed in on the fake page should change their Google password and review their account’s recent security activity."
