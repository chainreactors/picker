# Dozens of credit unions hit by supplier breach that went unnoticed for six weeks

*Updated at 2:30pm EST with comments from Wellington IT.*

Payment details belonging to members of more than 40 Irish credit unions have been accessed by hackers who exploited a vulnerability in the systems of software supplier Wellington IT.

Ireland’s Data Protection Commission told Recorded Future News on Thursday it had received more than 40 breach notifications from credit unions that used the Belfast-based company as a data processor.

With a customer base in Ireland and Wellington IT’s headquarters in Northern Ireland, a part of the United Kingdom, the incident straddles two jurisdictions.

The U.K.’s Information Commissioner’s Office told Recorded Future News it had not received a report from Wellington IT as of Thursday, but was “starting to see breach reports come through from Credit Unions,” suggesting the incident may also affect credit union members in the U.K.

Colin Mitchell, CEO of Wellington IT, said the firm immediately alerted customers of the cybersecurity incident, and has taken steps to address the issue. "We are responding to customer inquiries as quickly as possible and we are collaborating fully with both regulators and law enforcement including the Data Protection Commissioner (DPC), the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), and the Garda National Cyber Crime Bureau (GNCCB)," he said.

"We are directly supporting our customers in their assessment of the potential impact on affected individuals and to help them meet any applicable regulatory obligations... We remain focused on completing a thorough investigation and communicating directly with our customers as the investigation progresses.”

Ireland’s Minister of State, Robert Troy, said in a [statement](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-finance/press-releases/statement-from-minister-of-state-robert-troy-following-meeting-with-wellington-it/) the incident only affected payee details stored by members using credit union mobile banking apps operated by Wellington IT. Members who did not use the apps were unaffected, he said.

“At present, there is no evidence that members’ funds have been accessed or that any fraudulent activity involving members’ accounts has occurred,” said Troy.

The attack occurred in early October, David Malone, the chief executive of the Irish League of Credit Unions, [told](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1119/1599774-credit-union-breach/) Ireland’s national broadcaster RTÉ.

Wellington said it closed the unidentified vulnerability itself on October 6, but that its initial investigation “did not identify that there was a data protection breach,” Malone said. The affected credit unions were not notified until November 16.

Robert Troy had previously described the delay as “simply not acceptable.” On Thursday, he said Wellington had brought in outside experts to determine “the exact number of accounts and users affected,” stressing that any information the company released publicly must be “accurate and verified.”

Wellington chief executive Colin Mitchell said the company notified customers “as soon as we confirmed the potential risk to customer data.”

Ireland’s National Cyber Security Centre said there was “no indication that member funds have been compromised or that unauthorised transactions have occurred.”
