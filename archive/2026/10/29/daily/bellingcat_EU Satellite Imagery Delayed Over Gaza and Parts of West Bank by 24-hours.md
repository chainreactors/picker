# EU Satellite Imagery Delayed Over Gaza and Parts of West Bank by 24-hours

October 29, 2026

* [Gaza](/tag/gaza)
* [Middle East](/tag/middle-east)

Imagery captured by the European Space Agency’s freely available Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 satellites appears to be delayed over Gaza and parts of the West Bank, a Bellingcat analysis has found.

While the delay remains relatively short at just 24 hours, this can still impact the speed at which analysts and researchers can observe what is happening in the region.

For example, the Israeli Defence Forces [demolished homes](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cqd09k1k7532o) in the West Bank Palestinian city of Hebron in recent days. New imagery captured on October 26, which included Hebron, would allow open source researchers to quickly confirm the demolitions, assess the impacted area and observe any unreported IDF activity. But imagery for the western half of Hebron was not available until October 27 due to the Sentinel-2 delay, inhibiting real-time fact-checking and reporting on this evolving situation.

A spokesperson for the European Union told Bellingcat that the delay was not related to events in Gaza but to “mitigate possible threats” due to the war in the Middle East “including Iran’s attacks on its neighbours and possible threats to European interests”. The delayed region of Sentinel tiles that cover Gaza as well as parts of Israel and the West Bank also extends over the Eastern Mediterranean Sea.

The European Union ordered a 24-hour delay on Sentinel satellite imagery over the Gulf of Oman earlier this year during US and Israeli strikes on Iran. However the delay impacting Gaza and parts of the West Bank has not been announced or previously reported.

“Such decisions are taken unanimously by all EU Member states and will stay in place as long as necessary,” the spokesperson said. They also said that while the Copernicus program, which includes the Sentinel satellites, operated a free and open data policy there were limitations to this for security.

When asked if the delay, which began in late March, was related to the presence of US vessels in the Eastern Mediterranean or if there were other areas of delay, the spokesperson said they had no further comment.

**Sentinel Delays**

The Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 satellites provide an [invaluable source of imagery](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2024/05/17/how-to-use-free-satellite-imagery-to-monitor-the-expansion-of-west-bank-settlements/) for open source researchers. This has been especially true this year, as commercial satellite imagery providers [restricted access to imagery](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c5y721yqe6ro) in the Middle East, including areas of [active human rights concern, such as Gaza](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/us-satellite-imagery-restrictions-leave-journalists-dark-iran-and-gaza).

However, in July, the European Union published Council Decision [2026/1716](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32026D1716), which [ordered a 24-hour delay on Sentinel imagery](https://spacenews.com/eu-delays-release-of-copernicus-imagery-over-gulf-of-oman/) over the Gulf of Oman, after a request from the US government which was involved in strikes on Iran at that time.

Just two weeks later, the European Union issued a [corrigendum](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2026/1716/corrigendum/2026-07-29/oj/eng) that unpublished decision 2026/1716 and removed it from the Council of the European Union record, although satellite data shows the delay has remained.

This decision, while it was available, was [reportedly](https://www.facebook.com/policyjournal.gr/posts/pfbid02bK1gEVnr39fC9BNBsZpJQ35T8HeoFjMty4prMMaqfTZZWjSx6wgAT6c13VbcpFHkl) the first public notification of delayed Sentinel imagery. Yet it was not the first time that Sentinel imagery has been restricted, analysis shows. Bellingcat found four distinct regions of delayed satellite imagery during 2025 and 2026: the Gulf of Oman, the Gulf of Aden/western Arabian Sea, the Red Sea as well as the previously unreported delay in the Eastern Mediterranean Sea and parts of Israel, the West Bank and the entirety of Gaza.

![](https://www.bellingcat.com/app/uploads/2026/10/Screenshot-2026-10-29-at-10.27.33-AM-1200x1076.jpg)

Bellingcat’s analysis shows the regions where Sentinel-2 imagery is subject to a 24-hour delay, and when the delay was imposed. Only the July 15, 2026 delay over the Gulf of Oman was referenced by European Commission directives and the region referenced in 2026/1716 encompasses only a portion of the delayed tiles.

Bellingcat’s analysis shows that these delays have been implemented in stages. Twenty-four hour delays of Sentinel-2 imagery were [first observed by open source researchers](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/eu-satellites) in March and April 2025 after the US launched a [naval and air strike campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March%E2%80%93May_2025_United_States_attacks_in_Yemen) against Houthi targets in Yemen.

The previously unreported imagery delay in the Eastern Mediterranean began on March 28, 2026, the same month that commercial American space imaging company Planet Labs [also restricted imagery](https://x.com/gbrumfiel/status/2031152197191311554) across the Middle East. The Gulf of Oman delay began on July 15, 2026, two days after the publication of Council Decision 2026/1716.

Decision 2026/1716 also [specified](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32026D1716) that the delay be implemented for both Sentinel-2 and [Sentinel-1 radar imagery](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1). Bellingcat’s analysis of Sentinel-1 image availability shows that this is the case for both the Gulf of Oman region and the unreported region over the Eastern Mediterranean Sea. However, the 2025 restrictions on Sentinel-2 imagery in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden are not implemented in Sentinel-1.

Sentinel-1 imagery uses “synthetic aperture radar” to capture lower resolution radar images that can collect data at night and on cloudy days. This differs from Sentinel-2, which captures optical satellite imagery at higher resolution.
