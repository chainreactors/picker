## Executive intelligence assessment

This reporting cycle produced several consequential developments across HUMINT, cyberespionage, covert action, military procurement, and counterintelligence. The clearest emerging pattern is the **conversion of civilian access, commercial infrastructure, and recruited intermediaries into intelligence and sabotage capabilities**.

Romania formally attributed the September arson attack near the CARFIL defense manufacturer in Brașov to a network coordinated from Russia. German authorities disclosed a related investigation involving two Belarusian suspects accused of preparing additional sabotage in Bavaria. Separately, Lithuanian police identified suspects linked to Starlink infrastructure attacks in Lithuania and Bulgaria, with investigators examining connections to the Polish case. These investigations substantially strengthen the picture of a cross-border sabotage ecosystem, although they do not establish that all operations share the same handlers. ## Russia: Romanian intelligence attributes CARFIL-area arson to Russia-coordinated sabotage network

26–30 October | Romania, Germany, Poland | HUMINT, covert action, military-industrial targeting

Romania’s domestic intelligence service, SRI, announced on October 26 that a network coordinated from the Russian Federation deliberately started the September 14 fire at an industrial site on the CARFIL platform in Brașov. The site is adjacent to CARFIL SA, a ROMARM subsidiary associated with weapons and ammunition manufacturing.

Romanian prosecutors identified three suspects, two Belarusian nationals and one Ukrainian national. The two Belarusian men were detained in Germany, while the Ukrainian suspect was located in Poland. Romania is pursuing their transfer through judicial proceedings. On October 29, the European Union formally condemned the sabotage at the OSCE, citing Romania’s attribution. [EEAS](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/vienna-international-organisations/eu-statement-current-issue-raised-romania-sabotage-act-brasov-romania-september-14-2026-osce_en?utm_source=chatgpt.com)[Euronews România](https://www.euronews.ro/articole/sri-avertizeaza-ca-o-retea-de-sabotaj-coordonata-din-rusia-ar-fi-provocat-incendi)[Știrile ProTV](https://stirileprotv.ro/stiri/actualitate/sri-incendiul-de-platforma-industriala-carfil-un-sabotaj-al-rusiei.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The investigation also extends into Germany. Munich prosecutors disclosed that the two Belarusian men, aged 39 and 50, allegedly planned an incendiary attack against a building or critical infrastructure in the Nuremberg area in exchange for payment. They have been in custody since October 7. German prosecutors initially described an unnamed foreign sponsor; *Der Spiegel* reported a Russian connection, while Romania’s SRI made an explicit Russia-coordinated attribution in the Brașov investigation. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/german-police-arrest-two-suspected-planning-sabotage-attacks-russia-spiegel-says-2026-10-28/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)[tagesschau.de](https://www.tagesschau.de/inland/bayern-ermittlungen-gegen-belarusen-sabotage-100.html)

**Tradecraft and objective.** The apparent operational model involves recruitment of third-country residents, travel between EU states, reconnaissance of industrial targets, and paid arson. The probable objective is disruption or intimidation of European defense-support infrastructure. A precise Russian service or command hierarchy has not been publicly identified.

**Veracity:** High confidence in the fire, arrests, and cross-border investigations. High-moderate confidence in Russian coordination based on SRI’s official assessment and the EU’s subsequent statement. The accused remain unconvicted.

**Deception assessment:** Moderate risk of competing attribution narratives. Russia denies involvement. The use of Belarusian and Ukrainian suspects could complicate attribution, but their nationalities alone neither establish nor disprove Russian sponsorship.

**Primary source:** [EU statement to the OSCE, 30 October](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/vienna-international-organisations/eu-statement-current-issue-raised-romania-sabotage-act-brasov-romania-september-14-2026-osce_en).
