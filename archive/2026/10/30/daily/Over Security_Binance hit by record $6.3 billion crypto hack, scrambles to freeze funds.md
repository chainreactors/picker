# Binance hit by record $6.3 billion crypto hack, scrambles to freeze funds

Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency platform, said hackers stole $6.3 billion worth of funds in a Thursday evening attack that likely amounts to the largest crypto theft in history.

Multiple blockchain security firms said the stolen funds were being routed through addresses previously tied to North Korean hackers who have repeatedly stolen billions worth of cryptocurrency from platforms over the last five years.

In a post mortem, Binance [traced](https://x.com/binance/status/2115993361517891592) the breach back to a compromised laptop owned by an engineer at the company. The compromised laptop allowed the attacker to gain access to systems that enabled unauthorized withdrawal transactions, Binance said.

While $6.3 billion was stolen, about $3 billion has been [frozen](https://x.com/arbitrum/status/2115961730920841644) by other crypto platforms that the hackers tried to use to launder the funds.

Tether, Circle, Ripple, World Liberty Financial and Arbitrum all joined forces to help Binance and freeze the funds but the attackers [quickly sold](https://x.com/dicksonwuml/status/2115960482825658847) stablecoins and took out loans against other stolen funds, leaving them with more than $2.4 billion in Ethereum and Bitcoin.

Binance officials pledged to cover all losses using company funds and reiterated that user funds are safe.

The company suspended deposits and withdrawals while also contacting law enforcement agencies and blockchain security companies to trace and recover the stolen funds.

“User funds are safe. Binance will cover all hacking losses. Deposits and withdrawals are temporarily suspended while our team conducts security checks, freezes funds, and works with law enforcement,” Binance co-founder Yi He [said](https://x.com/heyibinance/status/2115958049244631357), adding that the withdrawal suspension would last 48 hours.

Blockchain security companies [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korea-steals-usd-6-36-billion-from-binance-in-the-largest-hack-in-history) and [Chainalysis](https://x.com/chainalysis/status/2116025255965999217) both pointed the finger at North Korea’s TraderTraitor hacking group.

TraderTraitor has been repeatedly spotlighted [by the FBI](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor) and [experts](https://x.com/mattiascaricato/status/2115966143127339469) as the most common culprit behind several high-profile cryptocurrency thefts. According to the FBI, the group was responsible for stealing [$1.4 billion](https://therecord.media/north-koreans-initial-laundering-bybit-hack) from Dubai-based crypto platform Bybit last year and [nearly $300 million](https://therecord.media/crypto-north-korea-theft-kelp) from another crypto company called KelpDAO in April 2026.

Last month, crypto platform Bitget said North Korean hackers [stole more than $387 million](https://therecord.media/crypto-ceo-accuses-north-korea-of-387-million-theft) in a cyberattack. Chainalysis said on Thursday evening that funds from the Binance attack were seen commingling in wallets with cryptocurrency stolen from Bitget.

Chainalysis said it has a “high degree of confidence that DPRK” was responsible for the Binance attack. In its own blog TRM Labs also attributed the theft to TraderTraitor “based on robust on and off-chain intelligence.”

“Several features of this attack match earlier North Korea-linked heists, including the Bybit hack and the KelpDAO hack in April 2026: small Tornado Cash withdrawals funding an operator wallet, staging that began well before the theft, and signatures obtained in advance then executed in one burst,” TRM Labs said, noting that evidence showed preparations for the attack began 36 hours before.

North Korea has waged an unprecedented assault on the crypto industry for years, stealing vast sums each year that U.S. officials say is used to fund Pyongyang’s government and military weapons program.

The country’s government stole [more than $2 billion in similar attacks last year](https://therecord.media/over-3-billion-crypto-stolen-2025-north-korea) and brought in $3 billion from attacks between 2017 and 2023, [according to United Nations investigators](https://therecord.media/north-korea-cryptocurrency-hacks-un-experts).

The FBI [previously said](https://therecord.media/north-korea-lazarus-behind-crypto-heists) TraderTraitor was behind multiple headline-grabbing incidents in [2023](https://therecord.media/millions-stolen-in-atomic-wallet-attack) and [2024](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor) as well as [the $100 million hack](https://therecord.media/north-korea-accused-of-orchestrating-100-million-harmony-crypto-hack/) of Harmony’s Horizon bridge and the [$600 million hack of Sky Mavis’ Ronin Bridge](https://therecord.media/more-than-625-million-stolen-in-defi-hack-of-ronin-network/).

On Thursday, Microsoft [published](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/29/tracking-ai-adoption-in-north-korean-threat-actor-tradecraft/) a lengthy investigation into North Korea’s use of artificial intelligence in cyberattacks. The company said North Korean hackers are using AI to create front companies, generate tailored phishing lures and more. Some North Korean groups were also seen leveraging [controversial AI penetration testing tool ARTEX](https://therecord.media/china-cyber-connectwise-ai) to conduct operations.

Microsoft said these operations “commonly target cryptocurrency and financial organizations, information technology companies and software supply chains, government entities, and education-related organizations.”
