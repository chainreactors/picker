# [Help TDS Linked to Hotejka as 20,000+ Cloudflare Pages Domains Host Tech Support Scams](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/help-tds-linked-to-hotejka-as-20000.html)

**Ravie Lakshmanan**Oct 15, 2026Cybercrime / Web Security

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgp2enQeK-5xZuEo_rLQje9W_odynVLALJCEvTyaMSolIfwVonZGMceQWA8yq9xPMD-C_RkIp-V03EqEBVTM5yMq9IHWRRfSHxWWDS6kCJVcVAsmlFXt5wD2pK7Bk-A6iBIDFS2o811n8e48B1Psni_qvrkJ0HMXtJlhaLwx7jkxEG-e0XDgtcAMUMLiNFV/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/hot.jpg)

A long-running Traffic Distribution System (TDS) known as Help TDS to a Russian-language affiliate program called **Hotejka**, which appears to be a rebrand of a traffic monetization operation dubbed ROI777.

Help TDS is assessed to be active since at least 2017, offering PHP code templates to partners and affiliates that are installed into compromised websites with an aim to direct unsuspecting users to tech support scams. These scams make use of full-screen browser manipulation and exit prevention techniques to serve bogus Microsoft Windows security alert screens instructing victims to contact a Windows Support number to address the problem.

"Help TDS delivers tech support scams (TSSs) that direct users to call centers, where social engineering is used to convince them to install remote desktop tools," Infoblox [said](https://www.infoblox.com/blog/threat-intelligence/inside-help-tds-hotejka-cloudflare-pages-and-predictive-detection/) in a report shared with The Hacker News. "It's not simple credit card fraud: it's remote access, living-off-the-land malware operations. Once in, the threat actor can exploit the victim time and again."

Help TDS's evolution was [documented](https://thehackernews.com/2025/08/shadowcaptcha-exploits-wordpress-sites.html) by GoDaddy in August 2025, highlighting the threat actor's use of a malicious WordPress plugin named "woocommerce\_inputs" on thousands of websites to steal WordPress user credentials and facilitate the malicious redirections by fetching the URLs from its command-and-control (C2) servers. The plugin, or alternatively a custom HTML block, is injected into the sites by Hotejka's affiliates.

Historical tracking of malicious redirect chains has also uncovered that Help TDS has routinely [handed off traffic](https://thehackernews.com/2025/06/wordpress-sites-turned-weapon-how.html) directly to [VexTrio Viper](https://thehackernews.com/2024/01/vextrio-uber-of-cybercrime-brokering.html), a cybercrime operator that runs an affiliate program to enable scams, riskware, spyware, adware, and pornographic content.

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/growth-ai-control-d)

The latest findings connect the TDS to Hotejka, which, in turn, is suspected to be a successor to ROI777, an entity that was exposed by Guardicore Labs (now part of Akamai) in June 2018 in connection with a traffic manipulation and cryptocurrency mining campaign codenamed [Operation Prowli](https://www.akamai.com/blog/security/operation-prowli-traffic-manipulation-cryptocurrency-mining).

The activity involved the compromise of vulnerable servers to deploy a malware named r2r2 and then leverage them to mine cryptocurrency. In tandem, WordPress sites and servers running other content management systems (CMS) were exploited for traffic monetization fraud.

"Traffic monetizers, such as ROI777, buy traffic from 'website operators' such as the Prowli attackers and redirect it to domains on demand," Guardicore Labs noted at the time. "Website 'operators' earn money per traffic sent through roi777. The destination domains frequently host different scams, such as fake services, malicious browser extensions and more."

The rebrand is believed to be driven by the leak of data obtained from ROI777's servers in March 2018, around when it was operating Disposable TDS, a precursor to Help TDS. The published details included [Telegram identities of its users](https://pastebin.com/96N1bg72) and specifics about [multiple campaigns](https://pastebin.com/hkhRi8GB).

The connection between Hotejka and ROI77 doesn't end there, for GoDaddy also [found](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/news/help-tds-malicious-plugins-redirect-tech-support-scams) that "[roi777[.]com](https://urlscan.io/result/3f166d52-7b13-4b44-8681-cb52b1e3adbf/#transactions)" was one of the historical C2 domains used between 2018 and 2020 to fetch the redirect domains.

"[Hotejka's] affiliates range from spam operators to website compromisers," Infoblox said. "Potential victims are redirected into the TDS from these affiliates via special URLs provided by Hotejka. The TDS then evaluates the user via browser fingerprinting and location to determine the course of action."

A TDS functions akin to a relay, intercepting internet traffic from compromised websites, malicious ads, social media posts, or SEO spam, analyzing the visitor, filter traffic based on predefined criteria (e.g., geographic location, device metadata, IP reputation, and headless browsers), and dynamically route them to scams, browser lockers, or malware drops.

"Help TDS and its precursors are the routing decision infrastructure for a small affiliate program," Infoblox explained. "Other threat actors, for example ones who have compromised websites, send potential victim traffic to the TDS through specially crafted URLs."
