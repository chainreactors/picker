The Everest extortion group posted Brenner Base Tunnel SE, the Austrian-Italian company building the 64-kilometre rail tunnel under the Brenner Pass, on its leak site on 17 October 2026 with a claim of 812 GB of stolen data. No news outlet reported it for 20 days. The Tiroler Tageszeitung broke the story on 6 November, and BBT SE confirmed the next day that attackers had reached part of its internet-accessible data in mid-October.

Our [Ransomtracker](https://ransomnews.com/ransomtracker/) recorded the entry at 04:31 UTC on 17 October, in the same batch as two Japanese victims that had already told the public they were breached. BBT SE had not. The company says construction is unaffected and no high-security data was touched; it will not say whether Everest has contacted it, and nothing on the record shows encryption.

### In brief

* Everest listed “Brenner Base Tunnel” on 17 October 2026 and claims 812 GB. The volume has not been verified, and Everest has not said what the files are.
* BBT SE confirmed a targeted cyberattack on 7 November, after the Tiroler Tageszeitung reported it on 6 November. It says the attack took place in mid-October, that the attackers gained partial access to data reachable from the internet, and that the affected systems were isolated and the authorities informed.
* The company says the construction of the tunnel was not affected and high-security data was not involved. It declined to comment on whether it is negotiating with the attackers.
* Everest put 26 organisations on its leak site in October, its busiest month on the Ransomtracker since February. Kissei Shoji and Chugoku Bend, posted within hours of BBT SE, had both disclosed their incidents before the listing.
* The Alerts.bar index that Ransomnews licenses holds nine infostealer-captured credentials tied to bbt-se.com, five from the company’s own login pages and four from staff accounts, plus 237 records in credential compilations.
