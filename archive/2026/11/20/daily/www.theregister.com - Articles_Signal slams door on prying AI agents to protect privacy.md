Signal, the encrypted messaging app, has barred Google Gemini, Apple Siri, and other AI agents from accessing messages unless the user has explicitly granted permission, based on privacy concerns.

Citing the screenshot blocking implemented last year to defend against Microsoft Recall, Signal CEO Meredith Whittaker said similar measures have become necessary to prevent agentic software available in iOS and Android from gaining access to communication that's intended to be private.

"Google and Apple are integrating agentic capabilities like [Gemini Intelligence](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15850607?hl=en) with Google Gemini and [Onscreen Awareness](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/) with Siri AI deep into their operating systems," wrote Whittaker in a [blog post](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/).

REG AD

"In order to gain access to information about you, these OS AI agents 'scan' the information that’s on your screen. In doing so, your Signal data is captured, then made available to these agentic systems in order to allow them to answer your questions and do your bidding."

REG AD

The result is that private messages ingested by these agentic systems may end up on someone else's server where they're no longer private.

Updates to Signal's iOS and Android apps now include controls that by default block Siri on iOS and Gemini and other agents on Android from taking screenshots, which AI agents process using optical character recognition to extract text.

Whittaker says that those who wish to take screenshots of their messages can disable this protection via Signal's settings menu, but warns doing so risks exposing your Signal content (and that sent by your contacts) to AI agents.

Screenshot blocking, Whittaker concedes, is only a partial solution since AI agents may have other ways to access application data through the device file system.

"We want to be very clear: it is a serious problem that Signal – and application developers beyond – lack robust tools to prevent AI agents from undermining application-level privacy," she said.

"Even where we are able to detect and block AI data access, Google, Apple, Microsoft, and others do not provide ways for developers to easily restrict such AI data access without making drastic compromises to the core user experience. This is also the reason we are currently unable to implement these protections on macOS."
