California’s bill, [A.B. 2047](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billStatusClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB2047), will not only mandate censorware — software which exists to bluntly block your speech as a user — on all 3D printers; it will also criminalize the use of open-source alternatives. Repeating the mistakes of [Digital Rights Management (DRM) technologies](https://www.eff.org/issues/drm) won’t make anyone safer. What it will do is hurt innovation in the state and risk a slew of new consumer harms, ranging from surveillance to platform lock-in. California must stand with creators and reject this legislation before it’s too late.

3D printing might evoke images of [props from blockbuster films](https://www.cnet.com/tech/computing/project-hail-mary-3d-model/), rapid [prototyping](https://techbullion.com/custom-3d-printed-automotive-parts-faster-prototyping-lower-costs/), [medical research](https://www.regmednet.com/3d-printed-implant-promotes-spinal-cord-injury-repair/), or even [affordable repair parts](https://3dprint.com/318446/philips-offers-the-right-to-repair-with-new-3d-printed-replacement-components-initiative/).
