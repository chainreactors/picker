# [Generative AI Is Erasing Behavioral Tells, Weakening Behavior-Only Detection, Proofpoint Reports](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/generative-ai-is-erasing-behavioral.html)

**The Hacker News**Oct 21, 2026Email Security / Cybercrime

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiH7zuBEyUN7xB2yVXHoNSB6jWMLhxTtu9LqItz3TNMkY5P59w2MW5dRYcLBp1hakchf5442zpMwNG3nzombXfaoo7O5IzgNw61nsbKWAjaKGP8D9ITLy11wkHBUUAwksYEqTiH27t7FTgpa5fpJQ42t6lVqO8qcbI57oWnxc7o8SWLupLpIiiZW6eBqj4/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/proofpoint.jpg)

Generative AI has changed what a malicious email looks like. Messages that once gave themselves away through clumsy wording, generic pretexts, and reused templates now arrive fluent, personalized, and unique to each recipient, and the behavioral signals email filters have relied on for years are fading as a result.

Proofpoint shared several examples of the shift at its Protect 2026 conference. In one, a single threat actor contacted more than 30 real estate agents at the same company, each from a different account, about a different property, and in different words, before closing every thread with the same request: join a video meeting.

Federal data points the same way. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center counted more than 22,000 complaints involving AI in 2025, with nearly $900 million in associated losses, and logged 24,768 business email compromise (BEC) complaints with about $3.05 billion in reported losses, according to its [2025 Internet Crime Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf).

## **What AI Changed**

Email filters were built for attackers who had to cut corners. Researching a target, writing convincingly in a second language, impersonating a supplier, and producing enough variants to defeat signatures all took time, so attackers reused templates and infrastructure, and that reuse left patterns. Behavioral detection learned to spot them: new infrastructure, awkward language, odd timing, and requests that did not fit the conversation.

Generative AI removes those constraints:

* **Language.** Fluent, localized text in any language, with no typos or translation artifacts.
* **Context.** Replies that fit the thread, the recipient’s role, and the timing.
* **Persona.** Matched writing style, signature blocks, and fully fabricated conversation histories.
* **Variation.** A unique message for every recipient, generated in seconds, so there is no template to match.

The result, Proofpoint argues, is that behavior has become a weakening signal. When AI lets attackers make every message look normal, the analysis has to change too, from asking whether a message looks unusual to asking what it is trying to do.

## **Thirty Conversations, One Ask**

The real estate campaign shows what that looks like in practice. Writing 30-plus distinct, plausible property enquiries, each tailored to a different listing and a different agent, is exactly the kind of work generative AI now does in seconds. The opening questions were mundane: whether a property was still open for inspection, what the price guide was, whether a viewing was possible that week. The sender mailboxes were real, and replies landed in threads the agents were already answering.

In one thread, an inspection enquiry sent at 2:58 a.m. turned into a meeting invitation 13 minutes later, complete with a branded template and a claim that the meeting required the agent’s “immediate attention.” The join link was a genuine google.com redirect, so reputation checks read clean, while a parameter in the URL handed the click to an unrelated site.

Because each sender was making first contact, behavioral filters had no history to compare against, and because every message was different, there was no repeated content to match. What the messages shared was not how they looked but what they were trying to get the recipient to do.

AI makes it easy to run this pattern against any business that depends on hearing from strangers, including clinics taking new patients, firms signing new clients, banks opening accounts, and insurers quoting policies, Proofpoint said. A variant aimed at insurance agents used quote requests, neighbor referrals, and coverage questions to build trust before sending a spoofed conference invitation that delivered the payload.

## **AI-Powered Disruption**

AI has also made it easier to run campaigns that aim to disrupt rather than steal. Proofpoint described hostile communications campaigns in which hundreds or thousands of senders write to the same executives, each on a topic tailored to the target’s industry. Some messages carry derogatory language; others carry AI-generated or manipulated images that mock the recipient.

These messages have no link to detonate and no credential to harvest, and any one of them could pass for a single angry correspondent. Scoring messages one at a time will not catch them. The analysis has to recognize disruptive intent across the whole set, and read text and imagery together, since the hostility may sit in an AI-generated picture.
