# In Defence of Unidentified Keys

Written by

[j4vv4d](https://javvadmalik.com/author/j4vv4d/)

in

[blog](https://javvadmalik.com/category/blog/)

I felt oddly defensive when my son asked why we had so many keys.

He was looking in the kitchen drawer for a battery. This was his first mistake. The kitchen drawer contains everything you might need, provided you need something else.

“What’s this one for?”

“No idea.”

“Then why have we got it?”

I didn’t like his tone. It was the tone of someone who has never had to keep a household running by refusing to throw anything away.

There was a small silver key I was fairly sure belonged to a suitcase. Possibly one we no longer owned. But suitcases do occasionally turn up again, and I’d hate to be caught unprepared for luggage returning from the dead.

Another had a little plastic label on it saying “BACK”.

Back of what? A house? A cupboard? I recognised my handwriting, which somehow made it worse. At some point I had known exactly what this meant and had felt no need to burden my future self with details.

My son suggested we bin the ones we couldn’t identify.

So I spent the next half-hour trying them in things.

None fitted anything. One went halfway into the shed lock, which I counted as progress until it wouldn’t come out.

He watched me wrestling with it for a while, then lost interest and wandered off.

I eventually freed the key and put the whole collection in a freezer bag. Organisation. Nobody could accuse me of hoarding now. This was a managed collection.

I even wrote “KEYS” on the bag, although being transparent it had already made a fairly strong case for itself.

Then I put it back in the drawer.

I still don’t know what “BACK” means. The most logical conclusion I can derive is that future me found a way to travel back in time and leave that key for current me to find and use at a critical point in life… it’s the only explanation that makes sense.
