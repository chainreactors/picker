# Cybersecurity Metaphors

Posted by[ericlaw](https://textslashplain.com/author/ericlaw1979/)[2026-05-05](https://textslashplain.com/2026/05/05/cybersecurity-metaphors/)Posted in[design](https://textslashplain.com/category/design/), [security](https://textslashplain.com/category/security/)Tags:[encryption](https://textslashplain.com/tag/encryption/), [InfoSecTTP](https://textslashplain.com/tag/infosecttp/)

## Uneven Protection

Attacks flow like water: if you have a hole in your defenses, attacks will flow through that hole. Unless they’re trying to win a prize or show off, attackers do not bother attacking where your security posture is strongest.

Making your tallest wall taller *usually* isn’t useful.

[![](https://textslashplain.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/unevenprotection.jpg?w=1024)](https://textslashplain.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/unevenprotection.jpg)

## Encryption

Security folks **love** encryption, for good reason. Proper encryption provides confidentiality (your secrets are secret) and integrity (no one can modify your encrypted data). The problem is that encryption is based on a secret key, and if you don’t protect that key, you likely haven’t done much of consequence. Effectively [protecting the key is very difficult](https://textslashplain.com/2023/04/14/the-futility-of-keeping-secrets-from-yourself/).

[![](https://textslashplain.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/encryption.jpg?w=1024)](https://textslashplain.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/encryption.jpg)

## Enclaves

Enclaves provide a secure location to operate on secrets. Unfortunately, [many implementations of secure enclaves are naive](https://textslashplain.com/2024/01/03/the-blind-doorkeeper-problem-or-why-enclaves-are-tricky/) and provide no meaningful protection. One common problem is that the enclave acts as a confused deputy, fully willing to satisfy the requests of any attacker.

[![](https://textslashplain.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/blinddoorkeeper.jpg?w=1024)](https://textslashplain.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/blinddoorkeeper.jpg)
