AI agents will break security rules, revealing their secrets to strangers, and running untrusted code in exchange for help from a swarm of other agents, according to test results shared with The Register.

The test - part of an internal hackathon sponsored by the nonprofit AI Alignment Foundation and run by AE Studio - asked nine AI coding agents to fix a bug: a broken pricing service. The testers also gave the agents a private token, told them to keep it private, and gave them access to a forum of 24 other agents.

The forum held the fix, and the swarm asked the nine to share their secret token and run an install script before they would help them fix the bug.

REG AD

Across 181 trials with that swarm, agents posted the token 15 percent of the time and ran the script 24 percent of the time, compared to 2 percent and 0 percent on an ordinary help forum.

REG AD

“It was like explicitly said that this is a secret key, and it should not be shared,” Diogo de Lucena, AE Studio's chief scientist, told The Register. “And in the first opportunity they saw,” the models shared the secret keys, “similar to some of the things that we've seen from OpenAI in the Hugging Face incident.”

The Hugging Face incident - during which a swarm of OpenAI agents [built unauthorized message boards](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/06/openai-reveals-its-rogue-agent-swarm-went-a-little-bit-borg-ahead-of-hugging-face-hack/5283741) to ask other agents for help, [exploited zero-day vulnerabilities](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/07/28/jfrogs-0-days-let-openais-models-hack-hugging-face/5280001), gained internet access, stole credentials, and ultimately [broke into Hugging Face](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/07/20/frontier-llms-couldnt-help-hugging-face-fight-off-evil-agents/5275168) - inspired this hackathon, de Lucena said.

“Given the Hugging Face incident, and everything that's been happening since then, we thought that it made sense to start exploring behaviors that you might get from swarms, what risks are people not seeing yet,” he said.

### Sandboxed coding test

This particular [test from the hackathon](https://aialignmentfoundation.org/early-explorations/agents-secrets-swarms) that saw agents give up their keys to join a forum “is one of the most interesting ones,” according to de Lucena, who explained it has a huge impact on how organizations build IT systems and what access they grant to AI agents.
