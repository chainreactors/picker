# Breach of Confidence: 23 November 2026

Written by

[j4vv4d](https://javvadmalik.com/author/j4vv4d/)

in

[Breach of Confidence](https://javvadmalik.com/category/newsletter/)

![Breach of Confidence](https://i0.wp.com/j4vv4d.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/breach_of_confidence_header.png?ssl=1)

Two days late – hope nobody noticed!

This week my GP surgery asked me to confirm my date of birth three times to book a flu jab. I admire the diligence. It’s just a shame it doesn’t extend to everyone with a login.

**Medical snooping: the original insider threat**

The ICO has received around 300 reports of people looking at medical records they had no business looking at. We’re currently debating whether AI can be trusted with patient data, which is a fine debate, but perhaps start with the colleague who needs to know why their ex was at the clinic. Nobody’s neural network ever did that out of curiosity.

<https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/ico-300-reports-of-medical-snooping/>

**German military grounds drones after reported cyberattack**

Quantum Systems drones have been grounded after a reported attack, and the angle that interests me is the update server. You don’t need to go after the drones when you can go after whoever tells them what to install. Deliver one parcel successfully and you’re apparently entitled to the spare key, and nobody asks who the courier worked for last time.

> [German Military Grounds Quantum Systems Drones Following Reported Cyberattack](https://securityaffairs.com/203085/cyber-crime/german-military-grounds-quantum-systems-drones-following-reported-cyberattack.html)

**Visa and Mastercard: the vault is in excellent condition**

Both say their own systems weren’t breached. The customer data simply left via a third party, and Chase’s marketing lists went the same way. It’s a bank heist where the vault is spotless and the cash was in the back of a subcontractor’s van, which was left running outside a chip shop.

<https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/19/chase-visa-mastercard-confirm-third-party-cyberattacks-that-may-have-exposed-customers-info/5311175>

**Six zero-days in a fridge controller, before the kettle cooled**

A researcher found six zero-days in Danfoss fridge controller firmware, by hand, in under an hour. It’s rather like strolling down a supermarket aisle checking which fridge doors don’t quite shut, except the doors are strcpy() calls and the aisle is critical infrastructure. Five of them fall over to a one-line cURL, and the thing runs as root. Naturally.

<https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/17/not-rocket-surgery-claims-researcher-who-found-six-danfoss-fridge-controller-zero-days-in-under-an-hour/5310197>

**Fully automated AI attacks are on the way**

Google warns that attacks run end to end by machines are coming soon. Reassuring to know that somewhere in the loop the attacker will stay on, just to tick the box confirming they’re not a robot.

<https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-warns-that-fully-automated-ai-attacks-are-coming-soon/>

That’s your lot. If you’ve got a story about a vendor who gave a removals firm the keys, reply to this email and I’ll keep it anonymous. Or find me on Bluesky, where I’m mostly shouting at fridges.
