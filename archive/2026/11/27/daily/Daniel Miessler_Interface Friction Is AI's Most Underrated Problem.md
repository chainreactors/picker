# Interface Friction Is AI's Most Underrated Problem

Usefulness is capability minus friction, and right now we're at a 1

by Daniel Miessler

I think one of the biggest ideas in AI that we're not thinking enough about is **interface friction**.

Capability is one thing, and AI is obviously getting more and more powerful. But total functionality is multiplication. It's something like:

Usefulness = Capability - Friction

On a scale of 1-10 for each, I'd say right now we're at like:

**Usefulness = Capability (6) - Friction (5)**

Which gives us a 1 total. That seems low, but let's continue.

## Where the friction comes from [​](#where-the-friction-comes-from)

What is the 5 friction?

It's the fact that we have to use AI "apps". It's the fact that we have to mentally context-switch into "I need to ask AI". Which involves stopping what you're doing and going over there, and doing that, and then coming back.

🧠 Every switch also means rebuilding what you were thinking about once you get back.Interface frictionThe cost of stopping what you're doing, going over to a separate AI tool to ask for something, and then coming back.

So what I expect to see, is AI's value will expand exponentially as we start so solve this in 2027 and beyond.

> The natural, intuitive, least-friction, and most efficient way to do *almost everything* will be to use your voice, and to have the AI capabilities of your DA sort it out.
>
> [From Apps to AI (August 2016)](/blog/mobile-ai-digital-assistants-business-apis)

## Claude Tag in Slack [​](#claude-tag-in-slack)

[Claude Tag](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-tag) is a great example of this. People work in Slack. It's replacing email and ticketing systems and all sorts of other work tools as the one place we get work done.

🤖 Anthropic launched Claude Tag in June 2026. Anyone on a team can tag it in a Slack channel, and it's always on.

Claude Tag brought AI to Slack. Directly integrated. They basically have Claude Code running in the background, combined with all the integrations and connectors to learn everything about your business, and a long term memory for everything you've done with it and information about the company in general.

So you can just be like, "Get the latest numbers for this. Oh and actually this would be a great report to have every morning at 9 am, so go ahead and do that."

And it just gets done. Permanently. You didn't leave the app, you didn't go do an "AI" thing. You just made a request to @Claude and it was done.

Expect to see more of this throughout [every aspect of using AI](/blog/personal-ais-will-mediate-everything). And if you are building AI stuff, this is one of the primary things you should be thinking about.

**How can I reduce the friction of people using this?**

## Where this is going [​](#where-this-is-going)

OpenAI is working on a piece of hardware that you can basically talk to. Apple, of course, is working on Siri AI, where you can do a lot more of this stuff now, but all of this is pointing in the same direction.

And if you look at sci-fi, you can see where it's going as well in movies like [Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_%282013_film%29), or [Interstellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstellar_%28film%29), or Iron Man with [Jarvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.A.R.V.I.S.).

🎬 In Her, Samantha is an operating system Theodore talks to all day through an earpiece, with nothing to open or switch to.

It's fully conversational. There's not a big switch. You just talk, and things happen. You're interacting with something that's always there, always listening, always available to help without any context change whatsoever. If one of these people is in the middle of a private conversation, you never hear from the AI because it's none of its business, but the moment you say, "Hey, research that," the AI kicks in and does it for you.

This transition from context breaking to fluid interaction is going to be absolutely massive, and I think we as builders should be thinking a lot more about it.

## Summary [​](#summary)

1. AI's usefulness is what it can do minus the friction of using it, and right now the friction is high
2. The friction is having to stop, go to an AI app, ask, and then come back
3. Claude Tag works inside Slack, so you ask where you already work and it gets done
4. If you build AI stuff, keep asking how to make it something people just talk to
