@ Bruce, ALL,

With regards,

> “Researchers set up a fake start-up company to study fraudulent North Korean IT workers.”

The “North Korean” aspect so often played to fanfare, flag waving and drum beats by the western ICT Sec industry kind of grinds my gears…

The reason is not that I have any love or sympathy of the North Korean system (in fact quite the opposite).

But that the “North Korean” aspect is now a “banner issue” which makes what is a way way more general and wider attack front, into something much more limited. Thus making corporate management make false assumptions about “supply chain attacks”.

That is,

“Are Indian H-1B workers –that some claim there are a million of working in the US– any more or less dangerous than North Korean workers?

The actual answer is in many cases by head count it is Indians…

They are looking for “high technology” to appropriate and exploit rather than money for their government.

Whilst the NK Gov will take what it can get in most cases they do not have the industry to take advantage of it. However money for their Treasury is always immediately usable.

But it does not matter what the reason for foreign workers is, they are all doing a country harm and often both countries. Because they are stoping citizens in that country developing needed “national skills” for the sake of “a quick buck” on the profit line to keep share holders allowing the managers bonuses…

Which is why certain Corporate Management types were so keen on AI systems… But have now mostly found that

“AI really does not pay or play well”.

It only has longterm ROI in certain very niche very constrained activities and usually requires experienced human oversight.

If this will ever change is open to debate, and I for one am not taking a bet on it because I really don’t expect to live that long.
