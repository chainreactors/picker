In the past year, cyber adversaries have started to exploit artificial intelligence (AI) technology to execute a wide range of attacks. AI is helping them accelerate, optimize, and scale up their techniques. Agentic AI lowers the work factor for savvy adversaries, and it lowers the barrier to entry for new entrants. There is already an increase in the extent, tempo, and sophistication of attacks, and they are targeting diverse sectors. There are the well-known [Salt Typhoon attacks](https://globalcyberalliance.org/new-report-salt-typhoon-across-the-internet/) on multiple prominent telecommunications providers and, more recently, [attacks on water systems attributed to Iran](https://www.csis.org/analysis/mapping-iranian-cyberattacks-us-water-systems).

AI and other new technologies are also creating opportunities for system developers and operational defenders. Large language models (LLMs) are now highly capable at rapidly finding vulnerabilities and, additionally, developing candidate repairs. In operational settings, AI can support more effective and rapid triage in security operations centers.

This combination of challenge and opportunity is creating an undeniable urgency in both executing and adapting the national cybersecurity mission. Leaders in cyber research, operations, and missions for national security must focus their efforts where they can rapidly and effectively bolster our country’s cyber safety.

In response to these disruptive changes, the SEI has recently released a report, [*Advancing Cybersecurity: A Framework of Cyber Research Priorities*](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/advancing-cybersecurity-a-framework-of-cyber-research-priorities/), that identifies seven areas of cybersecurity focus and elaborates on potential directions for research. In developing the framework, we led a team of more than 25 technical experts who identified the 37 highest priority opportunities within those seven areas that hold the greatest potential cyber benefit for national security:

* Analytic and Operational Tradecraft for Cybersecurity
* Securing AI-Based Systems and Workflows
* Cyber-Physical System Security
* Cybersecurity for Complex Integrated Systems
* Insider Threat and Human–Systems Interaction
* Secure Engineering and Mission Confidence
* Modeling and Simulation in Support of Security

Our team selected the particular topics on the basis of an explicit set of criteria. We aimed not to be fully comprehensive, but rather to identify topics that have the greatest potential impact on cyber operations and envisioned cyber capabilities in the Department of War (DoW), the intelligence community, the Department of Homeland Security, and other federal agencies. The set of topics considered in this framework focuses specifically on technical challenges that have the following characteristics:

1. Significant for national security and civil infrastructure.
2. Progress is likely to make the greatest difference.
3. Emphasizes both cybersecurity and also resilient response of complex systems under attack.
4. The talents, resources, and leadership of CERT and partners can be applied to best advantage.
5. Areas where the CERT Division and the SEI can build strong collaborative relationships.
6. Areas where both immediate and longer-term benefits can be realized.

The sections below take a closer look at the seven framing areas. The full report is available [online](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/advancing-cybersecurity-a-framework-of-cyber-research-priorities/) and includes numerous references to the technical literature.

## 1. Analytic and Operational Tradecraft for Cybersecurity

Tradecraft encompasses operationally focused topics, combining computational and human elements to support secure operation of systems in an environment of active threats. We distinguish operational tradecraft from related topics associated with the design, engineering, evaluation, and sustainment of systems.

The tactics, techniques, and procedures of cybersecurity tradecraft to protect data and systems must constantly evolve to keep up with adversarial tactics and with the rapid advance in computational capabilities, most recently AI-based system elements, highly distributed system designs, and modern heterogeneous hardware designs.

The scope of the tradecraft challenge is extensive. In developing recommendations for research areas, the following issues were identified in our analysis process as particularly pressing and relevant:

* **Event Analysis for Networked and Distributed Systems.** Event analysis for networked and distributed systems includes practices and concepts for monitoring, detecting, analyzing, and diagnosing cybersecurity-relevant events in the operation of a system.
* **Data Retention and Analysis.** Event analysis is an issue at nearly all levels of scale, ranging from individual applications and systems to infrastructures and nation-state capabilities. In nearly all cases, data retention poses challenges because instrumentation associated with system and network processes usually generates a large amount of highly granular data, and organizations often can’t retain all of it.
* **Analysis of Cyber Adversary Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs).** A full-scope analysis of cyber-adversary TTPs builds on data from threat intelligence, system operations, and software understanding (such as reverse engineering).
