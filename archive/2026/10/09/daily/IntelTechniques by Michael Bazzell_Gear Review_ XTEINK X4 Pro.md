#### Gear Review: XTEINK X4 Pro

---

10/09/2026

I read a lot. I also travel a lot. Many years (maybe decades) ago I would buy a paperback book and throw away the pages as I traveled, making the book lighter and smaller as the trip went on. E-readers solved that. Just a few years ago, I reviewed the largest readers I could find. I wanted a huge 13.3" device which could serve me my daily newspapers in PDF format. I still use my Boox Note Max every day to read two newspapers.

This review goes in the opposite direction. I have been seeing pre-orders and gossip about tiny e-readers, some smaller than a cellular phone (which isn't hard these days with the oversized Pop-Tarts we are forced to carry around). I finally settled on the XTEINK X4 Pro.

The 13.3" Boox serves a very specific purpose for me. I do not take it on most travel due to the size. It is my morning companion at home. The XTEINK X4 Pro is tiny with a 4.3" screen and can go everywhere. The following compares the XTEINK X4 Pro (middle) with a 6.1" Pixel (left) and Kindle Paperwhite (right).

![](../images/xteink1.jpg)

When I powered the unit, it asked me to select my language. It then prompted me to download additional fonts for a smoother experience. When browsing Wi-Fi networks, it only detected 2.4 GHz networks and not my 5 GHz routers. I was fine with this, as I don't need fast internet access. Actually, I don't need any internet access.

Most of the e-readers I have tested over the past several years were simply e-ink tablets running a minimal version of Android. This is great for side-loading apps, but I never really needed Android for this purpose. The XTEINK X4 Pro runs on its own minimal and lightweight firmware which appears to be less than 6 MB in size.

This is the main reason I wanted the XTEINK X4 Pro. It allows you to install your own open-source firmware to run the system. The most popular are CrossPoint and CrossInk. These are backed by a large community of e-reader enthusiasts. I wanted to install CrossInk, so I ignored all of the onboarding.

There is no USB port on the device. Instead, you also receive a magnetic adapter which connects to the back and supplies a USB-C port. I connected the device to my computer and navigated to the online tool at https://inky.crossink.dev/#flash-tools. This will prompt you to allow an extension, but it is actually just using Firefox's built-in serial access software. There is nothing to install or uninstall.

The online tool prompted me to select my device model and the latest version of the software. I had to flash it twice, but the process was quick and smooth. I now had a fully open-source firmware with many more features and fonts than the stock option.

Loading books is as easy as dragging them to the included 16 GB microSD. You can create any folders desired if you want organization. You can connect to the device via USB or connect the card to your computer. I prefer the latter. I try to keep my entire eBook collection in EPUB format, and every file I threw at it loaded fine. The first load takes a few seconds but subsequent loads are fast.

I have used traditional Kindle-sized readers which sit in the middle of the XTEINK X4 Pro and a 13.3" reader. At first, reading on the XTEINK X4 Pro was awkward. The small screen sort of reminded me of those speed-reading trainers which show you a limited number of words in fast succession. After about 5 minutes, my brain understood what was going on.

The default font was a little small for my eyes. I increased the font within settings and allowed my book to re-index. This looked better to my eyes.

![](../images/xteink2.jpg)

The Pro model includes a front light, but I typically read in decent lighting. The following images show the front light in use on both a light and dark theme. The dark theme is only available with the custom firmware.

![](../images/xteink3.jpg)

![](../images/xteink4.jpg)

My initial complaint is the same issue I have always had with books on these devices. It is hard to understand how far into the content you are. This is not a fault of this unit, but all readers in general. With PDFs on large readers, this is somewhat remedied because I am reading the intended layout. Each page flip is a true page flip. The EPUB files reposition the text as needed for the reader. A 200-page eBook on a Kindle might be 1200 pages on the XTEINK X4 Pro. Again, no fault of the devices.

After a while, I got over this. I was sitting on a plane holding the XTEINK X4 Pro. I noticed my eyes did not need to travel as much with the smaller screen. It felt as if I was reading faster than a traditional book or Kindle. I was definitively ingesting data faster than a large device.

The biggest feature to me was the page turn buttons on both sides of the screen. I am used to readers which have "up and down" buttons on one side of the device. This is not natural to me. This unit has a button on the left to go back and on the right to go forward. It made sense, especially holding with one hand. Battery life was phenomenal, as expected with such a small screen. Page refreshes were quick and clean. I saw no ghosting of previous text. After reading for any extended time, my mobile device felt huge.

At the time of this review, the XTEINK X4 Pro was $85 on Amazon (<https://link.amazon/B0crT7xQO>). Retail is $99. If you do not want the front light and touch screen, the X4 was only $75 with a retail of $90 (<https://link.amazon/B09plEaDk>). Personally, I believe it is worth the $10 for these features.

If you want a truly portable and tiny e-reader, this is the way to go. Understand the limitations. You are not going to read large PDFs on this thing or any DRM-protected files. EPUBs work great. It really is a single-purpose device. It makes sense for me during travel or for notes during a presentation. I like that mine has never connected to the internet, has no apps, and runs on open-source firmware. I just want to read.
