# FIFA says hackers accessed ‘limited set of personal and business information’

The Fédération Internationale de Football Association (FIFA) confirmed that internal data was stolen during a recent cyberattack.

The Switzerland-based soccer organization said hackers breached its systems and gained “unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and business information.”

"FIFA is working closely with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement,” a spokesperson said.

"FIFA condemns in the strongest possible terms the unlawful acquisition and publication of information, particularly personal data. Such actions constitute a serious and unacceptable violation of privacy and can have significant consequences for the individuals concerned."

The organization did not respond to follow up questions about the nature of the incident. FIFA is the governing body of international soccer and organizes the World Cup, which was held this summer. It has about 700 employees and is headquartered in Zurich.

The Handsome Boy Hacking Club (HBHC) cybercriminal group [claimed](https://x.com/H4ckmanac/status/2123705209696207228) last week that it stole more than 12 GB of data from the organization and attempted to extort FIFA but failed.

The group eventually [published](https://x.com/DarkWebInformer/status/2124186095222976650) the stolen data on a cybercriminal forum, claiming to have accessed FIFA’s Microsoft environment. The stolen data allegedly includes identity and immigration documents, financial records, legal documents, governance information and more.

The hacking group emerged last week and little is known about its members or associations.

The FIFA incident follows other recent attacks on European soccer leagues. Spain’s top soccer league LaLiga [said](https://therecord.media/laliga-hacked-spain-top-professional-league-confirms-cyber-incident) on Monday that hackers stole personal information belonging to fans in a cyberattack affecting its digital services and those of several Spanish clubs.

In May, Dutch police [arrested](https://therecord.media/dutch-police-arrest-man-over-cyber-breach-ajax-football) a 35-year-old man suspected of hacking into the computer systems of Ajax, one of the Netherlands’ most prominent soccer clubs. The French Football Federation also [disclosed](https://therecord.media/french-rugby-club-restores-systems-after-cyberattack) a cyberattack in 2025.
