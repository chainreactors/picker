# Mississippi mayor says ransomware incident led city to shut down systems

A ransomware attack has shut down the computer systems of Vicksburg, Mississippi, the city’s mayor told residents on Thursday evening.

Mayor Willis Thompson published a statement in the local newspaper saying the city is investigating a ransomware attack that has not impacted emergency services but has affected payments for utilities. He said the system shutdown was “temporary.”

Thompson said no one's services will be shut off while the investigation is ongoing and no penalties will be issued for late payments. He later [told](https://www.vicksburgpost.com/news/ransomware-attack-shuts-down-vicksburg-computer-systems-5a84f22d) the Vicksburg Post that the city has been working on the recovery effort with the FBI, Department of Homeland Security and other state officials alongside private cybersecurity experts.

“One of the top priorities of our investigation is determining whether there was any compromise of personal or confidential information relating to current and/or former customers, contractors, vendors, employees, or affiliated business partners of the City,” Thompson said in a statement.

“At this time, the investigation is ongoing, and the City has not reached a final determination regarding what information, if any, may have been accessed or acquired without authorization.”

The city did not respond to requests for comment about ransom demands or the identity of the attackers.

Thompson’s statement said the city will not be disclosing any technical information “that could interfere with the investigation, recovery efforts, or the security of City systems.”

Vicksburg has more than 20,000 residents and is about 40 minutes west of the state’s capital of Jackson.

The state recently dealt with a devastating [ransomware attack on the University of Mississippi Medical Center (UMMC)](https://therecord.media/medusa-ransomware-mississippi-cyber) — the largest and most important healthcare organization in the state.

The hospital system — which has the state’s only children's hospital, only Level I trauma center, only Level IV neonatal intensive care unit and the state’s only organ transplant programs — spent weeks in the dark after the attack and needed FBI assistance to recover.

Ransomware gangs also targeted a [prominent electricity utility](https://therecord.media/mississippii-electric-utility-residents-breach) in Mississippi last year and [another county in 2023](https://therecord.media/coastal-mississippi-county-recovering-from-ransomware-attack-digital-hurricane).
