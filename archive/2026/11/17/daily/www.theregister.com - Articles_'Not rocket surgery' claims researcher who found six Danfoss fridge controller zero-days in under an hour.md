A Dutch cybersecurity researcher claims he found six zero-days in Danfoss refrigeration controller firmware in under an hour, then delivered a withering assessment of the vendor's defenses against memory corruption.

The [Centre for Cybersecurity Belgium](https://ccb.belgium.be/advisories/warning-six-new-danfoss-vulnerabilities-refrigeration-controllers-risk-immediate-action) (CCB) warns that the flaws could let attackers take control without credentials, potentially retain access after resets, or disable controllers altogether.

Carlo Meijer of cyber consultancy Midnight Blue examined the latest AK-SM800A firmware, version 5.3.2, manually – no AI required.

REG AD

He said that he went looking in random places where strcpy() is invoked, given the vendor's "extremely liberal use" of the function (which doesn't check whether the destination buffer is large enough), and swiftly found five cases in which he said he could trigger a segfault with a one-line cURL command.

REG AD

"Of course, remotely triggering a crash is bad in itself, but being able to leverage the buffer overflow to obtain remote code execution is much worse," he [wrote](https://www.midnightblue.nl/blog/multiple-rce-danfoss-aksm800a).

Meijer added: "Any reasonably experienced exploit developer will agree exploiting straightforward stack-based buffer overflows on ARM Linux without exploit mitigations is very straightforward. And if they don't, there's probably a [frontier](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/23/frontier-ai-keeps-racing-despite-calls-to-slow-down/5298448) or [open-weight model](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/03/open-models-can-do-90-of-agentic-work-at-a-twentieth-of-the-cost-of-a-frontier-llm-gartner/5307132) out there willing to help them agree."

He noted that before achieving RCE, an attacker would need to overcome the limitation of strcpy() where it stops copying bytes after hitting a null byte, but said "it's not exactly rocket surgery."

He went on to claim: "Also, the exploited application runs as root (because of course it does)."

Later firmware adds "session control," an authentication mechanism whose strict setting blocks unauthenticated access to most web interfaces. But Meijer found a memory corruption flaw in that protective layer too.

He described the final zero-day as a "trivial" pre-auth memory corruption bug...
