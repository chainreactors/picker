Cozy up next to the fireplace and we'll catch you up on the latest digital rights news with EFF's EFFector newsletter.

In [our latest issue](https://www.eff.org/effector/37/16), we’re exposing surveillance logs that [reveal racist policing](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/11/license-plate-surveillance-logs-reveal-racist-policing-against-romani-people); explaining the harms of Google’s plan for [Android app gatekeeping](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/11/application-gatekeeping-ever-expanding-pathway-internet-censorship); and continuing our new series, Gate Crashing, exploring how the internet [empowers people to take nontraditional paths](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-4wCAKHBzgE) into the traditional worlds of journalism, creativity, and criticism.

Prefer to listen in? Check out our audio companion, where EFF Staff Attorney Mario Trujillo explains why Ring's [upcoming facial recognition tool](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/11/legal-case-against-rings-face-recognition-feature) could violate the privacy rights of millions of people. Catch the conversation on [YouTube](https://youtu.be/okOtK62ayfY) or the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/37.16).

Since 1990 EFF has [published EFFector](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/09/eff.org/effector) to help keep readers on the bleeding edge of their digital rights. We know that the intersection of technology, civil liberties, human rights, and the law can be complicated, so EFFector is a great way to stay on top of things. The newsletter is chock full of links to updates, announcements, blog posts, and other stories to help keep readers—and listeners—up to date on the movement to protect online privacy and free expression.

Thank you to the supporters around the world who make our work possible! If you're not a member yet, [join EFF today](https://eff.org/effect) to help us fight for a brighter digital future.
