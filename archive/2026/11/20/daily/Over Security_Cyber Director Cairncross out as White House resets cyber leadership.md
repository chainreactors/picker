# Cyber Director Cairncross out as White House resets cyber leadership

U.S. National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving the White House post, creating upheaval among the Trump administration’s digital security leadership as the country wrestles with recent technology outages.

Axios first reported Cairncross’s exit. A White House official confirmed his departure, which reportedly had been pushed by President Donald Trump’s chief of staff Stephen Miller, who argued Cairncross lacked credentials to perform the job.

Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon’s chief information officer, has been named the acting cyber czar, which advises the president on cybersecurity matters and coordinates digital policy across the federal government.

“Our nation stands in the crossroad of analog, digital, and quantum technologies, our American way of living increasingly interconnected. Our opportunity is to dominate the cyber landscape for generations to come … but only if we modernize and drive resilience into the foundations our great nation runs on,” Davies, who the Senate confirmed for her role about a year ago, [wrote on X](https://x.com/_KirstenDavies_/status/2123595758465089690?s=20).

[Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth](https://x.com/SecWar/status/2123594274721644701) and [Secretary of State Marco Rubio](https://x.com/SecRubio/status/2123591426814460410) congratulated Davies in social media posts on Thursday.

The high-profile departure comes amid a series of technology outages, including a nationwide aviation ground stop, a global Microsoft Outlook outage and disruptions to the National Security Agency’s classified communications network. [Administration officials have insisted](https://therecord.media/tech-disruptions-house-homeland-committee-testimony) that there are no indications a coordinated cyberattack caused the blackouts.

Cairncross, who previously was an executive at the Republican National Committee, was nominated by President Trump and confirmed last year. His office oversaw the crafting of the administration’s cyber strategy, all seven pages of which were released in March.

The brief document vowed to "detect, confront, and defeat cyber adversaries before they breach our networks and systems,” maintain an advantage in artificial intelligence and update federal networks.

His office also played a role in the administration’s executive order on AI.

Cairncross’ successor will need to be confirmed by ​the Senate, which will be controlled by Democrats beginning in January.

His exit came just hours after Michael LoSapio, the president’s pick to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), [put his nomination in deep jeopardy](https://therecord.media/cisa-losapio-nomination-trump) by refusing to answer questions about who won the 2020 presidential election and denying the U.S. Capitol was attacked on January 6, 2021.

Since LoSapio’s comments, Sens. Thom Tillis (R-NC) and Ron Wyden (D-OR) have publicly said they each would place a hold on his nomination to prevent him from being included in any en bloc package of nominees during the lame duck session of Congress.
