# Signal blocks screenshots by Apple, Google operating systems to protect data from AI agents

The encrypted messaging platform Signal has announced it will by default stop users from taking screenshots of messages if they are using Google or Apple operating systems, a move fueled by concerns about how the tech giants deploy artificial intelligence agents.

Last year, the widely used messaging app restricted Microsoft 11 users from screenshotting and keeping messages. Similar restrictions will now apply to Google’s Gemini Intelligence and Apple's Siri AI, Signal Foundation president Meredith Whittaker said in a Friday blog post.

“These agentic AI integrations are changing this environment in a way that’s making it increasingly hostile to privacy,” the [blog post](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/) said. “This is an existential threat to Signal, and our ability to responsibly uphold our mission.”

Whittaker said Signal plans to specifically restrict the agentic systems integrated into iOS 27 and versions of Android where Signal detects AI assistants trying to read text.

The operating systems’ AI agents attempt to glean information from users by scanning what’s on their screens, Whittaker said, and in doing so hoover up the data in order to “answer your questions and do your bidding,” the blog post said.

Whittaker warned the agents are making the captured data accessible to random local databases and remote servers at random.

“All of this is outside of Signal’s control once the data has been captured, and imperils the privacy of all the participants in a conversation,” the blog post said.

While screenshots won’t be permitted by default, the blog post said users who choose to can disable the block in settings.

The “remedy is incomplete,” Whittaker warned, because blocking screenshots does not address “other more sneaky ways AI agents could potentially access application data, such as retrieving content already present on a user’s device via the filesystem.”

The dearth of effective tools available to Signal and other app developers to use to stop AI agents from weakening privacy is a significant concern, the blog post said.

Whittaker condemned Microsoft, Apple and Google for failing to provide app developers with a mechanism to block agents’ access to data without significantly compromising the “core user experience.”

The same lack of cooperation from tech giants is also to blame for Signal’s inability to place the restrictions on macOS, the blog post said. Additionally, Signal has struggled with a profound lack of documentation about how the agents function.

“Without better documentation and clear technical specifications, it will be increasingly difficult for Signal to provide robust privacy and security to the many millions of people who rely on us,” Whittaker said.

“These three operating systems are the oceans in which Signal, and other applications, must swim; they dictate the conditions of life for the application ecosystem,” the blog post said of Apple, Google and Microsoft. “These agentic AI integrations are changing this environment in a way that’s making it increasingly hostile to privacy.”

Whittaker called on the companies to provide an easy way for users to opt-out of data access permissions tools, stop third-party agents from accessing data without users’ consent and bolster documentation lending transparency to how agents work.

Apple did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Microsoft and Google declined to comment.
