# [FBI Says North Korean Hackers Stole Over $400 Million in Crypto Using Deepfake Video Calls](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/fbi-says-north-korean-hackers-stole.html)

**Ravie Lakshmanan**Nov 13, 2026Cybercrime / Cyber Espionage

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhDmjJA-t4vvigohRIfUR6JExYEPR0Dohh29eooVagQOvRyRDgdisxAy3wSWni5iFHjf2Dk8X2BavSeGO8hJ-wCTbDuffsdtamOuqj1PVTb75EWNF5vNe3tCsS6pyS_pSzNhEELOVU0DxHdmGx4Kt8KNqwz8YheAM9kEoVmpnHeVV1qsofLCBYDEt0E2IqX/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/flash.jpg)

The U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) has established that threat actors tied to the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK aka North Korea) have stolen more than $400 million worth of cryptocurrency as part of a social engineering scheme codenamed **FaceStealer** in the last 18 months.

As part of a coordinated set of actions announced by the U.S. government, the cloud-based infrastructure used to pull off the ["hard-to-detect" campaign](https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/facestealer-cryptocurrency-theft-scheme) has been seized.

In tandem, the U.S. Department of Justice (DoJ) has announced the court-authorized seizure and restraint of more than $1.2 billion worth of cryptocurrency recently [stolen](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/binance-reveals-how-fake-job-interviews.html) by the North Korea-linked TraderTraitor actors following the hack of cryptocurrency exchange Binance. The plundered digital assets are used to fund Pyongyang's illicit weapons program, the DoJ added.

"The North Korean regime relies on proceeds from large-scale cryptocurrency theft to fund its illicit weapons program, stealing billions of dollars in recent years," [said](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency) FBI Cyber Branch Operations Director Brett Leatherman in a statement.

"North Korean cyber actors have used the FaceStealer scheme to aggressively target individuals in the cryptocurrency and Web3 sectors through deceptive social engineering tactics, resulting in the theft of more than $400 million in the last 18 months."

According to the Justice Department, FaceStealer relies on surreptitiously recorded videos and deepfakes to facilitate cryptocurrency theft. Some aspects of the activity dovetail with a campaign documented by Kaspersky under the name [GhostCall](https://thehackernews.com/2025/10/researchers-expose-ghostcall-and.html) in October 2025.

The activity is linked to a prolific North Korea-linked [financially motivated](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/dprk-operatives-impersonate.html) threat cluster [tracked](https://thehackernews.com/2026/04/drift-loses-285-million-in-durable.html) as **[BlueNoroff](https://www.picussecurity.com/resource/blog/bluenoroff-group-the-financial-cybercrime-arm-of-lazarus)**, which is also known by the cryptonyms APT38, CageyChameleon, CryptoCore, DangerousPassword, Nickel Gladstone, Sapphire Sleet, Stardust Chollima, and UNC1069.

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/ai-governance-platform-d)

The campaign involves luring targets with invitations to video meetings delivered from stolen accounts belonging to prior victims. Targets who join the meeting have their video stream recorded, while simultaneously serving an authentic, prerecorded webcam video of a previous victim or victims known to the target to build trust.

"The North Korean hackers also use super intelligence (SI) to develop deepfake videos of well-known and influential individuals often in the cryptocurrency and web3 industries that make the FaceStealer scheme even more effective," the DoJ said. "Here again, they use the deepfake videos in the meetings with targets to gain the target's trust."

A few seconds into the meeting, a mock technical error is presented to the victim, claiming that they cannot be heard, and they are provided a link to address the non-existent issue. The fix is a ClickFix-style lure that's designed to install malware on the victim's workstation and steal cryptocurrency, as well as abuse the target's privileges to other computer infrastructure.

In an extensive report shared with The Hacker News in July 2026, JUMPSEC [revealed](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/bluenoroff-zoom-phishing-kit-profiles.html) how the social engineering operation is built atop a phishing kit designed to impersonate videoconferencing platforms like Zoom and Microsoft Teams. The cybersecurity company has characterized FaceStealer as a victim acquisition platform that involves using compromised trusted contacts as a pathway to fuel a self-propagating attack chain via Telegram.

This includes hijacking social media and communications accounts such as Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack, and Discord for extended periods of time, and then accessing the victim's contact list to target the victim's personal and professional circles.

The [entire attack](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2026/261112.pdf) plays out as follows -

* **Compromise a victim:** This involves harvesting cryptocurrency wallets, seed phrases, and other valuable access; hijacking their Telegram account or other communication app and locking them out; gaining access to their contacts and chat histories; and secretly recording them during the video chat.
* **Exploit the victim's Telegram account:** The attacker impersonates the victim and sends tailored messages to other contacts on their behalf, proposing a Zoom or Teams meeting under the pretext of discussing an investment opportunity.
