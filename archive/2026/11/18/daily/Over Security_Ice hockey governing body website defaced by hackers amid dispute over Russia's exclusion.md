# Ice hockey governing body website defaced amid dispute over Russia's exclusion

The International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) confirmed a cyberattack on its website after hackers replaced its homepage with a pro-Russian message criticizing the country's exclusion from international competitions.

The Switzerland-based federation said it was investigating the attack and working to restore its systems. Its website remained offline Wednesday, displaying a notice that maintenance work was underway.

"We strongly condemn these actions and are taking all necessary steps to investigate the source of the attack and restore our systems," the IIHF said in a [statement](https://x.com/IIHFHockey/status/2122764095120093215) Tuesday.

The attack follows the governing body’s [confirmation](https://apnews.com/article/russia-iihf-hockey-u20-worlds-boycott-canada-64dafbad4501c34b8d404b69321892ae) that Russia would not be allowed to compete in the upcoming World Junior Ice Hockey Championship in Canada.

According to media [reports](https://www.svt.se/sport/ishockey/iihfs-hemsida-hackad-publicerade-ryskt-meddelande), the attackers briefly replaced the federation's homepage with an image of Russian hockey players celebrating, accompanied by a message criticizing the organization's decision to exclude Russia from international competition.

"Scared to face Russia? You banned the only team you can't beat," the message read.

The image reportedly remained visible for several minutes before the website was taken offline. The hackers behind the incident have not been identified.

"Our platforms will be back online as soon as we are confident they are secure," the IIHF said.

The Russian Ice Hockey Federation (RIHF) also [condemned](https://en.fhr.ru/news/item/123663/) the hack, saying it did not support illegal attempts to influence decisions about Russian athletes' participation in international competitions.

"The RIHF does not condone interference with information resources or any actions that fall outside the legal framework," the organization said Tuesday.

The attack comes amid a growing legal dispute over Russia's return to international ice hockey, more than four years after the country was barred from IIHF competitions following its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. Belarus, a close Russian ally, also remains excluded.

In late October, the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) [ruled](https://valloni.ch/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CAS-Media-Release_12573-et-al_final.pdf) in Russia's favor in a dispute over its participation in five international championships, including the upcoming World Junior Championship.

The IIHF, however, has resisted Russia's immediate reinstatement, arguing that the ruling failed to account for significant logistical, security and sporting complications.

The federation has cited visa restrictions, security arrangements and the difficulty of changing tournament formats on short notice. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney previously [said](https://www.reuters.com/world/canada-will-not-let-russians-compete-ice-hockey-tournament-says-carney-2026-11-03/) Russian players would not be allowed to compete in Canada while Moscow's war against Ukraine continued.

Russian sports officials criticized the decision, calling for the tournament to be relocated and threatening further legal action.

Russian state-linked hackers have previously targeted international sports organizations amid disputes over the country's participation in major competitions.

In 2016 and 2017, hackers linked to Russia's military intelligence agency, the GRU, stole and [leaked](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/us-charges-russian-gru-officers-international-hacking-and-related-influence-and) confidential medical information from international anti-doping bodies and sports organizations, including the World Anti-Doping Agency.

Russian military hackers were also [blamed](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/six-russian-gru-officers-charged-connection-worldwide-deployment-destructive-malware-and) for a destructive cyberattack that disrupted IT systems at the 2018 Winter Olympics in South Korea, after Russia was suspended from the games over a state-sponsored doping scandal.

More recently, French authorities [accused](https://therecord.media/france-blames-russian-military-intelligence-for-hacks-against-local-orgs) Russian intelligence operatives of targeting an organization involved in preparations for the 2024 Paris Olympics.

There is currently no public evidence linking the attack on the IIHF to Russian intelligence services or any previously identified hacking group.
