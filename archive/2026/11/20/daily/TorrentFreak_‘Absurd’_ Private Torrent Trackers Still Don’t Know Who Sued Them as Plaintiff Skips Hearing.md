# ‘Absurd’: Private Torrent Trackers Still Don’t Know Who Sued Them as Plaintiff Skips Hearing

today by
[Ernesto Van der Sar](https://torrentfreak.com/author/ernesto/)

[Home](https://torrentfreak.com "Go to TorrentFreak.") > [Lawsuits](https://torrentfreak.com/category/lawsuits/ "Go to the Lawsuits category archives.") >

The person who sued PassThePopcorn, BroadcasTheNet and other private trackers as filmmaker “Matthew Schneider” failed to show up for a hearing that was supposed to confirm his identity. He now has one week to explain himself. The tracker operators, meanwhile, will file their identities with the court under seal, out of reach of the public and the plaintiff.

![ptp](https://torrentfreak.com/images/popcornpass-600x400.jpg)Last month, the person litigating as Matthew Schneider was [ordered to prove his identity](https://torrentfreak.com/accused-impostor-in-private-tracker-lawsuit-faces-contempt-for-withholding-passport/) to an Illinois federal court.

The self-proclaimed filmmaker had already dropped his copyright lawsuit against several private torrent trackers, but two of the Doe defendants are seeking sanctions against him.

The plaintiff was supposed to email his passport, driver’s license and Copyright Office paperwork to the courtroom deputy by October 9. However, he didn’t and instead offered to show one apostilled UK ID to the judge alone.

On Tuesday, Judge Andrea R. Wood planned to ask him what had changed his mind, but she never got the chance to do so as the plaintiff didn’t show.

## No Show

The status hearing was scheduled for 11 AM Chicago time, by video. The courtroom deputy called for the plaintiff twice, but he didn’t appear, on video or by phone. The court also emailed him, without response. After about twenty minutes the hearing started anyway.

“We waited long enough to account for any reasonable delay,” Judge Wood said.

At a video hearing last month Schneider did appear, but by phone only, citing technical difficulties on his end. This time, Judge Wood asked him to test his video connection with the court in advance, but that also didn’t happen.

Judge Wood did not jump to conclusions and gave him one week to explain in writing why he failed to appear. If he doesn’t respond, he forfeits the ability to seek discovery, and his own proposed discovery requests won’t go out either.

## ‘Absurd’

At the hearing, defense attorney Erin Russell, who represents Doe #1, argued that the pending discovery requests should move forward as soon as possible. This, because some information, including IP-address logs, is only retained for a limited time.

“It’s absurd that we are conducting discovery to figure out who the plaintiff in this lawsuit really is,” Russell said.

The third-party subpoenas the defense proposed last month are the way to find that out, she argued. These include one to the PACER Service Center, for the IP addresses the plaintiff used to access the court’s website. Russell also pointed to the plaintiff’s mail-drop address in Sanborn, New York, which may hold records of where his mail is forwarded.

While Schneider must prove his identity, the tracker operators remain anonymous. Judge Wood directed them to file their identities with the court under seal, a routine step for parties litigating under a pseudonym, and she said she will consider whether anonymous defendants can seek sanctions at all.

The judge offered no guarantees, but she noted that the plaintiff’s absence makes it harder to justify giving him the defendants’ real names, particularly while he isn’t appearing to defend the sanctions motion.

The defense will also draft a protective order covering whatever discovery reveals about the plaintiff’s identity and address, something both sides agreed to in October.

## Discovery Fight

The hearing was also meant to settle the discovery requests both sides filed in October. The plaintiff’s questions came in four days late and turned the tables on the defense. Among other things, he asked the tracker attorneys to admit that they contacted the judge’s chambers to delay rulings, and that they never viewed his “Do Us Part” film before filing the sanctions motion.

The defense objected to all these requests. In its objections, Doe #1 also mentioned that the film the plaintiff submitted as evidence in September “appears to have been created using generative AI technology and does not qualify as an ‘audiovisual’ submission.”

*Generative AI*

![Generative AI](https://torrentfreak.com/images/schneider-ai-objection.png)

Doe #10 added that the plaintiff has yet to provide evidence for the ten works he sued over. “Plaintiff has not produced a registration certificate, application, or deposit copy for any of them,” the filing reads.

Judge Wood will address these discovery requests in a written order.

For now, the mystery of who sued the trackers continues. The next hearing is set for December 17, in person this time, and the judge noted that the cost of showing up in Chicago may be part of what the defense tries to recover.
