A Russian national is being held in custody after being arrested for allegedly spying on a US defense tech company in Palo Alto, shoulder-surfing employees, and dropping remote surveillance devices at their homes.

Igor D'iu was indicted after FBI agents surveilled his activities between June and October 2026, which they say were issued as "tasks" from what is thought to be Russia’s Federal Security Service (FSB), according to an [affidavit](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndca/media/1472991/dl?inline) [PDF] signed by an FBI special agent.

After FBI agents secured a warrant to raid D'iu's temporary premises in October, they say that Telegram messages harvested from his seized devices confirmed investigators' findings and revealed that he was spying on an unnamed company.

REG AD

Court documents describe the company in question as a large technology and defense company with offices in Palo Alto, and media-reported links to the Russia-Ukraine war.

REG AD

While unconfirmed officially, the company has widely been reported as [Palantir](https://www.theregister.com/public-sector/2026/11/13/palantir-adds-three-uk-police-forces-to-its-beat-with-101m-deal/5309699), which did not respond to our request for confirmation.

D'iu allegedly received instructions from [Telegram](https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/06/19/telegram-founder-accuses-meta-of-sabotaging-access-in-india-with-bgp-hijacks/5258866) contacts, whom he only suspected were representatives of the Russian government, weeks after entering the US illegally in July.

The indictment alleges he was ordered to surveil employees' homes, take photographs of their screens while working remotely at coffee shops, and permit his handlers to remotely access his laptop for the purpose of capturing data from nearby devices owned by the company’s employees.

In some cases, the filing alleges, D’iu was asked to leave a laptop outside one employee's apartment complex, all while allowing his Telegram handlers to remotely operate the device.

The first time, he was asked to leave the laptop for one hour while the handlers worked, and another time, he was told to drop it off in the middle of the night and only come back to collect it three hours later, the filing claims.

D'iu is also allegedly to have followed two female employees in his rented car, driving dangerously and violating multiple road laws to do so.
