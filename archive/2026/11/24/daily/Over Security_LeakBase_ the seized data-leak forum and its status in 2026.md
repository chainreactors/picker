LeakBase was an English-language forum on the open web where stolen databases and [stealer logs](https://ransomnews.com/stealer-logs-explained-2026/) were traded from 2021. Police in 14 countries, led by the FBI and coordinated by Europol, [seized it](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/united-states-leads-dismantlement-one-worlds-largest-hacker-forums) on 3 and 4 March 2026, when it had more than 142,000 members. Russian police later detained its suspected administrator, and a site using the name [reappeared](https://spycloud.com/blog/cybercrime-update-19-bluekit-bitm-saas-breach-leakbase-return/) in late July 2026.

### In brief

* LeakBase ran in English on the open web from 2021 and specialised in leaked databases and stealer logs, [Europol says](https://www.europol.europa.eu/media-press/newsroom/news/major-data-leak-forum-dismantled-in-global-action-against-cybercrime-forum).
* The forum had more than 142,000 members and 215,000 private messages when police took its database and two of its domains, according to the US Justice Department.
* Russia’s Interior Ministry announced on 25 March 2026 that a Taganrog resident suspected of creating and running the forum was in custody, and it did not name him, [Mediazona reported](https://zona.media/news/2026/03/25/tgnrg).
* A site using the LeakBase name came back in late July 2026 under an administrator whose link to the original owner SpyCloud calls unclear.
* LeakBase.pw, a paid password-lookup service that [closed in December 2017](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2017/12/hacked-password-service-leakbase-goes-dark/), shares the name, and we found no documented connection between it and the forum.

## What is LeakBase?

LeakBase was a cybercrime forum on the ordinary web where members traded and gave away stolen databases and login credentials. The US Justice Department’s [announcement of the seizure](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/united-states-leads-dismantlement-one-worlds-largest-hacker-forums) described it as “available on the open web and in English” and said its archive held hundreds of millions of account credentials, along with card numbers, bank account and routing details, and other personal and business records.
