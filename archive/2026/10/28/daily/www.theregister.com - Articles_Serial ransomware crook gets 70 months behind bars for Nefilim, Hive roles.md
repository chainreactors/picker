Ukrainian ransomware crook Artem Stryzhak has been sentenced to 70 months in the slammer by a New York federal court for his role as an affiliate of the Nefilim and Hive ransomware gangs.

Prosecutors believe the cybercriminal began working with Nefilim in or around June 2021, while living in Barcelona, Spain.

The Nefilim ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) operation was known for targeting wealthy companies, with its operators encouraging affiliates to pursue businesses capable of paying substantial ransom demands.

REG AD

One of Nefilim's most high-profile attacks was on [Australian logistics giant Toll Group](https://www.theregister.com/security/2020/05/06/transport-biz-toll-group-suffers-second-ransomware-infection-in-just-three-months/638478) in 2020, encrypting its files roughly three months after it suffered a separate Mailto ransomware attack.

REG AD

Court [documents](https://regmedia.co.uk/2025/09/10/volodymyr_tymoshchuk_superseding_indictment.pdf) [PDF] state that Stryzhak agreed with alleged Nefilim boss [Volodymyr Tymoshchuk](https://www.theregister.com/security/2025/09/10/us-indicts-alleged-ransomware-boss-tied-to-18b-in-damages/1350934), who remains at large, to pay the ransomware crew a 20 percent cut of the ransom proceeds he collected.

Tymoshchuk told Stryzhak that his preference was for attacks to target companies based in the US, Canada, and Australia that turn over more than $100 million annually. This guidance was soon tweaked to encourage targeting companies in the same regions with more than $200 million in annual revenue.

Prosecutors claim that around the time Stryzhak joined forces with Nefilim, he also signed up to the Hive ransomware operation as "one of its earliest affiliates," and used its platform to attack approximately seven victims. Hive's operators claimed they targeted circa 1,500 victims worldwide between June 2021 and January 2023.
