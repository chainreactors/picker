# European Commission Plan to Give Frontex Trusted Flagger Status Is A Threat to Free Speech

![EU-flag-circuits](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/eu-flag-11.png?itok=cbBBK27b)

EFF and 27 other human rights organizations [called](https://www.eff.org/document/civil-society-letter-frontex-powers-under-dsa) on the European Commission today to retract a dangerous proposal granting its border agency Frontex power to request that national authorities issue takedown orders for social media content and giving Frontext “trusted flagger” status under the Digital Services Act (DSA), the EU regulation governing online platforms and content moderation in the region.

The extraordinarily improper proposal not only disregards the provisions of the DSA but also places a mechanism of enforcement overreach in the hands of a law enforcement agency that has [failed to safeguard people](https://www.hrw.org/news/2021/06/23/frontex-failing-protect-people-eu-borders) against serious human rights violations at EU borders.

Under the [proposal](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/document/regulation-amending-european-border-and-coast-guard-regulation_en), Frontex has the power to “monitor publicly accessible social media content, detect developments relevant to external border management, and support action against illegal online content linked to border-security risks.” Granting Frontex trusted flagger privileges on top of its existing mandate risks creating a powerful tool for over-enforcement, stifling the free expression of migrants, asylum seekers, and other marginalized groups. We warned the Commission of these risks in a [letter](https://www.eff.org/document/civil-society-letter-frontex-powers-under-dsa).

The DSA is supposed to make online platforms more accountable and protect users’ fundamental rights, not give the EU power to sic its border police on people for exercising their right to free expression.

EFF and other rights organizations have for years [sounded alarms](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/02/enforcement-overreach-could-turn-out-be-real-problem-eus-digital-services-act) about potential abuse under the DSA’s trusted flagger provision, which gives “Digital Services Coordinators” the ability to award entities—including law enforcement agencies—a privileged channel to alert online platforms about illegal content.
