The UK government is paving the way for rules requiring essential service operators and digital providers to guard against losing control of AI agents and report incidents when safeguards fail.

An amendment to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, which is [passing through Parliament](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/4035), would give ministers powers to regulate the deployment and reporting of agentic AI in certain organizations. The new clause was introduced during the bill's third reading in the House of Lords this week.

In [an explanatory note](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cyber-security-and-resilience-network-and-information-systems-bill-factsheets/agentic-ai-resilience-measures), ministers outlined plans for safeguards, notification of affected organizations, preservation of evidence, and government-led incident reviews. The measures would require consultation and parliamentary approval through secondary legislation.

REG AD

The move follows a series of incidents. Last month, OpenAI revealed that its [agents repeatedly strayed beyond their intended scope](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/02/openai-alerts-100-orgs-that-its-misaligned-models-attempted-to-break-in-or-worse/5300891), prompting the company to notify more than 100 organizations about the issues. That came after July's incident, in which [an OpenAI agent accessed the internet and breached machine learning platform Hugging Face](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/07/22/openai-admits-it-was-the-source-of-the-agent-swarm-that-attacked-hugging-face/5275939). The episode, and the potential for more damage from poorly designed AI agent deployments, have prompted [MPs to question](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/15/uk-keeps-ai-safeguards-in-the-maybe-pile-for-cyber-security-bill/5303026) whether UK law can deal with such threats.

REG AD

Speaking to The Register, Stuart Davey, a partner at law firm Pinsent Masons, described the clause as "quite novel." Rather than directly imposing new duties, it would allow ministers to introduce rules and investigatory powers covering regulated organizations. Davey said that could include powers "to look where there has been such an AI agentic compromise or issue, and do some things... appoint inspectors, enter and search premises, seize and retain documents." Under the proposals, the minister could confer certain powers on regulators, requiring action from targeted organizations.
