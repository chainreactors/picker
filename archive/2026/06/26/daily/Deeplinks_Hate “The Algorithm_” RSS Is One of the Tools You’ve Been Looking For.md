Poke your head into just about any online social network—or any general conversations about internet culture—and you’ll likely find a boogieman: the algorithm. Since at least the moment [Facebook introduced](https://mashable.com/archive/facebook-gets-egg-on-its-face-changes-news-feed-feature) ([and apologized for](https://web.archive.org/web/20061024024254/http%3A//blog.facebook.com/blog.php?post=2208197130)) its News Feed, “the algorithm” has been shorthand for the ways the tech giants control what we see and when we see it. In the age of enshittification, there is a push to reclaim our feeds and networks. Good news: there’s a tool that’s been around for decades that can help wrangle many of your feeds into something manageable: Really Simple Syndication, more commonly known as RSS.

## What’s RSS and How Do I Use It?

RSS has been around since 1999, but its real publicity glow-up [came from Google Reader](https://www.theverge.com/23778253/google-reader-death-2013-rss-social), a newsreader service that Google offered [between 2005 and 2013](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/company-announcements/a-second-spring-of-cleaning/). Despite the [alarm bells people rang at the time](https://www.wired.com/2013/06/why-google-reader-got-the-ax/), the death of Google Reader wasn’t the death of RSS, and many replacements have come and gone over the years.
