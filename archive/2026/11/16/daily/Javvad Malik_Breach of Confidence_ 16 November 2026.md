# Breach of Confidence: 16 November 2026

Written by

[j4vv4d](https://javvadmalik.com/author/j4vv4d/)

in

[blog](https://javvadmalik.com/category/blog/)

![Breach of Confidence](https://i0.wp.com/j4vv4d.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/breach_of_confidence_header.png?ssl=1)

Welcome to Breach of Confidence.

I spent forty minutes on hold this week while a recorded voice told me my call was important to them. If it were, I suspect they’d have answered it. Anyway, on to the news.

**Nvidia’s weakest link wears a suit**

Nvidia spends a fortune protecting its chips, but the BBC reports the more interesting target was the chief executive. Why burn a zero-day when a honey trap has been working since roughly the invention of secrets? No patch exists for loneliness and a flattering message.

<https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckgel338xql3o>

**Microsoft’s spam fix works beautifully, on the wrong people**

Microsoft’s answer to spam was a form. The spammers filled it in with great enthusiasm. Meanwhile Outlook.com has been bouncing legitimate email, and in my case that meant my dentist got blocked and I missed two appointments. Everything worked as intended, which is the most worrying sentence in this business.

<https://www.theregister.com/applications/2026/11/09/outlookcom-bounces-legit-emails-as-microsofts-authentication-crackdown-goes-sideways/5308428>

**Your car’s weakest link is the app**

Researchers have been pulling apart automakers’ Android apps and found they can unlock cars remotely and honk the horn. If you ask nicely enough, one will read your number plate out loud first, which is the most polite break-in I’ve heard of. The vehicle has all the steel and crumple zones, and the keys are sitting behind a login page written by someone on a deadline.

<https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/09/researchers-pwn-automakers-android-apps-to-remotely-unlock-cars-honk-horns/5308483>

**Turla gets a desk in the post room**

Turla didn’t bother with a safehouse. It moved into government Exchange servers, pulled up a chair in the post room and started sorting every message bound for NATO and EU addresses. It did this for years, with the new Ceral malware as its franking machine.

The traffic looked so normal that nobody thought to ask who’d added themselves to the guest list. You have to admire the commitment. Most of us can’t manage a fortnight in a job without being asked for our badge.

<https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/turla-hackers-use-new-ceral-malware-to-spy-on-govt-exchange-servers/>
<https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/turla-cereal-exchange-email/>

**Britain and France ask commercial hackers to be nice**

Britain and France have set out guidelines for commercial hacking firms. This isn’t a crackdown on spyware. It’s a “please be responsible” sticker in the window of a shop that sells lock picks to anyone with cash. I’m sure the customers will read it carefully, right before they pay.

<https://therecord.media/britain-france-set-out-guidelines-for-commercial-hacking-firms>

That’s your lot. If you’ve got a story I’ve missed, or a dentist I should be worried about, reply to this email or find me on Bluesky.

Stay patched, and never trust a car that says hello.
