# FIFA World Cup Triggers a Global Anti-Piracy Crackdown

today by
[Ernesto Van der Sar](https://torrentfreak.com/author/ernesto/)

[Home](https://torrentfreak.com "Go to TorrentFreak.") > [Piracy](https://torrentfreak.com/category/piracy/ "Go to the Piracy category archives.") >

During the FIFA World Cup, law enforcement and private partners across multiple continents have seized or blocked well over a thousand pirate streaming domains, arrested suspects, and cut off advertising revenue. Taken together, "Operation Offsides" and "Operación Tarjeta Roja" are part of the broadest anti-piracy effort ever carried out during a sporting event.

![fifa logo](https://torrentfreak.com/images/fofalo26-600x403.png)As the FIFA World Cup is gearing up for the grande finale in New York this weekend, international law enforcement offices look back at one of the largest anti-piracy efforts in sports history.

With help from private and public partners from all over the world, authorities seized well over a thousand domain names of pirate sports streaming sites. In the United States, the effort was branded [“Operation Offsides”](https://torrentfreak.com/feds-seize-domain-names-of-nearly-400-pirate-sports-streaming-sites/), which initially targeted nearly 400 domain names offering unauthorized World Cup streams. That turned out to be just the start.

## Operation Offsides

The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) [announced](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/united-states-seizes-hundreds-internet-domains-used-illegally-stream-world-cup-matches) Operation Offsides on June 26, targeting pirate sites that streamed World Cup matches. The seizure warrant was filed in the Eastern District of Virginia, but this appears to be sealed, as we were unable to locate a copy in the court database.

The DOJ and Homeland Security Investigations [ran a similar crackdown during the 2022 Qatar World Cup](https://torrentfreak.com/u-s-govt-seizes-domains-of-popular-sports-streaming-piracy-sites-221210/), seizing 78 domains at the time. The 2026 action is at least five times larger, however, and was the result of international cooperation.

The domains were identified with assistance from [FIFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA), beIN Media Group, NBCUniversal, [ACE](https://www.alliance4creativity.com/), the UFC, and Warner Bros. International coordination through the DOJ’s [ICHIP network](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-ccips/international-computer-hacking-and-intellectual-property-ichip-program) extended the operation’s reach to Peru and Bulgaria, described in the announcement as known centers of online piracy activity, with supporting actions in Croatia, Romania, Poland, and Colombia.

The authorities did not mention any domains by name but TorrentFreak traced many through passive DNS data, with the current tally at 627 identified domains (listed below). While the seizures hit several major sports streaming sites, other popular sites operating under foreign-registry TLDs outside U.S. jurisdiction managed to survive.

Visitors of the blocked domain names, meanwhile, ran into the following splash banner, listing many of the partners involved.

The “Operation Offsides” seizures came in waves. Last weekend, [more than 100 additional domains were seized](https://torrentfreak.com/u-s-seizes-more-pirate-sports-streaming-domains-but-iranian-fallbacks-remain/), many of them fallback domains that pirate sites had switched to after the initial round. That same report also revealed that several operators switched to Iranian .ir fallback domains, in an effort to switch to infrastructure that is harder for U.S. law enforcement to reach.

## Colombia Leads “Operation Red Card”

Two days before the World Cup final, Colombia’s Fiscalía General [announced](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2026/07/17/operacion-tarjeta-roja-ocho-paises-se-unieron-durante-el-mundial-2026-contra-el-streaming-ilegal-y-la-falsificacion-de-marcas/) the results of a separate multinational enforcement effort it had coordinated under the name “Operación Tarjeta Roja” (Operation Red Card).

The international action brought together prosecutors, law enforcement agencies, and public ministries from nine countries: Colombia, the United States, Ecuador, Peru, Paraguay, Argentina, the Dominican Republic, Brazil, and Chile, with support from private-sector audiovisual and sports organizations.

In Colombia alone, 1,140 domains and URLs used for unauthorized sports streaming were ‘suspended’. Across all participating countries, the total reached 1,840. Similar to the U.S. operation, the authorities did not name any domains.

“While the planet focused on the FIFA World Cup 2026, a group of nations went after the structures that profit from counterfeiting and theft of digital content,” said Fiscalía’s Raúl González Flechas, commenting on the news.

The Colombian operation, which targeted both counterfeiting and piracy, also resulted in 15 arrests. Four suspects in Soacha, near Bogotá, were [accused](https://www.eltiempo.com/bogota/megaoperativo-internacional-destapo-en-bogota-y-soacha-redes-de-cable-ilegal-y-falsificacion-de-marcas-por-el-mundial-3572066) of hacking into a telecom company’s platforms to offer cheap ‘pirated’ cable TV, posing as employees of the company. Two of the four accepted the charges.

Separately, 11 people running shops selling counterfeit World Cup merchandise were charged with trademark infringement and related offenses. Authorities seized 4,223 counterfeit sports items, 11,353 fake logos ready to be applied to garments, and 2,150 counterfeit Colombian Football Federation shields during raids in Bogotá, Itagüí, Cali, Acacías, and Bucaramanga.

## Argentina Blocks FútbolLibre

Argentina also contributed to Operación Tarjeta Roja earlier in the tournament. In early July, the country’s [UFECI](https://www.mpf.gob.ar/ufeci/) cybercrime unit [opened investigations](https://www.infobae.com/judiciales/2026/07/02/operacion-tarjeta-roja-ordenan-bloquear-el-acceso-a-sitios-que-transmiten-ilegalmente-los-partidos-del-mundial-2026/) against three platforms: Stella TV, Tele Latino, and sites associated with FútbolLibre, one of the most widely used pirate sports streaming brands in the country.

FútbolLibre had already been under investigation since 2022. This time, complaints from rightsholders DirecTV and Personal Flow provided additional evidence.

Three federal judges ordered [ENACOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ente_Nacional_de_Comunicaciones), Argentina’s communications regulator, and [NIC Argentina](https://nic.ar/), the country’s domain registry, to block access to 14 platforms and sites. However, as is often the case, there is no shortage of copycats using the same brands.
