# Japan’s education ministry discloses breach of government procurement system

Japan’s education ministry disclosed earlier this week that hackers had breached its government procurement system, potentially exposing records containing business information and email addresses.

The Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) said it detected the intrusion on October 1, nearly two months before publicly disclosing the incident. An investigation found that hackers may have obtained information stored in the system’s database.

The potentially compromised data includes nearly 1,700 email addresses and 164,000 records containing company names, the names of employees and other business information, the ministry said in a [statement](https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/mext_00012.html) Wednesday.

The affected system is used to publish information about government tenders, contracts and procurement opportunities. The potentially exposed business information relates to companies eligible to participate in the ministry’s bidding processes.

The ministry said it had blocked all communications between the affected system and internal and external networks and found no evidence that the potentially compromised information has been publicly disclosed or misused.

The incident was reported to Japan’s data protection authorities, and the ministry is continuing its investigation in coordination with relevant government agencies. It has not publicly identified the perpetrators or disclosed how they gained access to its systems.

“We will continue to investigate the incident in close cooperation with the relevant authorities and take appropriate action,” MEXT said.

This is the latest cybersecurity incident affecting major Japanese companies and public institutions in recent months.

Earlier this week, Japanese railway operator Keio Corporation [said](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/11/25/japan/keio-personal-data-leakage/) a ransomware attack in September may have compromised the personal information of more than 11 million customers, business partners, employees and job applicants. The ransomware group Qilin, believed to have Russian origins, claimed responsibility for the attack.

McDonald’s Japan also recently [disclosed](https://therecord.media/japan-mcdonalds-cyberattack-breach) a breach involving a database linked to its official mobile app. The compromised information included customers’ purchase histories, IP addresses, internal customer identification numbers and mobile device location data.

Earlier this month, the city of Osaka [disclosed](https://therecord.media/city-of-osaka-japan-email-data-breach) that a breach involving a third-party technology provider had exposed more than 1 million email addresses associated with an online platform for municipal services.

Other major Japanese companies that have reported cybersecurity incidents in recent months include telecommunications giant [KDDI](https://therecord.media/japan-cyber-breaches-aflac-sapporo-nidec-kddi), the Japanese unit of U.S. insurer [Aflac](https://therecord.media/22-million-impacted-aflac-breach), electronics manufacturer [Nidec](https://therecord.media/japan-cyber-breaches-aflac-sapporo-nidec-kddi) and brewer Sapporo Holdings. There is no indication that these incidents are connected.
