# Treasury's FinCEN takes systems offline after cybersecurity incident

The Treasury Department has taken some systems offline after a cyberattack, according to a notice posted Tuesday evening.

The department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network — commonly known as FinCEN — is “investigating a cybersecurity incident,” the notice posted at the top of the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) E-Filing System website said.

The incident “resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems,” according to the banner.

“As a proactive measure, FinCEN has taken some systems offline. The BSA E-Filing website remains operational; however, some services may be temporarily unavailable,” the agency said.

“FinCEN is working diligently to return all systems to normal operations as soon as possible.”

The incident was first [reported](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1041957/fincen-treasury-cybersecurity-incident) by The Verge. FinCEN said it is extending deadlines for filers affected by the outages. It urged financial institutions to call a hotline if they need to send suspicious activity reports to law enforcement.

A spokesperson for FinCEN said the FBI and Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency are involved in the investigation. Investigators currently believe there is no evidence of any breach of the Bank Secrecy Act or beneficial ownership databases.

No hackers have taken credit for the attack, but several Chinese state-backed groups were accused of breaching the Treasury Department multiple times in [2024](https://therecord.media/beijing-hackers-penetrated-treasury-systems) and [2025](https://therecord.media/office-comptroller-currency-email-hack-report).

Twelve Chinese nationals, including two officers with China’s Ministry of Public Security (MPS), [were charged](https://therecord.media/doj-charges-chinese-nationals-isoon-cyberattacks-treasury) for the 2024 Treasury breach — which caused outrage across the federal government because the hackers accessed computers used by then-Secretary Janet Yellen and then-Deputy Secretary Adewale Adeyemo.

The FinCEN incident capped a whirlwind Tuesday in which the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) and at least two other spy agencies reportedly [suffered](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/24/us/politics/spy-agencies-computer-network-breaches.html) their own breaches that exposed unclassified information.

The New York Times and NextGov [reported](https://x.com/ddimolfetta/status/2125383051018473884?s=46) that the hackers involved in the CIA breaches allegedly used [Artex](https://therecord.media/artex-ai-tool-fbi-flash-alert), a Chinese-made platform that can take any large language model and automate multiple phases of cyber intrusions.

The cyberattacks emerged just days after the FBI [said](https://therecord.media/artex-ai-tool-fbi-flash-alert) it is investigating its own breach revolving around a critical fugitive database used by U.S. law enforcement agencies.

U.S. federal agencies have been battered by hackers this year. The departments of [Defense](https://abcnews.com/Politics/pentagon-breach-exposed-sensitive-data-3-million-people/story?id=136832909), [Transportation](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/14/department-of-transportation-motus-senture-cyberattack-01119368), [Justice](https://therecord.media/doj-atf-cyberattack-qilin-ransomware) and [Agriculture](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/usda-grapples-security-breach-it-staff-take-multiple-systems-offline/417350/) have all dealt with outages or data breaches caused by cyberattacks. The federal bankruptcy courts were [impacted](https://therecord.media/federal-bankruptcy-courts-dealing-with-cyberattack-outage) by another cyberattack last month.
