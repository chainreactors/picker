# How India’s Ruling Party is Using AI to Boost Hate Speech in States Near Bangladesh

March 31, 2026

* [AI](/tag/ai)
* [India](/tag/india)

The video posted by a state branch of India’s ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) showed Assam chief minister Himanta Biswa Sarma [shooting an image of two men](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/9/ai-video-of-assam-cm-sarma-shooting-muslims-causes-outrage-in-india) in Muslim skull caps. “Foreigner-free Assam”, read one caption across the video. “Why did you not go to Pakistan?” said another.

![](https://www.bellingcat.com/app/uploads/2026/03/bjp_ai_image2-1200x675.jpg)

*Screenshots of the now-deleted video shared by BJP on Feb. 7 showing Assam Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma shooting an AI-generated version of INC leader Gaurav Gogoi (in a white skull cap) and another unidentified, bearded man. Source: BJP4Assam/X*

One of the men in the photo that Sarma was portrayed as shooting was Gaurav Gogoi, a leader of the Indian National Congress (INC), the BJP’s main competitor in Assam for the state’s upcoming legislative elections [next month](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/assam-assembly-election-2026-bjp-finalises-assam-seat-plan-pm-modi-to-address-3-rallies-in-april-11232426).

Gogoi has stated that [he is Hindu](https://x.com/GauravGogoiAsm/status/1436572797984145412) but enjoys visiting different religious sites and observing their norms. He has been photographed wearing traditional Muslim attire during religious occasions [such as Eid](https://www.instagram.com/p/DH2s1HoR5_i/).

But the image of him in the video shared by BJP Assam, wearing a casual singlet with a skull cap, was not one of those occasions.

Bellingcat has seen several dozen videos posted by the BJP that use generative artificial intelligence (AI) alongside anti-Muslim and anti-Bangladeshi messaging in the border states of Assam and West Bengal in December last year, ahead of legislative elections scheduled in both states for April.

![](https://www.bellingcat.com/app/uploads/2026/03/bjp_ai_image5-e1774958244897.jpg)

*Left: Original photo shared by Gogoi on Jun. 17, 2025. Right: An image shared by BJP Assam that was edited with AI to show Gogoi with a skull cap, beard and Quran. Source: gauravgogoiasm/Facebook, BJP4Bengal/Facebook*

Bellingcat analysed 499 social media posts containing photos and videos shared on Facebook, Instagram and X by the BJP’s official accounts in the two states for this time period, finding 194 posts that appeared to meet the United Nations’ [definition of hate speech](https://www.un.org/en/hate-speech/understanding-hate-speech/what-is-hate-speech): discriminating against persons or communities based on inherent characteristics such as religion and national origin. Of these, 31 (about one in six of the hateful posts) contained the obvious use of AI-generated imagery.

![visualization](https://public.flourish.studio/visualisation/28247135/thumbnail)

*Chart: Galen Reich*

These appear to be part of a larger pattern of politicians and parties globally using generative AI to amplify hateful or divisive content, particularly ahead of major political events such as elections.

Ahead of the New York City mayoral race last year, Andrew Cuomo’s official X account shared, then deleted, an [AI-generated video](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2025/oct/23/cuomo-zohran-mamdani-ai-ad) depicting Mamdani eating rice with his hands and a Black man in a keffiyeh shoplifting. In Italy, several opposition parties [complained](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/apr/18/italian-opposition-complaint-far-right-matteo-salvini-lega-racist-ai-images) to a communications watchdog after deputy prime minister Matteo Salvini’s League party published a series of AI-generated images depicting men of colour attacking women or police officers. And in the UK, videos by an [AI-generated rapper](https://www.thebureauinvestigates.com/stories/2026-03-12/danny-bones-meet-the-ai-rapper-funded-by-a-far-right-party) funded by the far-right Advance UK party, with lyrics targeting Muslims, were viewed millions of times.

## A Campaign of Hate

Both Assam and West Bengal share a border with Bangladesh. BJP, the [world’s largest political party](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/65537-largest-political-party-membership), is currently in power in Assam, where legislative elections are [scheduled on Apr. 9](https://www.indiatoday.in/elections/assembly/assam/schedule). West Bengal, which goes to the polls [on Apr. 23](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/kolkata/west-bengal-election-date-2026-announced-check-wb-assembly-election-schedule-polling-dates-result-date-10587138/), is governed by the Trinamool Congress (TMC).

![](https://www.bellingcat.com/app/uploads/2026/03/bjp_ai_image1.jpg)

*Map: Pooja Chaudhuri. Source: Goran tek-en, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0), via Wikimedia Commons*

Tensions between India and Bangladesh worsened after former Bangladeshi Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina, who [enjoys close ties with Delhi](https://www.theguardian.com/world/article/2024/sep/03/a-myopic-policy-indias-backing-of-ousted-bangladesh-leader-sheikh-hasina-leaves-it-in-a-bind), was [ousted in 2024 and fled to India](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/2024/08/07/bangladesh-overthrow-resignation-sheikh-hasina-footage-open-source/).

US-based international affairs expert Mohammed Zeeshan told Bellingcat that the “dehumanising and debasing” terminology used in India to refer to alleged illegal Bangladeshi immigrants, including by senior ministers, has caused resentment towards India in Bangladesh.

“The situation, in fact, was so bad that Hasina herself had subtly warned the Modi government in public statements that Indian domestic rhetoric was endangering Bangladeshi Hindus, who bore the brunt of that resentment,” Zeeshan said.
