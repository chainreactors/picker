Britain's privacy watchdog is getting a taste of its own medicine, with an independent review set to examine the culture, governance, and accountability surrounding the events that led to former Information Commissioner John Edwards' resignation.

Karon Monaghan KC will examine whether the regulator's culture and governance allowed problems to develop or made concerns harder to raise.

Edwards [quit in June](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/06/19/britains-privacy-watchdog-quits-after-poor-judgment-admission/5258926) after an independent workplace investigation found there was a "case to answer" over his behavior and that his conduct fell short of what was expected of a public official. He disagreed with some of its findings, but ultimately conceded that he had shown "poor judgment."

REG AD

That investigation, however, focused on Edwards alone. According to the [terms of reference](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/independent-review-of-culture-accountability-and-governance-at-the-information-commission/independent-review-of-culture-accountability-and-governance-at-the-information-commission-terms-of-reference) published on Thursday, it "did not consider the wider conditions in which that conduct occurred, and it was not designed to."

REG AD

The new review will cast a wider net. Monaghan wants to know whether staff could call out bad behavior further up the food chain, and what awaited those who tried.

It will also poke around the regulator's leadership and governance arrangements and its relationship with its sponsoring government department, including whether there were adequate ways for concerns to be escalated and those at the top to be challenged.

Current and former staff can give evidence confidentially and voluntarily, while Monaghan will be given access to relevant documents and existing material, including staff surveys and data on bullying, harassment and discrimination.

The ICO has confirmed it won't use confidentiality clauses in settlement agreements to prevent people from contributing to the review.

Monaghan decides which witnesses and documents are relevant, rather than the ICO or DCMS. Access remains subject to legal restrictions, and she must be told when material is withheld and why. She can speak to whoever she thinks is relevant and ask for the complaints, correspondence, and other paperwork she needs.
