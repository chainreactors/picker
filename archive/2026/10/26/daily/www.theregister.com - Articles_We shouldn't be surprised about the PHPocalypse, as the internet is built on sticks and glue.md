We've spent the past several decades using duct tape, string, and chewing gum to chain the world's computers together into this thing we call the internet, so there's really no good reason to be surprised that most of it is full of software containing vulnerabilities that are practically just as old.

Those platforms also let you subscribe to Kettle, so you are always notified when the latest episode goes live.

The world is still reeling from the [PHP2Shell](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/21/php-devs-publish-critical-bug-fix-as-site-owners-warned-pull-em-offline-if-youre-unpatched/5304519) revelations that hit the news cycle this week, and on The Kettle there's no better topic for host [Brandon Vigliarolo](https://www.theregister.com/author/brandon-vigliarolo), cybersecurity editor [Jessica Lyons](https://www.theregister.com/author/jessica-lyons), and FOSS reporter [Liam Proven](https://www.theregister.com/author/liam-proven) to tease apart.
