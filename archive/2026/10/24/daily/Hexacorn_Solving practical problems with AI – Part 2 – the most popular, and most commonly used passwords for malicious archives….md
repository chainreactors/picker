# Solving practical problems with AI – Part 2 – the most popular, and most commonly used passwords for malicious archives…

Posted on [2026-10-24](https://www.hexacorn.com/blog/2026/10/24/solving-practical-problems-with-ai-part-2-the-most-popular-and-most-commonly-used-passwords-for-malicious-archives/ "9:04 pm")  by  [adam](https://www.hexacorn.com/blog/author/adam/ "View all posts by adam")

I remember that a few years ago, when I still used Twitter, I asked an open-ended question there: what are the most common passwords used for malware archives?

This may sound silly, but there are a looooooot of the ‘default’ ones out there. And while at that time I got a few good answers, most of them circulated around the usual suspects:

* malware
* virus
* infected
* inf3cted
* etc.

and their variants.

Today, using AI, we can dig deeper.

Googling:

```
default malware sample passwords including infected virus malware "inf3cted"
```

adds a few possible candidates, but then we start asking more questions and we end up adding the following entries:

* [1337](https://github.com/LJ9859/Malware-Database)
* [mysubsarethebest](https://dl.malwarewatch.org/)
* [history](https://snorrefagerland.github.io/virushistory/)
* [pass](https://support.kaspersky.com/krs/3.0/DefaultArchivePasswords)
* god
* [123](https://bazaar.abuse.ch/sample/805963dc775b0107e60e83570e05ca7ef4f1c47b5537f850bd65819ff0d75667)
* 1234
* 123456
* 12345678
* 123456789
* 111111
* qwerty
* [virussign](http://freelist.virussign.com/freelist/)
* [8466](https://bazaar.abuse.ch/sample/a938f6ae441a83c2bd48e32d0dadefb40bb791a1d5abe4428f8b9126bc17a3a0/)
* infected\_pcap
* infected666 + the last character before the zip extension
* clean (this is for clean samples)
* velvet
* VelvetSweatshop (old, default password for Excel)
* [S1BinaryVault](https://www.reddit.com/r/SentinelOneXDR/comments/1mh27yc/downloading_a_threat_file/)
* p@ssword
* shipped
* invoice
* doc

The most powerful bit here is not the list of all the possible passwords we can generate, but the list of prompts that got us here. I don’t include them in this post, because I want to emphasize that it was not really AI that did all the work, but… me.
