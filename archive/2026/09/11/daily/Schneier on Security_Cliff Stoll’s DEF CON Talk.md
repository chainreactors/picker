@ Weather,

With regards,

> “I thought 40 years ago you had typewriters”

There were a few around but after 1973 they started disappearing as Personal Computers and dot matrix computers started taking over around 1977 with the Apple ][ doing “spread sheets” and early “Wordprocessing” then in the 1980’s 8bit micros for home computing kind of banged the nails in the lid of typewriters. With students writing their homework inside the editor that the command interface that BASIC provided.

But I suspect you don’t mean “typewriters” but “teletypes” hung of serial lines using either Baudot or from early 1960’s onwards RS232 [1]. These electromechanical beasts such as the KSR and ASR machines, gave us /tty in Unix device driver naming.

Untill not so long ago I still had one but “somebody made me an offer” so they could complete a working historical set piece for a museum display. The scary thing was I also provided boxes of consumables of paper rolls, ink tapes and 7hole punch paper tape…

[1] The electrical interface was +-80V for 5bit Baudot, and +-12V for 7bit RS232. One of the first development jobs I did in the Army unit was to design and build a “converter” based around a Z80 8bit CPU and a circuit not unlike small CP/M machines that also had a Texas TCM3105 modem chip with both a 4wire and 2wire interface so you could connect to just about any serial interface you would come across including “Post Office” lines (oh and VHF/UHF FM radios. Whilst I still have the CCT Diagrams and assembler listings, I don’t think I’ve still got a working unit in the loft scrap box, as I was later developing a MUX unit to connect several 75baud teletypes into a single line and needed some of the bits.

Oh the TCM3105 circuit diagram is a rip-off of an App-Note Data sheet very like,

‘https://freecircuitdiagram.com/521-bell-202-and-ccitt-v23-compatible-fsk-modem-circuit/

And yes the transformer is important otherwise the 2wire to 4wire conversion won’t work (technically if forms part of a balanced bridge circuit).
