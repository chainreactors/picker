An Electrolux-branded Wi-Fi boiler bought in Ukraine was logged connecting to a server in Moscow, and the Russian app behind the connection lists the model sold in EU shops by name, [research published by Mysterium VPN](https://www.mysteriumvpn.com/blog/data-and-research/electrolux-smart-boiler-moscow-server) shows. The boiler is made under licence by a Swiss company whose own app says it keeps data in Frankfurt.

### In brief

* The owner’s router recorded his Electrolux EWH 80 looking up mqtt.cloud.rusklimat.ru on 23 November 2026 and trying to connect to it.
* Mysterium VPN traced that name to 130.193.49.173, an address registered to Yandex.Cloud LLC in Moscow.
* The owner had set the boiler up with Hommyn, an app from the Russian appliance group Rusklimat. The manual names a different app, ClimatOn.
* Hommyn’s Google Play page shows an Electrolux boiler, and its privacy policy says user data is stored in Russia.
* Owners can search their router’s log for the word rusklimat and block the address. The boiler heats water without Wi-Fi.

## What did Mysterium VPN find?

Mysterium VPN found that the same boiler works with two apps, and the app decides which country it reports to. The researchers started from [a Facebook post by Alexander Unger](https://www.facebook.com/ungewissheit/posts/%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%B9%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BB-%D1%83%D0%BC%D0%BD%D1%8B%D0%B9-%D0%B1%D0%BE%D0%B9%D0%BB%D0%B5%D1%80-electrolux-ewh-80-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B5-%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%8C-%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%BD%D1%8B%D0%B5-%D0%B2-%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B2%D1%83-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%B0/28991670663837762/), who bought an Electrolux EWH 80 water heater from the Ukrainian retailer Rozetka in 2025. His router logged the boiler looking up mqtt.cloud.rusklimat.ru at 18:00 on 23 November 2026 and trying to connect to it.
