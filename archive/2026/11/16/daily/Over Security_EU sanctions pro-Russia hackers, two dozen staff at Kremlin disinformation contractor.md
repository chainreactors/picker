# EU sanctions pro-Russia hackers, two dozen staff at Kremlin disinformation contractor

The European Union on Monday sanctioned a pro-Russian hacktivist group and two dozen employees of the Moscow firm behind the Doppelgänger disinformation campaign — including many who had already been designated elsewhere.

In total, the [decision](http://data.europa.eu/eli/dec/2026/2556/oj) adds 31 names to the bloc’s regime targeting Russian destabilization activities, including the Social Design Agency (SDA) itself, 24 of its staff and six other pro-Kremlin commentators. Britain had already sanctioned 22 of the SDA employees, and Canada two of the media figures.

While the SDA, the propaganda outfit behind Doppelgänger, has been under other European sanctions [since 2023](https://therecord.media/eu-sanctions-russian-operation-rnn-media) over the war in Ukraine, the new listing adds it to a regime aimed at tackling operations against the EU itself.

While the practical effects are similar, the new sanctions come under a different legal regime that gives Brussels extra tools it could use against the SDA in the future, including bans on transactions involving physical infrastructure such as servers and network equipment.

The European Council also formally alleged that the SDA’s major foreign interference operations require sign-off from senior Kremlin figure Sergey Kiriyenko, the first deputy chief of staff in Russia’s presidential administration, while officials including Sofia Zakharova oversee its daily work.

Kiriyenko was first [identified](https://therecord.media/doj-seizes-russian-disinfo-domains-election) by U.S. prosecutors in 2024, when the Department of Justice [seized domains](https://therecord.media/doj-seizes-russian-disinfo-domains-election) that it alleged were linked to the agency’s attempts to interfere in that year’s presidential election.

Echoing [longstanding reporting](https://therecord.media/russians-fake-news-anti-ukraine) on Doppelgänger, the EU accused the SDA of cloning news websites, creating Wikipedia-style platforms and staging physical provocations, including around the European parliamentary elections in 2024.

The EU listings name Andrey Perla as the SDA’s “chief ideologue” alongside other staff at the agency including caricaturists, monitors, a system administrator and the editor of the “Pulse” system — an internal network of Telegram channels for monitoring and aggregating news, as [examined](https://mpf.se/download/18.7cffbee41969f6d83e115221/1747230166207/Beyond%20Operation%20Doppelganger.pdf) by Sweden’s Psychological Defense Agency.

The six others are part of the wider machinery behind Moscow’s narratives abroad, from state media to covert networks and wartime bloggers.

Chay Bowes, an Irish correspondent for RT, is accused of amplifying Storm-1516, a disinformation operation that has included [impersonating](https://therecord.media/russia-group-spoofing-journalists-disinfo) European journalists to spread inflammatory content. Sputnik journalist Aleksey Stefanov was also listed over funding for his Baltic Telegram channels from the Foundation for the Support and Protection of the Rights of Compatriots Living Abroad (Pravfond), which the EU [sanctioned](https://therecord.media/eu-targets-russian-propaganda-networks-sanctions) in April.

Television hosts Vitaliy Eliseev and Petr Marchenko were also listed, alongside military bloggers Yurii Podoliaka, who the EU says raised over 1 billion rubles ($12 million) for Russian forces, and Daniil Bezsonov, a former separatist official in Donetsk.

The European Council separately added NoName057(16) — a pro-Russian hacktivist group — to its cyber sanctions list over what it described as denial-of-service attacks targeting critical infrastructure and election candidates.

U.S. prosecutors [unveiled charges](https://therecord.media/us-extradites-member-of-russian-hacking-groups-critical-infrastructure) against an alleged NoName057(16) member in September, and researchers recently tied the group to a campaign in which hackers [published](https://therecord.media/hackers-russia-italy-noname) the personal information of senior European officials on the dark web, offering cryptocurrency rewards for additional details about them and their families.
