# Russia denies intercepting Romanian government emails

Russia has denied accusations that its state-backed hackers targeted a Romanian government institution in an attempt to intercept emails exchanged with NATO and the European Union.

The Russian Embassy in Bucharest on Monday dismissed the allegations as fabricated, accusing Romanian intelligence services of spreading false claims about Moscow's involvement in cyberattacks.

"This is yet another fabrication by the Romanian intelligence services, part of their ongoing narrative about Russia's mythical 'hybrid attacks,'" the embassy said in a statement [published](https://t.me/ambasadarusiei/6845) on Telegram in response to questions from Reuters.

The embassy accused Romanian authorities of trying to discredit Russia and said Bucharest had provided no concrete evidence to support its claims.

The denial comes days after Romania's domestic intelligence agency (SRI) [said](https://therecord.media/romania-russia-cyberattack-emails) it had detected and stopped a cyberattack attributed to Turla, a long-running Russian espionage group linked to Moscow's Federal Security Service (FSB).

According to the agency, the hackers infiltrated the systems of an unidentified government institution in December 2025 by exploiting vulnerabilities in Microsoft software.

Romanian cybersecurity specialists contained the intrusion and restored the affected email service, the agency said, adding that investigators had found no evidence that sensitive information had been stolen.

The disclosure followed [research](https://therecord.media/russia-hackers-espionage-romania-kazakhstan) published earlier this month by Slovak cybersecurity firm ESET, which uncovered a previously unknown hacking toolkit used by Turla to compromise government email servers in Romania and Kazakhstan.

According to ESET, the malware deployed against the Romanian target was specifically configured to collect emails exchanged with NATO and EU institutions.

Romania's intelligence agency said it was also investigating other potentially related cyber operations linked to Russia.

Moscow has for years denied accusations of carrying out cyberattacks and other hostile operations against Western countries.
