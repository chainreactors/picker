The worst problems don't always come with a warning. Sometimes they arrive through a service everyone trusts, a device someone bought, or a system nobody thought to check. By the time something looks wrong, the damage may already be done.

This week had plenty of that. Trusted connections became a problem. Hardware wasn't as safe as it looked. Old bugs found new victims, and some systems were left so exposed that breaking in barely required any effort. Even AI agents crossed lines they weren't supposed to.

Some attacks were clever. Others were painfully simple. The full recap shows just how much can go wrong, and how little it sometimes takes.

## **⚡ Threat of the Week**

**[ConnectWise Suspends Cloud Backup Following Unauthorized Access](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/connectwise-suspends-cloud-backup-after.html)** — ConnectWise disabled seven ConnectWise application identities as it continues its investigation into an incident in which unknown threat actors obtained unauthorized access to the application identities and compromised Microsoft 365 tenants. The IT management software provider said it took the action on October 17, 2026, at 07:00 UTC, adding it has also suspended Cloud Backup service by revoking certificates used for affected application connections across all users.

## **🔔 Top News**

* **[BeyondTrust Remote Support Flaw Exploited as 0-Day](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/china-linked-hackers-exploited.html)** — The China-nexus cyber espionage group tracked as Armory Castle (previously UNC5221 or Warp Panda) exploited a newly disclosed security flaw in BeyondTrust Remote Support (formerly Bomgar) as a zero-day in late August 2026. The vulnerability, tracked as CVE-2026-102764 (CVSS score: 9.3), is a case of improper origin validation in an internal messaging component that could be exploited by a remote attacker to perform file operations on an affected appliance. According to Google-owned Mandiant, the attack involved the deployment of a lightweight PHP web shell codenamed SAWFLY for interactive remote access and a tunneling program called BARECLAW to route traffic through the device. Only a small number of organizations are assessed to be impacted.
* **[Microsoft Disrupts 3 Phishing Kits Linked to Over 1 Billion Scam Messages](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/microsoft-disrupts-3-phishing-kits.html)** — Microsoft says it has dismantled a criminal marketplace that sold three phishing kits, namely CoGUI (aka NX or FA), Haozi (aka Phoenix), and Iron Man System, that were responsible for sending over 1 billion malicious messages to more than 75,000 organizations worldwide. Redmond said it took down Yam Store (aka Storm-3119), a coordinated underground network that offered commercial phishing-as-a-service (PhaaS) toolkits that allowed other threat actors to readily purchase and deploy phishing pages and steal data and credentials. The kits were also capable of advanced evasion, such as geofencing, fake CAPTCHA gates, IP address blocklists and allowlists, and anti-detection configurations. Yam Store functioned as an initial access broker, peddling not just phishing kits but also infrastructure built on compromised or fraudulently obtained Microsoft and Google cloud environments.
* **[Ledger Finds Unauthorized Hardware Implants](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/ledger-finds-unauthorized-hardware.html)** — Cryptocurrency hardware maker Ledger temporarily paused sales of its products via CryptoBilis and other third-party resellers in Southeast Asia and Germany after a backdoor embedded into a handful of wallets led to the theft of an [estimated $100 million in cryptocurrency](https://www.zetter-zeroday.com/who-is-planting-backdoors-in-ledger-crypto-wallets/). The implants are said to be an advanced version of a backdoor that hardware hacker Joe Grand found earlier this year and notified Ledger in March 2026. It's not clear how this occurred, but signs point to a compromise of Ledger's supply chain. One of the victims of the incident is a fraudster who engaged in a task scam and lost more than $4.8 million. "In this task scam, people were paid to fake sales and reviews of real items," Chainalysis [said](https://x.com/chainalysis/status/2110748973971468382). "Then a 'negative balance' appeared, and they were told to add their own money in order to get paid. On Sep 22, the person behind the scam moved the proceeds into three separate Ledger addresses. On Oct 9, thieves emptied all three. One transfer each, into the same wallet." According to cybersecurity journalist Kim Zetter, Trezor wallet devices sold by third-party non-authorized sellers have also been found to [contain implants](https://www.zetter-zeroday.com/trezor-wallets-also-compromised-with-implants/) that generated pre-determined seed phrases so as to allow the attackers to guess them easily and gain access to the accounts.
