# Why VPNs Are Worth Defending

![A highway sign shows the upcoming turns for different IP addresses](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/vpn-highway-tunnel-banner.jpg?itok=oU05fh98)

Developers of [virtual private network (VPN)](https://ssd.eff.org/module/choosing-vpn-thats-right-you) apps are [reporting](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/09/24/russia-crackdown-on-bypassing-online-censorship) that their products are again being pulled from Apple’s App Store in Russia. This is an unacceptable crackdown on a vital tool to protect privacy and speech online.

Why? VPNs give people a way to control their own online activities. When you visit a website through a VPN, that website only sees the VPN server's location (well, IP address) rather than your actual location. It's like sending a letter through a P.O. box so the recipient doesn't know where you really live. It can also disguise where you’re accessing information from, [which can make it possible](https://ssd.eff.org/module/understanding-and-circumventing-network-censorship) to access information banned by your government.

This is an unacceptable crackdown on a vital tool to protect privacy and speech online.

Going after tools that give people the space to communicate and access information freely is a common tactic from the totalitarian playbook. This latest demand comes after several steps the Russian government’s taken to surveil speech specifically on Apple products. In 2024, the [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2024/07/04/russia-forces-apple-to-remove-vpns-from-app-store-a85608) reported that Russia’s state media watchdog, Roskomnadzor, went after VPN apps on the Apple App Store, after banning access to Western social media platforms and blacklisting opposition media sites. Russia then [blocked Apple’s FaceTime software](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/12/04/russia-shutters-facetime-as-it-tightens-control-over-apps-communication) in 2025, according to Apple Insider.
