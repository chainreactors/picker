# DOJ, DHS partner on new unit tackling scams and cybercrime

The Department of Justice and Department of Homeland Security announced Thursday the creation of a new task force aimed at disrupting the flow of victim funds to foreign criminal networks powering scams, cybercrime and sextortion schemes that target Americans.

The departments are partnering on the Threat Disruption Center to better triage victim complaints, map cybercriminal networks and provide a direct channel to private sector organizations that work with victims.

Senior FBI official Heith Janke said Threat Disruption Center members have already worked together on efforts to warn thousands of victims before they lose money to scammers, and the center will allow them to better coordinate their efforts.

“The Threat Disruption Center builds on those efforts by providing advanced tools and operational capabilities that enable faster action at greater scale, strengthening the work of every agency on the task force,” Janke said.

TRM Labs, which is providing technology support for the effort, said that the center has already moved to take down more than 1,600 scam websites, added more than 6,600 scam-linked cryptocurrency wallet addresses to a screening network and identified 101 new fraud networks for investigation.

The center is part of the federal government’s efforts to implement an [executive order issued in March](https://therecord.media/white-house-floats-victim-restoration-program-cybercrime-executive-order) centered around stopping cyber scams, which continue to siphon billions of dollars each year from unwitting Americans.

Cyber scam losses reported to the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) grew to $20.9 billion in 2025 — a number that does not account for the thousands of people who never file victim reports.

The staggering losses have prompted multiple concurrent efforts by the federal government and Congress to [disrupt the scams](https://therecord.media/strike-force-southeast-asia-scams) and provide better avenues for victims to [report crimes](https://therecord.media/treasury-urges-banks-report-cyber-scams).

Much of the center's work will focus on collaborating with tech companies that get a firsthand view on criminal network activity because of how many gangs use social media platforms, web hosting, domain infrastructure, cryptocurrency services and banking systems.

“The Center establishes a standing mechanism for law enforcement and participating companies to appropriately share intelligence in near real-time, and convert that intelligence into action, for example, by pausing victim payments; taking down malicious web domains; or banning social media accounts used by scammers,” federal officials said in a statement.

“Participating private-sector companies can take these actions independently under their terms of service, while law enforcement agencies proceed with investigations, prosecutions, and court-authorized seizures.”

The center is being housed within the Homeland Security Task Force — a permanent interagency law enforcement network of more than 30 federal agencies and over 440 state and local partners that focus on transnational criminal organizations, cartels and foreign terrorist organizations.

## Prince Group sanctions

The initiative is just one part of a multi-prong effort the U.S. is undertaking to address cyber scams. On Thursday, for example, the Treasury Department issued more sanctions on scam operators in Myanmar and affiliates of the Prince Group, a Cambodian company accused of managing an empire of scam centers across Southeast Asia.

The sanctions include Zhao Hui, a Cambodian national who leased a scam center from Prince Group and paid the company fees to run the compound. U.S. officials accused Zhao of being directly involved in the scam center’s operations — which targeted Americans and Europeans with cryptocurrency investment scams.

Two of Hui’s companies, Bei Fang Bo Lin Construction Decoration and ZHZ Investment, are included in the sanctions.

The Treasury Department also sanctioned two leaders running the Democratic Karen Benevolent Army (DKBA), an armed group backing scam centers located along the Thailand-Myanmar border.

General Saw Shwe Wah is the commander-in-chief of the DKBA and Colonel Saw A One is a commander who runs the Three Pagodas Pass in Karen State near the Thai border. Both own land along the border and provide security to scam compound operators.

"These scammers rarely stop after one payment — they drain a victim's savings, or their retirement, over months and years," Esteban Castaño, CEO of TRM Labs, said of the DOJ and DHS joint venture. "Stepping in sooner changes everything: a warning before the next payment, a scam website removed before it victimizes another family, or stolen funds frozen while they can still be recovered. That's the difference between a recoverable setback and a loss from which an entire family may never recover.”
