# Google Forcing AI on Students Is BS

![](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/og-studentprivacygoogle_0.png?itok=VTfCs_rt)

Google is rightfully facing blowback for forcing K-12 schools to enable AI if they wanted to keep using Google search. The company quickly backtracked and decoupled and turned off the services by default, but made it clear search will continue to be just another AI chatbot. This mandate forces an ultimatum on educators: accept generative AI or de-google your school.

Students, teachers, and parents across the U.S. are now years into an argument about how schools should respond to the growth of AI-enabled tools. Google decided to skip this debate and take this choice from schools. In August it activated AI chatbot Gemini [for 150 million K-12 students](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/08/gemini-in-google-classroom-is-expanding-to-users-of-all-ages-with-contextualized-Gemini-starter-prompts-for-students.html) on Google Classroom, leaving educators [scrambling to respond](https://www.edweek.org/technology/schools-caught-flat-footed-after-google-makes-gemini-chatbot-available-to-all-students/2026/09). A few months later, Google introduced a new way to turn it off — but now administrators [would also be turning off Google search](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/03/us/google-search-schools-restrict-ai.html?unlocked_article_code=1.NlE.VMKr.Cr-g-C6phyfH).

Google [walked this back](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/google-responds-to-outrage-over-removing-search-in-schools-that-ban-ai.html) somewhat, clarifying that Gemini, Gemini notebook, and Search will have their own controls and will be off by default. But because of Google’s broader approach to force AI on all search users, this change is still an example of a tech monopolist pushing unwanted changes into the classroom.

## Tying AI to Search

It’s no secret that the biggest companies in AI  are [operating at a huge loss](https://www.forbes.com/sites/eriksherman/2026/10/30/ai-sees-a-first-shake-out-as-worries-build/), in the hope they can follow [Uber’s enshittification playbook](https://locusmag.com/feature/cory-doctorow-no-one-is-the-enshittifier-of-their-own-story/). Offer a cheap convenience [at a major loss](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2026-07-23/alphabets-205-billion-ai-spending-plan-spooks-investors) until old systems atrophy, then flip the script to reduce wages and raise consumer costs—whether that’s paying with cash or with data.

This puts Google in the odd position of an incumbent, with around [90% of search traffic](https://www.searchengines.net/guides/search-engine-market-share-by-country/). It needs to not only make good on its own AI investment, but protect its dominance from new AI tools.
