Smarter SOC Spending in 2027: Reduce Costs, Recover Capacity, Strengthen Detection

Cybersecurity budget planning for 2027 presents a growing challenge: security leaders must address emerging threats, expanding infrastructure, and increasing SOC workloads while keeping costs under control.

According to [IANS and Artico Search](https://www.ians.com/blog/security-budgets-stall-as-ai-reshapes-2027-spending), 55% of CISOs reported flat or declining security budgets in 2026. Meanwhile, [PwC’s 2027 Global Digital Trust Insights](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/consulting/cybersecurity-data-tech-risk/library/global-digital-trust-insights.html) found that 84% of surveyed security and finance leaders expect cybersecurity budgets to increase.

For decision-makers, the priority is to determine which investments can reduce operational costs, improve threat detection, and help existing security teams handle growing demands.

This guide explains how to assess your current SOC spending, identify opportunities to reduce operational costs, calculate potential savings, and make smarter investment decisions for your 2027 security budget.

## 1. Establish Your 2027 SOC Budget Baseline

Before allocating funds to new investments, determine how much it will cost to maintain your existing security operations in 2027.

Start by calculating projected 2026 spending:

* **2026 projected spending = Actual expenses YTD + Remaining committed expenses + Forecast uncommitted expenses**

Avoid double-counting expenses already recorded. Separate recurring commitments, usage-based charges, and one-time costs that won’t carry into 2027.

### Forecast Changes in SOC Spending

Review current contracts and expected operational demand to identify potential cost increases.

| SOC budget area | Key cost drivers |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Personnel | Analyst salaries, staffing requirements, training |
| SIEM and monitoring | Log ingestion, retention, licensing |
| Investigation and response | Alert volume, analysis services, external support |
| Threat intelligence | Subscriptions, data feeds, API usage |
| Integrations and maintenance | Implementation, technical support, infrastructure |

For example, if your SIEM processes 900 GB of logs daily and ingestion is expected to grow by 25%, your 2027 requirement will reach 1,125 GB/day. If your contract covers only 1,000 GB/day, estimate the additional costs using your vendor’s pricing model.

Account for contract renewals, expected price increases, and planned infrastructure changes.

**Your planning output:** A 2027 SOC spending baseline separating existing commitments, projected cost increases, and new investment requirements.

## 2. Identify Where Your SOC Is Losing Time and Money

Manual investigations, repeated IOC research, and unnecessary escalations can consume hundreds of analyst hours each month. Understanding these costs helps identify where your 2027 budget could deliver greater value.

### Measure Your SOC’s Operational Costs

Use SIEM, SOAR, and case-management data to identify the processes consuming the most resources.

| SOC process | What to measure | Potential cost driver |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alert triage | Average time to verdict | Manual validation and repetitive analysis |
| Malware investigation | Analyst handling time per case | Complex attack chains and evidence collection |
| Threat intelligence | IOC enrichment time | Repeated searches and infrastructure research |
| Escalations | Tier 2 handling hours | Incomplete findings and duplicated investigations |
| Detection engineering | Hours spent maintaining detection rules | Manual IOC collection and correlation |
