**Source base:** Research, translated Chinese publications, uploaded documents, and investigative findings accumulated in the PRC project.

### Executive assessment

China is developing the doctrinal, technical, and organizational foundations for LLM-enabled cognitive warfare. The evidence points toward an emerging model of information operations that combines population surveillance, behavioral profiling, synthetic-content generation, autonomous agents, and continuous assessment of audience reactions. This differs from traditional propaganda. The proposed architecture does not simply distribute a centrally approved message to a large audience. It seeks to understand individual and collective cognitive characteristics, tailor information to those characteristics, influence behavior through sustained interaction, and modify subsequent messaging according to observed results.

In People’s Liberation Army (PLA) military doctrine, cognitive warfare, or cognitive-domain operations (认知域作战), seeks to influence an adversary’s perceptions, beliefs, emotions, judgment, and decision-making to achieve strategic and military objectives. Chinese military theorists regard the cognitive domain as a battle-space alongside land, sea, air, space, cyberspace, and the electromagnetic spectrum, where controlling information and shaping how populations interpret events can weaken an adversary’s political cohesion, public confidence, and willingness to fight.

[Cognitive warfare](https://www.ahdjbh.com/dengbaozixun/997.html) extends beyond traditional propaganda and psychological operations by exploiting social media, information algorithms, artificial intelligence, behavioral profiling, and increasingly large language models (LLMs) to identify audiences, manipulate information exposure, construct persuasive narratives, and induce desired reactions. PLA publications describe integrating these activities with conventional military operations while employing civilian institutions, commercial technology platforms, and information networks to extend their reach.

The intended effect is not necessarily to convince an adversary of a particular falsehood, but to *shape the information environment and cognitive processes through which individuals and societies understand events, make decisions, and respond to conflict*. In this framework, cognitive warfare aims to degrade an adversary’s ability to make independent, coherent decisions while advancing Chinese political and military objectives, potentially achieving strategic effects before conventional hostilities begin.

The project’s evidence establishes three distinct developments.

* **First, the People’s Liberation Army has articulated a theory of algorithmic cognitive warfare.** Chinese military publications describe AI and algorithms as mechanisms for influencing perception, exploiting psychological vulnerabilities, and shaping political decisions. The 2023 paper *Mechanisms of Effectiveness and Key Technologies of Algorithmic Cognitive Warfare* provides one of the clearest descriptions of this approach.

* **Second, Chinese researchers are extending that theory into LLM-driven cognitive agents and multi-agent simulation.** Publications from 2024 through 2026 describe systems capable of modeling social behavior, generating personalized narratives, maintaining persistent digital identities, coordinating multiple agents, and evaluating influence strategies through simulation.

* **Third, commercial and government-linked capabilities increasingly overlap with the proposed military architecture.** Chinese firms have developed or advertised social-media monitoring, behavioral analysis, information recommendation, synthetic-content generation, and cognitive decision-support systems. Separately, public threat-intelligence reporting has documented Chinese-linked influence operations using generative AI.

The most important intelligence distinction is that **the individual capabilities are documented, but their integration into a single, deployed PLA or Ministry of State Security cognitive-warfare platform remains unverified.**

# 1. The doctrinal foundation

The PRC research collected in this project describes a progression from psychological warfare and public-opinion warfare toward algorithmic cognitive warfare.

The central shift is from influencing an audience through information to influencing how that audience discovers, interprets, discusses, and acts upon information.

Four publications are especially important.

### 1.1. Intelligent communication as a cognitive-domain weapon, 2023

**Chinese title:** 《智能传播：认知域作战的重要场域》
**Author:** 李晓阳, Li Xiaoyang
**Publication:** *PLA Daily*, April 18, 2023.

[Original Ministry of National Defense publication](https://www.mod.gov.cn/gfbw/jmsd/index.html)

This publication, previously translated and analyzed in the project, describes intelligent communication as an important environment for cognitive-domain operations.

Its principal concepts include algorithmic control of information exposure, social-media bots, synthetic media, audience profiling, information cocoons, emotional manipulation, and behavioral influence.

The article treats modern communication platforms as more than distribution infrastructure. Their algorithms become mechanisms capable of influencing the cognitive processes through which audiences form judgments.

**Intelligence significance:** The article provides a PLA-affiliated conceptual justification for exploiting commercial information systems as instruments of cognitive influence.
