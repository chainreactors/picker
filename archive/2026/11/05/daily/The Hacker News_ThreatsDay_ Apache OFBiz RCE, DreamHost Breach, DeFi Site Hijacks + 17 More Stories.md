How much damage can someone do without breaking much? Quite a lot, it seems. A stolen login can open doors across several systems. A trusted setting can be used to lock people out. Even a simple file copy can reach places it shouldn't. And while new tools are helping find security holes faster, some important systems are still taking months, sometimes over a year, to get patched. Not exactly a great match.

The stranger part is how little some attacks now need. Ransom demands without encrypted files. Fake websites that look real enough to steal money. AI tools picking up dangerous instructions while trying to help. Even the attackers are making mistakes in their own code.

This week's stories are a reminder that the biggest problems don't always come from clever tricks. Sometimes the easy way works just fine. Here's what else happened.

**The threats change every week. Subscribe, and we’ll alert you when each new ThreatsDay Bulletin is out.**

1. AI Accelerates Zero-Day Discovery

   [NCSC Warns of AI-Enabled Attacks](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/report/artificial-intelligence-and-cyber-intrusion)

   The U.K. National Cyber Security Center (NCSC) has warned that the widespread availability of AI-powered cyber tools has "almost certainly" led to a significant increase in the discovery of zero-day vulnerabilities. "Defensive AI-cyber tools have reduced the window of a vulnerability moving from zero-day to an n-day vulnerability, and have made patching a quicker process," NCSC [said](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/report/artificial-intelligence-and-cyber-intrusion). "However, without correct and widespread configuring of this technology, many systems are still vulnerable to attackers using AI-cyber tools to identify and exploit system weaknesses. Additionally, an organisation’s internal patching and testing processes will need to evolve to meet the pace of accelerating vulnerability discovery. Attackers have subsequently displayed less caution over losing an access to achieve an objective, as the cost to replace is minimal."
2. Critical Systems Face Prolonged Exposure

   [Network Appliances Go More Than a Year Without Patches](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/02/exploited-vulnerabilities-common-data-driven-analysis-real-world-attacks/)

   An analysis of roughly 383,000 CVEs disclosed from 1999 through September 2026 has found that the median time to patch a vulnerability exceeds a year for network appliances. About 2,000 CVEs (roughly 0.5%) have been linked to real-world exploitation. "The median vulnerability is patched in about two weeks on Windows workstations but in more than a month on Windows Servers," Microsoft [said](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/02/exploited-vulnerabilities-common-data-driven-analysis-real-world-attacks/). "It stretches to 10+ weeks on Linux Servers and for applications running on macOS, and beyond a year on network appliances. That gap is the threat actor's window, widest on the critical systems that stay exposed the longest." The tech giant also said threat actors disproportionately target flaws that maximize impact and exposure and enable system takeover, particularly remote code execution, privilege escalation, and authentication bypass.
3. $4.5 Million Fraud Network Busted

   [Investment Fraud Scheme Dismantled in Europe](https://www.eurojust.europa.eu/news/international-investment-fraud-scheme-dismantled-seven-arrests)

   Authorities have [arrested seven people](https://www.eurojust.europa.eu/news/international-investment-fraud-scheme-dismantled-seven-arrests) allegedly involved in an investment fraud scheme that claimed potential victims in more than 50 countries, according to Europol. The operation caused estimated losses of at least $4.5 million. The "crime-as-a-service" offering was run by a company from Cyprus and Israel, and allegedly provided cybercrime groups with an assortment of trading websites and fraudulent call centers tasked with tricking victims into transferring their savings. These call centers operated from Albania, Kosovo, and North Macedonia. "More than 100 servers were seized and seven suspects arrested during coordinated action on 3 November 2026," Europol [said](https://www.europol.europa.eu/media-press/newsroom/news/investment-scams-potential-victims-in-least-50-countries-over-100-servers-seized). "Authorities searched 17 locations and seized servers, other evidence and assets." Like other fraudulent investment schemes, the operation leveraged deceptive advertisements to funnel people towards fraudulent investment platforms, promising them substantial, quick returns from cryptocurrency trading and foreign currencies. Although victims were encouraged to transfer a small amount, they displayed illusory gains to push them into transferring increasingly large sums. The operators resorted to delaying tactics to block payments when they attempted to withdraw their funds. "Behind seemingly legitimate platforms, supposed brokers used fictitious profits and sustained psychological pressure to persuade victims to invest more," Europol said. "Personal data collected through this process was sold to criminal groups operating call centers. Operators then contacted potential victims with supposed investment opportunities, turning their initial interest into repeated demands for money."
