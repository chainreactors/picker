An online ad for Ben McAdams, the Democratic candidate for Congress in Utah, emphasised that he could be trusted because he “fought back against MAGA”.

What’s interesting is that both ads were ultimately paid for with money from the same organisation – Leading the Future (LTF), a Super PAC which opposes regulations that [“constrain” innovation](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/openai-president-urges-ai-safeguards-group-he-funds-opposes-regulation-2026-10-21/) in AI. The network has received US$125 million in funding in total from OpenAI president Greg Brockman, his wife Anna Brockman, tech-focused venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) and a16z’s founders, [Federal Election Commission (FEC) filings](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00916114/?tab=raising) show.

[Directly opposing LTF](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/07/16/anthropics-ceo-gives-1-million-to-super-pac-amid-feud-of-ai-big-money-groups-01000461) is Public First Action (PFA), which calls for AI safety regulations and guardrails. PFA has received [$40 million in funding](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donation-public-first-action) from AI company Anthropic.

Like the ads run by organisations funded by LTF, the Super PACs funded by PFA also ran ads that appeared supportive or critical of Trump depending on the voters they were targeting.

None of these ads mentions AI.

As the US 2026 midterm elections approach on Nov. 3, Super PAC spending on political advertising has [reportedly soared to record highs](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/politics/midterm-elections-political-ad-spending-record-2026/), surpassing US$11 billion last week and exceeding the amount spent on [any other election cycle](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/2026-midterms-become-expensive-election-cycle-ever-rcna605964) to date.

Super PACs are a particular type of political action committee (PAC) that may receive [unlimited contributions](https://www.fec.gov/press/resources-journalists/political-action-committees-pacs/) from individuals, corporations, labour unions and other PACs for the purpose of financing independent expenditures and other independent political activity.

In this year’s elections, LTF’s and PFA’s Super PACs, funded by the AI industry heavyweights, have played an [outsized role](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/03/ai-crypto-super-pac-spending-primaries-01062929) in supporting or opposing candidates through advertising. But as polls show [distrust of AI](https://time.com/article/2026/10/21/how-america-s-ai-trust-issues-could-impact-the-midterms/) [crosses party lines](https://www.cnet.com/news/politics/polls-experts-ai-data-centers-midterm-voting/), the advertisements that Silicon Valley’s leaders are [paying millions of dollars](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/09/29/us/politics/ai-super-pac-money-midterm-elections.html) to try to sway the results in their chosen candidates’ favour often do not mention AI at all.

While LTF is a registered Super PAC itself, FEC filings show it mainly disburses money to other Super PACs in its network which run political ads.

Of about $52 million that LTF has [disbursed to date](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00916114/?tab=spending), $20 million went to Think Big, which targets the Democratic voter base, and another $20 million went to American Mission, which focuses on Republicans.

PFA, meanwhile, is registered as a “social welfare”, or [501(c)(4)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/other-non-profits/social-welfare-organizations), organisation. This type of non-profit is [not required](https://www.opensecrets.org/outside-spending/faq) to disclose its donors or disbursements. However, PFA [contributes funds to three Super PACs](https://www.axios.com/2026/02/27/ai-influence-power-players): the bipartisan Public First PAC, Jobs and Democracy for Democratic candidates and Defending Our Values for Republicans. FEC filings for these Super PACs show Public First PAC received $1 million, [Jobs and Democracy](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00928374/?tab=raising) received $15.4 million and Defending Our Values received $7.9 million from PFA. [Public First](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00930503/?tab=spending) PAC’s FEC filings also show that $4 million of its $4.4 million in disbursements disclosed so far went to Jobs and Democracy, while another $300,000 went to Defending Our Values.
