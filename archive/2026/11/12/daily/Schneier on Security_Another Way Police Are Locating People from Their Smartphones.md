## Another Way Police Are Locating People from Their Smartphones

Penlink’s Webloc is an application that [locates](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-police-phone-tracking-advertising-tech/?accessToken=eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJzb3VyY2UiOiJTdWJzY3JpYmVyR2lmdGVkQXJ0aWNsZSIsImlhdCI6MTc5Mzg4OTgyNSwiZXhwIjoxNzk0NDk0NjI1LCJhcnRpY2xlSWQiOiJUTzEyT0pUM0JaTUwwMCIsImJjb25uZWN0SWQiOiJCRkMyNTg1RUFENTA0RTM2OUU3MzBBNTBCNkVGRTk5RSJ9.uWvJBwAKCyQsXzSli3He-XStaEQCVyprRIKmrsheWZs&utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email&leadSource=article-gifting) people using information from the weather, gaming, email, and religious apps on their phone. It’s sold to the police.

> The tool collects vast quantities of location data from smartphones via commonly used weather, gaming, email and religious apps. It can build a detailed picture of where a smartphone has been over the past 12 months, and update the data hourly, showing the movements of people on a near real-time basis. Law enforcement officers can use Webloc to zero in on a map and look at all phones that were present at a specific place and time, analyzing “billions of location data points,” according to company documents reviewed by Bloomberg.

There’s a nice infographic that explains how the system works at the link.
