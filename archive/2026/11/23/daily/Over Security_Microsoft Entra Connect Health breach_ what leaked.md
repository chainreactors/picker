Microsoft has told customers that attackers used a flaw in its Entra Connect Health API to read administrator and technical contact email addresses and tenant configuration data. [The notice, reported by BleepingComputer on 21 November 2026](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/microsoft-entra-connect-health-flaw-exploited-to-steal-tenant-data/), dates the flaw to 23 July, its discovery to 9 November and the fix to 19 November. Microsoft says passwords and tokens were not exposed and has not said how many tenants were read.

### In brief

* The flaw was open for 119 days and went unnoticed for the first 109.
* Microsoft fixed it on its own servers, so customers have nothing to patch.
* Tenant IDs and organisation names were already public for any Microsoft 365 domain. The administrator email addresses, licence tier and sync status were not.
* Microsoft warns affected customers to expect targeted phishing and to put privileged technical contacts on phishing-resistant MFA.
* Nobody outside Microsoft has a list of affected tenants. Affected customers received a Security Advisory in Azure Service Health.

## What happened to Microsoft Entra Connect Health?

Attackers exploited a vulnerability in the Entra Connect Health API to read tenant-level metadata, and Microsoft confirmed the access on 20 November 2026. [Help Net Security, which received the notification](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/23/microsoft-entra-connect-health-vulnerability-tenant-data/), reports that Microsoft’s investigation found “unauthorized read-only access to tenant-level metadata” at the organisations it notified. The flaw could also have let an outsider change Connect Health monitoring settings, and Microsoft says it found no unauthorised changes.
