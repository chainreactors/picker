A database run by the Latvian branch of the SMS provider CSC Telecom sat open on the internet with more than 125 million text messages sent between October 2022 and September 2026, Cybernews reported on 11 November. The messages came from Swedbank, SEB, airBaltic, Latvia’s 112 emergency line and retailers in all three Baltic states, and new ones appeared as they were queued. Nobody has shown that anyone else read them.

Nothing on the record says money was taken or a bank was breached. The risk is quieter: four years of who texts whom, with phone numbers attached, is a targeting list. It also fits neatly with a second supply of stolen data that already circulates: customer logins harvested by infostealer malware.

### In brief

* Cybernews researchers found the open ClickHouse database on 28 September 2026, reported it the next day and found access closed on 27 October. How long it was open before they found it is not known.
* It held sender names, recipients’ phone numbers, the full text of each message and routing metadata, and it updated in real time. More than 3 million messages passed through in September 2026 alone.
* Swedbank has stopped using the provider and opened an investigation. SEB says it used CSC Telecom only for pension-fund information and was not told about the incident. CERT.LV has asked CSC Telecom for an in-depth investigation and passed the case to law enforcement.
* CSC Telecom has not commented publicly. Cybernews says it has no evidence of malicious access.
* The exposure matters most in combination with other stolen data. Phone numbers tied to named services make scam calls and texts far more convincing.
