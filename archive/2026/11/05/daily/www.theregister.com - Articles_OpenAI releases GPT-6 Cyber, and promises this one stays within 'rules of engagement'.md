OpenAI, on Thursday, released its latest cybersecurity-tuned model, GPT-6 Cyber, which the House of Altman says combines "stronger cybersecurity capabilities with better adherence to researchers’ rules of engagement."

The new model, we’re told, is much better at finding and exploiting vulnerabilities, compared to GPT-5.6 and GPT-6 Astra.

OpenAI [released GPT-6 Astra](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/03/openai-throws-astra-into-the-top-tier-model-ring/5294378) in early September, and less than a month later, [killed off the planned release](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/29/openai-benches-gpt-61-astra-for-overstepping-the-mark/5299743) of GPT-6.1 Astra after the model got better at pursuing tasks but worse at staying within the boundaries of what it had actually been authorized to do.

REG AD

The model maker said the latest cyber model was "trained to stay within authorized scope, respect restrictions and report results accurately."

REG AD

In OpenAI's ExploitGym training evaluation, GPT-6 Cyber scored 48.5 percent. For comparison, GPT-5.6 Cyber scored 32.1 percent on these same benchmark tests.

"In a separate evaluation of whether the model respects the boundaries of an assigned task, it avoided exploiting out-of-scope services over 99 percent of the time," Open AI said. Only 0.07 percent of cases went beyond the intended scope of the training exercise.

OpenAI declined to provide additional details about what happened in that 0.07 percent of cases and whether they involved any real-life safety or security incidents - like [hacking companies](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/23/red-team-report-found-openai-utterly-failed-to-monitor-its-agents-during-hugging-face-attack/5305200) or [carrying out supply chain attacks](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/28/openai-gpt-6-astra-really-good-at-supply-chain-attacks-uk-gov-warns/5299588) as its earlier models have done during training. We do know, however, that OpenAI recently began [denying internet access to the frontier models](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/23/openai-disables-internet-access-during-model-training-while-anthropic-issues-another-mea-culpa/5305238) it is training to help prevent its models from doing bad things.

The latest cyber model is also really good at finding and exploiting serious security vulnerabilities, OpenAI claimed. Its researchers used GPT-6 Cyber to find 81 previously unknown high-severity vulnerabilities in Firefox, which [Mozilla has started fixing](https://openai.com/index/a-thousand-fewer-paths-to-root/).
