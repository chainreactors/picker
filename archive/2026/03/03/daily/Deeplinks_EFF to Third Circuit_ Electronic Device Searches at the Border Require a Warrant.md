EFF, along with the national ACLU and the ACLU affiliates in Pennsylvania, Delaware, and New Jersey, filed an [amicus brief](https://www.eff.org/document/us-v-roggio-3d-cir-eff-aclu-amicus-brief) in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit urging the court to require a warrant for border searches of electronic devices, an [argument](https://www.eff.org/issues/border-searches) EFF has been making in the [courts](https://www.eff.org/cases/alasaad-v-duke?type=document#related-content) and [Congress](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2021/10/come-back-warrant-congress-should-pass-protecting-data-border-act) for nearly a decade.

The case, [*U.S. v. Roggio*](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/6344329/united-states-v-roggio-etal/?page=1), involves a man who had been under ongoing criminal investigation for illegal exports when he returned to the United States from an international trip via JFK airport. Border officers used the opportunity to bypass the Fourth Amendment’s warrant requirement when they seized several of his electronic devices (laptop, tablet, cell phone, and flash drive) and conducted forensic searches of them.
