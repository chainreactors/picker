# California privacy agency director Kemp to leave after two-year tenure

The director of the trailblazing California Privacy Protection Agency (CalPrivacy) on Tuesday announced his departure after nearly two years leading the organization as it implemented a first-in-the-nation platform allowing consumers to easily opt out of data sharing.

Tom Kemp made the announcement in a LinkedIn [post](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7531013941020372992/) highlighting his work on the so-called Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP). The system is now used by more than 600,000 Californians who can block data sharing and foil [data brokers](https://therecord.media/dozens-of-data-brokers-disclose-selling-info-on-kids-geolocation-data-reproductive-health) with the [push of a button](https://therecord.media/state-data-broker-registries-california-vermont).

The platform debuted in January, but penalties for companies violating consumers’ data privacy wishes only went into effect in August. Each DROP user has, on average, forced more than 200 deletions by data brokers, Kemp said.

He will begin a new position in January, but did not disclose any details about his new role.

“I am proud that CalPrivacy under my tenure has made great progress in making it significantly easier for Californians to exercise their privacy rights, and that our work represents a case study of how to deliver innovative government services that can make a tangible difference,” he [said](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/11/calprivacy-executive-director-to-depart-in-december-2026/).

Under Kemp’s stewardship, CalPrivacy also led the push to pass two pieces of legislation expanding consumer privacy, lobbied against federal preemption of California’s tough privacy laws and oversaw the issuance of the [largest fine](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/11/federal-express-to-change-practices-and-pay-close-to-3-million-fine-over-ccpa-privacy-violations/) in CalPrivacy’s history.

The agency also adopted the country’s most comprehensive regulations mandating how industry deploys cybersecurity audits, risk assessments and automated decisionmaking technology. The latter refers to software, including algorithms and AI, that processes personal data to make decisions about individuals relating to employment and other significant events.
