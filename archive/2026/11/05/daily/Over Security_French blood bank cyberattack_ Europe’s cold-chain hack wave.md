Attackers raised the temperature and defrost thresholds on plasma cold rooms at three regional sites of the Établissement français du sang (EFS), France’s national blood service, overnight on 1 to 2 November 2026, through remote monitoring software run by its refrigeration contractor Coolworld. Le Canard enchaîné, which broke the story in its 4 November edition, reported that rooms held at minus 30°C were set to climb to 110°C. A duty technician and EFS’s own temperature alarms caught the rise, the plasma and samples were moved to other sites, and EFS says no blood products were lost and no patient was affected.

It is the first healthcare target in a run of attacks on internet-connected refrigeration controllers that began with about 700 French supermarkets on the night of 11 to 12 October, reached more than 100 shops in nine other European countries on 2 November and has since touched meat processors in the UK. Two pro-Russian hacktivist groups claimed the supermarket attacks on Telegram on 4 November and deleted the claim within hours. Nobody has claimed the blood bank, and French officials have not linked the two.

### In brief

* Cold rooms at three EFS cold-storage sites, one of them Besançon in Bourgogne-Franche-Comté, had their thresholds changed in the early hours of 2 November. They held plasma bags for transfusion and for fractionation into medicines, plus donor samples for EFS biobanks. EFS’s statement of 3 November reports no loss of product, no health consequences and no interruption to supply for the roughly 1,900 hospitals and clinics it serves.
* The way in was Coolworld, a cold-room and refrigerated-container rental firm. Its notice of 4 November blames remote monitoring software built on a component from a third party it does not control, on a limited number of units. Neither Coolworld nor EFS has named the component or its maker.
