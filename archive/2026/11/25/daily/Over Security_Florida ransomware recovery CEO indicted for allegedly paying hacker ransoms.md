# Florida ransomware recovery CEO indicted for allegedly paying hacker ransoms

The CEO of a ransomware recovery firm was indicted by a federal grand jury after prosecutors accused him of lying about his services and simply paying out ransoms to cybercriminals.

Colombian national Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, is the co-founder of a company based in Florida that marketed itself as a data recovery service for ransomware victims.

Palacio claimed he had decryption tools that allowed him to help victims without negotiating or paying ransomware gangs. Instead, Palacio allegedly paid ransoms and charged his customers enough to make a profit.

Federal agencies have increasingly sought to crack down on the murky industry of ransomware recovery and cybercriminal mediation in recent months.

“As alleged, the defendant preyed on ransomware victims by falsely marketing a solution he did not possess, secretly negotiating with cybercriminals, and profiting from the difference,” said Charles Calenda, first assistant U.S. attorney.

“Businesses and individuals seeking to recover from a cyberattack should not be victimized for a second time by those they turn to for help.”

He is facing three counts of wire fraud, which carry a maximum sentence of 60 years in prison.

Before the DOJ announced the charges, the news outlet [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/ransomware-fraud-attack-fbi-indictment/) and independent journalist [Brian Krebs](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/11/cto-of-ransomwarehelp-indicted-for-wire-fraud/) published details of Escobar’s operation, writing that he ran a company called Ransomwarehelp.com. The company’s website currently says it is under maintenance.

On Wednesday, Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva confirmed previous reports that the FBI staged an operation in June where they posed as a ransomware gang and victim.

The FBI went to Palacio for assistance and was told his company “never negotiated with or paid the ransom to ransomware attackers but instead could recover the data through its sophisticated decryption technology.”

“The indictment alleges that despite these representations, Palacio did negotiate with and make a payment in the amount of approximately $6,000 in Bitcoin to the purported ransomware actor, which was also an undercover FBI operative, in exchange for the decryption key,” prosecutors said.

“Palacio charged the undercover company approximately $13,260 for his company’s purported decryption technology services.”

He was arrested in Las Vegas on October 22 before being transferred to Rhode Island. He was released on pretrial bond on November 20.

“As alleged, Mr. Palacio Escobar is the proverbial wolf in sheep’s clothing, representing both himself and his company as experts in data recovery, when all the while he was paying the perpetrators of these ransomware attacks, under the guise of helping his clients,” FBI agent Ted Docks said. “Little did they know he was ripping them off.”

A cybersecurity firm that spoke to Krebs claimed it was hired by a ransomware victim who alleged Palacio had deleted the ransomware notes from its system so that he would be the sole person in charge of communicating with the hackers.

Last month, the DOJ [charged](https://therecord.media/ransomware-recovery-charges-doj) another Florida-based owner of a ransomware recovery firm for conducting a similar scheme.

Two ransomware negotiators were [given four-year sentences](https://therecord.media/ransomware-cyber-incident-responders) in May after pleading guilty to conducting their own covert ransomware attacks and coordinating with ransomware gangs while purportedly negotiating ransoms on behalf of at least five victims.

Another cybersecurity executive was recently [arrested and indicted](https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/edward-dubrovsky-court-hearing-9.7393755) for allegedly being involved in a prominent cybercriminal group that hacked the FBI.
