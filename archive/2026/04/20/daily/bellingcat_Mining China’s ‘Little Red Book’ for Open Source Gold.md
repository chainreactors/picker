# Mining China’s ‘Little Red Book’ for Open Source Gold

April 20, 2026

* [China](/tag/china)
* [Social Media](/tag/social-media)

The [challenges of conducting open-source](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2023/04/18/china-challenges-open-source-osint-social-media/) research in China are well-documented. Consistently named one of the [most digitally oppressive countries](https://www.beyondidentity.com/resource/countries-with-the-most--and-least--digital-freedom#least-digital-freedom-1-china) in the world, China blocks some of the world’s largest social media platforms, such as Facebook, Google, and YouTube. Those that are still accessible are mostly Chinese-owned, strictly regulated and monitored in real time by [AI systems](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-06-04/beijing-ai-and-censors-erase-tiananmen-square-massacre/105370772) as well as [tens of thousands of “internet police”](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zptxxnb/revision/7).

But despite these strict controls, Chinese apps – which boast more than [a billion](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-china) estimated users – remain an information goldmine for investigative journalists covering stories both within and outside China.

Since most foreign sites are banned, Chinese platforms are the largest resource available to journalists and researchers interested in what’s going on in the world’s [second-most populous country](https://www.worldometers.info/world-population/population-by-country/). Even when a topic is being censored, patterns in the censorship can themselves serve as investigative leads: a [2020 BuzzFeed News investigation](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/alison_killing/satellite-images-investigation-xinjiang-detention-camps), for example, mapped out detention camps in Xinjiang by examining areas that had been blanked out on China’s [Baidu Maps](https://bellingcat.gitbook.io/toolkit/more/all-tools/baidu-maps).

With [millions of Chinese people](https://www.culturaldiplomacy.org/academy/index.php?chinese-diaspora) living overseas, social media activity by members of the diaspora can also turn into global stories.

Serial rapist Zou Zhenhao, a Chinese PhD student, was [jailed in London last year](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c4g8dy38qxjo) after one of his victims [posted a warning](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cr53p9v6l6mo#:~:text=The%20alleged%20victim%20said%20she,eight%20alleged%20victims%20is%20unknown.) on Xiaohongshu, also known as Little Red Book or Rednote, an app popular with young Chinese women living abroad. Another woman Zou had raped reached out to the original poster, who put her in touch with the police – leading to the conviction of a man described by police as possibly [one of the worst sexual predators](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2025/jun/19/serial-rapist-zhenhao-zou-jailed-for-life) in British history.

Founded in 2013 as a Hong Kong shopping guide, Xiaohongshu has evolved into a lifestyle and e-commerce platform that has been compared with [Instagram, Pinterest and Amazon](https://www.businessinsider.com/china-instagram-killer-xiaohongshu-explained-2022-3#:~:text=The%20app%20%E2%80%94%20called%20Xiaohongshu%20in,images%20tailored%20to%20your%20interests.&text=But%20everything%20is%20geared%20to,products%2C%20services%2C%20or%20experiences.). Last year, it reported about [300 million monthly active users](https://www.qian-gua.com/information/detail/3149), rivalling some of China’s largest social media platforms.

![](https://www.bellingcat.com/app/uploads/2026/04/2025-01-15T080212Z_655487166_MT1VCG000CRRKI3_RTRMADP_3_VCG-1200x782.jpg)

*Xiaohongshu saw a surge in international users in January 2025 amid a threatened ban on short video app TikTok. Photo: VCG via Reuters Connect*

The app’s [600 million daily searches](https://www.ithome.com/0/817/514.htm) by the end of 2024 also accounted for half of market leader Baidu’s search volume, demonstrating that it is emerging as a critical search and discovery engine, not just a social platform.

Although primarily a Chinese-language app, Xiaohongshu gained attention in the English-speaking world last year, when millions of American TikTok users [flocked to the platform](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/jan/18/chinese-rival-app-xiaohongshu-is-overwhelmed-by-tiktok-refugees-in-us) in anticipation of a TikTok ban under US President Donald Trump.

Responding to the surge of international users – sparked by the [#TikTokRefugees](https://www.reuters.com/technology/over-half-million-tiktok-refugees-flock-chinas-rednote-2025-01-14/) trend – Xiaohongshu rolled out an [AI-powered translation feature](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/01/20/business/tiktok-ban-rednote-xiaohongshu-adapts.html), making the app more accessible to non-Chinese audiences. This also meant that journalists without Chinese language skills can more easily communicate on and navigate the platform.

Despite its growing popularity both within and outside China, the app is relatively new and underexplored compared to more well-established platforms such as Weibo.

This guide aims to provide a starting point for those looking to explore Xiaohongshu for open-source investigations, including an overview of its main user demographics, potential topics to explore and strategic search methods specific to the app.

## User Demographics and Topics

According to Xiaohongshu’s [official data](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/xiaohongshu-unveils-trend-report-on-search-habits-among-chinas-youth-302220915.html), the platform’s demographic profile is mainly young, female and urban. As of 2024, 70 percent of its users were women, with half of all users belonging to [Gen Z](https://www.beresfordresearch.com/age-range-by-generation/) and living in China’s largest cities.

As previously mentioned, the app has also gained popularity with the [Chinese diaspora](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-12-10/how-super-app-red-influences-chinese-diaspora-in-australia/103205362). Many Chinese nationals living abroad [use it as a search engine for local information](https://tong.global/point-of-view/insights/rednote-the-lifestyle-handbook-for-overseas-chinese/), posting and searching for content related to their daily lives, from restaurant recommendations and apartment hunting to navigating foreign bureaucracies and finding community resources.
