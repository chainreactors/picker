After years of tech industry experiments, smartglasses with embedded cameras and microphones have finally gone mainstream. And, disturbingly, sometimes it's not just their owners who are watching what these devices record. In [this week's EFFector newsletter](https://eff.org/effector/38/6), we're taking a closer look at the privacy implications of Meta Ray-Bans, and sharing all the latest in the fight for privacy and free speech online.

[JOIN OUR NEWSLETTER](https://www.eff.org/effector/)

For over 35 years, [EFFector](https://eff.org/effector) has been your guide to understanding the intersection of technology, civil liberties, and the law. This week's issue covers [EFF's new executive director](https://www.eff.org/press/releases/nicole-ozer-named-electronic-frontier-foundations-executive-director); how publishers blocking the Internet Archive [threaten the web's historical record](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/03/blocking-internet-archive-wont-stop-ai-it-will-erase-webs-historical-record); and why you should [think twice](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/03/think-twice-buying-or-using-metas-ray-bans) before buying or using Meta’s Ray-Bans.
