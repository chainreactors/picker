# Join EFF to Celebrate the Life of Aaron Swartz

![Aaron Swartz at a Boston Wiki Meetup in 2009 photo by Sage Ross via Wikipedia](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/og-aaronswartz-credit.jpg?itok=Orc7jiis "Aaron Swartz at a Boston Wiki Meetup in 2009 photo by Sage Ross via Wikipedia")

Photo by Sage Ross (CC Attribution Share Alike License)

Join us to [celebrate the legacy of Aaron Swartz](https://www.eff.org/event/aaron-swartz-international-hackathon-2026) on Nov. 8, 2026. With the organizers of [Aaron Swartz Day](https://www.aaronswartzday.org/), we’re honoring his life with an in-person and remote hackathon. This event is supported by several organizations that carry on his work, including the Internet Archive—which is hosting the in-person event—the [Freedom of the Press Foundation](https://freedom.press/), [Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/) and the [Free Law Project](https://free.law/).

Aaron Swartz was a [digital rights champion](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2013/01/farewell-aaron-swartz) who believed in the need for an open internet. His life was cut short in 2013, after federal prosecutors charged him under the [Computer Fraud and Abuse Act](https://www.eff.org/issues/cfaa) (CFAA) for systematically downloading academic journal articles from the online database JSTOR. Facing the prospect of a long and unjust sentence, Aaron died by suicide at the age of 26.

EFF was proud to call Aaron a friend and ally. We continue to stand with programmers and developers engaged in cutting-edge exploration of technology through our [Coders' Rights Project](https://www.eff.org/issues/coders).

Hackathon participants can contribute code, documentation, and design to several open-source and public-interest projects. These include [Secure Drop](https://securedrop.org/), [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/), [Quantifying the Commons](https://cdss.berkeley.edu/project/quantifying-commons), [Onion Press](https://onionpress.org/onionhome/), and our own [Atlas of Surveillance](https://www.atlasofsurveillance.org/).  EFF Director of State Affairs Hayley Tsukayama will also join hackathon participants at the Internet Archive at 11 a.m. for an Atlas of Surveillance on-boarding session.

Contributors across experience levels are welcome. The Aaron Swartz Day organizers are also open to adding new projects and workshops, so please contact them at aaronswartzday@gmail.com if you’d like to suggest a workshop or project—particularly open-source projects on Github that participants can continue to work on after the event. For more details on how to participate in-person or remotely, please visit the Aaron Swartz Day and International Hackathon page at [www.aaronswartzday.org](http://www.aaronswartzday.org/).
