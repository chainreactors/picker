The PHP2Shell security fiasco has forced 32 UK government services offline and left another four partially unavailable as Whitehall scrambles to patch the critical vulnerabilities wreaking havoc across the web.

The figures were [disclosed](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Lords/2026-10-22/debates/14CB4C6D-4E16-4377-8FCF-C2CF77BB5CC0/details) in the House of Lords on Thursday, where tech minister Baroness Lloyd of Effra outlined the disruption [following the discovery of two critical flaws in PHP](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/21/php-devs-publish-critical-bug-fix-as-site-owners-warned-pull-em-offline-if-youre-unpatched/5304519).

"Currently, the information I have is that 32 government services are unavailable and four are partially unavailable," Lloyd told peers. "That has resulted largely from precautionary measures taken to protect systems while security updates and mitigations are applied."

"We are working very carefully with departments to restore the services safely and securely following that remediation activity," she added.

Whitehall has not published a full list of affected services, although the Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR) is among those known to have taken their websites offline, rather inconveniently with next week's Budget looming. Peers also raised reports of disruption affecting the DVLA and police services.

Britain isn't alone in pulling the plug. [Government websites in Germany, France, Belgium, Finland, and the Netherlands](https://www.reuters.com/technology/websites-taken-offline-over-php-risks-2026-10-21/) have also been taken offline as a precaution, along with Australia's own cybersecurity agency website. Across the Atlantic, the [NSA has urged organizations](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Press-Releases-Statements/Press-Release-View/Article/4630238/nsa-advisory-php-updates/) to patch or disconnect vulnerable systems, although the US government has not publicly confirmed any website shutdowns.

PHP2Shell comprises two critical vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, that could allow unauthenticated attackers to execute code on vulnerable servers. The National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) warned organizations to patch immediately or consider pulling affected systems offline until they could be fixed.

"The NCSC was briefed by OpenAI last week on this potential specific vulnerability and has been working to ensure that disclosure happened in a co-ordinated way with vendors, so there was sufficient preparation to protect customers," Lloyd said.
