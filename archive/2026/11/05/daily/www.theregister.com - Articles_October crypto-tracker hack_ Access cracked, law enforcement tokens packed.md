Blockchain analysis outfit Chainalysis has confirmed that when attackers broke into its systems in October, they managed to grab usernames, password hashes, and live tokens.

In an update written late Wednesday evening, the company, a leader in blockchain analysis which has some very high-profile global customers and partners, confirmed the impact of the attack for the first time.

In previous updates, which Chainalysis has issued regularly since it detected the intrusion on October 30, it confirmed that user IDs, full names, email addresses, and organizational role labels were exposed. The news of password hashes and live tokens also being accessed reflects a worsening picture for the company.

REG AD

Chainalysis did not confirm the type of hashing algorithm it used to encrypt users' passwords, which makes it difficult to draw conclusions about the offline crackability of users' hashed credentials.

REG AD

Some users received emails instructing them to change their passwords, but Chainalysis said in its latest update that the password reset functionality is currently down. Nevertheless, users were also encouraged to change their passwords on any site where they reuse credentials.

In addition to the exposed data, Chainalysis also detected "abnormal activity" on its transaction screening products, and has taken services down while "our team completes critical work."

These include, but are not limited to, the Reactor blockchain investigation platform used by international law enforcement customers, the Data Solutions threat intelligence platform, and the KYT (know your transaction) anti-money laundering solution's user interface and login page.

Regarding Reactor and the associated investigation data, Chainalysis [said](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/security-incident-october-30/): "At this time, we continue to have no evidence that any investigations data has been accessed or Chainalysis attribution data has been manipulated."

The company plans to reinstate KYT and the risk-intelligence Address Screening tool at some point today (Thursday November 5).

Customers will have to provide the company’s support team or their account manager with trusted IP addresses, which will be added to an allowlist. From there, users can begin resetting their [passwords](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/14/ukgov-begins-killing-off-passwords-for-23-million-users/5296088).
