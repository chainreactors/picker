# “A Recurring Pattern”: Civilians Paying the Price in Mali’s Drone Campaign

September 16, 2026

* [Drones](/tag/drones)
* [Geolocation](/tag/geolocation)
* [Mali](/tag/mali)

**WARNING: This report contains links to graphic images and footage.**

A joint Bellingcat and [Jeune Afrique investigation](https://www.jeuneafrique.com/1841995/politique/ils-ont-vu-les-enfants-ils-ont-quand-meme-tire-au-mali-enquete-sur-la-guerre-des-drones/) into drone strikes carried out by the Malian military adds new details about the attacks which frequently are carried out in civilian areas and have reportedly resulted in the deaths of civilians.

The incidents, concentrated in northern Mali, often involved strikes on vehicles or commercial activity. Video footage of the strikes is often broadcast on national television, portrayed as precise attacks against terrorists and their logistics.

Mali’s military government acquired its first Turkish-made [Bayraktar TB2 drones](https://x.com/casusbellii/status/1605684958206390272) in late 2022, and regularly releases footage of drone strikes to be broadcast on national television, describing them as “precise” and describing the targets as “terrorists and their logistics”. This month marks three years since Mali joined with Burkina Faso and Niger to form the Alliance of Sahel States (AES). Its military government has made restoring national sovereignty a central part of its political message.

Bellingcat geolocated more than a dozen drone strikes including instances in which civilian deaths and injuries were reported. However, this represents just a fraction of the strikes that have taken place in northern Mali, with many more conducted for which footage was not available or further details were harder to verify as the locations are so remote.

Yvan Guichaoua Senior Researcher at the Bonn International Centre for Conflict Studies told us the Malian military’s drone strikes appeared to fit a pattern of “guilt by location” in which people may be treated as targets based on where they are rather than who they are.

He added that the broadcast of drone strikes on national television, often accompanied by dramatic commentary created a familiar narrative for veiwers that was akin to daily episodes “of a national saga”. Yet the impact of the drone war on civilians and non-combatants is seldom covered in such reports.

Mali’s civil war involves separatist and Islamist groups and has been ongoing since 2012, predating the formation of the AES. But the conflict has spiked in 2026 as Tuareg separatists from the Azawad Liberation Front (FLA) and militants from the al-Qaeda affiliated Jama’at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM) seized control of parts of the country’s north in coordinated attacks against the Malian military and Russia’s Africa Corps forces. The Malian military (known hereafter by their official name *Forces Armées Maliennes* or FAMa) has relied heavily upon drone strikes in response.

We contacted FAMa and put our findings to them but did not received a substantive response at time of publication.

## Destruction in Zouera

In one notable case – on July 8, 2025 FAMa presented a drone strike in the town of Zouera, in the Timbuktu region, as a successful operation against a supply vehicle for terrorists in footage [broadcast on ORTM1](https://youtu.be/RViCjTEyrzE?t=293) [4:50-5:31]. Drone footage shows a person walking near the vehicle before it is struck. The explosion immediately ignites a nearby structure, while several people can be seen running away.

[One video](https://x.com/AmakanessAg/status/1942621373819674881) of this incident, apparently showing the aftermath of the attack, shows the remains of a person inside the burnt-down structure, which was located directly beside the vehicle that was hit in the strike. We also checked satellite imagery and found a noticeable burn scar at the location in imagery taken on Jul 9, 2025, that wasn’t visible the previous day.

Bellingcat geolocated the strike site to the coordinates [16.84589, -3.85213](https://www.google.com/maps/place/16%C2%B050%2745.2%22N%2B3%C2%B051%2707.7%22W/%4016.8459187%2C-3.8520269%2C62m/data%3D%213m1%211e3%214m4%213m3%218m2%213d16.8458933%214d-3.8521404?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MDgwMy4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D), inside Zouera. [Several](https://x.com/Djoudjou_Sako/status/1942683721070698509/video/2) [videos](https://x.com/apmazawad/status/1942651188467376562) filmed in the immediate aftermath show the destroyed vehicle, a large plume of black smoke, and the nearby structure engulfed in flames.

## Amasrakad Strike

Zouera was not an isolated incident. Footage from March 2024 that was released by the Malian military and again aired on [ORTM1](https://www.youtube.com/live/KErMo9WnUNo?si=n815BT4PIrsjQzKR&t=535) showed two drone strikes in the town of Amasrakad that resulted in the deaths of civilians, according to witness testimony and previous [reporting](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2024/03/civilians-seeking-shelter-were-killed-by-drone-strike-in-town-in-gao-region/) by Amnesty International.
