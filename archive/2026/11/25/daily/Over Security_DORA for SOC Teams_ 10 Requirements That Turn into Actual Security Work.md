DORA for SOC Teams: 10 Requirements That Turn into Actual Security Work

Under the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), financial entities need to demonstrate how effectively they detect, investigate, and respond to ICT-related incidents. For SOC teams, this means greater pressure to identify threats early, make accurate decisions, and document findings within regulatory deadlines.

What does DORA compliance look like in everyday SOC operations? This guide breaks down 10 key requirements of DORA for SOC teams and the security work behind each.

## What Does the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) Mean for SOC Teams?

Applicable since January 17, 2025, the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) establishes a common framework for managing ICT risks and strengthening operational resilience across the EU [financial sector](https://any.run/by-industry/finance/?utm_source=anyrunblog&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=dora-for-soc-teams&utm_term=251126&utm_content=linktofinance).

The regulation covers five main areas:

* ICT risk management
* ICT-related incident management and reporting
* Digital operational resilience testing
* ICT third-party risk management
* Cyber threat intelligence and information sharing (voluntary sharing arrangements)

Much of the work involved in supporting DORA compliance already falls within the scope of SOC operations: continuous monitoring, threat detection, incident investigation, and response.

DORA does not mandate specific security technologies or require a dedicated SOC. Instead, financial entities must implement security measures appropriate to their size, risk profile, and operational complexity.

## 10 DORA Requirements SOC Teams Need to Put into Practice

What does DORA compliance look like in practice for SOC teams? The following ten regulatory requirements have a direct impact on security operations, shaping how teams monitor infrastructure, investigate threats, respond to incidents, and document their work.

### 1. Identify, Classify, and Document ICT Assets

Article 8 of DORA requires financial entities to identify, classify, and document their ICT assets, the business functions they support, and their dependencies. This classification and documentation must be reviewed at least annually.

For SOC teams, understanding which assets support critical or important business functions is essential for setting monitoring priorities and assessing the potential impact of security incidents.

**To put this requirement into practice, SOC teams should:**

* Map security monitoring coverage against ICT asset inventories.
* Identify visibility gaps across endpoints, networks, applications, and cloud environments.
* Prioritize alerts based on asset criticality and potential business impact.
* Adjust detection coverage as ICT infrastructure changes.

**Compliance evidence:** ICT asset inventories, dependency maps, asset classification records, and detection coverage reports.

### 2. Implement Logging Procedures and Protect Security Logs

Article 12 of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1774 requires financial entities to establish documented logging procedures, define which events must be recorded and how long logs must be retained, and protect log data against unauthorized access, deletion, and tampering.

Continuous log analysis in SOC operations helps uncover suspicious activity, reconstruct attack timelines, and determine the scope of an incident.

**Effective log management involves:**

* Collect and correlate logs from identity systems, endpoints, applications, and network infrastructure.
* Use SIEM to centralize security events and support continuous log analysis.
* Monitor logging system failures to prevent gaps in security visibility.
* Synchronize system clocks to ensure accurate incident timelines.
* Maintain auditable security logs with appropriate access controls and retention policies.
