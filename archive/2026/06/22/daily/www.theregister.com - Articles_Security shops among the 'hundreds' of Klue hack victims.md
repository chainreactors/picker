# Security shops among the 'hundreds' of Klue hack victims

As yet another extortion crew Icarus exploits Salesforce-linked integrations

Jessica Lyons
[Jessica
Lyons](https://www.theregister.com/author/jessica-lyons)

Published
mon 22 Jun 2026 // 20:50 UTC

The list of Klue customers whose Salesforce data was stolen in the latest supply-chain heist keeps growing, with an increasing number of cybersecurity companies disclosing that they are among the victims of a new data-theft and extortion crew called Icarus.

Klue, which provides market intelligence to more than 250,000 companies worldwide, hasn’t said how many of its customers were caught up in the breach and didn’t immediately respond to The Register’s inquiries.

Huntress was one of the first cybersecurity vendors to sound the alarm, and, in an email to The Register, said that it was among the “hundreds of Klue customers” affected. However, it said that the breach did not affect its tools or highly secure information such as passwords.

REG AD

“Huntress believes in radical transparency about security incidents, including when it affects our company,” the security shop [wrote](https://www.huntress.com/blog/klue-breach-investigation) on Thursday. “The data that was copied from our Salesforce account includes business contacts, price quotes, and other sales-related data and messaging. No threat data, passwords, payment card information, or engineering data relating to the Huntress agent or telemetry we collect was affected.”

REG AD

Huntress, along with the other victim companies, said that there is no indication that any of its products or infrastructure were compromised, and that this security incident was specific to CRM data.

Here’s what we do know about what happened and who is behind this latest extortion campaign.

The breach occurred on June 11, and Klue spotted the intrusion a day later. This unauthorized activity affected “a portion” of its integration infrastructure, according to the software provider.

Klue has since disconnected all of its [integrations with Salesforce](https://status.salesforce.com/), Gong, HubSpot, SharePoint, and Google Drive. It also hired CrowdStrike to assist in the investigation and security response.

“Our investigation determined that an attacker gained access through a compromised legacy credential associated with an integration service,” Klue CEO Jason Smith [said](https://klue.com/blog/an-update-on-recent-klue-security-incident) in a Friday blog post. “The attacker used that access to obtain OAuth tokens used to connect Klue with certain third-party platforms, including Salesforce, and subsequently accessed data within a number of connected customer environments.”
