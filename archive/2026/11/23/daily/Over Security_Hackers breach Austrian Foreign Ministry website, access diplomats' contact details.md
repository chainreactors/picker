# Hackers breach Austrian Foreign Ministry website, access diplomats' contact details

Austria's Foreign Ministry said late last week that hackers breached one of its websites and accessed the names and official contact information of employees at its Vienna headquarters and at Austria’s diplomatic missions abroad.

The breach occurred between November 12 and 13 and affected a website operated by an external service provider, according to a ministry statement [reported](https://orf.at/stories/3448812/) by public broadcaster ORF on Friday.

The ministry said the breach had been contained and that no personal information belonging to members of the public was compromised. The hackers have not been identified, and it remains unclear how they gained access to the website or whether they stole any information.

The ministry said it was taking the incident "very seriously" and had notified Austria's cybersecurity and data protection authorities.

A spokesperson told Recorded Future News: “For security reasons, we do not disclose details of specific technical vulnerabilities or security measures. The incident has not been attributed to any particular actor.”

The attack coincided with a global Microsoft service outage that disrupted several of the company's services for approximately 24 hours. Microsoft [attributed](https://ismg-cdn.nyc3.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/asset_files/external/microsoft-report.pdf) the disruption to a software bug and infrastructure failures. Austrian authorities have not established whether the two incidents were connected.

Austria's Foreign Ministry has previously been targeted by suspected state-sponsored hackers linked to Moscow.

In late 2019 and early 2020, the ministry suffered a cyberattack that lasted several weeks and was described by Austria's National Security Council as a targeted intelligence-gathering operation. In July 2026, Vienna publicly [attributed](https://cert.europa.eu/publications/threat-intelligence/cb26-08) the attack to Russia.

The latest breach comes amid worsening diplomatic relations between Austria and Russia.

In October, Vienna [expelled](https://orf.at/stories/3445933/) four Russian diplomats, bringing the number of Russian diplomatic personnel declared persona non grata by Austria since 2020 to 18.

Tensions escalated further last week after a Russian drone strike [damaged](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/11/19/8063700/) the Austrian Embassy in Kyiv. Austria condemned the strike as "unacceptable" and "a violation of international law," and Foreign Minister Beate Meinl-Reisinger summoned Russia's ambassador in Vienna.

There is no public evidence linking Russia to the latest cyberattack. Moscow has repeatedly denied allegations of involvement in cyberattacks against European countries.
