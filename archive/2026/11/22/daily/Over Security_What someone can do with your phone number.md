Someone with your phone number can send scam texts and calls, look up your name and address on people-search sites and find out which apps the number is registered on. Taking over an account, or the number itself, needs more: a login code you hand over, or a SIM swap at your carrier. Breaking into the handset takes spyware that costs millions and targets a few high-risk people.

### In brief

* Scam texts and calls are the most likely result. Text was the way scammers most often reached people in reports to the US Federal Trade Commission for 2025, and four in ten UK mobile users told Ofcom they had received a suspicious message in the previous three months.
* A number does not open an account by itself. WhatsApp says an attacker also needs the verification code sent to the phone, and a SIM swap needs the carrier to move the number.
* SIM swaps are uncommon and expensive: the FBI recorded 971 complaints and $17.4 million in losses in 2025, about $17,900 a complaint. UK cases reported to Cifas rose 402% in the first half of 2026.
* US carriers must offer a free lock against SIM changes by 17 December 2026 and against number transfers by 18 December under FCC rules. Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile already have one in their apps.
* Leaks that Ransomnews reported on in 2026 held more than 125 million text messages in one case and 63 million phone numbers in another.

## What can someone do with just your phone number?

With only a phone number, a stranger can call or text it, sell it on, look up its owner and check which messaging apps it is registered on.
