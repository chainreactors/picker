For years, EFF has [pushed technology companies](https://www.eff.org/document/human-rights-and-technology-sales) to make real human rights commitments—and to [live up](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2018/07/should-your-company-help-ice-know-your-customer-standards-evaluating-domestic) to them. In response to growing evidence that Palantir’s tools help power abusive immigration enforcement by ICE, we sent the company a detailed letter asking how the promises in its own human rights framework extends to that work.

This post explains what we asked, how Palantir responded, and why we believe those responses fall short. EFF is not alone in raising alarms about Palantir; [immigrants' rights groups](https://mijente.net/blog/5-things-you-should-know-to-fight-against-palantir/), [human rights organizations](https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/amr51/3124/2020/en/), [journalists](https://www.404media.co/elite-the-palantir-app-ice-uses-to-find-neighborhoods-to-raid/), and [former employees](https://www.npr.org/2025/05/05/nx-s1-53...
