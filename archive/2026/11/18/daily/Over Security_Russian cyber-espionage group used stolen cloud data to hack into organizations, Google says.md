# Russian cyber-espionage group used stolen cloud data to hack into organizations, Google says

Russian state-backed hackers are using information stolen from cloud accounts to impersonate trusted contacts and launch new espionage attacks against people in their victims' professional networks, according to new research.

The hacking group, known as Cold Relic or [ColdRiver](https://therecord.media/coldriver-callisto-russia-hackers-new-malware-google), has previously targeted government officials, defense contractors and nonprofit organizations across Europe and North America. U.S. authorities have linked the group to Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB).

In a report [published](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/cold-relic-a-case-study-in-impersonation-and-compromise) Wednesday, Google's cybersecurity unit Mandiant described how the hackers used data stolen from one U.S. organization in late 2025 to help compromise a second entity.

During the first attack, the hackers took control of an administrator account and used it to download the organization's entire cloud drive and employee directories. They also searched for emails, passwords and personnel records, apparently looking for information they could use in future operations.

The hackers remained inside the organization's systems for several months. They continued reading emails through third-party applications and found ways to regain access after the organization changed passwords and reset user sessions.

Even after losing access to the network, the hackers continued targeting the organization and its contacts by sending emails that appeared to come from employees.

In a separate attack this year, Cold Relic used information stolen in the earlier breach to pose as an employee and approach someone at another U.S. organization through an encrypted messaging app.

The hackers first struck up a conversation with the target before sending malicious links designed to steal Microsoft account login details and authentication tokens. To make the messages appear more convincing, the links led to documents related to their conversation, making the phishing attempt harder to spot.

The hackers ultimately gained access to an account at the second organization and downloaded large amounts of data from the user's cloud storage, Google said.

The researchers did not identify either U.S. organization or disclose what information was stolen.

Google said Cold Relic frequently uses compromised websites to redirect targets to phishing pages, concealing the actual destination and allowing the attackers to assess visitors before displaying malicious content.

The approach reflects Cold Relic's broader strategy of exploiting relationships between victims. Rather than relying solely on phishing emails, the hackers use stolen correspondence, employee information and familiar identities to make subsequent attacks more convincing, according to the report.

Google has tracked the group since 2015 and observed evidence of new phishing operations as recently as this month. Although Cold Relic has attracted less public attention than some other Russian state-backed hacking groups, Google said it has maintained a steady pace of operations.
