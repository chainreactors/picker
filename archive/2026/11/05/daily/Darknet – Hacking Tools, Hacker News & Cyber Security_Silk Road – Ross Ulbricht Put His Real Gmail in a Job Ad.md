# Silk Road – Ross Ulbricht Put His Real Gmail in a Job Ad

Published November 5, 2026 | Updated November 6, 2026 |

Views: 95

Silk Road was a hidden marketplace on the dark web, reachable only through Tor and paid for in Bitcoin, that the Justice Department called “the most sophisticated and extensive criminal marketplace on the Internet”. Its anonymous boss went by Dread Pirate Roberts.

![Silk Road: Ross Ulbricht put his real Gmail in a job ad, beside an open laptop glowing orange on a library table next to an empty chair.](data:image/svg+xml...)![Silk Road: Ross Ulbricht put his real Gmail in a job ad, beside an open laptop glowing orange on a library table next to an empty chair.](https://www.darknet.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/silk-road-ross-ulbricht-dread-pirate-roberts-1024x576.webp)

This Stickler Files episode follows how investigators connected that name to Ross Ulbricht, starting with a 2011 job advert that told applicants to reply to his own Gmail address.

## Key highlights

* Silk Road opened in January 2011, and the FBI’s complaint counted about 1.2 million transactions between February 2011 and July 2013.
* In January 2011 an account called altoid promoted the site on two forums, posts the FBI complaint calls the earliest publicity for Silk Road it found.
* In October 2011 the same altoid account posted a job advert that told applicants to reply to rossulbricht at gmail dot com.
* A Stack Overflow question about reaching a Tor hidden service was asked under his name, and the account was renamed “frosty” less than a minute later.
* Agents arrested him at a public library in San Francisco on 1 October 2013 and seized his laptop while it was still open.
* At trial in 2015 the jury convicted him on all seven counts.

## How Ross Ulbricht got caught

No single clue settled it. The job advert supplied a name, and Google’s records tied the Gmail account to Ross Ulbricht. Smaller leads in the FBI complaint pointed the same way: logins to his Gmail from a Hickory Street address; the last login to what an agent believed was a rented VPN, from a café less than 500 feet away; and nine counterfeit IDs seized at the border, after which Homeland Security agents questioned him at a 15th Street address. At the library, his open laptop held Silk Road’s chat logs, a journal and Dread Pirate Roberts’s private PGP key.

## Questions answered

### Was Ross Ulbricht pardoned?

Yes. On 21 January 2025 President Trump granted him a full and unconditional pardon, after more than eleven years in custody. Trump had promised clemency during the 2024 campaign. Ulbricht had been sentenced to life in prison without parole in May 2015.

### How did the FBI find Silk Road’s server?

By its own account, through Silk Road’s login page. In a September 2014 sworn declaration, former FBI agent Christopher Tarbell said code behind the login page leaked the server’s real IP address, and that typing it into an ordinary browser brought up Silk Road’s CAPTCHA. The defence’s expert called that explanation “vague”, and security researchers were divided. The judge refused to suppress the evidence because Ulbricht had not established a personal privacy interest in the server, a ruling that did not settle whether the FBI’s account was technically correct.
