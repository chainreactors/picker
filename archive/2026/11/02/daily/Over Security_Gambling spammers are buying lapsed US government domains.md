The official web addresses of 276 US towns and counties now serve gambling or spam pages, most of them Indonesian online slots, according to a census of 15,751 local governments [published by Mysterium VPN](https://www.mysteriumvpn.com/blog/data-and-research/old-american-government-websites) on 28 October. A companion census found 42% of county election websites still on domains anyone can register, days before the 3 November midterms.

The two studies describe one problem from opposite ends. The first counts what became of old official addresses once local governments stopped renewing them. The second counts the election offices still running on the kind of domain that ends up in the first study’s tables. Both datasets were collected on 17 October 2026.

## What happened to the old official addresses?

Of the 12,791 local governments whose listed website could be read, 2,220 (17%) are listed at an address that no longer reaches them. For 499 (3.9%), the address now serves someone else. The other 1,721 (13%) are dead: the domain is gone, has no web host, never answers or shows an error page. A further 2,960 sites refused automated visitors, and the researchers left them out of every rate rather than guess.

The starting list was Wikidata, the structured database behind Wikipedia’s infoboxes and Google’s knowledge panels, which records an “official website” for 15,751 US counties and municipalities. The team added every county and city domain in CISA’s .gov registry, visited all 17,907 hosts once, followed every redirect and classified the final page with published rules. Registration records came from RDAP, the registration data protocol that succeeded WHOIS.

| **In someone else’s hands** | **499** |
