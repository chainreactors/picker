# British government takes services offline following ‘cyber incident’

Britain’s Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) said Friday that it had experienced a cyber incident, with several services taken down as a precaution.

The nature of the incident has not been disclosed. In an emailed statement, a spokesperson for the DWP said the department had “taken some DWP online services offline as a precaution following a cyber incident.”

The department is the largest in the British government by spending and administers the state pension and a range of other benefits. Millions of people in the country depend on its payments for essential bills.

“Regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal,” the spokesperson added.

The department said it was “working closely with the National Cyber Security Centre and Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate and to bring services back online as quickly as possible to minimise any disruption.”

The incident comes days after the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) [said](https://therecord.media/cyberattacks-uk-artificial-intelligence) it has observed “a marked rise” since September in cyberattacks assisted by artificial intelligence.

Earlier this year, the British government [acknowledged](https://therecord.media/uk-government-cyber-action-plan) its yearslong approach to its own cybersecurity was flawed and warned it will be impossible to meet a previous target of securing all government organizations from known cyber vulnerabilities and attack methods by 2030.

“To protect our critical national infrastructure, defend public institutions and maintain public confidence in essential public services, we must achieve a radical shift in approach and a step change in pace,” it said at the time, warning the public sector faced a “critically high” cyber risk.
