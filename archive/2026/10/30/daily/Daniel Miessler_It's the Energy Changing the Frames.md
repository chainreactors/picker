My problems don't change when my energy does; I just see them completely differently

I think so much of winning at life comes down to something that's hard to conceptualize. And I guess the way I encapsulate it is **energy management**.

As I've gotten older and wiser, I have started noticing a pattern where my creativity and my positivity, and therefore my output and outlook and attitude and everything, are all based around how energized I feel. For me, when I have energy and I'm excited, I have all the answers.

All sorts of positive outcomes come from it. These are things like wanting to help people, and wanting to make more stuff, and wanting to write more, and wanting to do art, and wanting to be more productive, and just absolutely loving my entire day, and my projects, and my work, and my partner, and my friends, and basically everything. I'm basically just so enthused about life and excited and positive.

And it doesn't mean I lose my realism, or my understanding of how bad things are or how bad things can get. It's not like mania. But it has a lot of the positives of mania, and I think it's really powerful.

## The low-energy version of me [​](#the-low-energy-version-of-me)

The opposite of this is basically **low energy**. In this mood:

* I am more concerned about what others think about my work.
* I am more worried about losing things than gaining or producing.
* I tend to [consume more versus create more](/blog/creativity-vs-consumption) in this mode.
* I tend to think more about myself and less about others.

And overall I just have low mood and low output. So it's very much not just a work output thing.

## It's the energy changing the frames [​](#it-s-the-energy-changing-the-frames)

If you remember what I talked about with [frames are everything](/blog/framing-is-everything), that's commentary on life outlook. **Frames** meaning perspectives and ways that we view the world.

What I'm really saying is that when I'm low energy, it's not just my energy that changes. All my frames change, like the way I perceive everything, including input from others and how I talk to myself. They all fundamentally change because of these frame shifts.

So in other words, I just have a dramatically different life experience when I am high energy versus low energy.

## Same problems, different energy [​](#same-problems-different-energy)

It's not that you can solve all your problems at a higher energy state. It's that you see the problems completely differently.

When I switch energy states, my problems don't change. I still have the same challenges and problems, but when I am in a low energy state, and therefore negative framing, those same problems feel insurmountable, numerous, and essentially unsolvable.

And when I switch energy states, I just see the problems completely differently. I see them as tractable, soluble, and things that I can deal with. And I dare say, even exciting.

And this change in perspective on how one observes adversity just cannot be overstated.

## What produces one or the other [​](#what-produces-one-or-the-other)

So that raises the question of, okay, well, what are the things that produce one or the other? And this is where a daily routine and discipline and habits and all these things start to make a massive difference. The things are fairly obvious, with some nuances, but in general it starts with **exercise, sleep, and diet**.

📚 I wrote about this same pattern in 2016: [How to Handle a Dip in Creativity and Productivity](/blog/how-to-handle-a-dip-in-creativity-and-productivity).

I already have a routine myself, which I am able to stick to when I am on routine. And when I am off routine, that's when my energy drops. When I am on routine, my energy is absolutely, insanely high.

> When I stop exercising my creative energy drops by probably 50-75%. And the more sedentary I am, the worse the effect. [How to Handle a Dip in Creativity and Productivity (2016)](/blog/how-to-handle-a-dip-in-creativity-and-productivity)

## You can't see it from inside [​](#you-can-t-see-it-from-inside)

And so, my whole game recently has become basically trying to understand what my current energy state is, what my current creativity state is, and what my current mood state is. So much so that I actually have these things which I try to log daily inside of my AI harness, [LifeOS](/blog/personal-ai-infrastructure). And it also displays them along with my **Oura readiness score**, which is kind of a proxy for predicting the energy level I'm likely to have, because it includes sleep and restfulness and stuff like that.

💍 Oura's readiness score combines sleep, resting heart rate and recovery into one number each morning, so a low score is an early hint of a low-energy day.

Essentially, I've tried to make this very visible to myself in order to be able to self-detect as accurately as possible when I am low energy. Because the problem is, when I'm in my low energy state, my frames have shifted. Therefore, I'm not thinking about causes, and I'm not thinking about, "Hey, I need to change my state."

What I'm instead doing is reacting to inputs and the lack of inputs, and negativity affects me quite a bit more, and criticism affects me quite a bit more.

## Change the state first [​](#change-the-state-first)

So my goal there is to basically say, okay, look: when my frames are shifted in that way, when I am low energy and responding to the world and consuming the world and I'm not in my positive creative mode, my priority should not be responding to all the negativity and working within the low energy and negative frames. My priority should be to change my energy state.

* Do I need to have a high quality meal?
* Do I need to sleep?
* Do I need to take a nap?
* Do I need to basically do a cleanse?
* Have I not been resting properly?
* Have I been eating a bunch of garbage?

And essentially get my basics back on routine as quickly as possible to get my state raised.

And obviously there are other ways to do that artificially, with caffeine or nootropics or other methods for raising energy, especially mental energy. But I find that really tactical ways to do this are to take a nap, or go for a walk outside.

Usually, if I get into one of these states, especially if it lasts a couple days or more, it's because I've stopped exercising. Maybe I've been stressed because of work, maybe I've been traveling, or whatever it is, but I'm locked into a spiral where I'm forgetting to do the basics.

🔄 Skipping exercise and eating badly hurts sleep, and bad sleep makes it more likely you skip exercise and eat badly again.

So, I'm literally by definition now off of routine, which means I'm not walking, I'm not exercising, I'm not lifting weights, I'm eating like shit, which all of those then hit my sleep, then my sleep is bad, which magnifies the chances that I'm going to repeat those spiraling behaviors.
