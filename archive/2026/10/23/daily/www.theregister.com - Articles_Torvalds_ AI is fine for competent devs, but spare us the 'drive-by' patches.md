Linus Torvalds is embracing AI to write Linux kernel code, and some people are having fits. He has a message for them. So do I.

Roy Schestowitz, editor of Tux Machines, summed up his reaction to the news as "[Linus Torvalds Turns to Slop](https://news.tuxmachines.org/n/2026/10/15/Linus_Torvalds_Turns_to_Slop_Creates_More_Problems_in_Linux.shtml)[.](https://news.tuxmachines.org/n/2026/10/15/Linus_Torvalds_Turns_to_Slop_Creates_More_Problems_in_Linux.shtml)" In the podcast [Pivot to AI](https://pivot-to-ai.com/)[,](https://pivot-to-ai.com/) podcaster David Gerard agreed: "Linux kernel goes full AI slop code." Meanwhile, in the [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/) comments section, commenter "LordVile" wrote: "[This is a publicity stunt from his AI overlords](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/linux/linus-torvalds-posts-his-first-llm-assisted-patch-to-the-linux-kernel-after-issuing-clear-pro-ai-directives-the-bossman-makes-use-of-ai-tooling-himself%23xenforo-comments-3901456) [The Linux Foundation] who pay his wage." They followed this by saying this is the "same reason he's flipped-flopped on AI."

I've known Linus for a few decades, and I don't take his use of AI as evidence that he's suddenly abandoned his standards. And the very idea that he'd do anything he didn't want to do with Linux on behalf of the Linux Foundation, or anyone else, is ludicrous.

REG AD

I get it, though. There are reasons to be suspicious of AI. I've written about many of them. For instance, [AI model collapse](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/05/27/ai-model-collapse-is-not-what-we-paid-for/1505941), which happens when AI engines learn from other AI models and their ties to the truth fray. It's a very real problem. Not, mind you, that LLMs find the grist for their answer mills from very reliable sources in the first place.

Even when AI gets good data, as it does in programming, its owners pay little to no attention to the copyrights and licenses protecting the open source code it picked up its coding tricks from. As for paying for the information it runs on, please! The very idea that [Big AI must pay writers, artists, you name it, for using their work is anathema](https://www.theregister.com/columnists/2026/09/27/big-ais-content-problem-take-the-work-keep-the-money/5299007) to them.
