# Voters Reject ALPRs and Surveillance Tech at the Ballot Box

![](https://www.eff.org/files/styles/resized_banner/public/banner_library/vote_1.png?itok=mJW0mrHm)

Do [automated license plate readers (ALPRs)](https://sls.eff.org/technologies/automated-license-plate-readers-alprs) have any place in our communities? That question has been a hot topic in [city council meetings](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/local/san-diego-city-council-votes-end-flock-contract-turn-off-alpr-cameras/4102682/), [community conversations](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/faculty-research/policy-topics/science-technology-data/democracy-happening-fight-over-flock-cameras), and [news coverage](https://www.themarshallproject.org/records/811-license-plate-readers) around the country for months. This past election day, a couple of U.S communities took it further: to the ballot.

And overwhelmingly, voters decided that, no, these devices should not be deployed on our streets. [We agree!](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/08/effs-policy-position-alpr-surveillance-eliminate-it-and-reduce-its-harms)

In Maine, the [coastal community of York voted](https://ballotpedia.org/York%2C_Maine%2C_Article_5%2C_Flock_Camera_Removal_and_Voter_Approval_for_Surveillance_Technology_Initiative_%28November_2026%29) to remove the town’s existing ALPR cameras and require that any future proposed installations receive voter approval before proceeding. The local initiative ended up on the ballot after constituents [gathered enough signatures](https://www.yorkmaine.org/DocumentCenter/View/13089/November-2026-General-and-Special-Budget-Referendum-and-Citizens-Petition-Ordinance-to-Stop-ALPRs) on a citizens’ petition for a local ordinance. On Election Day, nearly three-quarters of York voters supported the ban.

Thousands of miles away, voters in League City, Texas also voted [against allowing the local police department](https://www.leaguecitytx.gov/2670/Elections) to continue use of ALPR cameras. That vote was formally approved on November 17, which began a 72-hour window to remove the cameras. Since the election, [the cameras have been “bagged”](https://www.leaguecitytx.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/6467) or covered to prevent the collection of data.

At least one city also decided to expand protections afforded to their residents when it comes to the use of surveillance technology.
