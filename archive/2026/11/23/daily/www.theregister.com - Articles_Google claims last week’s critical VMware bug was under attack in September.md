Google’s Mandiant unit has claimed that the critical flaw VMware last week revealed in its vCenter product was under attack by a China-linked threat actor which knew of the bug in July and exploited it in September.

VMware [revealed several flaws](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/20/flaws-in-vmware-and-kvm-need-attention-if-you-have-time-while-fixing-java-php-and-everything-else/5311193) on November 19. The big nasty is CVE-2026-123890, which allows unauthorized access to vCenter, the management console for hosts and guest VMs.

According to seven researchers from Google’s threat intelligence group (formerly Mandiant), a crew it calls ARMORY CASTLE that it suspects of having links to the People’s Republic of China exploited CVE-2026-123890 by first compromising remote access appliances from BeyondTrust.

REG AD

Google’s [analysis](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/armory-castle-exploits-vsphere) suggests the crew uses that toehold to establish access to other Linux appliances before moving deeper into victims’ networks.

REG AD

If the crew finds vCenter, it deploys a custom malware tool called “BONESAW” designed to slice into the VMware product. In the incident Google observed, BONESAW let attackers “forge authentication tokens, bypass MFA, and impersonate any user.”

The gang put that access to work by conducting an inventory of the victim’s VMware setup, scooping up data describing the ESXi file system, attached disks, and mounted Virtual Machine File System volumes. ARMORY CASTLE also added an SSH public key to the root account’s authorized keys, and “conducted further reconnaissance and data theft on the ESXi filesystem using the root account.”

Google also found evidence of BONESAW being used in July, on a server an unidentified entity had patched against CVE-2026-59309 – another authentication bypass vulnerability in vCenter.

And BONESAW was just one of five tools Google thinks ARMORY CASTLE created as part of its plan to attack vCenter. It also made webshells named SLIMSAVAGE and CAGEMATCH, a backdoor called BEARBONES, and a SAML utility called SAWSNAKE.
