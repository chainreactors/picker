# [Ex-OpenAI Employee Accused of Bugging Company Offices Granted $1 Million Bail](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/ex-openai-employee-accused-of-bugging.html)

**Ravie Lakshmanan**Nov 25, 2026Insider Threat / Artificial Intelligence

[![](data:image/png;base64...)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgDaKaEIQ9XIhyphenhyphen7wjO1dtGE_h6MwLEEd4or1U6Ud39Ce7BbZfU817kR-mLr4bT5n_dMzAujT3VzKACvto1wsiHVfLjOLyGFnYPsf4PH_DHuog0NWylpF1RXoFIYhb84sb8T7RHCx4qnBCCJnNDKTkjoMQ-KPDaKgHaLW7fII7cOhPzc9XKC0OGGOjjE-ygf/s1700-nu-rw-lo-l85-e365/openai.jpg)

A former OpenAI employee accused of concealing a covert recording device at the company's San Francisco offices will be allowed to be released from jail on a $1 million bond, a San Francisco federal court judge ruled Tuesday.

Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national, was [arrested](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/openai-employee-arrested-for-allegedly.html) on October 9, 2026, for allegedly bugging the artificial intelligence (AI) company's offices with the aim of recording sensitive conversations.

Subsequent court filings have [pointed out](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/ex-openai-engineer-detained-in-spy.html) that Alfozan was in touch on WhatsApp with a member of the Saudi royal family, as well as expressed interest in moving back to Saudi Arabia following OpenAI's initial public offering (IPO).

He has been charged with using or endeavoring to use an electronic, mechanical, or other device to intercept oral communications. If convicted, Alfozan faces a maximum penalty of 5 years in prison and a $250,000 fine, followed by 3 years of supervised release.

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/threat-protection-d)

"At this time, we have no indications of this individual inappropriately accessing or exfiltrating model weights, source code, or customer data," the ChatGPT-maker said at the time.

Alfozan [pleaded not guilty](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/former-openai-engineer-pleads-not.html) to the charges during his appearance in San Francisco federal court earlier this month.

According to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/openai-bugging-aflozan-release-saudi.html), the decision comes after prosecutors told the court last week that they had "found no new evidence to support the theory that Mr. Alfozan was stealing trade secrets, or to support the speculation that he was acting as an unregistered foreign agent for Saudi Arabia."

However, they said investigators have found [hundreds](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/23/technology/openai-engineer-secret-recordings.html) of [audio files](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/openai-staffer-likely-captured-secrets-on-hidden-bug-us-says) on the defendant's personal devices, including dozens of recordings of meetings or conversations that took place at OpenAI throughout September 2026.

Some of these involved meetings with OpenAI customers and contained material protected by attorney-client privilege, prosecutors said, adding he possessed a photograph of what's believed to be a term sheet for a "strategic partnership" between OpenAI and another company.

"What the defendant did or intended to do with those audio files remains under investigation," they said.

[![Cybersecurity](data:image/png;base64...)](https://thehackernews.uk/network-defense-dd)

Under the bail conditions, Alfozan will be required to wear a GPS ankle monitor, observe a curfew from 7 p.m. to 7 a.m., stay within the Northern District of California, and avoid airports and train and bus stations. He is expected to be released on Wednesday morning.

"There is no spy story that we're talking about right now," U.S. Magistrate Judge Alex G. Tse said, per [The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/tech/saudi-engineer-accused-of-bugging-openai-to-be-released-on-1-million-bond-954f048e). "The government doesn't have that."
